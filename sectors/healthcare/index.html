What healthcare marketing needs to get right

Patients research carefully before they book. They want to know who will treat them, what a treatment involves, what it costs and whether the clinic is properly registered. Healthcare marketing also sits under rules that most sectors do not face, including the CAP Code and ASA guidance on health claims, and, depending on the service, professional body and regulator guidance.

We plan content and campaigns with those constraints in mind from the start: clear, factual service pages, careful claims and calls to action that respect patient choice.

What a healthcare marketing agency in the UK should do for your clinic

A healthcare marketing agency in the UK should help patients find, trust and book with your clinic, while keeping every claim within advertising rules and your own clinical sign-off. In practice that means a fast, accurate website, strong local search visibility, carefully worded paid campaigns and content your clinicians are happy to put their names to.

This guide goes deeper than the overview above. It covers how UK patients actually choose a private provider, which channels earn their place, what to measure, the rules to respect and the questions worth asking before you hire anyone. For a longer read on choosing a partner, see our guide to healthcare marketing agencies in the UK.

How UK patients choose a private provider

Most private healthcare journeys start with a symptom, a treatment name or a frustration with waiting times. A patient might search for a scan, a physiotherapy appointment, a dental treatment or a specialist consultation, often adding a town name or “near me”. From there the journey tends to follow a predictable pattern:

  1. Research. Reading about the condition or treatment, often on several sites, and increasingly asking AI assistants for an overview.
  2. Shortlist. Comparing local clinics on Google Maps, reviews, location, opening hours and whether prices are published.
  3. Verify. Checking who the practitioners are, their registration with the relevant professional body and whether the clinic is registered with the appropriate regulator for the services it provides.
  4. Book or enquire. Choosing whichever clinic makes the next step easiest: an online booking slot, a short form or a clear phone number.
  5. Return and refer. Coming back for follow-ups and recommending the clinic to family, which reviews and email reminders can support.

Each stage needs something different. Content and SEO matter most during research, local search and reviews during the shortlist, practitioner pages during verification and a clean booking route at the end.

Rules to respect in healthcare marketing

Healthcare is one of the most tightly governed areas of UK advertising. The points below are general information rather than legal advice, and final sign-off always rests with your clinical lead, compliance team or solicitor.

  • The CAP Code and ASA rulings generally require health claims to be backed by evidence and not to exaggerate outcomes. Words such as “guaranteed”, “painless” or “permanent” tend to attract scrutiny.
  • Prescription-only medicines generally cannot be advertised to the public in the UK, which affects how some aesthetic and weight-management services can be described.
  • Professional bodies such as the GMC, GDC, NMC or HCPC may publish their own guidance on advertising by registrants.
  • Platform policies on Google and Meta add another layer, and some healthcare categories may need certification or face restrictions.
  • Patient data in enquiry forms is sensitive under UK GDPR, so forms, tracking and remarketing need to be set up with care.

We draft with these constraints in mind, flag anything that looks risky and never publish without your approval.

Common problems we see on clinic websites

When clinics come to us, the issues are often similar. Treatment pages are thin or copied from suppliers, practitioner profiles are missing, prices are hidden behind a phone call and the booking button disappears on mobile. Old blog posts may make claims nobody would sign off today. A technical SEO audit plus a content review usually shows where to start, and a careful redesign that protects existing rankings is often better than starting from scratch.

What good healthcare content looks like

Good clinic content is specific, calm and honest. It explains who a treatment is suitable for, who it may not suit, what happens on the day, typical recovery and how pricing works, and it encourages patients to ask questions at consultation rather than promising outcomes.

Questions to ask a healthcare marketing agency

  • How do you handle clinical review and sign-off before anything goes live?
  • Which advertising rules and platform policies do you check content against?
  • How will you track bookings, not just clicks, without exposing patient data?
  • Who owns the website, ad accounts and analytics if we part ways?
  • What will you not do, for example fake reviews or before-and-after claims we cannot support?
  • What does reporting look like each month, and who reads it?

An agency that answers these clearly, and is comfortable saying no to risky tactics, is usually a safer partner than one promising quick wins.

How projects run remotely across the UK

We work with clinics across the UK without needing to visit, which keeps costs focused on the work itself. A typical engagement runs like this:

  1. Discovery call. We learn your treatments, locations, capacity and which services you most want to grow.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you market private clinics within advertising rules?

Yes. We write and review healthcare content with the CAP Code and platform healthcare policies in mind, and every claim is signed off by you before publishing.

Do you build clinic websites?

Yes, on WordPress or another suitable platform, with treatment pages, practitioner profiles and enquiry or booking routes.

How do patients find local clinics online?

Mostly through Google searches and Maps. Local SEO and a complete Google Business Profile are usually the first priorities.

Can you run Google Ads for healthcare?

Yes, where the treatment is permitted to advertise. Some healthcare categories need certification or have restrictions, which we check first.

Who checks medical accuracy?

Your clinicians. We write clear, plain-English content, but medical facts are always reviewed and approved by your team.

What does a healthcare marketing agency in the UK do?

It helps clinics and healthcare providers attract and convert patients through websites, local SEO, paid search and content, while working within advertising rules and leaving final sign-off with the clinic.

Should a private clinic publish its prices online?

Many patients look for prices before enquiring, so publishing starting prices or ranges can help. It is your decision, and any prices shown should be accurate and kept up to date.

Can you write practitioner profile pages?

Yes. We draft profiles from information you provide, such as qualifications and registrations you genuinely hold, and each practitioner approves their own page.

How do you track bookings without sharing patient data?

We track that a booking or enquiry happened, not what it contained, and we avoid sending health information to analytics or ad platforms. Your data protection lead should review the set-up.

Can you help a clinic with several locations?

Yes. Multi-site clinics usually need a page and Google Business Profile for each location, with treatment content shared sensibly across them.

Do you respond to patient reviews for us?

We can draft polite, general responses for you to approve. Replies should never confirm that someone is a patient or discuss their treatment.
