Marketing for financial advisers in the UK: what actually works

Marketing for financial advisers in the UK works best when it builds trust slowly and clearly: a credible website, search visibility for the advice areas and towns you serve, and content that explains topics without crossing into personal advice. Every piece also needs to pass through your compliance sign-off, which shapes the whole plan.

This guide adds depth to the overview above, covering how clients choose an adviser, which channels earn their place, what to measure, the rules to respect and the questions to ask any agency. For a deeper look at search, see our financial adviser SEO guide.

How UK clients choose a financial adviser

Choosing an adviser is a high-trust decision, usually triggered by a life event: retirement approaching, an inheritance, selling a business, a divorce or buying a home. The journey often looks like this:

  1. Trigger and research. Searches such as “pension advice near me” or “inheritance tax planning”, and questions to AI assistants.
  2. Referral check. Many clients come from accountants, solicitors or friends, then look you up online before calling.
  3. Verification. Checking the FCA register, adviser profiles, qualifications, fees and reviews.
  4. First contact. A call or enquiry form, often asking about fees and whether you work with people like them.
  5. Ongoing relationship. Reviews, newsletters and introductions to family members.

Because referrals are so important, your website often does its main job after someone has already heard your name. It needs to confirm the recommendation, not just attract strangers.

Rules to respect

The points below are general information, not compliance or legal advice. Prism Works Studio is a marketing agency, and final approval of every promotion rests with your compliance team, network or solicitor.

  • Financial promotions. Marketing by regulated firms is generally expected to be fair, clear and not misleading, with balanced information about risks as well as benefits.
  • Consumer Duty. The FCA expects firms to communicate in a way customers can understand, which affects tone, structure and how fees are explained.
  • Past performance and projections. These usually need appropriate warnings and context, and are best avoided in general marketing.
  • Social media. Posts, including short ones, can count as financial promotions, and the FCA has published guidance on this.
  • Testimonials and reviews. Handle with care and in line with your compliance team’s view.
  • Platform policies. Google and Meta have their own financial services advertising rules, which may require verification.

Common symptoms and fixes

Adviser websites often share the same issues. Service pages are generic and could belong to any firm. Adviser profiles are missing or out of date. Fees are hidden entirely, which can put off the right clients. Blog posts are bought-in market commentary that adds little and takes compliance time. Local search is ignored even though most clients live within a reasonable distance. The fix is usually fewer, better pages: one strong page per advice area, clear adviser profiles, plain-English fee information where you choose to publish it, and a short set of genuinely useful guides. A technical SEO audit and local SEO work usually come next.

What good adviser content looks like

Useful adviser content explains a topic in plain English, sets out the general options and the questions a client should consider, and makes clear that personal circumstances matter and advice should be taken. It avoids predictions, implied guarantees and one-sided enthusiasm for any product. Good examples include guides to how pension drawdown works in general terms, what to expect from a first meeting, how ongoing advice fees are usually structured, and checklists for life events such as retirement or receiving an inheritance.

Content like this tends to be easier for compliance teams to approve, because it is educational rather than promotional, and it gives search engines and AI assistants clear, citable pages about the areas you advise on. It also gives introducers such as accountants and solicitors something genuinely helpful to share with their own clients.

We suggest a short content calendar agreed with compliance each quarter, so review time is planned rather than rushed, and older pages are checked regularly so rates, allowances and rules mentioned are kept current.

Where your network supplies approved content, we can adapt layout and internal links around it rather than rewriting it.

Questions to ask a marketing agency

  • How will your process fit with our compliance or network sign-off?
  • Do you understand the difference between general guidance and personal advice?
  • How will you measure lead quality, not just the number of enquiries?
  • Who owns the website, domain, ad accounts and content?
  • How do you handle reviews and testimonials?
  • What will you avoid doing, even if it might bring more leads?

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is our website a financial promotion?

Generally, marketing content from regulated firms is treated as a financial promotion. Your compliance team or network should approve it; we prepare content to make that process straightforward.

Can you guarantee FCA compliance?

No marketing agency can. We write carefully and follow your compliance process, but sign-off is always your firm’s responsibility.

Which channels work for IFAs?

Search (for people actively looking for advice), LinkedIn (for professional audiences) and referrals supported by a credible website usually work best.

Do you work with mortgage brokers?

Yes. Mortgage brokers, IFAs and wealth managers are all part of this sector.

Can you write blog content for us?

Yes. Educational content works well for advice firms, as long as it informs rather than gives personal recommendations.

What does marketing for financial advisers in the UK involve?

Usually a credible website, local and niche SEO, a Google Business Profile, selective LinkedIn activity and useful content, all approved through the firm’s compliance process.

Should financial advisers publish their fees?

Many clients look for fee information first, so publishing how you charge can help. What you publish and how is a decision for your firm and compliance team.

Can you work with our network’s approval process?

Yes. We build review time into the plan and supply drafts in a format your network or compliance team can comment on.

Do adviser profiles help with enquiries?

Usually, yes. Clients want to know who they will speak to, so clear profiles with genuine qualifications tend to build trust.

Is it worth advertising on Google as an IFA?

It can be for specific advice areas, but costs can be high and platform financial services policies apply. We usually start with SEO and the website first.

Can you help us get more introducer referrals?

We can support introducer relationships with a stronger website, useful guides introducers can share and LinkedIn content, though the relationships themselves stay with you.
