What a hospitality marketing agency in the UK should focus on

A hospitality marketing agency in the UK should focus on one thing above all: turning people who are already looking for somewhere to stay, eat or celebrate into direct bookings on your own website. That means a fast mobile site, a booking button that never hides, a complete Google Business Profile and campaigns timed around the moments guests actually plan.

This guide adds detail to the overview above: how UK guests choose a venue, which channels to prioritise, what to measure, the rules worth knowing and the questions to ask before hiring anyone. For a broader read on choosing a partner, see our hospitality marketing agency guide.

How UK guests choose a hotel, restaurant or venue

Hospitality journeys are short for some decisions and long for others. A Friday-night table might be booked within minutes of a search on a phone, while a wedding, Christmas party or summer break can involve weeks of comparison. Most journeys still pass through the same steps:

  1. Inspiration. Social posts, friends, local guides or an AI assistant suggesting places in a town.
  2. Search and maps. Searches such as “restaurant near me”, “dog-friendly hotel Cotswolds” or “private dining Manchester”, with Google Maps doing much of the shortlisting.
  3. Comparison. Reviews, photos, menus, room types, availability and price, often on an online travel agent as well as your own site.
  4. Booking. Whichever route is quickest and feels safest. If your own site is slow or confusing, the guest books through a platform and you pay commission.
  5. Return visits. Email, offers and good memories bring guests back, ideally booking direct the second time.

The commercial goal is simple: shift more of steps three and four onto your own website, and own the relationship from step five onwards.

Seasonality and timing

UK hospitality runs on a calendar. Christmas party bookings often start being researched in late summer, wedding venues are compared a year or more ahead, and summer breaks peak in the first months of the year. Campaigns and content work best when they go live before demand builds, not when it peaks. We plan a simple twelve-month calendar with you covering events, menus, school holidays and local occasions, so pages and ads are ready in time.

Rules and practicalities to respect

The points below are general information, not legal advice. Your own solicitor or licensing adviser should confirm anything specific to your business.

  • Price display. Consumer protection rules generally expect prices to be clear, including any mandatory fees or service charges, so “from” prices should be genuinely available.
  • Alcohol advertising. The CAP Code has specific rules on alcohol ads, including not targeting under-18s and not linking drinking to social success.
  • Allergen information. Menus online should be consistent with the allergen information you provide in the venue.
  • Reviews. Incentivised, fake or selectively suppressed reviews can breach consumer law and platform policies. We help you ask genuine guests for reviews, nothing more.
  • Cookies and email. Marketing emails and tracking need consent under UK GDPR and PECR.

Common symptoms and fixes

The same issues come up across hotels, pubs and restaurants. The menu is a PDF that is hard to read on a phone. The booking button sits below the fold. Opening hours on Google differ from the website. Room pages lack photos and key details. Each of these is fixable, often without a full rebuild, and a careful redesign protects any rankings you already have. For independent venues, our local SEO guide is a good starting point, and SEO for small businesses covers the basics in plain English.

What a strong venue website includes

The best hospitality websites are simple. They show the venue in genuine, recent photos, keep menus and room details current, display opening hours that match Google, and put a booking button in the same place on every page. Private hire, events and gift voucher pages give guests reasons to book beyond a standard table or room, and each can be promoted on its own when the season is right. Accessibility information, parking and directions answer common questions before guests need to phone.

Questions to ask a hospitality marketing agency

  • How will you measure direct bookings when our booking engine sits on another domain?
  • Do you have experience with our booking or reservation system, or will you need its documentation?
  • How do you plan campaigns around our seasons, events and quiet periods?
  • Who creates photos and video, and who approves posts before they go out?
  • Who owns the website, social accounts and ad accounts?
  • What does a monthly report include, and how is it tied to bookings?

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How can a hotel get more direct bookings?

Make booking on your own site fast and obvious on mobile, keep rates competitive with OTAs, bid on your brand name in Google Ads and keep your Google Business Profile complete.

Do you work with restaurants?

Yes. Restaurants are one of the business types we work with most, from websites and menus to local SEO and social retainers.

Can you integrate my booking system?

Usually, yes. Most booking and reservation tools offer a widget or link that we integrate cleanly into the website.

Is social media included?

Social media retainers are one of our core starting services for local businesses.

Which retainer suits a single venue?

The Launch retainer is designed for small local businesses. See our pricing page for what each level includes.

What should a hospitality marketing agency in the UK do?

Help your venue win more direct bookings through a fast website, local search, paid campaigns and email, with tracking that shows which channels actually produce bookings.

How do I track bookings from a separate booking engine?

Usually with cross-domain tracking in GA4 and, where the booking system supports it, a confirmation event. We check what your system allows before promising anything.

Should a restaurant menu be a PDF?

A PDF can sit alongside it, but a proper web page is easier to read on a phone, easier for search engines to understand and simpler to update.

When should we promote Christmas bookings?

Many venues start promoting Christmas parties in late summer or early autumn, because groups often plan well ahead. Your own past booking data is the best guide.

Can you sell gift vouchers online for us?

Yes, through a simple online shop or a voucher tool connected to your site, scoped as a separate project.

Do you work with pubs and bars?

Yes. Pubs and bars benefit from the same basics: strong local search, clear menus and events, and a simple way to book a table.
