UK ecommerce

Built for UK shoppers

UK shoppers expect clear delivery options and costs, easy returns, and familiar ways to pay such as cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal and buy-now-pay-later options like Klarna or Clearpay. Prices should be shown including VAT for consumers, and stores need a clear privacy notice and cookie consent under UK GDPR and PECR.

What an ecommerce marketing agency in the UK should deliver

An ecommerce marketing agency in the UK should grow profitable orders, not just traffic, by joining up your store, product feed, search visibility, paid campaigns and conversion rate. The right partner looks at the whole path from first search to repeat purchase and fixes the weakest link first.

The overview above lists what we do. This guide explains how UK shoppers buy, how each channel earns its place, what to measure and how to judge any agency, including us. For a longer comparison of options, read our ecommerce marketing agency guide.

How UK shoppers find and buy online

Online shopping journeys vary by price point. A low-cost repeat purchase might happen in minutes, while a sofa or bike can involve weeks of research. Most journeys still share a shape:

  1. Discovery. Social feeds, Google Shopping results, AI assistant suggestions or a friend’s recommendation.
  2. Research. Category searches, buying guides, reviews and comparisons with competitors and marketplaces.
  3. Consideration. Delivery cost and speed, returns policy, payment options and whether the store looks trustworthy.
  4. Checkout. Where small frictions, such as forced account creation or surprise delivery charges, cause abandoned baskets.
  5. Retention. Post-purchase email, reorders and reviews, where much of the profit in ecommerce is made.

Symptoms and fixes

When a store stalls, the cause is often one of a handful of issues:

  • Traffic but few sales. Usually product pages, delivery messaging or checkout friction. See our ecommerce CRO guide.
  • Shopping ads with poor results. Often weak product titles, missing attributes or disapproved items in the feed.
  • Category pages that do not rank. Thin copy, duplicate filtered URLs or weak internal linking. Our ecommerce SEO guide goes into detail.
  • Slow mobile pages. Heavy apps, large images and scripts, which affect both rankings and conversion.
  • One-off buyers. No post-purchase email flow or reason to come back.

Platform choice and when to rebuild

Most UK small and mid-sized retailers do well on Shopify or WooCommerce. Shopify suits teams who want a managed platform and an app ecosystem; WooCommerce suits those who want more control on WordPress. Our Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison covers the trade-offs. A rebuild is not always the answer: improving the existing store is often faster and cheaper, and a rebuild should only happen with a redirect plan that protects your rankings.

Rules to respect

This is general information rather than legal advice; your solicitor or compliance adviser should confirm anything specific.

  • Consumer contracts rules generally give online shoppers cancellation rights, and your terms and returns page should reflect them.
  • Price and discount claims such as “was” prices should reflect genuine previous prices under consumer protection rules and ASA guidance.
  • Reviews must be genuine; fake or selectively published reviews can breach consumer law.
  • Cookies and email marketing need consent under UK GDPR and PECR.
  • Restricted products such as supplements, alcohol or age-restricted goods have extra advertising rules and platform policies.

The foundations to fix first

Before spending more on ads, a few foundations decide whether that spend can work. Tracking comes first: purchases, revenue and key events should be recorded accurately in GA4 and passed to ad platforms, with consent handled properly. The product feed comes next, with clear titles, accurate prices, availability, GTINs where products have them and good images. Then the store itself: fast mobile pages, clear delivery and returns information near the add-to-basket button, and a checkout with as few steps as your platform allows.

Once those are in place, channels can be judged fairly. Without them, it is hard to tell whether a campaign is failing or simply not being measured. We usually spend the first weeks of an engagement on these basics, because they make every later decision more reliable.

Merchandising also matters. Best sellers, seasonal lines and higher-margin products should be easy to find from the homepage and category pages, and collections can be built around how customers actually shop, such as by occasion, size or budget.

Retention and repeat purchase

Acquiring a new customer usually costs more than selling again to an existing one, so retention deserves a place in any ecommerce plan. Simple email flows do much of the work: a welcome series for new subscribers, abandoned basket and browse reminders, a post-purchase sequence with care tips or related products, and a win-back message for lapsed customers. Reviews collected after delivery support both conversion and search, provided they are genuine and published in full.

Questions to ask an ecommerce marketing agency

  • Will you report on profit or margin, or only on revenue and return on ad spend?
  • How will you handle the product feed, and who fixes disapprovals?
  • What will you check before recommending a rebuild over improvements?
  • Who owns the store, ad accounts, Merchant Center and analytics?
  • How do you test changes to product pages and checkout?
  • What happens to our rankings if we change platform?

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do you build on Shopify or WooCommerce?

Both. We recommend the platform that fits your catalogue, budget and how much control you want.

How long does an ecommerce build take?

Ecommerce builds range from 5–12 weeks depending on catalogue size and custom functionality.

Can you run Google Shopping for my store?

Yes. We set up or audit your Merchant Center feed and manage Shopping and Performance Max campaigns.

Do you help with marketplace listings?

Our ecommerce SEO covers marketplace listings such as Amazon and eBay as well as your own store.

Can you improve my store without rebuilding it?

Often, yes. Speed, product page and checkout improvements can make a real difference before a full rebuild is needed.

What does an ecommerce marketing agency in the UK do?

It grows online sales by improving the store, product feed, SEO, paid campaigns, conversion rate and email, measured against orders and profit rather than traffic.

Is return on ad spend the right measure?

It is useful but incomplete. Margin, discounting and returns all affect profit, so we look beyond return on ad spend wherever your data allows.

Should I fix my store or rebuild it?

Often fixing is quicker and cheaper. A rebuild makes sense when the platform genuinely limits you, and it needs a redirect plan to protect rankings.

Do I need Google Merchant Center?

For Google Shopping and Performance Max campaigns, yes. A clean, accurate product feed is the foundation of those campaigns.

Can you set up abandoned basket emails?

Yes, using your platform or email tool, with consent handled properly under UK GDPR and PECR.

How quickly can CRO changes show results?

It depends on traffic. Busy stores can see clear signals within weeks; smaller stores may need longer or rely on best-practice fixes rather than formal tests.
