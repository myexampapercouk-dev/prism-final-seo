Digital marketing agency prices in the UK: how to choose the right plan

Digital marketing agency prices in the UK usually depend on how much work is done each month, which channels are covered and whether advertising spend is managed. At Prism Works Studio, retainers start at £350 a month for Launch, £750 for Growth and £1,500 for Scale, with websites and ecommerce builds priced as separate projects and ad spend always paid separately.

This guide explains what each plan is designed for, how to decide between them, and what to check in any agency’s pricing before you sign. For market-wide context, read our guides to SEO costs in the UK and PPC management costs.

What each retainer includes

PlanFromBest forIncludes
Launch£350 a monthSmall local businesses4 posts a month, basic local SEO, monthly report
Growth£750 a monthEstablished SMEs8 posts a month, SEO, tracking set-up, regular review
Scale£1,500 a monthGrowing businesses12 posts a month, SEO, ads management, reporting

Post counts, channels, revisions and response times are defined in your agreement. Ads management is only part of the Scale plan; if paid advertising is central to your goals, that is the plan to look at.

How to choose a plan

Launch: getting found locally

Launch suits a business that serves customers in a particular town or area and needs a steady base of content plus basic local SEO. Typical examples are trades, clinics, salons and local professional services. If most of your customers search with a place name, our local SEO guide explains what this work covers.

Growth: building organic visibility with proper measurement

Growth suits an established business that wants to compete for wider searches, not only local ones. It doubles the content to 8 posts a month, includes SEO beyond the basics, and adds tracking set-up so enquiries and sales can be measured properly. That measurement also lays the groundwork if you later add paid advertising.

Scale: SEO and paid ads together

Scale suits a business ready to invest in both organic and paid growth. It includes 12 posts a month, SEO, ads management and reporting. Advertising spend is on top, paid by you directly to the platforms.

Quick plan checklist

  • Do you mainly serve one local area? Launch is often enough to start.
  • Do you need to measure enquiries or sales properly? Growth includes tracking set-up.
  • Do you want paid ads managed for you? Only Scale includes ads management.
  • Is your website slow, outdated or not converting? Fix that first with a separate website project.
  • Can you fund advertising on top of the fee? If not, focus on SEO and content first.
  • How much content can you review? 4, 8 or 12 posts a month each need your approval.

One-off projects and how they fit with retainers

Website and ecommerce builds are priced separately from retainers. A small website starts from £1,000–£2,000 and typically takes 3–5 weeks. An ecommerce site starts from £1,500–£3,500, with product volume and apps scoped separately, and typically takes 5–12 weeks. Automation projects are quoted by scope.

Many businesses start with a project and move on to a retainer once the site is ready, because marketing a slow or confusing website wastes budget. Our guides to website design costs for small businesses and ecommerce website costs explain what drives project prices.

What to check in any agency’s pricing

Whether you work with us or someone else, these questions help you compare quotes fairly:

  1. Is ad spend included or separate? A low fee that includes spend may leave little for the ads themselves.
  2. What exactly is delivered each month? Look for post counts, channels and reporting written into the agreement.
  3. How are revisions and response times handled? These should be defined up front.
  4. Is major technical or website work included? Usually it is a separate project; check before assuming.
  5. Who owns accounts and content? They should stay yours.
  6. Are results guaranteed? Be cautious if they are. We never promise rankings, sales or results we cannot control.

For a broader framework, read how to choose a digital marketing agency in the UK and our comparison of an SEO agency vs a freelancer.

Common pricing mistakes to avoid

The cheapest option is not always the lowest cost. These are the mistakes we see most often when businesses compare digital marketing prices.

Comparing fees without comparing scope

Two quotes at the same monthly price can include very different amounts of work. One may cover a handful of posts and a report; another may include SEO fixes, tracking and regular reviews. Always line quotes up against the same checklist of deliverables before deciding.

Starting ads before tracking works

Advertising without reliable conversion tracking means platforms optimise towards the wrong signals. That is why tracking set-up sits in the Growth plan and why we check it before any ad budget increases on Scale.

Spreading a small budget across too many channels

A modest budget usually does more when it is focused. A local business is often better served by strong local SEO and useful content than by small amounts of activity on every platform at once.

Expecting results in the first month

The first weeks of any retainer are about audits, fixes and planning. Judging a plan after one report rarely gives a fair picture, so agree in advance what progress should look like at each review.

Paying for a retainer when the website is the real problem

If visitors cannot find what they need or the site is slow on mobile, more traffic will not fix it. A one-off project first, followed by a retainer, is often the better route. Our guide to SEO for small businesses explains how to prioritise a limited budget.

What to expect from your budget over time

SEO and paid results typically build over 3–6 months. Content and technical improvements take time to be crawled, ranked and trusted, while paid campaigns need a testing period before performance settles. Regular reviews let us adjust priorities as data comes in.

How we work with businesses across the UK

All plans are delivered remotely, so it makes no difference where in the UK you are based.

  1. Introductory call to understand your business, goals and current marketing.
  2. Recommendation of a plan or project, with scope written down.
  3. Agreement setting out deliverables, revisions and response times. Retainers are billed in advance; projects take a deposit.
  4. Onboarding with access to your website, analytics and any ad accounts, all kept in your name.
  5. Monthly delivery and reporting by shared documents and video calls.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How much does a digital marketing retainer cost?

Our retainers start at £350 a month (Launch), £750 a month (Growth) and £1,500 a month (Scale). Final prices reflect scope and complexity.

Is advertising spend included?

No. Advertising spend is paid separately by the client, directly to the ad platforms.

How much does a website cost?

A small website starts from £1,000–£2,000 and an ecommerce site from £1,500–£3,500, with product volume and apps scoped separately.

How is payment taken?

Deposits are collected for projects; retainers are billed in advance.

Do you guarantee results?

No. We never promise rankings, sales or results we cannot control. We do commit to clear scope, reporting and regular reviews.

What are typical digital marketing agency prices in the UK?

Prices vary widely with scope and channels. Our retainers start at £350 a month (Launch), £750 (Growth) and £1,500 (Scale), with websites and ad spend priced separately.

Which plan includes paid ads management?

Only the Scale retainer includes ads management. Growth includes tracking set-up, which prepares the ground for advertising, but not ongoing management of ad campaigns.

Can I start on one plan and move to another?

Yes. Many businesses start on Launch or Growth and move up as their needs grow. Changes are agreed and reflected in your agreement.

Should I build a new website before starting a retainer?

If your current site is slow, outdated or not converting, it usually makes sense to fix that first, because marketing a weak site wastes budget. We will tell you if we think that applies.

Do I need to approve every post?

Content is sent to you before publishing, so you stay in control of what goes out under your name. Review processes are agreed during onboarding.

What is the difference between Launch and Growth?

Launch covers 4 posts a month, basic local SEO and a monthly report. Growth doubles content to 8 posts a month, includes fuller SEO, and adds tracking set-up and regular review.
