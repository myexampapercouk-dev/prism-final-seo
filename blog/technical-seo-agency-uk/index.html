Technical SEO Agency UK: What They Fix, When You Need One and How to Choose

What a technical SEO agency does, the signs your UK site needs one, how to judge the fixes it recommends and the questions to ask before you hire a provider.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A technical SEO agency finds and helps fix the problems that stop search engines crawling, understanding and indexing your important pages.
  • A useful audit ranks issues by business impact and says who needs to do what, rather than handing you a long list of automated warnings.
  • Check whether a provider will implement fixes or only recommend them, because findings that never reach your live site will not help.

A technical SEO agency improves the parts of a website that affect how search engines crawl, understand and index it. That includes site structure, redirects, indexing, speed, mobile usability, schema markup and JavaScript rendering. You are most likely to need one when good pages are not being indexed, traffic has dropped after a change, your site is large or complex, or a redesign or migration is coming up. Technical fixes remove barriers, but they cannot make up for weak content or low demand.

This guide explains how to decide whether you need this kind of help and how to choose a provider. If you would rather check your own site first, use our technical SEO audit checklist.

Signs you may need specialist help

  • Important pages are listed as not indexed in Search Console, and you do not know why.
  • Traffic fell after a redesign, migration, platform change or URL update.
  • Your site has thousands of URLs, filters or generated pages.
  • The site relies heavily on JavaScript and search results seem to miss some of the content.
  • Your developers want clear, prioritised SEO requirements before they start a build.
  • Different pages keep competing for the same search, or duplicate URLs keep appearing.

A small brochure site with a few well-linked pages may not need ongoing technical work. A one-off review, followed by content and local SEO, may be a better use of your budget.

Technical issues that often matter, and some that may not

Audit tools can produce hundreds of warnings, and they do not all deserve equal attention. Issues that affect your most valuable pages should come first.

Usually worth prompt attention

  • Key pages blocked from crawling or marked noindex by mistake.
  • Canonical tags pointing away from the page you want to rank.
  • Old URLs left without appropriate redirects after a migration.
  • Important pages with few or no internal links.
  • Large numbers of near-duplicate pages created by filters or parameters.
  • Main content that does not load properly for search engines.

Often lower priority

  • Small score changes in a speed testing tool where real users already have a good experience.
  • Meta descriptions slightly over a tool's length limit.
  • Warnings on old pages that have no commercial or traffic value.
  • Generic site health scores with no link to business outcomes.

A good specialist explains the difference. Fixing a score is not the goal. The goal is making it easier for the right pages to be found and used.

What a useful audit looks like

An audit should leave you knowing what to do next. It should normally include:

  • A short summary in plain English, with the main risks and opportunities.
  • Evidence for each issue, including example URLs and data sources.
  • A priority based on likely impact, effort and risk.
  • Clear instructions for developers, including how to check a fix has worked.
  • A note of anything that could not be checked, such as a missing data source.

Be careful with a report that looks like a raw export from a crawling tool. Tools are helpful for finding patterns, but someone still has to decide what those patterns mean for your site. Our guide to SEO audit costs explains how scope can change what you are comparing.

Audit only, or audit and implementation?

This is one of the most important things to clarify. Some providers only recommend changes. Others can make them directly, or work with your developers.

  • Recommendations only can suit a business with capable developers and time set aside for SEO work.
  • Agency implementation can suit a business without technical resource, as long as access, testing and approval are agreed.
  • Shared delivery works when the agency writes requirements, your developers build and the agency checks the result.

Whichever you choose, ask how changes will be tested before release and how they could be rolled back. Technical edits can have site-wide effects, so a staging site and a record of changes both help.

Platforms and technical limits

Good advice takes account of what your platform allows. Shopify has its own URL patterns. WordPress setups vary widely depending on the theme and extensions. Custom builds can offer more freedom but depend on developer time. Ask how a provider has approached your type of platform, and what they would do where the ideal fix is not possible. A practical workaround is often more useful than a perfect recommendation that never gets built.

Technical SEO during a redesign or migration

The best time to bring in technical SEO is before a new site is built, not after traffic drops. Early involvement can help protect URLs, content, internal links and tracking. Work typically includes mapping redirects, testing a staging site, checking templates and monitoring results after launch. See our SEO migration checklist for what should be covered.

Diagnosing a sudden traffic drop

A sudden fall does not always mean a technical fault. A careful provider will first establish when the drop began, which pages and searches were affected and whether anything changed on the site at that time. Seasonal demand, a tracking fault, a search engine update or stronger competition could each be the cause, so the evidence should come before the fix. Comparing Search Console with analytics also helps to show whether visibility really fell or only the reporting changed, which can save you from fixing the wrong thing.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • How do you decide which issues matter most?
  • What data will you use besides a crawling tool?
  • Will you implement the fixes, or brief our developers?
  • How do you test changes and confirm they worked?
  • Which of our platform's limits do you foresee?
  • How will you report progress in a way non-technical colleagues can follow?
  • What access do you need, and how will it be removed when the work ends?

Treat promises of instant rankings or guaranteed positions with caution. A technical fix can remove a serious barrier, but results also depend on content, competition and authority.

What to prepare

Give any provider access to Search Console and analytics if you can, along with a list of your most important pages. Share recent changes such as redesigns, migrations and platform updates, including dates, as these often explain sudden movements. Tell them who can make changes and how your release process works.

FAQs

What is the difference between technical SEO and on-page SEO?

Technical SEO deals with how search engines access and process your site. On-page SEO focuses on the content of a page, including its headings, copy and titles. The two overlap, and most sites need both.

Is a technical SEO audit a one-off job?

A one-off audit can be enough for a small and stable site. Larger sites, or those that change often, usually benefit from regular checks because new issues can appear with every release.

Will fixing technical issues improve my rankings?

It can, when those issues were holding back useful pages. It will not guarantee rankings, because content quality, competition and links also matter.

Can my developer handle technical SEO?

A developer can often make the changes, and many understand the basics. Specialist input helps with deciding what matters for search, diagnosing traffic drops and planning migrations.

Do small businesses need technical SEO?

They need the basics to be sound. Most small sites do not need a large ongoing programme, but a review is sensible, particularly after a rebuild or a sudden fall in visibility.

Next steps

Open Search Console and check whether your most important pages are indexed. If any are missing, or you cannot explain a recent drop, a closer review is likely to be worthwhile.
