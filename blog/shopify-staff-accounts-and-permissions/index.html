Shopify Staff Accounts: Managing Access to Your Store Safely

How Shopify staff accounts and permissions work, how to give team members and developers the right access, and how to keep your store admin secure.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Give every person their own Shopify login rather than sharing the store owner's account.
  • Grant only the permissions each person needs, and review access whenever roles change or people leave.
  • Agencies and developers should usually access your store through collaborator accounts, which you can approve and remove.

As a Shopify store grows, more people need access to it. Someone packs orders, someone answers customer emails, someone updates products, someone runs marketing, and at some point a developer or agency needs to work on the theme. It is tempting to hand everyone the store owner's password, but that creates real risks: no record of who did what, no way to remove one person without changing the password for everyone, and full access for people who only need a small part of the admin.

Shopify provides proper tools for managing access: staff accounts, granular permissions and collaborator accounts. This guide explains how they work and how to set them up sensibly.

The store owner account

Every Shopify store has one store owner. This account has full access, including billing, plan changes, store transfer and the ability to close the store. Only the store owner can carry out certain sensitive actions, and ownership can be transferred to another staff member if needed.

Because the store owner account is so powerful, it should be protected carefully and used only by the person who genuinely owns or is responsible for the business. Ideally, it should be tied to an email address the business controls for the long term, not a personal address belonging to a temporary employee or an outside developer. Our guide to website ownership and handover explains why this matters.

Staff accounts

Staff accounts are separate logins for people who work on your store. Each person receives an invitation by email, creates their own login and sees only the parts of the admin they have been given permission to use.

The number of staff accounts available depends on your Shopify plan, and Shopify adjusts its plans from time to time, so check your current plan details in the admin or on Shopify's website.

Why separate accounts matter

  • Accountability. Shopify records certain actions against the staff member who made them, so you can see who changed an order or edited a product.
  • Easy removal. When someone leaves, you can remove or suspend their account without affecting anyone else.
  • Least privilege. Each person gets only the access they need, reducing the damage a mistake or a compromised login could cause.
  • Personal security. Each person can set up their own two-step authentication.

Permissions

When you invite a staff member, you choose which areas of the admin they can access. Shopify's permissions are fairly granular and cover areas such as:

  • orders, including whether staff can edit, refund or export them,
  • products and inventory,
  • customers and customer data,
  • analytics and reports,
  • marketing and discounts,
  • content, such as blog posts, pages and navigation,
  • online store themes,
  • apps and channels, and
  • settings, including sensitive areas such as payments and domains.

The exact list changes as Shopify adds features, so review the options when inviting someone rather than relying on memory.

Matching permissions to roles

A simple way to plan permissions is to think in roles. For example:

  • Fulfilment: orders and perhaps inventory, without access to settings or analytics.
  • Customer service: orders and customers, possibly with permission to issue refunds if your process allows.
  • Merchandising: products, collections and inventory.
  • Content and marketing: blog posts, pages, navigation, discounts and marketing.
  • Finance: reports, orders and payouts information, without theme access.

Some plans let you save permission sets as reusable roles, which makes onboarding new staff quicker and more consistent. Where that is not available, keep a short written record of what each role should have.

Be careful with sensitive permissions

A few permissions deserve particular caution:

  • Customer data and exports. Customer details are personal data, and UK data protection law, overseen by the Information Commissioner's Office, expects businesses to limit access appropriately. Only give access to people who genuinely need it, and take advice if you are unsure of your obligations.
  • Refunds. Decide who can issue refunds and whether there should be checks.
  • Themes. Theme access allows changes to the live storefront and its code.
  • Apps. Installing an app can grant a third party access to store data, so limit who can approve new apps.
  • Settings. Payments, domains and checkout settings can affect every order.

Collaborator accounts for agencies and developers

If you work with an agency or freelance developer, they will usually request access through a collaborator account rather than a staff account. Collaborators work through Shopify's partner system. They send a request, you approve it and choose permissions, and you can remove their access at any time.

Collaborator accounts generally do not count towards your staff account allowance, and they keep a clear separation between your team and outside contractors. You can also require a collaborator request code, found in your store's user settings, so that only people you have shared the code with can request access.

When briefing a developer, agree in advance what they need access to. Theme work usually needs theme permissions; it may not need customer data or payment settings. Our guide on how to brief a Shopify developer covers what else to agree up front.

Securing logins

Two-step authentication

Two-step authentication adds a second check, such as a code from an authenticator app or a security key, when someone logs in. It is one of the most effective ways to protect an account even if a password is leaked. Encourage, and where possible require, every staff member to turn it on. Shopify allows store owners to require it for staff.

Strong, unique passwords

Every person should use a strong password that they do not use anywhere else. A password manager makes this practical.

Be alert to phishing

Fake emails pretending to come from Shopify, an app or a customer are a common way for criminals to try to steal logins. Staff should check the sender and the web address carefully before entering details, and log in by going to the admin directly rather than through email links when in doubt.

Onboarding and offboarding

Access management is not a one-off task. Build simple routines around it:

  • When someone joins: invite them with the permissions for their role, confirm they have set up two-step authentication and explain any rules, such as who approves refunds.
  • When someone changes role: update their permissions, removing anything they no longer need.
  • When someone leaves: remove or suspend their account promptly, and check whether they had access to other connected tools, such as email marketing, shipping or accounting apps.
  • When a project ends: remove collaborator access for developers or agencies who no longer need it.

It is worth reviewing your full staff and collaborator list periodically to catch anyone who has slipped through.

Apps and connected services

Staff accounts control people's access, but apps have their own access too. When an app is installed, it requests permission to read or change certain store data. Review those permissions before installing, and uninstall apps you no longer use. A regular Shopify apps audit helps keep this under control.

Also remember connected services outside Shopify, such as your domain registrar, email platform and analytics accounts. These often have their own user lists that need the same care.

Point of sale staff

If you also sell in person using Shopify POS, staff who work only at the till may not need full admin access at all. Shopify POS has its own approach to staff, with PINs and POS-specific permissions, so shop-floor staff can process sales without logging in to the main admin. Check how this works on your plan and set up retail staff with only what they need at the counter.

Keeping a simple access record

A short shared document listing who has access to the store, what role they have and when access was last reviewed makes audits far easier. Include outside collaborators and the main connected services. When something goes wrong, or when ownership of the business changes, that record saves time and confusion.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Sharing the store owner login among several people.
  • Giving every staff member full permissions by default.
  • Registering the store owner account to a developer's or ex-employee's email address.
  • Leaving former staff and old collaborators with active access.
  • Not requiring two-step authentication.
  • Letting anyone install apps without review.

FAQs

How many staff accounts can I have on Shopify?

It depends on your plan, and Shopify updates its plans periodically. Check your current plan in the Shopify admin or on Shopify's pricing information.

What is the difference between a staff account and a collaborator account?

Staff accounts are for your own team. Collaborator accounts are for agencies and developers working through Shopify's partner system, and you approve and remove them yourself.

Can I limit what a staff member can see?

Yes. When inviting or editing a staff member, choose only the permissions they need, such as orders and products, and leave everything else switched off.

What should I do when a staff member leaves?

Remove or suspend their Shopify account promptly and review any connected apps or services they could access.

Can I transfer store ownership?

Yes. The current store owner can transfer ownership to another staff member through the store's user settings, subject to Shopify's requirements.
