How people look for a lawyer in Vancouver

Legal searches are often urgent and personal. Someone searches for a family lawyer after a separation, a real estate lawyer before a closing, an immigration lawyer about a work permit or a personal injury lawyer after an accident. They may add a neighbourhood or nearby city, such as Downtown, Kitsilano, Burnaby, Richmond, Surrey or North Vancouver. They compare a few firms, read reviews, scan practice-area pages and choose who to call.

Business clients behave differently. A company looking for employment, corporate or commercial advice often researches more slowly, checks lawyer profiles, reads articles and asks for referrals. Both journeys need a website that shows clear expertise without overpromising. A website that earns trust

For a law firm, the website is often the first real impression. Visitors want to know whether you handle their type of matter, who they would work with, how the first consultation works and how to get in touch. They also look for signs that the firm is established and careful: clear lawyer profiles, plain-English explanations, accurate contact details and a privacy policy.

We design law firm websites with a page for each practice area, profiles that explain experience without exaggeration, and contact routes that suit the matter, such as a phone line for urgent matters and a form for less urgent ones. Our guide to law firm website design covers layout choices in more detail, and our guide to web design in Vancouver explains our build process for the city. A small firm website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and usually takes three to five weeks. Prices in Canadian dollars or other currencies are quoted on request.

Conversion matters as much as traffic. Small changes, such as clearer calls to action, shorter forms and visible office hours, can increase enquiries from the same visitors. Our guide to conversion rate optimisation for law firms explains what to test.

SEO for Vancouver law firms

Legal SEO is competitive, but it rewards depth and clarity. Searchers rarely type a firm name; they search for a legal problem. That means each practice area needs a page that explains who you help, what the process usually looks like, what to bring to a first meeting and how to get started. Supporting articles can answer narrower questions, such as what happens at a strata dispute hearing or how a separation agreement is usually drafted, always written as general information rather than advice.

Technical foundations also matter: fast pages, clean structure, structured data for the firm and its locations, and consistent details across legal directories. Our guide to SEO in Vancouver covers the wider local market, and results typically take three to six months to build.

Google Ads for lawyers in Vancouver

Legal keywords can be among the more expensive on Google Ads, especially in personal injury and family law. That makes structure and discipline essential. We separate campaigns by practice area, exclude searches for free advice or jobs where they do not fit, schedule ads around when your team can answer calls and send traffic to focused landing pages rather than the homepage.

Regulation and professional conduct

Lawyers in British Columbia are regulated by the Law Society of British Columbia, and its rules cover how lawyers market their services. Rules typically address areas such as claims about expertise or specialisation, comparisons with other lawyers, testimonials, fee statements and how results are described. We do not provide legal or regulatory advice. We give general marketing support and follow cautious good practice, and approval of every page, ad and claim rests with the firm, its own compliance lead and official Law Society guidance.

Confidentiality also shapes marketing. We avoid tracking that captures sensitive details from enquiry forms, keep forms short and explain what each tool collects. Your firm decides what is appropriate under applicable privacy law.

Serving a multilingual city

Vancouver has large communities who prefer Cantonese, Mandarin, Punjabi, Tagalog, Korean, Farsi and other languages, and many firms serve clients in more than one language. Prism writes in English. Content in other languages should come from your own lawyers or a professional legal translator, because legal wording needs particular care. We can then build the language structure and settings so the right page reaches the right reader.

Reviews, directories and reputation

Reviews influence which firm a client calls, but they need careful handling in a regulated profession. We help you set up a simple, consistent process for inviting reviews from clients who are happy to leave one, and we draft reply templates that thank people without confirming confidential details or discussing the matter. Replies to negative reviews should be calm, brief and reviewed by a lawyer before posting.

Legal directories and professional listings also matter for local search. We check that your firm name, address, phone number and practice areas match across the listings you use, and that each lawyer profile links back to the right page on your website. Inconsistent details confuse both search engines and potential clients.
