Who we build websites for in Riyadh

Riyadh is the commercial and government centre of the Kingdom, and the business mix is broad. The websites we plan most often for Riyadh-based firms fall into a few groups:

  • Professional and business services, such as consultancies, engineering firms, legal and accounting practices, that need credibility pages and clear contact routes.
  • Clinics and healthcare providers that want location pages, service pages and simple booking requests, with medical wording approved by the client.
  • Retail and ecommerce brands selling across the Kingdom and the wider Gulf.
  • Hospitality, food and leisure businesses where menus, maps and quick mobile actions matter most.
  • Technology and B2B companies selling to procurement teams, which often need case-study style pages, downloadable documents and enquiry forms.

If your sector has its own rules on advertising or claims, the content and compliance sign-off stays with you. We keep any regulatory notes general and suggest you check official guidance or your own adviser.

Web design Riyadh: what a good site needs

A website for Riyadh buyers has to work on a phone first. Most first visits come from mobile search, social media or a link shared in a messaging app, so speed and clarity on a small screen decide whether someone stays. Beyond that, these are the parts we treat as essential:

  • A bilingual plan from day one. Even if you launch in English first, the structure should allow Arabic pages later without a rebuild.
  • Right-to-left layout done properly. Navigation, icons, forms and tables need mirroring, not just translated text dropped into a left-to-right template.
  • Clear calls to action. A visible phone number, a WhatsApp link if you use it, and a short enquiry form.
  • Fast loading. Compressed images, light themes and few third-party scripts.
  • Search-ready structure. One page per main service, clean URLs, proper headings and language tags.
  • Trust signals you can actually support. Your registration details, real photos, clear policies and accurate contact information.

Arabic and English: how we handle a bilingual site

Many Riyadh businesses need both Arabic and English. Government-facing firms, consumer brands and clinics often lead with Arabic, while B2B and technology companies may lead with English for international buyers. We plan for both.

Our role is the structure, the design system and the build. We set up language switching, mirrored layouts, correct language and direction attributes, hreflang tags and separate URLs for each language, so search engines can show the right version to the right person. The Arabic text itself is written by your team or a translator you appoint. That keeps the tone natural and lets someone fluent check every phrase before launch.

Fonts matter more than people expect. We choose an Arabic typeface that pairs well with your Latin font, test line heights and check that numbers, dates and prices read correctly in both directions.

Platform choices: WordPress, Shopify or a static build

We build on the platform that fits how your team will run the site.

  • WordPress suits content-heavy sites where your staff will add pages, news and service updates. Multilingual plugins handle Arabic and English well when set up carefully. Our WordPress guide for Saudi Arabia covers this in more detail.
  • Shopify suits brands selling products, with local payment options and shipping set up through the platform and its apps. See our Shopify agency guide for Saudi Arabia.
  • Lightweight static builds suit smaller brochure sites where speed and low maintenance matter more than frequent edits.

Whatever the platform, we hand over a site your team can manage, with a short written guide and a recorded walkthrough.

How we work remotely with Riyadh clients

Working with a remote studio is straightforward when the process is written down. Here is how a typical project runs:

  1. Discovery call. We talk through your goals, audience, languages and the pages you need, then send a written scope.
  2. Sitemap and content plan. We agree the page list and who is writing which copy, including any Arabic.
  3. Design. We share designs for the home page and a key inner page, in both directions if the site is bilingual.
  4. Build. We build on a private preview link so you can review on your own phone and laptop.
  5. Testing. We check speed, forms, mobile layouts, right-to-left behaviour and tracking.
  6. Launch and handover. We go live, submit the sitemap and hand over logins and guides.

The UK and Riyadh are only two or three hours apart depending on the time of year, so there is a good overlap in the working day. We schedule calls around the Saudi working week and keep everything else in writing so nothing gets lost.

Speed, hosting and technical basics

Hosting choice affects speed for visitors in the Kingdom. We recommend reputable hosting with good regional performance or a content delivery network in front of the site. We compress images, serve modern formats, limit heavy scripts and test on mid-range phones over mobile data, not just on fast office connections.

We also set up basic security: SSL, regular updates for WordPress sites, backups and spam protection on forms. Data protection rules apply to the personal details your forms collect. We keep the setup tidy, but you should check your own obligations with official guidance or a legal adviser.

Design that suits Riyadh buyers

Good design for the Saudi market is clean, confident and respectful of local culture. Photography should feel local and authentic, which usually means your own images rather than generic stock. Colour, imagery and wording should suit your audience, and we review these points with you during the design stage.

We focus on clear hierarchy: what you do, who it is for, where you operate and how to get in touch.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Machine-translated Arabic. It reads badly and hurts trust. Use a fluent writer or translator.
  • Mirroring only the text. Right-to-left pages need mirrored navigation, icons and forms too.
  • One page for every service. Combining everything on one page makes it hard to rank for specific searches.
  • No tracking. Without analytics and conversion events you cannot tell which channels bring enquiries.
