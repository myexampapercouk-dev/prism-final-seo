Mobile-first and fast

Most people who find a Tampa business through a local search do so on a phone, often on the move. If your site is slow or awkward on a small screen, they go back to the results and choose a competitor. We design every page for phones first, keep layouts simple and make buttons easy to tap.

Speed is built in from the start rather than added later. We compress images, limit heavy scripts and avoid bloated page builders where possible. Our articles on mobile website design and website speed optimisation explain the techniques we use on every build.

Local details that help you rank

A website that ranks for searches like "roofer in Brandon" or "family lawyer St. Petersburg" needs to show clearly that you serve those places. We build service pages and location pages that include genuinely useful information, such as the areas you cover, how quickly you can attend, directions to your premises and answers to local questions.

For Tampa Bay businesses, local details can include how you handle hurricane season, whether you serve both sides of the bay, or how bookings change during the busy winter months. Content like this helps visitors and gives search engines clearer signals than generic copy. Once your site is live, our SEO retainers can build on these foundations.

Accessibility and privacy

Every visitor should be able to use your website, including people who rely on screen readers, keyboard navigation or larger text. We build to recognised accessibility good practice, including clear headings, descriptive links, sufficient colour contrast and alt text for images. Our guide to website accessibility explains the approach.

Web accessibility under the ADA and privacy rules for forms, cookies and tracking are areas where US businesses should take their own legal advice. We keep our notes on these topics general, follow good practice in the build and are happy to implement changes your adviser recommends. Approval of policies and legal wording rests with you.

Our website process

We keep projects structured so you always know what happens next:

  1. Discovery. A video call to understand your business, customers, competitors and goals.
  2. Content planning. We map out the pages you need and the information each one should contain. Our article on website content before a build explains why this stage saves time later.
  3. Design. We create the look and layout of key pages for your feedback.
  4. Build. We develop the site on WordPress or Shopify, with speed and SEO foundations built in.
  5. Review and launch. You test the site, we make agreed changes, then we launch and check everything works.
  6. Handover. We show your team how to update pages, and we offer ongoing support if you want it.

A small website typically takes three to five weeks, and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks, depending on the number of pages and products and how quickly content is ready.

Redesigning an existing Tampa website

Many of the projects we take on are redesigns rather than brand new sites. A redesign is a good chance to fix long-standing problems, but it also carries a risk: if page addresses change without planning, a site can lose rankings it has built up over years. Before we change anything, we review your current site to understand which pages bring traffic and enquiries.

  • Content inventory. We list every page, decide what to keep, improve, merge or remove, and identify gaps.
  • Redirect plan. Every old page address that changes is redirected to its closest new equivalent, so visitors and search engines are not sent to dead ends.
  • Tracking check. We make sure analytics, call tracking and form tracking carry over, so you can compare performance before and after launch.
  • Post-launch review. We check indexing, broken links and key forms in the weeks after launch and fix anything that slipped through.

Handled this way, a redesign should improve the experience for visitors without throwing away the visibility your business already has.

Photos and video
