Who we work with in Halifax

Halifax has a busy food scene shaped by seafood, a large student population, cruise and summer visitors, and a strong local following for independent spots. We work with:

  • Independent restaurants that want more direct bookings and fewer empty tables midweek.
  • Cafes and bakeries that rely on regulars and want to be found by newcomers nearby.
  • Bars, pubs and breweries promoting events, live music, trivia nights and seasonal menus.
  • Small restaurant groups with two or more locations that need one site with clear location pages.
  • Takeaway and delivery-led kitchens that want more orders through their own channels.
  • Caterers and event venues looking for private dining, wedding and corporate enquiries.

A restaurant website that fills tables

Many restaurant sites are slow, hide the menu in a PDF and make booking harder than it needs to be. Diners usually decide on their phone in a minute or two. We design restaurant websites that:

  • Show the menu as real text on the page, so it loads quickly and search engines can read it.
  • Put the booking button, phone number and directions where people expect them.
  • Keep opening hours, holiday hours and seasonal changes easy for your team to update.
  • Include separate pages for private dining, catering and gift cards where you offer them.
  • Link to your chosen booking and ordering tools rather than replacing them.
  • Load fast on mobile data, which matters for visitors walking around downtown.

Our restaurant website design guide covers the principles in more detail, and our Halifax web design guide explains how we build for businesses in the city.

Local SEO for Halifax restaurants

Most restaurant searches are local and urgent. People search for a cuisine, a dish or a situation, such as lobster rolls on the waterfront, a patio in the North End or a family-friendly spot in Bedford. To show up in the map results and organic listings, we:

  • Set up and optimise your Google Business Profile with accurate categories, hours, menu links and photos.
  • Make sure your name, address and phone number match across your site and directories.
  • Write useful pages for private dining, events and catering rather than squeezing everything onto the home page.
  • Build a steady, honest review routine and reply to reviews in your own voice.
  • Add structured data so search engines understand your menu, hours and location.

Our Google Business Profile guide explains the basics, which apply in Canada too, and our Halifax SEO agency guide covers local search in the city more broadly.

Google Ads for bookings and events

Search ads are useful when someone is ready to choose. They work best for specific needs, not for broad terms like restaurants. We usually build campaigns around:

  • Cuisine and location searches close to your restaurant.
  • Occasion searches such as birthday dinners, group bookings and holiday parties.
  • Private dining and catering enquiries, which often carry higher value.
  • Brand protection so people searching your name reach your site first.

Each campaign sends people to a matching page and we track calls, booking clicks and form enquiries. Our guide to Google Ads in Halifax explains our setup in more depth.

Meta and Instagram ads for menus and events

Food is visual, so Instagram and Facebook suit restaurants well. We use Meta ads to promote new menus, weekend brunch, live music and special events to people within a sensible distance of your venue. We also retarget people who visited your menu or booking page but did not book.

Prism does not film or edit video, and we do not run photo shoots. If your team or a photographer supplies images and short clips, we turn them into well-targeted campaigns. Our guide to Meta ads in Halifax has more on how we work.

Seasonality and the Halifax calendar

Halifax restaurants feel the seasons. Summer and early autumn bring patios, cruise visitors and festivals. Winter can be quieter, with more focus on locals, comfort food and holiday parties. Student term dates also shape demand in the areas around the universities.

We plan marketing around your own busy and quiet periods. That might mean pushing patio and seafood searches in summer, private dining and holiday parties in late autumn, and midweek offers for regulars in January and February. Budgets can move between channels as the year changes, so you are not paying the same amount in every month regardless of demand.

Direct orders versus delivery apps

Delivery apps can bring volume, but commissions eat into margin. Many restaurants want to move some of that demand to their own ordering. We can help by:

  • Making your own ordering link the clearest option on your website and Google profile.
  • Running search and social ads that point to direct ordering instead of third-party apps.
  • Collecting email sign-ups with clear consent so you can share offers directly.

We do not build ordering systems ourselves. We connect your website to the ordering provider you choose and make sure the journey is simple.

Rules, consent and privacy

At a general level, commercial emails and texts in Canada are covered by CASL, Canada's anti-spam law, which expects clear consent and an easy way to unsubscribe. Personal information is generally covered by PIPEDA. Promotions involving alcohol may also be subject to provincial rules in Nova Scotia.

We build clear sign-up forms, consent wording and privacy page templates, but compliance approval rests with you. Please check official guidance from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, the relevant Nova Scotia bodies, or your own adviser before campaigns go live.

Common restaurant marketing mistakes

  • Menus only available as PDFs or images that are hard to read on a phone.
  • Out-of-date opening hours on Google, especially over holidays.
  • Ads that send people to the home page instead of the booking or menu page.
  • Posting on social media with no plan, no budget and no tracking.
  • Collecting customer emails without clear consent.
  • Ignoring reviews, or replying to negative ones defensively.

Fixing these basics often makes more difference than adding a new channel. Our wider restaurant marketing guide goes deeper on each one.
