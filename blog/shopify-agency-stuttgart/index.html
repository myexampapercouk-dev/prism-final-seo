Why Stuttgart brands choose Shopify

Stuttgart and the wider Baden-Württemberg region are known for engineering, automotive suppliers, machinery and a strong base of family-owned companies. Alongside that sit many consumer brands in fashion, homeware, outdoor gear, food and drink, cosmetics and spare parts, plus manufacturers starting to sell direct to customers for the first time.

Shopify suits these businesses because it is hosted, secure and quick to launch, and the team can manage products, orders and content without a developer for every change. It handles several languages and currencies through Shopify Markets, and it connects to the payment methods and shipping providers German shoppers expect.

For brands with complex catalogues, wholesale customers or high order volumes, Shopify Plus adds features such as checkout customisation and B2B tools. Our post on Shopify Plus migrations explains when the upgrade is worth it.

Building a Shopify store for German shoppers

German online shoppers tend to be careful buyers. They read delivery details, check return terms, look for clear company information and expect a familiar way to pay. A store that skips these points often sees high basket abandonment, even with good products.

When we build for the German market we focus on:

  • Payment methods: Shopify Payments and the methods common in Germany, such as PayPal, cards, Klarna and other options offered through Shopify's payment settings and apps. We check what is available for your account.
  • Delivery and returns: clear costs, delivery times and returns information on product pages and in the cart, not hidden in the footer.
  • Trust pages: Impressum, privacy policy, terms and withdrawal information, written or approved by your legal adviser.
  • Cookie consent: a consent banner set up so tracking only fires with permission, without slowing the store down.
  • Mobile speed: lean themes, compressed images and only the apps you really need.
  • Language and currency: German as the main language and euros as the main currency, with other markets added through Shopify Markets if needed.

We keep legal guidance general. Your own adviser should confirm what your shop must show, and approval of legal text rests with you.

Migrating to Shopify without losing search traffic

Many Stuttgart brands already sell online on WooCommerce, Shopware, Magento or a custom system. Moving to Shopify can cut maintenance and speed up the team, but a careless migration can lose rankings built up over years.

Our migration process covers product and customer data, order history where needed, a full URL map with 301 redirects, metadata, image alt text and a post-launch crawl to catch broken links. If you are coming from WordPress, our guide to WooCommerce to Shopify migration walks through the main steps.

Growing the store after launch

A new store is only the start. Most of the growth comes from steady work on search, ads and conversion in the months after launch.

  • Shopify SEO: clean collection structure, unique product descriptions, internal links and structured data. Search results typically take 3 to 6 months to show clear movement.
  • Speed: our post on Shopify speed optimisation covers the fixes that matter most.
  • Conversion rate: product page layout, trust signals, cart and checkout review. See our Shopify conversion rate optimisation guide.
  • Marketplaces: many German shoppers start on Amazon, so listing SEO there can sit alongside your own store. Our post on Amazon SEO in Germany explains how.
  • Paid social: Meta, TikTok and YouTube Ads to reach new shoppers. We manage campaigns and work with the creative assets your team supplies, as we do not film or edit video.

Shopify for manufacturers selling direct

A growing number of Swabian manufacturers and suppliers want to sell spare parts, accessories or smaller products straight to end customers or trade buyers. This brings questions that a typical fashion or lifestyle store never faces.

  • Large catalogues: thousands of parts with technical attributes need clean collections, filters and search so buyers can find the right item quickly.
  • Product data: specifications, compatibility notes and downloadable documents should sit in structured fields, not loose text, so they can be filtered and read by search engines.
  • Trade and retail pricing: wholesale customers may need their own price lists, minimum order quantities or payment terms, which Shopify Plus B2B features or carefully chosen apps can handle.
  • Integrations: stock levels and orders often need to sync with an ERP or warehouse system. We scope these early because they affect cost and timeline more than design does.

We plan these needs in the discovery stage and put them in the written scope, so there are no surprises halfway through the build.

Selling beyond Germany from Stuttgart

Many Stuttgart brands sell into Austria and Switzerland as well as Germany, and some reach further into Europe or the UK. Shopify Markets lets one store show local currencies, prices and languages, with separate domains or subfolders for each market.

Each new market adds work: translated content, local delivery and returns information, tax settings and sometimes different payment methods. Switzerland, for example, sits outside the EU, so customs and duties need clear explanation at checkout. We help you decide which markets to open first, set up the structure and hreflang tags for search, and plan ads by country. Tax and customs points should be confirmed with your own adviser.

A simple rule we follow is to launch one market well before adding the next. A strong German store gives you the data and confidence to expand, while a store that launches in five markets at once often does none of them properly.

Choosing a Shopify theme and apps

Most of our builds start from a well-supported theme that we customise, rather than a fully custom theme. That keeps cost down, makes updates easier and gives your team a familiar editor. We add custom sections only where the brand or product genuinely needs them.

Apps are where many stores slow down. Each one can add scripts to every page. We start with a short list of essentials, such as reviews, consent, email marketing and shipping, and add more only when there is a clear reason.
