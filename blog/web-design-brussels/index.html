Web Design for Brussels Businesses: Multilingual Sites Done Well

Looking for web design in Brussels? How to plan a site for French, Dutch and English audiences, what a remote UK studio can build, timelines and pricing.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Brussels websites often need to serve French, Dutch and English speakers, so language structure should be planned before design starts, not added afterwards.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Belgium; we design and build remotely in English and work with your team or a translator on French and Dutch pages.
  • Small business websites are £1,000 to £2,000 and usually take three to five weeks; we price in pounds, with euro and other currencies quoted on request.

Web design for a Brussels business should produce a fast, clear website that works for the languages your customers use, usually some mix of French, Dutch and English, and that turns visitors into enquiries or sales. The language plan, the content and the structure matter as much as the visual design.

Our website and ecommerce development service runs online, one hour behind Brussels time, through video calls, shared design files and a staging site you can review at every step. Why web design in Brussels starts with languages

Brussels is officially bilingual, French and Dutch, and it is also one of the most international cities in Europe, home to EU institutions, trade associations, consultancies, law firms, NGOs and the many businesses that serve them. English is common in professional settings, while day-to-day customers may prefer French or Dutch.

That mix shapes almost every design decision:

  • Which languages to launch with. A consultancy serving EU institutions may lead with English, while a local clinic or shop needs French and Dutch first.
  • How visitors switch language. A clear, consistent language switcher that takes people to the matching page, not back to the homepage.
  • Text length. French and Dutch text often runs longer than English, so buttons, menus and headings must flex without breaking.
  • Search structure. Separate, crawlable pages for each language with the right signals, so search engines show the right version to each searcher.

Our guide to multilingual website design explains these choices in more depth, including when to use subfolders and how to avoid duplicate content between language versions.

Web design Brussels: sites for consultancies, associations and B2B firms

A large share of Brussels businesses sell to other organisations: public affairs consultancies, legal and advisory firms, trade associations, event organisers, technology suppliers and specialist service providers. Their websites need to show expertise quickly, explain complex services plainly and make it easy for a busy reader to get in touch.

For these firms we focus on clear service structures, well-organised insight or news sections, simple routes to contact and pages that load fast for visitors on any device. English often leads for this audience, with French or Dutch versions of core pages added as needed. Our B2B sector page covers how we support business-to-business companies beyond the website itself.

Accessibility and regulation for Belgian websites

Accessibility is now a central concern for many European websites. The European Accessibility Act applies to certain products and services, including some ecommerce and consumer-facing services, and national rules implement it in each member state. Whether and how it applies to your business depends on what you offer and your size, so check current official guidance with your own advisers.

In practice, we design to recognised accessibility standards wherever we can: readable contrast, clear headings, keyboard-friendly navigation, descriptive links and properly labelled forms. These choices help every visitor, not only those using assistive technology. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act and websites sets out the main points in general terms.

Privacy and cookie rules apply too. We set up consent banners and analytics within your chosen approach, and responsibility for legal compliance rests with your business and its advisers.

How a remote web design project works

Working with a studio outside Belgium is straightforward when the process is clear. Here is how our projects usually run:

  1. Discovery. We agree goals, audiences, languages, pages and features, and confirm what content you will provide.
  2. Structure and content. We plan the sitemap and page outlines, write English copy and brief your translator or team on other languages.
  3. Design. We design key pages for desktop and mobile, testing longer French and Dutch text in the layouts.
  4. Build and review. We build on a staging site you can check at any time, then refine based on your feedback.
  5. Launch and handover. We launch, check analytics, forms and language switching, and hand over full access.

Content is the most common cause of delay, especially when several languages are involved. Our guide on preparing website content before the build explains what to gather early. You own your domain, hosting and site from day one; our guide to website ownership and handover explains what that should include with any supplier.

Getting found after launch

A new website only helps if the right people find it. For a multilingual Brussels site, that starts during the build rather than after it. Each language version needs its own page titles, descriptions and headings written for how people search in that language, not copied across from English.

We build in the technical foundations as standard:

  • Clean language structure. Separate addresses for each language, with signals that tell search engines which version suits which searcher.
  • Fast pages. Optimised images, lean code and sensible hosting, so pages load quickly on mobile connections.
  • Structured data. Business details marked up so search engines and AI tools understand who you are and what you do.
  • Redirects. If you are replacing an older site, old addresses are mapped to new ones so existing visibility is kept.
  • Analytics. Measurement set up from day one, with consent respected, so you can see which pages and languages bring enquiries.

If you are replacing an existing Belgian site, protecting what already ranks matters. Our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings explains the checks we run before and after launch. Ongoing SEO, in English and coordinated with translators for other languages, can follow once the site is live.

Euro and other currency quotes are available on request, and professional translation is arranged and priced separately. Full details are on our pricing page.
