LinkedIn Ads Agency in Toronto: Reaching Buyers, Not Just Browsers

A LinkedIn Ads agency for Toronto businesses: how we target decision makers, build lead forms and landing pages, and manage budgets remotely from the UK.

  • LinkedIn Ads suit Toronto firms that sell to other businesses, because you can target by job function, seniority, company size and industry rather than by interests alone.

A LinkedIn Ads agency in Toronto helps businesses that sell to other businesses put their offer in front of the people who sign off on purchases: directors, heads of department, founders and procurement teams. LinkedIn costs more per click than most platforms, so the value comes from precise targeting, a strong offer and a fast route from the ad to a sales conversation.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely. We have no office in Toronto or anywhere in Canada. We work with Toronto companies through video calls, shared dashboards and written monthly reports, and we plan our working hours so there is a regular overlap with Eastern Time.

Why LinkedIn matters for Toronto businesses

Toronto has one of the largest concentrations of business services in North America. Finance and insurance around Bay Street, technology firms along King Street West and in the wider region, professional services, engineering consultancies, construction suppliers, software vendors and recruitment agencies all compete for the attention of the same senior buyers. Many of those buyers spend time on LinkedIn during the working day, which makes it one of the few channels where you can reach them by role rather than by guesswork.

The challenge is that LinkedIn is not cheap. A badly built campaign can spend a large budget reaching job seekers, junior staff and people outside your market. A good LinkedIn Ads setup narrows the audience to the people who can actually buy, gives them a reason to act and makes it easy for your team to follow up quickly.

What a LinkedIn Ads agency in Toronto actually does

Our LinkedIn work sits inside our wider paid media service. For Toronto clients it usually covers five areas:

  • Audience design: building target groups by job function, seniority, industry, company size and location, and excluding people who are not buyers.
  • Offer and creative: choosing what to promote, such as a guide, an assessment, a demo or a consultation, and writing the ad copy and image briefs.
  • Lead capture: setting up LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms or a dedicated landing page, and connecting them to your CRM or inbox.
  • Measurement: installing the Insight Tag, defining conversions and making sure leads can be traced back to campaigns.
  • Ongoing optimisation: testing creative, adjusting bids and budgets, and pausing audiences that bring in poor-quality leads.

We write in English. If you want French-language versions of ads or landing pages for buyers in Quebec, that copy comes from your own team or a translator, and we build and run the campaigns around it.

Targeting decision makers in the Greater Toronto Area

LinkedIn lets you target by location, so a campaign can focus on Toronto and the surrounding region, Ontario as a whole, or all of Canada. For many B2B firms the right choice depends on where they can actually deliver. A Toronto IT support company might stick to the GTA, while a SaaS vendor may target the whole country and parts of the United States.

Within that area we layer job and company filters. A common structure looks like this:

We avoid stacking too many filters at once. An audience that is too small becomes expensive and slow to learn from, so we start with a sensible middle ground and refine it as lead quality data comes in.

Offers that work on LinkedIn

People on LinkedIn are rarely ready to buy the moment they see an ad. The offers that tend to work give something useful in exchange for contact details or attention. Examples include a short buyer's guide for your category, a checklist, a benchmark-style self-assessment, a webinar invitation, a free audit or a short consultation.

For higher-value services, a two-step approach often works better: an educational ad or document ad to warm up the audience first, then a retargeting campaign with a direct offer to people who engaged. LinkedIn's matched audiences make this possible without relying on third-party cookies.

If you are not sure which offer suits your market, our guide to LinkedIn Ads in Canada covers the broader options, and our B2B marketing page explains how we plan longer sales cycles.

Lead Gen Forms or landing pages?

LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms pre-fill a user's name, email, job title and company, which usually means more submissions. The trade-off is that some leads are less considered, because filling in the form takes seconds. Landing pages take more effort for the user but let you explain your offer in more depth and can filter out casual interest.

We often test both. For a quick, low-commitment offer like a guide, Lead Gen Forms tend to perform well. For a consultation or demo, a focused landing page on a fast website gives the visitor the context they need. If your current site is slow or hard to update, our web design for Toronto businesses article explains what a good landing page needs.

Budgets, costs and what to expect

LinkedIn has a minimum daily budget per campaign, and clicks are usually more expensive than on Google, Meta or TikTok. That does not make it poor value, because the people clicking can be far more relevant. It does mean small budgets spread across too many campaigns rarely produce enough data to learn from.

Our management fees follow our standard pricing:

  • Launch, £350 a month: one focused LinkedIn campaign with basic reporting, suited to testing the channel.
  • Growth, £750 a month: several campaigns, ongoing creative testing and lead quality reviews, often alongside Google Ads.
  • Scale, £1,500 a month: multi-channel paid media with deeper testing and reporting.

Ad spend is paid directly to LinkedIn and is separate from our fee. Our prices are in pounds; pricing in Canadian dollars is quoted on request. Paid campaigns typically take three to six months to settle into a reliable pattern, as audiences, creative and follow-up are refined.

LinkedIn alongside Google and Meta

LinkedIn rarely works best on its own. Buyers who see your ad on LinkedIn often search for your company name later, so a strong search presence matters. Many Toronto firms pair LinkedIn with Google Ads in Toronto to capture people actively searching for a service, and with SEO in Toronto to build long-term visibility. Meta can be useful for retargeting at a lower cost, and our Meta Ads in Toronto article explains when that fits.

We can also use AI-powered tools to help draft ad variations, group search terms and summarise lead feedback, with every output checked by a person before it goes live. Our AI marketing in Canada guide explains how we use these tools carefully.

Privacy and consent in Canada

Collecting leads in Canada brings privacy and anti-spam obligations. Federal privacy law and Canada's anti-spam legislation cover how personal information is collected and how commercial electronic messages are sent, and some provinces have their own rules. In general terms, that means being clear about why you collect data, getting the right consent before adding people to email lists and offering an easy way to opt out.

We set up forms and tracking with those principles in mind, but we are not legal advisers. Please check the official guidance from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and the CRTC, or speak to your own adviser, for decisions about your specific situation.

How we work with Toronto clients remotely

Working with a remote studio is straightforward if the process is clear. Our usual approach:

  1. Discovery call: we learn your offer, ideal customer, deal size and sales process.
  2. Account review: if you already run LinkedIn Ads, we review targeting, creative and conversion tracking.
  3. Campaign plan: we propose audiences, offers, budgets and how leads will reach your team.
  4. Build and launch: we set up campaigns, forms, tracking and reporting.
  5. Monthly review: a written report and a call covering spend, leads, lead quality and next steps.

Your sales team's feedback is the most valuable input. If leads from one audience never turn into conversations, we need to know so we can shift budget elsewhere.

Common LinkedIn Ads mistakes we see

  • Targeting by job title only, which misses people with unusual titles and includes people who are not buyers.
  • Leaving LinkedIn Audience Network switched on without checking where ads appear.
  • Running one ad for months until the audience ignores it.
  • Sending leads to a generic home page instead of a page that matches the ad.
  • Following up days later, by which time the prospect has forgotten the ad.
  • Judging success on cost per lead alone without checking whether leads become opportunities.
