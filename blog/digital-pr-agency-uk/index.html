Choosing a Digital PR Agency in the UK: Tactics, Scope and How Results Are Judged

A good digital PR agency in the UK earns relevant coverage and links through stories journalists want. Here is what's included and how to judge results.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Digital PR earns coverage and links by giving journalists genuinely newsworthy stories, data and expert comment, not by buying placements.
  • Judge an agency on the relevance and quality of coverage, referring domains and brand mentions, not on raw link counts or vanity reach figures.
  • Ask for the full process up front: ideation, data, outreach lists, reporting and what happens when a campaign does not land.

A digital PR agency in the UK creates stories, data and expert commentary that journalists and publishers want to cover, then earns links and brand mentions from that coverage. The right agency is the one whose tactics are transparent, whose coverage is relevant to your customers and whose reporting ties results back to search visibility and enquiries.

Digital PR sits between traditional press relations and SEO. It still relies on pitching journalists, but the goal is measurable: authoritative links, branded search, and mentions that help both Google and AI assistants understand who you are. This guide explains the tactics, what a typical scope includes, how to judge results and the red flags to watch for before you sign anything.

What a digital PR agency actually does

At its core, a digital PR agency turns your expertise into something a newsroom can use. That might be an original survey, an analysis of public data, a reactive comment on a breaking story, or a visual asset a publisher can embed. Each piece is pitched to journalists who cover your sector, and the resulting articles link back to a relevant page on your site.

The link is only part of the value. Coverage in trusted UK publications puts your name in front of potential customers, supports brand searches and creates the kind of third-party mentions that generative search tools draw on when they recommend businesses. Common digital PR tactics

Most agencies use a blend of proactive and reactive work. Proactive campaigns take longer to build but can earn a cluster of coverage; reactive work is quicker and keeps a steady drip of mentions going between campaigns.

What should be included in a digital PR service

Scopes vary, but a serious digital PR service should cover the whole journey from idea to report. When you compare proposals, check that each of these stages is spelled out rather than implied.

  • Research and ideation: brainstorming angles that fit your brand, your audience and the current news agenda, with a clear rationale for why a journalist would care.
  • Asset creation: survey design, data analysis, copywriting and any landing page or visual needed to host the story on your site.
  • Media list building: relevant journalists and publications by sector and region, not a bulk list of every outlet.
  • Outreach and follow-up: personalised pitches, embargo handling and responses to journalist questions.
  • Coverage tracking: monitoring for linked and unlinked mentions, followed by reclamation where appropriate.
  • Reporting: what was earned, where, and how it connects to rankings, referral traffic and enquiries.

Digital PR works best when it supports a wider plan. Coverage pointing at thin pages achieves little, so most agencies will also look at the content and technical health of the pages receiving links. How results are judged

Digital PR is easy to report badly. A huge "potential reach" number or a list of syndicated copies on low-quality sites can look impressive while doing very little. Better agencies report on measures that tie to commercial outcomes.

MetricWhy it mattersWatch out for
Relevant referring domainsNew, unique sites linking to you that your customers actually readCounting syndicated duplicates as separate wins
Coverage qualityEditorial placements in trusted national, regional or trade titlesPaid advertorials presented as earned coverage
Brand mentionsUnlinked mentions still build recognition and AI visibilityIgnoring them entirely in reports
Target page rankingsShows whether links are lifting the pages that sellLinks all pointing at the homepage
Referral traffic and branded searchEvidence that real people noticed the storyExpecting large spikes from every piece
Enquiries and revenueThe commercial outcome that justifies the budgetClaiming direct attribution without evidence

Be realistic about timing. Campaigns can land within weeks, but the SEO impact of new links typically shows over three to six months, alongside other on-site work. A single campaign that fails to earn coverage is not unusual; an agency should explain why and what it will change next time.

Red flags when hiring a digital PR agency

  • Guaranteed link numbers: editorial coverage cannot be guaranteed. A promise of a fixed number of links per month often means paid or low-quality placements.
  • No named publications in reporting: you should see every piece of coverage and the URL it sits on.
  • Pay-for-placement networks: sponsored posts sold as PR breach Google's spam policies if the links are not qualified, and can put your site at risk.
  • Irrelevant coverage: a link from a site with no connection to your sector rarely helps rankings or customers.
  • Opaque process: if you cannot see the pitch, the media list or the asset before it goes out, you cannot protect your brand.

Digital PR agency checklist

Use this checklist when comparing UK digital PR agencies:

  1. Can they explain, in plain English, how their campaigns earn coverage without paying for it?
  2. Do they research your sector and existing coverage before proposing ideas?
  3. Will they share campaign concepts and pitches for approval before outreach?
  4. Do they build media lists by relevance rather than volume?
  5. Will they link coverage to specific pages that need authority, not just the homepage?
  6. Do they track unlinked mentions and attempt reclamation?
  7. Does reporting include every coverage URL, referring domains and target page rankings?
  8. Have they explained how they handle regulated topics, with sign-off resting with your compliance team where relevant?
  9. Are the scope, deliverables and notice period written down?
  10. Do they connect PR activity to your wider SEO and content plan?

How digital PR fits with SEO and content

FAQs

How do I choose a digital PR agency in the UK?

Look for transparent tactics, examples of relevant editorial coverage, clear reporting on referring domains and target pages, and no guarantees of fixed link numbers. Ask to see how a campaign moves from idea to outreach and how they report what was earned.

What is the difference between digital PR and traditional PR?

Traditional PR focuses on reputation and media profile, often measured by coverage volume. Digital PR uses similar journalist relationships but aims for online coverage with links and mentions that improve search visibility, referral traffic and AI visibility.

How long does digital PR take to work?

A campaign can earn coverage within weeks of launch, but the SEO effect of new links usually builds over three to six months. Results depend on the strength of the story, the state of your site and how competitive your market is.

Can a digital PR agency guarantee links?

No reputable agency can guarantee editorial links, because journalists decide what they publish. Fixed link guarantees often point to paid placements or networks, which carry risk under Google's spam policies.

Is digital PR worth it for small businesses?

It can be, particularly with regional or trade angles and reactive commentary, which need less budget than national data campaigns. Small businesses usually see more benefit once their core pages and technical setup are solid.

Does digital PR help with AI search?

Yes, indirectly. Coverage and mentions on trusted sites help AI assistants recognise your brand and expertise, which can influence whether you are cited in AI answers. It works best alongside clear, well-structured content on your own site.
