WooCommerce VAT Settings: How to Set Up Tax on a UK Store

How to set up WooCommerce VAT settings for a UK shop: enabling taxes, price display, standard and reduced rate tables, shipping tax and testing orders.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • WooCommerce only calculates tax from the options and rate tables you give it, so deciding whether and how VAT applies remains your responsibility.
  • Decide whether your prices are entered including VAT before you build or import the catalogue, because changing it later affects every product.
  • Use tax classes for reduced and zero-rated products, then test real basket scenarios and confirm the set-up with your accountant.

WooCommerce VAT settings control whether your shop charges tax, at what rates, and whether the prices you type in already include VAT. For a UK store, you switch taxes on in the general settings, choose how prices are entered and displayed, and then add rate tables for the standard rate and any reduced or zero-rated products you sell. WooCommerce does the arithmetic, but it relies entirely on the instructions you give it.

This guide is for UK owners of WooCommerce shops, whether you are building a new store or checking one that has been running for a while. It explains where the options sit, what each one does, how to handle products that are not standard rated, and how to test the result before customers see it. It is general guidance on how the software works, not tax advice. VAT rules depend on your products, customers and circumstances, so always confirm your set-up with an accountant and check HMRC's current guidance.

Decide what your shop needs to do first

The settings are the easy part. The decisions behind them are what matter, so answer these questions before you change anything.

  • Are you VAT registered? Whether you must register depends on your taxable turnover against HMRC's threshold. If you are not registered, you generally should not be charging VAT, and the simplest set-up is to leave taxes switched off.
  • Which rates apply to your products? UK goods can be standard rated, reduced rated, zero rated or exempt. The lines can be fine, especially in food, children's items and some health-related goods.
  • Where do your customers live? Selling into Northern Ireland, the EU or further afield can bring separate rules. Our guide to selling internationally from a UK online store covers the wider decisions.
  • Do you sell digital products? Downloads and other digital goods sold to consumers can follow different rules from physical goods.

If any answer is uncertain, speak to your accountant before you configure anything. Changing a setting takes seconds. Correcting VAT charged on hundreds of past orders does not.

How to set up WooCommerce VAT settings for the UK

The menu labels below reflect how WooCommerce has generally been organised, but plugins and updates do move things around, so treat this as a map rather than a script.

  1. Enable taxes. In WooCommerce settings, on the General tab, tick the option to enable tax rates and calculations. A Tax tab then appears.
  2. Check your store address. The base location on the General tab matters, because WooCommerce can use it when calculating tax.
  3. Choose how prices are entered. On the Tax tab, decide whether the prices you type in include tax or exclude it.
  4. Choose the calculation basis. Pick whether tax is worked out from the customer's shipping address, billing address or your shop's base address.
  5. Set shipping tax. Decide which tax class applies to delivery charges.
  6. Set display options. Choose how prices appear in the shop and in the basket and checkout, and how tax totals are shown.
  7. Add your rate tables. Enter the UK standard rate, then any additional classes you need.
  8. Test. Place test orders covering each type of product and delivery option you offer.

If you also run a Shopify shop, our guide to Shopify VAT settings covers the same decisions on that platform, which can help if you are comparing the two.

Prices entered including or excluding VAT

This is the setting most worth getting right first time. UK consumers generally expect to see prices that already include VAT, and most UK retailers price that way. If you enter prices including tax, a product you list at £24 shows as £24 to a UK shopper, and WooCommerce works out the VAT portion behind the scenes.

If you enter prices excluding tax, WooCommerce adds VAT on top. That can suit trade-only shops whose customers think in net prices, but it often produces awkward retail prices such as £28.79 rather than a tidy £29.

Switching from one approach to the other after launch is risky. Every price in the catalogue suddenly means something different, so a full price review is needed. Settle this before you import products.

Standard, reduced and zero-rate tax classes

WooCommerce uses tax classes to apply different rates to different products. By default it comes with a standard class plus classes for reduced and zero rates, and you can add your own names on the Tax tab.

For each class, you add one or more rows to its rate table. A simple UK row usually includes the country code GB, leaves the county, postcode and city fields empty so it applies across the country, enters the current rate as a percentage, gives it a clear name such as VAT, and ticks whether it applies to shipping. Check HMRC's current guidance for the rates themselves rather than copying a figure from an old tutorial.

Then, on each product, choose the right tax status and class in the product data panel. Variable products can set a class per variation if needed, which is useful when, for example, some sizes in a range fall under a different rate. Our guide to product variants and bundles covers how to structure those products more generally.

A few habits keep this tidy:

  • Keep a spreadsheet listing which products sit in which class and why, agreed with your accountant.
  • Add the tax class column to any product CSV you import, so new stock does not quietly default to standard rate.
  • Review the list whenever you add a new product type.

Delivery charges and VAT

Delivery is where many shops go wrong. The VAT treatment of delivery charges can depend on what is being delivered, and mixed baskets can make it more complicated. WooCommerce gives you a shipping tax class setting, including an option to base it on the items in the basket.

Whatever you choose, make sure it matches how your shipping methods are configured. If your flat rate is entered as £4.95 and taxes are calculated on top, a customer may see a different figure from the one you advertise on your delivery page. Our guide to online shop shipping rates walks through setting up those methods, and it is worth reviewing both together.

Showing VAT to customers

Customers, especially business buyers, want to see VAT clearly. Check these places:

  • Product pages: many shops add a short note such as "inc. VAT" beside the price, which can be set as a price display suffix on the Tax tab.
  • Basket and checkout: the VAT amount should be visible and correct for mixed baskets.
  • Order emails: confirmation emails should show the tax total. Our guide to ecommerce transactional emails covers what else they should contain.
  • Invoices: WooCommerce does not create full VAT invoices out of the box. If customers need them, you will typically use an invoicing extension, and your accountant can tell you what details they must include.

Tax extensions and automated calculation

Some shops use an extension or service that calculates tax automatically instead of maintaining rate tables by hand. These can help shops selling into many countries, but they add a dependency, sometimes a cost, and another thing to update. For a UK shop selling mainly to UK customers, well-maintained manual tables are often enough.

If you do add an extension, treat it like any other plugin. Check it is maintained, compatible with your WooCommerce version and genuinely needed. Our WordPress plugin audit guide explains how to review plugins without breaking your site.

Test before you trust it

Never assume the set-up is right because the settings look right. Test on a staging copy where possible, as covered in our guide to website staging and testing, then repeat a few checks on the live shop.

  1. Order a single standard-rated product with UK delivery and check the VAT figure by hand.
  2. Order a reduced or zero-rated product and confirm the right class applied.
  3. Order a mixed basket and check both the line totals and the delivery tax.
  4. Apply a discount code and see how it affects the tax.
  5. Try an address outside the UK if you ship abroad.
  6. Check the order email, the admin order screen and any invoice for matching figures.
  7. Refund a test order and confirm the tax is reversed as expected.

Your payment provider also affects how orders appear in your reports, so check that the figures line up there as well. Our guide to payment gateways for UK online shops covers the main options.

Keeping it right over time

VAT settings are not a one-off job. Rates and rules can change, plugins update, and new product lines arrive. Put a short review in the calendar after each major WooCommerce update and whenever you launch a new type of product, and keep notes of what you changed and why. When you register for VAT, cross the threshold or start selling overseas, revisit the whole set-up with your accountant.

FAQs

How do I set up WooCommerce VAT settings in the UK?

Enable tax on the General tab of WooCommerce settings, then use the Tax tab to choose whether prices include VAT, what the calculation is based on and how prices display. Add a GB rate to the standard rate table, set up any reduced or zero-rate classes you need, assign products to them and test several orders.

Should my WooCommerce prices include VAT?

For most UK shops selling to consumers, yes. Shoppers generally expect VAT-inclusive prices, and entering prices that way keeps them tidy. Trade-only shops sometimes prefer to work in net prices. Decide before you import products, because changing it later affects every price.

Do I need to charge VAT if I am not VAT registered?

Generally, a business that is not VAT registered should not charge VAT, so taxes can stay switched off in WooCommerce. Whether you need to register depends on your turnover and circumstances, so check HMRC's current guidance or ask your accountant.

How do I add a reduced rate product in WooCommerce?

Make sure a reduced rate class exists on the Tax tab, add a GB row with the current reduced rate, then open the product and choose that class in its product data. Confirm with your accountant that the product genuinely qualifies before relying on it.

Does WooCommerce create VAT invoices?

Not fully out of the box. Order emails show tax totals, but shops that need proper VAT invoices usually add an invoicing extension. Check with your accountant which details your invoices need to show.

Why is WooCommerce charging the wrong amount of VAT?

Common causes include prices entered on the wrong basis, a product left in the wrong tax class, a missing rate row, shipping tax set differently from the goods, or a plugin overriding the calculation. Work through a test order step by step to find where the figure changes.
