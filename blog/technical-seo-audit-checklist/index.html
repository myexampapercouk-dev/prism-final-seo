Technical SEO Audit Checklist: Crawling, Indexing, Core Web Vitals and Redirects

A technical SEO audit checklist covering crawling, indexing, Core Web Vitals, redirects and structured data, with a symptom-to-fix table for common issues.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Audit in order: crawling, then indexing, then speed and page experience, then redirects and structured data; fixes higher up unlock everything below.
  • Google Search Console is the most important free tool for a technical audit, because it shows what Google actually sees.
  • Prioritise fixes by impact on revenue-driving pages rather than trying to clear every warning a crawler reports.

A technical SEO audit checks whether search engines can crawl, index and understand your website, and whether your pages load quickly and work well for visitors. The essentials are crawlability, indexation, Core Web Vitals, redirects, internal links and structured data, checked in that order because problems early in the chain block everything after them.

Use this checklist to run your own audit or to judge an audit from an agency. It includes a symptom-to-fix table for the issues that come up most often on UK business and ecommerce sites.

Before you start: tools you need

You do not need expensive software for a useful audit. The essentials are:

  • Google Search Console for indexing reports, Core Web Vitals, sitemaps and manual actions
  • A site crawler to list every URL, status code, title, canonical and internal link
  • PageSpeed Insights for lab and real-user speed data
  • Google's Rich Results Test for structured data
  • Your analytics to see which pages drive enquiries and sales, so you can prioritise

Technical SEO audit checklist

1. Crawling

  • Robots.txt does not block important sections, CSS or JavaScript
  • XML sitemap exists, lists only indexable 200-status URLs and is submitted in Search Console
  • Important pages are reachable within a few clicks from the homepage
  • No large sections of crawlable junk URLs, such as endless filter or search combinations
  • Server responds reliably, without frequent 5xx errors
  • Key content is visible in the rendered HTML, not hidden behind scripts that fail to load

2. Indexing

  • Search Console page indexing report reviewed for excluded pages that should be indexed
  • No accidental noindex tags on important pages, a common leftover after a site launch
  • Canonical tags point to the correct, preferred version of each page
  • One version of the site resolves: HTTPS, with www or non-www chosen and enforced
  • Duplicate and thin pages consolidated, redirected or kept out of the index
  • Soft 404s investigated, especially discontinued products on ecommerce sites

3. Core Web Vitals and page experience

  • Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint and Cumulative Layout Shift checked in Search Console field data
  • Oversized images compressed and served in modern formats
  • Third-party scripts (chat, reviews, tracking, pop-ups) audited and deferred where possible
  • Layout shift from late-loading banners, fonts and cookie notices reduced
  • Pages usable on mobile, with readable text and tappable buttons

4. Redirects and status codes

  • No redirect chains or loops; old URLs go straight to the final destination
  • Permanent moves use 301 redirects rather than temporary ones
  • Internal links updated to point at final URLs, not redirects
  • Broken internal links (404s) fixed, and valuable deleted pages redirected to close equivalents
  • Redirect map kept from any past migration and checked for gaps

5. Site structure and internal links

  • Clear hierarchy from homepage to categories or services to detail pages
  • Money pages receive plenty of internal links with descriptive anchor text
  • No orphan pages that nothing links to
  • Breadcrumbs used where the site has depth

6. On-page technical elements

  • Unique title tags and meta descriptions on important pages
  • One clear H1 per page
  • Descriptive alt text on meaningful images
  • Hreflang set correctly if you run separate sites or sections for other countries

7. Structured data

  • Organisation or LocalBusiness markup on the site
  • Product, breadcrumb and review markup on ecommerce pages, matching visible content
  • Article and FAQ markup where appropriate
  • No errors in Search Console enhancement reports

Reading Search Console during an audit

Search Console is where an audit becomes specific. The page indexing report groups URLs by reason: not found, excluded by noindex, alternate page with proper canonical, duplicate without user-selected canonical, crawled but not indexed and discovered but not indexed. Each reason points to a different fix. "Alternate page with proper canonical" is usually fine, because it means Google has understood your duplicates. "Duplicate without user-selected canonical" suggests you need clearer canonical tags. "Discovered, currently not indexed" on a large site can mean Google is not prioritising those pages, often because they are weakly linked or there are too many low-value URLs competing for attention.

Use the URL Inspection tool on a few representative pages from each template: the homepage, a service or category page, a product or article page and a location page if you have them. It shows the canonical Google has chosen, whether the page is indexed, when it was last crawled and how the rendered page looks. If Google's chosen canonical differs from yours, that is a signal worth investigating before anything else.

Also check the Core Web Vitals report, which groups URLs by template, the crawl stats report for spikes in server errors or slow responses, and the manual actions and security issues sections, which should be empty. Note the date of any sudden change and compare it with releases, plugin updates or content changes on your side.

How to prioritise what you find

A crawler will often report hundreds of warnings. Most do not matter much. Prioritise by asking two questions: does this issue affect pages that drive enquiries or sales, and does it stop Google crawling or indexing them? Blocking problems on money pages come first. Cosmetic warnings on low-value pages can wait.

It also helps to group fixes by who does them. Content changes can often be made by your team; template, server and redirect changes usually need a developer. Ecommerce-specific checks

Online shops have extra technical risks: filter combinations, product variants, out-of-stock pages and duplicate product URLs. How often to audit

Run a full technical audit at least once a year, and always before and after a redesign or platform migration. Our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings covers the migration checks in detail. Between full audits, review Search Console monthly for new indexing or Core Web Vitals problems.

FAQs

What should a technical SEO audit checklist include?

Crawling, indexing, Core Web Vitals, redirects and status codes, site structure, on-page technical elements and structured data, prioritised by their effect on your most valuable pages.

How long does a technical SEO audit take?

A small site can be reviewed in a day or two. Large ecommerce sites with many templates and filters take longer, especially if the findings need to be prioritised and specified for developers.

Can I do a technical SEO audit myself?

Yes, with Search Console, a crawler and PageSpeed Insights. The harder parts are interpreting findings, prioritising them and implementing template or server changes.

What is the most common technical SEO problem?

On many sites it is duplicate or low-value URLs being crawled and indexed, closely followed by missing redirects after a redesign and slow mobile pages.

Do Core Web Vitals really affect rankings?

They are one of many signals and rarely outweigh relevance and content quality. They matter more for conversions, because slow, jumpy pages lose visitors.

How often should I run a technical SEO audit?

At least once a year, before and after any migration, and whenever Search Console shows a sudden change in indexed pages or traffic.
