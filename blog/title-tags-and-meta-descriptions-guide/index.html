How to Write Title Tags and Meta Descriptions That Get Clicks

Title tags tell Google and searchers what a page is about; meta descriptions sell the click. Learn how to write both for a UK small business website.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Write a unique title tag for every page that leads with the main search phrase and stays short enough to display in full.
  • Use the meta description to answer the query and give a reason to click, even though Google may rewrite it.
  • Check Search Console for pages with impressions but few clicks, and rewrite those titles first.

To write good title tags and meta descriptions, give every page a unique title that starts with the phrase people search for, keep it short enough to show in full, and use the meta description to answer the query and give a clear reason to click. The title helps Google understand the page; the description mainly helps a searcher choose your result over the others.

These two small pieces of text are among the quickest SEO improvements a small business can make, and they need no developer for most website platforms. This guide explains what each one does, how to write them, how to edit them on common platforms and how to decide which pages to fix first. Search engines adjust how they display results from time to time, so check Google Search Central for current guidance on titles and snippets.

What title tags and meta descriptions are

A title tag is a line of HTML in the head of a page. It usually appears as the blue clickable headline in search results and as the text in the browser tab. A meta description is a short summary, also in the head, which search engines may show under the title.

Title tagMeta description
Where it showsSearch result headline, browser tab, social sharesSnippet text under the headline, when used
Ranking roleA meaningful signal about the page topicNot a direct ranking factor, but affects clicks
Rough lengthAround 50 to 60 characters to avoid truncationAround 120 to 160 characters
Can Google rewrite it?Yes, sometimesYes, often

Display is based on pixel width rather than a strict character count, so the lengths above are practical guides rather than hard rules.

How to write title tags and meta descriptions

Start with the search phrase. Before you write anything, decide which query the page should rank for. Our keyword research guide explains how to find the phrases your customers use. Then follow a simple process for each page.

  1. Put the main phrase near the start of the title, in the words a searcher would use.
  2. Add a qualifier that sets the page apart, such as a location, the type of customer or what the page offers.
  3. Add your brand at the end if there is room, separated by a dash or pipe.
  4. Write the description as a direct answer or promise, then a reason to click.
  5. Read both aloud. If they sound like a list of keywords, rewrite them as a sentence a person would trust.

Meta description tips

  • Answer the question or describe the offer in the first sentence.
  • Include the main phrase naturally, because matching words are often shown in bold.
  • Mention something concrete: areas covered, delivery options, booking method or what the guide explains.
  • Avoid promises you cannot keep and claims you cannot support.
  • Keep each one unique. Duplicate descriptions across many pages give searchers no reason to choose one over another.

Why Google sometimes rewrites them

Google may show a different title or snippet from the one you wrote, especially if your title is very long, stuffed with keywords, duplicated across pages or does not match the content well. For descriptions, Google often pulls text from the page that better matches the specific query.

You cannot fully control this, but you can reduce it. Keep titles accurate and concise, make sure the main heading on the page supports the title, and write descriptions that genuinely summarise the content. Clear page structure also helps AI-driven results understand your content, which we cover in our guide to AI Overviews and SEO.

How to edit titles and descriptions on common platforms

Most platforms let you change these fields without touching code. The general locations are below, but menu names change, so check your platform help pages.

PlatformWhere to look
WordPressUsually an SEO plugin panel below the editor, or theme settings
ShopifyThe search engine listing section at the bottom of a product, collection or page
SquarespacePage settings, under the SEO tab
WixPage SEO settings or the SEO panel
Custom sitesAsk your developer whether the CMS has fields for them

If your platform only generates titles automatically from page names, it is worth fixing that, because templated titles such as Products | Shop are rarely helpful to searchers.

Which pages to fix first

On a small site you might rewrite everything in an afternoon. On larger sites, prioritise. Google Search Console is the best free tool for this. Our Search Console set-up guide explains how to get it running.

  1. Open the performance report and look at pages, then queries.
  2. Find pages with plenty of impressions but a low click-through rate compared with similar pages.
  3. Check what queries they appear for and whether the title matches those queries.
  4. Rewrite the title and description to match the main query more closely.
  5. Note the date, then compare clicks over the following weeks.

Pages sitting just off the first page of results are often the best candidates, because a more relevant title can help both rankings and clicks.

Change one batch of pages at a time and keep a simple log of the old and new wording with the date. That way, if clicks rise or fall, you can see which change is likely responsible and roll it back if needed.

Titles for different page types

Service pages should name the service and the area. Our guide to service page design covers the content that should support that title. Product pages work best with the product name, a key attribute and sometimes the brand; see our product page SEO guide for more. Blog posts should mirror the question or task the reader is searching for.

The home page title should say what you do and where, not just your business name, unless your brand is already well known. Structured data can add more detail to how results appear, which we explain in our schema markup guide.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • The same title on many pages, often the business name alone.
  • Keyword stuffing, such as repeating a town name or service three times.
  • Titles that promise something the page does not deliver.
  • Missing descriptions on key pages, leaving Google to pick random text.
  • Changing titles constantly, which makes it hard to judge what worked.
  • Forgetting titles when pages are moved or renamed during a redesign.

A technical review will usually flag missing, duplicate and overly long titles across the whole site at once. Our technical SEO audit checklist includes these checks alongside crawling and indexing issues.

Where this fits in wider SEO

Titles and descriptions help pages that are already relevant get found and clicked. They cannot make up for thin content, slow pages or a site Google struggles to crawl. For the bigger picture, our SEO guide for small businesses covers the main priorities in order.

FAQs

How do I write title tags and meta descriptions?

Start each title with the main search phrase, add a qualifier such as location or audience, and finish with your brand if there is room. Write the description as a short answer or offer with a reason to click, and make both unique to the page.

How long should a title tag be?

Around 50 to 60 characters usually displays in full on desktop, though Google measures by pixel width. Put the most important words first so nothing vital is cut off.

Do meta descriptions affect rankings?

Google has said they are not a direct ranking factor, but they influence whether people click your result. A clearer description can bring more visitors from the same position.

Why has Google changed my title in search results?

Google may rewrite titles that are too long, repetitive, duplicated or a poor match for the page. Keeping titles concise, accurate and aligned with the main heading reduces the chance of this.

Should every page have a meta description?

Your important pages should. On very large sites, Google can generate snippets from page content, but writing descriptions for key service, product and category pages gives you more control.

Should I include my business name in the title tag?

Usually at the end, if there is room. On the home page and for well-known brands it can come earlier. On most other pages, the search phrase matters more.
