Choosing a WordPress Agency in the UK: Builds, Plugins, Security and Costs

A good WordPress agency in the UK builds lean, secure, fast sites with few plugins. Here is what to ask, what it costs and the red flags to avoid.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A strong WordPress agency in the UK keeps plugins to a minimum, builds on a maintained theme and hands you full ownership of the site and hosting.
  • Small WordPress websites typically sit around £1,000 to £2,000 and WooCommerce stores around £1,500 to £3,500, with builds taking 3 to 5 or 5 to 12 weeks.
  • Ask about security, updates, backups, speed and SEO migration before you sign, not after launch.

The right WordPress agency in the UK builds you a lean, secure site on a maintained theme, with as few plugins as possible, and hands you full ownership of the code, domain and hosting. For most small businesses that means a budget of roughly £1,000 to £2,000 for a brochure site and £1,500 to £3,500 for a WooCommerce store, delivered in a few weeks rather than months.

WordPress powers a huge share of the web, which is both its strength and its weakness. Almost anyone can install it, so the gap between a careful agency and a careless one is wide. This guide explains what a good WordPress build looks like, how to judge an agency on plugins and security, what drives cost, and the questions worth asking before you commit.

What a WordPress agency in the UK should actually do

A WordPress agency should do more than install a theme and add your logo. The job covers planning the site structure, designing pages around what your customers need to know, building them so they load quickly, setting up the basics of technical SEO, and making sure the site can be updated safely after launch. If you are unsure how WordPress compares with hosted platforms, our guide to Shopify vs WooCommerce in the UK covers the trade-offs for online shops.

In practice, a sensible WordPress project includes:

  • Discovery: goals, audience, services, and the pages you need to rank and convert.
  • Information architecture: a clear menu and URL structure that search engines and people can follow.
  • Design: page layouts built around calls to action, not decoration.
  • Build: a maintained theme or block-based build, a short list of trusted plugins, and clean templates.
  • Content and SEO setup: titles, meta descriptions, headings, redirects and an XML sitemap.
  • Launch and handover: testing on mobile, analytics, backups and a short guide to editing the site.

Builds: theme, page builder or block editor

There are three common ways to build a WordPress site, and each suits a different situation.

Build approachBest forWatch out for
Block editor (Gutenberg) with a lightweight themeMost small business and service sites that want speed and easy editingNeeds a thoughtful setup of reusable patterns so editors stay on brand
Page builder (drag and drop)Teams that want lots of visual control without codeHeavier pages, more scripts, and lock-in to the builder if you switch later
Custom themeSites with unusual layouts or integrationsHigher cost and a dependency on whoever wrote the code
WooCommerce on any of the aboveOnline shops that want to own their platformExtra plugins for payments, shipping and tax, so updates matter more

For most UK small businesses a lightweight theme with the block editor is the sensible default. It keeps pages fast, avoids builder lock-in and makes day-to-day edits simple. If you plan to sell online, our WooCommerce agency guide goes deeper on store builds.

Plugins: fewer is better

Plugins are where most WordPress problems start. Each plugin adds code, can slow pages down, and needs updating. Abandoned plugins are a common route for attackers. A careful agency will justify every plugin it installs and prefer one well-maintained tool over three overlapping ones.

A reasonable core plugin set for a brochure site might include an SEO plugin, a forms plugin, a caching or performance tool, a backup tool and a security tool. Anything beyond that should have a clear reason. Ask the agency for the full plugin list before launch and check that each one is actively maintained and compatible with your version of WordPress.

Security and maintenance

WordPress is secure when it is kept up to date and configured sensibly. Most incidents come from out-of-date plugins, weak passwords, shared admin logins and cheap hosting with no isolation. A good agency will set up:

  • Individual user accounts with the right roles, and two-factor authentication for admins.
  • Automatic or scheduled updates for core, themes and plugins, with testing.
  • Daily off-site backups and a tested restore process.
  • An SSL certificate and secure hosting with server-level firewalling.
  • Limits on login attempts and removal of unused plugins and themes.

Security is not a one-off launch task. Our guide to website maintenance in the UK explains what ongoing care involves and why it matters for both security and rankings.

Speed and SEO on WordPress

WordPress can be very fast or painfully slow depending on how it is built. Image sizes, theme weight, plugin count and hosting quality all play a part. Fast pages help visitors, help conversion and support search performance through Core Web Vitals. Ask how the agency handles image compression, caching and script loading.

On the SEO side, the build should include clean URLs, sensible headings, structured data where relevant, an XML sitemap and correct indexing settings. If you are replacing an older site, redirects are essential. Our guide to a website redesign without losing rankings walks through the migration steps, and the technical SEO audit checklist is useful once the site is live.

How much does a WordPress agency cost in the UK?

Costs depend mostly on page count, design complexity, integrations and whether you need ecommerce. As a guide, a small WordPress website typically sits around £1,000 to £2,000, and a WooCommerce store around £1,500 to £3,500. A small website usually takes 3 to 5 weeks and an ecommerce build 5 to 12 weeks, depending on content readiness and feedback speed.

Questions to ask before you hire

A few direct questions will tell you a lot about an agency. Use these in any first call:

  1. Will I own the site, the theme licence, the domain and the hosting account?
  2. Which plugins will you install, and why each one?
  3. How do you handle updates, backups and security after launch?
  4. How will you protect my current rankings if this replaces an existing site?
  5. How fast will the pages load on mobile, and how do you test it?
  6. What training or documentation do I get for editing content?
  7. What is included in the price, and what costs extra?

For a broader framework, our guide on how to choose a digital marketing agency in the UK covers contracts, reporting and red flags in more detail.

Red flags

  • The agency keeps the hosting or admin login in its own name and will not transfer it.
  • A long plugin list with no explanation, or nulled premium plugins.
  • No mention of redirects when replacing an existing site.
  • No plan for updates, backups or security after launch.
  • Guaranteed rankings or traffic numbers.

WordPress agency checklist

  • Clear scope, page list and price in writing.
  • You own the domain, hosting, theme licence and admin account.
  • Lightweight theme or block-based build, with a justified plugin list.
  • Two-factor authentication, roles, backups and an update plan.
  • Mobile testing and a speed check before launch.
  • Titles, meta descriptions, sitemap and redirects set up.
  • Analytics installed with consent handled properly.
  • Handover guide so your team can edit pages safely.

FAQs

How do I choose a WordPress agency in the UK?

Look for an agency that explains its plugin choices, keeps you as the owner of the site and hosting, has a clear plan for security and updates, and can protect your rankings during a migration. Ask for a written scope and price before you start.

How much does a WordPress website cost in the UK?

A small WordPress website typically costs around £1,000 to £2,000, and a WooCommerce store around £1,500 to £3,500. Hosting, domains and premium plugin licences are usually separate.

How long does a WordPress build take?

A small website usually takes 3 to 5 weeks and an ecommerce build 5 to 12 weeks. The biggest factor is often how quickly content and feedback are ready.

Is WordPress secure enough for a business website?

Yes, when it is kept updated, uses few well-maintained plugins, has strong logins with two-factor authentication and is backed up regularly. Most problems come from neglect rather than WordPress itself.

Should I use a page builder on WordPress?

A page builder gives visual control but often adds weight and lock-in. For most small business sites, a lightweight theme with the block editor is faster and easier to maintain.

Can a WordPress agency also help with SEO?

A good one will set up the technical basics at build stage, such as clean URLs, headings, sitemaps and redirects. Ongoing SEO is a separate piece of work that builds on that foundation.
