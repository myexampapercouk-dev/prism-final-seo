Shopify Custom Product Options: How to Sell Personalised and Made-to-Order Products

How to add Shopify custom product options such as engraving text, colour choices and file uploads, when to use variants or apps, and how to fulfil them.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Use variants for a small number of fixed choices that affect stock or price, and custom options for free text, uploads or large numbers of combinations.
  • Custom options are usually added through theme settings, a small code change or an app, and the customer's choices travel with the order to fulfilment.
  • Clear instructions, a preview where practical and a written policy on personalised items reduce mistakes, disputes and wasted stock.

Shopify custom product options let customers personalise a product before adding it to the basket, for example by typing a name for engraving, choosing a thread colour or uploading a photo. Standard variants handle a limited set of fixed choices, so most personalisation is added with custom fields, often called line item properties, through your theme or a product options app.

This guide explains the difference between variants and custom options, the ways to add them, how to charge for extras, and how to make sure personalised orders reach the person making them without errors. Shopify and its apps change regularly, so check current help pages and app documentation for the exact steps that apply to your theme.

Variants versus custom options

Choosing the right tool is the most important decision. Variants are built into Shopify and suit choices that are fixed and limited, such as size or colour, where each combination may have its own stock level, SKU or price. Custom options suit choices that are open-ended, such as a message, or where the number of combinations would be unmanageable as variants.

Many products use both. A bracelet might have variants for metal type and size, with a custom option for engraving text. Our guide to Shopify product variants covers how to plan options and variants, and our article on product variants and bundles looks at the customer side of the same decisions.

How to add Shopify custom product options

There are three common routes. Which is best depends on how complex your options are and how comfortable you are with theme changes.

Theme settings and blocks. Some themes include a block for adding a text field or similar input to the product page. If yours does, this is the simplest route, with no code and no extra app. Check your theme's documentation and the product template in the theme editor. Our guide to Shopify theme sections and blocks explains where to look.

A small theme change. A developer can add custom fields to the product form so the customer's input is saved against the item in the basket. This is lightweight and avoids an app subscription, but it needs care so it survives theme updates and works with your basket and checkout. Make these changes in a duplicate theme first.

A product options app. Apps offer more features, such as conditional options that appear only when another choice is made, file uploads, price add-ons and live previews. The trade-off is a monthly cost, extra code on the page and another dependency to manage. Our Shopify apps audit guide helps you judge whether an app earns its place.

Designing the options on the product page

Personalisation only works if customers understand what they are choosing. A confusing form leads to typos, wrong choices and support emails.

  • Explain each field. Say what the text will be used for, where it will appear and any limits, such as maximum characters or which symbols are supported.
  • Use the right input type. Use a dropdown or swatches for fixed choices, a short text box for names and a longer box only where a message is expected.
  • Make required fields clear. If the product cannot be made without a field, mark it as required so the customer cannot add it to the basket empty.
  • Show a preview where practical. Even a simple text preview helps customers spot spelling mistakes.
  • Repeat the choices in the basket. Customers should see their personalisation in the basket so they can check it before paying.
  • Keep it mobile friendly. Most shoppers will fill in the form on a phone, so test it on small screens.

Good product photography helps customers picture the result. Our Shopify product media guide covers images and video that show personalised examples clearly.

Charging for personalisation

If personalisation costs extra, there are several ways to handle the price. One is to use variants, for example with and without engraving, which works when the extra is a simple fixed price. Another is to use an app that adds a price for selected options, often by adding a hidden add-on product to the basket. A third is to create a separate add-on product, such as a gift box, that customers add alongside the main item.

Whichever route you choose, make the total clear before checkout and test that discounts, VAT and refunds behave correctly with the add-on. Price add-ons that rely on hidden products can sometimes affect stock reports or discount rules, so check how your app handles them. If you sell gift extras, our guide to Shopify gift wrapping and order notes covers related options.

Getting personalised orders to fulfilment

The customer's choices need to reach the person making or packing the product in a clear, consistent form. Custom fields are normally attached to the line item on the order, so they appear on the order page in the admin. Check that they also appear on packing slips, in any fulfilment app and in notifications, and that long text is not cut off.

  • Edit the packing slip template to show custom fields clearly beside each item.
  • Tag personalised orders so they can be filtered and handled first if they take longer.
  • Agree a rule for checking spelling before production, especially for engraving or printing that cannot be undone.
  • Show realistic production times on the product page and in order confirmation emails.

Our guide to the Shopify order fulfilment workflow covers packing slips, tags and order status in more detail.

Returns and personalised products

UK consumer law treats goods made to the customer's specifications or clearly personalised differently from standard goods in some respects, particularly around the right to cancel an online order. However, personalised items must still be as described and of satisfactory quality, and faults or mistakes on your side still need putting right. The rules have detail and exceptions, so check current official guidance and take legal advice if you are unsure.

Whatever your position, explain it plainly on the product page and in your returns policy, so customers know before they order. Our guide to Shopify store policies covers how to set up and present these pages.

Common problems and how to avoid them

  • Options missing from orders. If a theme change or app conflict stops custom fields saving, orders arrive without the personalisation. Place a test order after any theme update.
  • Express checkout buttons skipping fields. Some quick-buy buttons on product pages can bypass required fields. Test them and hide them on personalised products if needed.
  • Slow product pages. Heavy option apps add scripts. Check page speed before and after installing one.
  • Too many choices. Long forms put people off. Offer the options that customers value and leave out the rest.

FAQs

How do I add Shopify custom product options?

Check first whether your theme offers a block for custom text fields. If not, a developer can add fields to the product form, or you can use a product options app for features such as conditional fields, uploads and price add-ons.

What are line item properties in Shopify?

They are extra pieces of information saved against an item in the basket, such as engraving text or a chosen font. They travel with the order so you can see them in the admin and on packing slips.

Can customers upload images on a Shopify product page?

Yes, usually through a product options or file upload app. Check where files are stored, any size limits, and how you will handle customer images in line with your privacy policy.

Should I use variants or custom options for colours?

If each colour needs its own stock level, SKU or price, use variants. If the colour is chosen for a made-to-order item and does not affect stock, a custom option may be simpler.

Can I charge extra for personalisation in Shopify?

Yes. Common approaches are a variant with a higher price, an app that adds a price for chosen options, or a separate add-on product. Test that discounts, VAT and refunds work correctly with whichever method you pick.

Do personalised items have to be returnable?

UK rules on cancelling online orders treat personalised and made-to-order goods differently in some ways, but faulty or incorrect items must still be put right. Check current official guidance and set out your policy clearly before customers order.
