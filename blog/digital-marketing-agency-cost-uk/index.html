How Much Does a Digital Marketing Agency Cost in the UK?

A UK digital marketing agency typically costs a few hundred to several thousand pounds a month. See retainer vs project pricing and what to compare.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most UK agencies charge a monthly retainer for ongoing work and a fixed project fee for builds such as websites.
  • Price is driven by scope, channels, competition and how much senior time you get, not by the agency's logo.
  • Compare quotes line by line: deliverables, reporting, who owns the accounts and whether ad spend sits outside the fee.

A digital marketing agency in the UK typically costs anywhere from a few hundred pounds a month for a focused local package to several thousand pounds a month for multi-channel work, with one-off projects such as websites priced separately. The right figure depends on what you need done, how competitive your market is and whether you are paying for ongoing work or a defined project.

This guide explains how UK agencies price their work, what pushes a quote up or down, and how to compare proposals so you are judging like for like. If you are still deciding whether you need an agency at all, start with our guide on how to choose a digital marketing agency in the UK, then come back here once you are ready to talk money.

How much does a digital marketing agency cost in the UK?

There is no single market rate, because "digital marketing" covers very different jobs. A local trade business needing basic search visibility and a few posts a month is buying something quite different from an online retailer running paid search, paid social, content and conversion work together. Broadly, UK pricing falls into three shapes:

  • Monthly retainers for continuous work such as SEO, content, paid media management and reporting.
  • Fixed-price projects for work with a clear start and end, such as a website build, a brand project or an audit.
  • Day rates or hourly rates for consultancy, training or ad hoc support.

Advertising spend is a separate cost in almost every case. The fee pays the agency for strategy, set-up and management; the media budget goes to Google, Meta, LinkedIn or another platform.

Retainer vs project pricing

A retainer makes sense when the work compounds over time. Search engine optimisation, content and paid media all improve through steady iteration: publishing, testing, measuring and adjusting each month. SEO and paid campaigns typically take 3 to 6 months to show meaningful results, so a retainer gives the work room to build. Our guide to how much SEO costs in the UK goes deeper on the search side.

A project makes sense when the output is a finished thing. A website, a brand identity or a technical audit has a scope you can define up front, so a fixed fee with agreed deliverables and revision rounds is normally fairer to both sides. A small website usually takes 3 to 5 weeks and an ecommerce build 5 to 12 weeks, depending on scope.

Many businesses use both: a one-off project to fix the foundations, then a retainer to grow from them. If your site is slow, hard to update or not converting, it is usually worth fixing that before paying for traffic.

What drives the price of a marketing agency

When two quotes for "digital marketing" differ by thousands of pounds, the gap almost always comes down to these factors.

Scope and number of channels

One channel done properly costs less than four channels done at once. Each channel needs set-up, tracking, creative or content, and ongoing optimisation. Adding paid social on top of SEO is not just "more posts"; it is a separate discipline with its own testing cycle. Our services overview shows how the channels fit together.

Competition in your market

Ranking a plumber in a market town is a different challenge from ranking a national online retailer. Competitive markets need more content, more technical work and often digital PR and link building, all of which add time.

Seniority and time allocated

Ask how many hours a month your account receives and who does the work. A lower fee may simply mean less time. Neither is wrong, but you should know what you are buying.

Starting point

A business with clean analytics, a fast site and existing content needs less foundational work than one starting from scratch. Broken tracking, a poor site structure or a history of low-quality links all add cost early on.

Reporting and meetings

Weekly calls, custom dashboards and board-level reports take time to produce. Decide what level of reporting you actually need rather than paying for presentations nobody reads.

Hidden costs to watch for

The headline fee is rarely the whole story. Before signing, check whether any of the following sit outside the quote:

  • Ad spend, and whether any percentage is added on top of it.
  • Software and tools such as rank trackers, reporting platforms or plugins that you may be asked to pay for.
  • Set-up or onboarding fees in the first month.
  • Creative production such as photography, video or design for ads.
  • Development time for on-site changes that SEO or conversion work recommends.
  • Exit costs, including notice periods and any charge for handing over accounts or files.

None of these are unusual. The problem is only when they come as a surprise. A good proposal lists them clearly.

How to compare agency quotes

Price only means something next to what it buys. Put every proposal into the same grid and compare the substance:

  • What exactly is delivered each month, in numbers (posts, pages, campaigns, hours)?
  • Which channels are included and which are extra?
  • Who owns the ad accounts, analytics, website and content?
  • How is success measured, and how often is it reported?
  • What is the minimum term and notice period?
  • Is ad spend paid directly by you to the platform?

Our article on SEO agency vs freelancer is also useful if you are weighing a smaller supplier against a studio.

Checklist before you sign

  • You have a written scope listing monthly deliverables.
  • You know whether ad spend is inside or outside the fee.
  • Ad, analytics and Search Console accounts are in your name, with the agency given access.
  • Reporting format and frequency are agreed.
  • Minimum term, notice period and exit process are clear.
  • You understand what results are realistic in the first 3 to 6 months.
  • Any extra costs such as tools, creative or development are listed.
  • You have budget left to act on recommendations, not just receive them.

Is a cheap agency a false economy?

Not necessarily. A small business with modest goals can get good value from a tightly scoped, low-cost package, provided the scope is honest. The risk is a cheap retainer that promises everything and delivers very little, or tactics that cut corners and cause problems later. Equally, an expensive agency is not automatically better; you may be paying for overheads and account management layers rather than more work on your account.

The best measure is clarity. If an agency can tell you exactly what will happen each month, why it matters and how you will know whether it is working, the price is much easier to judge.

FAQs

How much does a digital marketing agency cost in the UK?

It ranges from a few hundred pounds a month for a focused local package to several thousand pounds a month for multi-channel work. Our retainers start from £350, £750 and £1,500 a month, with ad spend paid separately.

Is ad spend included in an agency fee?

Usually not. The agency fee pays for strategy and management, and the media budget is paid to the advertising platform. Always check how a proposal treats spend.

Should I choose a retainer or a project?

Choose a retainer for work that builds over time, such as SEO, content and paid media. Choose a fixed project for work with a defined end, such as a website or audit.

How long before I see results from an agency?

SEO and paid campaigns typically show meaningful results within 3 to 6 months. Technical fixes can help sooner, while competitive terms take longer.

Why do agency quotes vary so much?

Differences in scope, channels, time allocated, seniority, competition and starting point explain most of the gap. Compare deliverables rather than headline prices.

What should a marketing agency proposal include?

Monthly deliverables, channels, reporting, account ownership, minimum term, notice period and any extra costs such as tools, creative or development time.
