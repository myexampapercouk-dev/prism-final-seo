Ecommerce Gift Cards and Vouchers: How to Sell Them Well Online

Selling gift cards and vouchers online: digital vs physical, product page design, delivery, redemption, expiry, VAT and fraud points for UK ecommerce stores.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Gift cards are a product in their own right and deserve a clear, well-designed product page.
  • Delivery, redemption and balance checking should be simple for both the buyer and the recipient.
  • Expiry, VAT treatment and fraud controls need careful thought, and legal and tax points should be checked with an adviser.

Gift cards and vouchers are a natural fit for many online shops. They help customers who want to give a present but are unsure what to choose, they can bring new people to your store through the recipient, and they are especially popular around Christmas, birthdays and other occasions. Yet many shops treat them as an afterthought: a plain product with a generic image, unclear terms and a confusing redemption process.

This guide is for UK ecommerce owners thinking about adding gift cards or improving the ones they already sell. It covers the main types, how to design the product page, delivery and redemption, terms and expiry, VAT and accounting points to check, fraud prevention and how to promote gift cards sensibly.

Gift cards versus discount codes

It helps to be clear on terms, as people use them loosely:

  • Gift cards or gift vouchers are bought with money and hold a stored value that the recipient spends in your shop.
  • Discount codes or promotional vouchers are given away to reduce the price of an order, usually as a marketing tactic.

This guide focuses on gift cards that customers pay for. The distinction matters because the two are often treated differently for accounting and tax purposes, and customers expect different things from them.

Digital, physical or both?

Most online shops start with digital gift cards, delivered by email. They are quick to set up, cost little to fulfil and suit last-minute buyers. Physical cards, posted in a card or envelope, appeal to people who want something to wrap and hand over.

Consider:

  • Digital gift cards. Instant or scheduled delivery, no postage, good for late shoppers.
  • Physical gift cards. A tangible present, but with printing, packaging and postage to handle, plus delivery cut-off dates.
  • Experience or service vouchers. For businesses selling services, such as a treatment or a class, rather than products.

Shopify includes gift card functionality on its plans, and WooCommerce can sell gift cards through extensions or custom development. The best setup depends on your platform, how you want cards to look and how they connect with your point-of-sale system if you also sell in person.

Designing the gift card product page

A gift card is a product, so its page should be as considered as any other. Include:

  • An attractive image. A designed card that reflects your brand, rather than a generic placeholder.
  • Clear value options. A sensible range of amounts, and a custom amount option if your platform supports it.
  • Recipient details. Fields for the recipient's name and email, plus a personal message.
  • Delivery date. The option to schedule delivery for a birthday or other date, where possible.
  • How it works. A short explanation of how the recipient receives and spends the card.
  • Key terms. Expiry, where it can be used, and whether it can be used alongside other offers, with a link to full terms.

Make it obvious whether the card is digital or physical, and for physical cards, show delivery options and cut-off dates. Our ecommerce product descriptions guide covers writing clear product copy.

The recipient's experience

The person receiving the gift card may never have heard of your shop. Their first impression matters. A good gift card email:

  • Clearly shows who sent it and includes their message.
  • Displays the value and the code prominently.
  • Explains how to spend it, with a link to the shop.
  • States any expiry date and links to terms.
  • Looks on-brand and works on mobile.

Test the email by sending gift cards to yourself. Check it does not land in spam, that the code works and that the link takes the recipient somewhere helpful, such as your homepage or a popular collection.

Redemption and balance checking

Spending a gift card should be effortless. Make sure:

  • There is a clear place to enter the code at checkout.
  • Partial use is supported, with the remaining balance carried over, if your setup allows.
  • Customers can check their balance, either through a dedicated page or at checkout.
  • Gift cards can be combined with another payment method if the order exceeds the balance.
  • If you have physical shops, cards work in-store as well as online, or the terms say clearly if they do not.

Confusion at checkout creates customer service work and can leave recipients with a poor view of your shop.

Terms, expiry and refunds

Write clear, fair terms for gift cards and make them easy to find. Points to cover usually include:

  • How long the card is valid.
  • Where it can be spent.
  • Whether it can be exchanged for cash (typically not).
  • What happens if the card is lost or the code is shared.
  • Whether it can be used with promotions.
  • What happens to refunds on items bought with a gift card.

Consumer protection law applies to how gift cards are sold and described, and expiry periods should be reasonable and clearly communicated. Requirements can change and depend on your circumstances, so take legal advice when setting terms. This guide is not legal advice.

VAT and accounting points to check

Gift cards can raise VAT and accounting questions. In the UK, the VAT treatment of vouchers depends on how they are classified, broadly whether they can only be used for one type of goods or service with a known VAT rate, or for a range of items. That affects when VAT is accounted for. Unredeemed balances and how you record gift card liabilities also need thought.

These rules are technical and can change, so speak to your accountant or check current HMRC guidance before launching gift cards. Do not rely on this guide for tax decisions.

Preventing fraud

Gift cards can attract fraudsters because they are easy to resell and hard to trace. Sensible precautions include:

  • Using your platform's or payment provider's fraud screening on gift card orders.
  • Keeping an eye on unusual patterns, such as many high-value cards bought quickly.
  • Using long, random codes that are hard to guess.
  • Limiting repeated failed attempts to check balances or redeem codes, where your platform allows.
  • Delivering to the email address provided and being cautious about changes after purchase.

Speak to your platform or payment provider about the controls available to you.

Promoting gift cards

Gift cards are easy to forget unless you remind people. Ideas include:

  • Adding a gift card link to your main navigation or footer, especially around key occasions.
  • Featuring gift cards in gift guides and seasonal collections.
  • Reminding email subscribers when physical delivery cut-offs pass that digital cards are still available.
  • Showing a gift card option on product pages for shoppers unsure about sizes or preferences.
  • Offering gift cards for corporate gifting, if that suits your audience.

Our website navigation guide covers where to place links like these, and our ecommerce email marketing guide covers seasonal campaigns.

SEO for gift card pages

People search for gift cards from specific shops and for gift ideas in general. Give your gift card page a clear, descriptive title, useful content explaining how it works and a clean URL. Link to it from relevant pages. Our product page SEO guide covers the basics.

Measuring performance

Track gift card sales over time, especially in peak seasons. Look at how many cards are redeemed, how many recipients become repeat customers and whether recipients spend more than the card value. These insights help you decide how prominently to feature gift cards.

Common mistakes

  • Using a generic image and vague product copy.
  • Hiding terms or failing to state expiry clearly.
  • Making redemption confusing at checkout.
  • Forgetting that the recipient may be new to your shop.
  • Launching without checking VAT treatment with an accountant.
  • Ignoring fraud screening on gift card orders.

FAQs

Can I sell gift cards on Shopify?

Yes. Shopify includes gift card functionality on its plans. Check your plan and settings for the options available.

Can WooCommerce sell gift cards?

Yes, usually through an extension or custom development, as gift cards are not part of the core plugin.

Should gift cards expire?

Many shops set an expiry period. Make it reasonable and clearly stated, and take legal advice on your terms.

How is VAT handled on gift cards?

It depends on how the voucher is classified. Check current HMRC guidance or speak to your accountant.

Should I offer digital or physical gift cards?

Digital cards are simpler and suit late shoppers. Physical cards suit people who want something to wrap. Many shops offer both.

How do I stop gift card fraud?

Use fraud screening, monitor unusual orders, use long random codes and speak to your platform or payment provider about available controls.
