Web Design in Edinburgh: Websites for Tourism, Festival and Financial Services Businesses

Web design for Edinburgh businesses in tourism, festivals and financial services: seasonal traffic, direct bookings, trust signals and compliant, clear content.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Edinburgh tourism and festival businesses need websites that cope with seasonal peaks, work well on phones and make booking direct easy.
  • Financial services firms need clarity, trust and careful wording, with regulatory information easy to find and claims kept accurate.
  • Prism Works Studio works with Edinburgh businesses remotely and has no office in the city.

Web design in Edinburgh has to serve two very different kinds of business. On one side are hotels, guest houses, tour operators, venues and festival performers whose demand rises and falls with the seasons and the August calendar. On the other are financial services, legal and professional firms for whom trust, clarity and careful wording matter more than anything. A good Edinburgh website starts by knowing which of those audiences it is for. Prism Works Studio works with Edinburgh businesses remotely, planning and building sites over video calls and shared drafts.

This guide covers what each type of business needs from its website, where the two overlap, and the questions worth asking before you commission a new site.

Tourism and hospitality: designing for visitors

Visitors to Edinburgh often plan on a phone, sometimes from abroad, sometimes while already walking between the Old Town, the New Town, Leith or Stockbridge. That shapes almost every design decision.

  • Mobile first, genuinely. Menus, booking buttons and maps should be easy to use with one thumb.
  • Location made obvious. Clear directions, nearest landmarks and transport links help people who do not know the city.
  • Booking direct. A prominent, simple booking route reduces reliance on third-party platforms and their commissions.
  • Clear policies. Cancellation terms, accessibility information and check-in details answered up front reduce emails and doubt.
  • Real photography. Photos of your actual rooms, tours or venue tend to build more confidence than stock images.

Our guides on hotel and B&B website design and increasing hotel direct bookings go deeper on booking flows and conversion.

Festival season and traffic spikes

Many Edinburgh businesses see a sharp change in demand around the summer festivals, Hogmanay and other events. A site that is fine for most of the year can slow down or confuse visitors at exactly the wrong moment.

Things to plan for include:

  1. Hosting that can handle peaks. Ask how your hosting copes with sudden increases in visitors, and what happens if it does not.
  2. Seasonal content you can switch easily. Event listings, show times and special offers should be simple for your team to update without a developer.
  3. Light pages. Large images and video should be compressed so pages still load on busy mobile networks in crowded streets.
  4. Clear sold-out and availability messages. Saving people a wasted click is good for your reputation.

For performers, producers and small venues, a simple, fast site with show details, dates, press quotes you are entitled to use and a clear ticket link can matter as much as listings elsewhere. Keep it updated after the festival too, so it keeps working for future bookings.

Restaurants, bars and attractions

Food and drink businesses and visitor attractions sit between tourism and local trade. Menus should be readable on a phone as text rather than a PDF, opening hours should be accurate on both the website and your Google Business Profile, and booking or ticketing should take as few steps as possible. Our restaurant website design guide covers this in more detail.

Financial services: designing for trust

Edinburgh is well known for financial services, from investment and pensions to insurance and fintech, alongside a strong professional services community. For these firms, a website is often the first test of credibility. A prospective client or partner wants to understand who you are, what you do, whether you are regulated and how to get in touch.

ElementWhy it matters
Plain-English service pagesVisitors should understand what you do without decoding jargon
Regulatory informationWhere a firm is regulated, its status and required disclosures should be easy to find, usually in the footer and on a dedicated page
People and expertiseAccurate profiles help visitors judge who they would be dealing with
Accessible designClear type, good contrast and keyboard-friendly navigation help all visitors, including older clients
Careful claimsStatements about returns, performance or outcomes need to be accurate and appropriately qualified

Regulated firms should check website content against their own compliance requirements. If your firm is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, its rules on financial promotions may apply to some website content, so it is sensible for your compliance team or adviser to review copy before launch. A web designer can build the structure, but sign-off on regulated wording should sit with the firm.

Our guide to website accessibility in the UK explains the standards worth building to, which are especially relevant if your clients include older or vulnerable people.

Where the two worlds overlap

Whether you run a guest house in Leith or an advisory firm near the West End, some principles apply to every Edinburgh website:

  • Fast, well-structured pages that are easy to read on any device.
  • Accurate contact details, opening hours and location on the site and your Google Business Profile.
  • Content written for the questions people actually ask, which also helps with AI search answers.
  • A clear next step on every page, whether that is booking, calling or sending an enquiry.
  • Privacy and cookie notices that reflect what the site actually does, in line with Information Commissioner's Office guidance.

SEO for Edinburgh businesses

Tourism searches are competitive and often dominated by large booking and review platforms. Smaller businesses usually do better by targeting specific needs, such as a type of accommodation in a particular neighbourhood, a niche tour or an accessible venue, rather than broad terms.

Financial and professional firms tend to benefit from authoritative service pages and helpful guides that answer real client questions. In both cases a well-maintained Google Business Profile, consistent business details and genuine reviews support local visibility. Our hotel SEO guide and local SEO guide explain the basics.

Choosing a platform

WordPress suits most tourism, hospitality and professional sites because content is easy to update and booking or ticketing tools can be connected. Squarespace or Wix can work for very small sites, though they offer less flexibility as you grow. If you sell gift vouchers, merchandise or products, Shopify or WooCommerce can handle the store side.

Multilingual visitors

Edinburgh attracts visitors from all over the world. Some tourism businesses add key information in other languages, such as booking terms, directions and essential policies. If you do, keep translations accurate and maintained, and use a proper multilingual structure with hreflang tags rather than an automatic translation widget, so search engines can index each version. Start small with the languages your guests most often use, and expand only if you can keep the content up to date.

Questions to ask before you start

  • How will the site handle seasonal traffic peaks?
  • Can our team update events, menus or offers without help?
  • How will direct booking or enquiry routes be made prominent?
  • Where will regulatory or policy information sit, and who signs off the wording?
  • How will accessibility be tested before launch?
  • Will we own the domain, hosting and admin logins?

FAQs

Can a website really help an Edinburgh hotel get more direct bookings?

A clear, fast site with a simple booking route and accurate information can make booking direct easier and more appealing. Results vary, but removing friction is usually the first step.

How should a festival performer or venue use their website?

Keep it simple: show details, dates, a clear ticket link, genuine press quotes and contact details for bookers. Update it promptly, and keep it live after the festival for future enquiries.

What should a financial services website include?

Plain-English service pages, accurate team profiles, easy-to-find regulatory information where relevant, accessible design and a clear way to get in touch. Regulated wording should be reviewed by the firm's compliance lead.

Does my website need to follow FCA rules?

If your firm is FCA authorised, some website content may count as a financial promotion and could fall under its rules. Check with your compliance team or adviser, as requirements depend on your permissions and content.

Do you have an office in Edinburgh?

No. Prism Works Studio works with Edinburgh businesses remotely, using video calls and shared documents throughout the project.

Which platform is best for an Edinburgh tourism business?

WordPress is a flexible choice for most, as it connects to booking tools and is easy to update. Very small sites may suit Squarespace or Wix, and Shopify can handle vouchers or merchandise.
