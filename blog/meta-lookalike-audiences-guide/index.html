Meta Lookalike Audiences: How to Find More Customers Like Your Best Ones

Meta lookalike audiences help you reach people similar to your customers. Learn how to build a good source list, set one up and test it in the UK.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A lookalike is only as good as its source: use buyers or qualified leads rather than every website visitor.
  • Customer lists must be collected and uploaded in line with UK GDPR and your privacy notice.
  • Test lookalikes against broad targeting, because Meta often finds similar people on its own.

Meta lookalike audiences let you reach people on Facebook and Instagram who share characteristics with an audience you already have, such as past customers, email subscribers or people who completed a purchase on your website. You choose a source audience and a country such as the UK, and Meta builds a new audience of people it judges to be similar.

For a small business, this can be a useful way to move beyond friends of existing followers and reach new people who are more likely to be interested. But Meta's targeting has changed a lot in recent years, with more automation and broader audiences, so lookalikes are now one option among several rather than a guaranteed shortcut. This guide covers how they work, how to build one well and how to test whether it helps. Meta changes its ads tools frequently, so check the current Meta Business Help Centre for exact steps and options.

How Meta lookalike audiences work

A lookalike starts from a source audience. Meta looks at patterns among the people in that source and finds others in your chosen location who resemble them. You can usually choose a size range, where a smaller percentage of the country's population is closer to your source and a larger percentage is broader but less similar.

Lookalike sizeSimilarityReachWhen it suits
Small rangeClosest to your sourceSmallest audienceTesting with a strong source list
Medium rangeReasonably similarModerateScaling once results look promising
Large rangeBroadestLargestBigger budgets, awareness campaigns

In some campaign types, Meta may treat your lookalike as a suggestion and show ads beyond it if it predicts better results. Check how your chosen campaign and audience settings handle this.

Choosing a good source audience

The source matters more than anything else. A lookalike based on people who bought from you is usually more useful than one based on everyone who ever visited your site. Common sources include:

  • Customer lists: emails or phone numbers of past buyers, uploaded and matched by Meta.
  • Website custom audiences: people who completed a purchase or enquiry, tracked by the Meta Pixel and Conversions API.
  • Engagement audiences: people who engaged with your page, profile, videos or lead forms.
  • High-value customers: repeat buyers or larger orders, where your data allows.

Meta generally requires a minimum number of matched people in the source, and more is usually better. Check the current minimum on the help pages. If your list is small, an engagement or website audience may give a larger base to start from.

Website-based sources rely on tracking being set up properly. Our Meta Pixel set-up guide explains how to track purchases and enquiries so you have reliable events to build from.

Customer lists and UK GDPR

Uploading a customer list to Meta means sharing personal data with a third party for advertising. Under UK GDPR you need a lawful basis for this, and your privacy notice should explain that you may use customer details for advertising on social platforms. Meta hashes the data during upload, but that does not remove your responsibilities.

Practical steps include checking how the data was collected, removing people who have opted out of marketing where that is appropriate, and keeping uploads limited to what you need. The ICO publishes guidance on direct marketing and data sharing, and if you are unsure, take advice. Our guide to building an email list covers collecting contact details in a compliant way from the start.

How to create a Meta lookalike audience

These are the general steps. Menu names may differ in your account.

  1. Open Meta Ads Manager and go to the audiences area.
  2. Create your source first, for example a customer list or a website custom audience of purchasers.
  3. Choose to create a lookalike audience.
  4. Select your source audience.
  5. Choose the location, such as the United Kingdom.
  6. Pick the audience size range.
  7. Create the audience and wait for Meta to populate it, which can take some time.
  8. Select the lookalike in the audience section of a new ad set.

Testing lookalikes against other targeting

Meta's delivery system has become good at finding people on its own, especially with broad targeting and strong conversion data. That means a lookalike will not always beat simpler options. A fair test compares them directly.

OptionWhat it doesWatch for
Lookalike audienceStarts from people similar to your sourceQuality depends on source size and relevance
Broad targetingSets basic limits such as location and age, then lets Meta find peopleNeeds good conversion tracking
Interest targetingTargets people Meta associates with chosen interestsCan be narrow or loosely defined
RetargetingReaches people who already visited or engagedSmall audiences that tire quickly

Run the options as separate ad sets or use Meta's testing tools, keep the same ads and budget across them, and judge on cost per purchase or enquiry rather than clicks. Give each test enough time and spend to produce meaningful numbers. Retargeting works best alongside prospecting, and our remarketing guide explains how to set it up.

Excluding existing customers

When you use a lookalike to find new customers, it often makes sense to exclude the people already in your source, such as existing buyers. Otherwise some budget goes on people who already know you. The exception is when you want repeat purchases, in which case a separate retention campaign is clearer than mixing the two.

Creative still does most of the work

Targeting decides who might see an ad, but the creative decides whether they stop scrolling and act. For lookalikes, assume the audience has never heard of you. Show the product or service clearly, explain who it is for, and give a simple next step. Send people to a page that matches the ad, using the principles in our landing page design guide.

Lookalike ideas for different business types

The best source depends on what a valuable customer looks like for you:

  • Online shops: purchasers from your website, or repeat buyers if you have enough of them.
  • Service businesses: people who submitted an enquiry form or booked an appointment, rather than all visitors.
  • Local businesses: customer lists, combined with a location restricted to the area you serve.
  • Event and hospitality businesses: past bookers, with seasonal timing in mind.
  • Businesses with lead forms on Meta: people who opened or submitted the form, where volumes allow.

Whatever you choose, write down why you picked that source, so later tests have a clear starting point.

Common lookalike mistakes

  • Building from all website visitors, including people who bounced after a few seconds.
  • Using a source list that is too small or out of date.
  • Uploading customer data without checking consent and your privacy notice.
  • Creating many overlapping lookalikes that compete with each other.
  • Judging results after a few days with very little spend.
  • Forgetting to refresh customer list sources as your customer base grows.

Budget and cost

Lookalikes do not have a separate price. You pay for ad delivery as usual, and costs depend on competition, the time of year, your creative and your campaign objective. FAQs

What are Meta lookalike audiences?

They are audiences Meta builds from people who resemble a source audience you provide, such as customers or website purchasers. You choose the country and size, and Meta finds similar users on Facebook and Instagram.

How big does a source audience need to be?

Meta sets a minimum number of matched people, which can change, so check the help pages. In practice, a larger and more relevant source tends to produce a more useful lookalike.

Are lookalike audiences still worth using?

They can be, but Meta's broad targeting has improved, so test lookalikes against broad and interest targeting with the same ads and budget. Use whichever produces a lower cost per sale or enquiry for your business.

Can I upload my customer email list to Meta?

Yes, Meta allows customer list uploads, but you need a lawful basis under UK GDPR and a privacy notice that covers this use. Check ICO guidance and take advice if you are unsure.

Should I exclude existing customers from a lookalike campaign?

Usually yes for prospecting campaigns, so budget focuses on new people. Run a separate campaign if you want to reach existing customers for repeat purchases.

How long does a lookalike audience take to build?

Often a few hours, though it can take longer depending on the source. Meta keeps it updated over time, but refreshing customer list sources is still worthwhile.
