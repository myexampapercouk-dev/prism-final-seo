Email Marketing vs Social Media: Where a UK Small Business Should Focus

Email marketing reaches people who chose to hear from you on a list you own; social media helps new people discover you. Compare reach, cost and effort.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Email reaches people who have already opted in, on a list you control, so it is usually stronger for repeat sales, bookings and nurturing enquiries.
  • Social media is better for discovery and staying visible, but organic reach is limited and the platform controls who sees your posts.
  • Most small businesses do best using social media and their website to grow an email list, then using email to turn that attention into sales.

Email marketing usually works better for turning existing interest into sales, because you reach people who chose to hear from you on a list you control. Social media usually works better for being discovered by new people and staying visible, but the platform decides who sees your posts. For most UK small businesses the real answer is not one or the other: use social media and your website to grow an email list, then use email to bring people back.

This guide compares email marketing vs social media on reach, ownership, cost, effort and measurement, with a simple way to decide where to put your time first. Platform features and data protection guidance change, so check the current official help pages and ICO guidance before relying on any specific setting.

Reach: owned audience versus rented audience

When someone joins your email list, you can contact them directly whenever you have something useful to say. Not every email will be opened, and some will land in promotions or spam folders, but you are not relying on an algorithm to decide whether your message is worth showing.

Social media works differently. You might have a few thousand followers, yet each post is shown to only a portion of them, depending on how the platform judges it. Platforms also change their feeds, rules and features over time, and accounts can be restricted. That does not make social media a bad investment; it just means it is risky to make it the only place your audience lives.

This is why many businesses treat email as an owned asset. If you change software or a platform changes direction, your list comes with you. Our guide to building an email list covers practical ways to grow one from your website, shop counter and social channels.

Discovery: how new customers find you

Email is weak at discovery. People rarely join a list for a business they have never heard of. Social media, on the other hand, can put your work in front of people who did not know you existed, especially through shares, short video, local groups and creator content.

For businesses that sell visually, such as cafes, salons, florists, fitness studios and product brands, social media can be one of the main ways people first come across you. For many service businesses, search and recommendations do more of the discovery work, with social media acting as proof that you are active and trustworthy.

Paid social advertising can extend reach beyond your followers when organic posts are not enough. If you are considering that route, our guide to Facebook ads for small businesses explains how to start with a sensible test.

Conversion: turning attention into sales

Email is usually stronger at the point of conversion. A subscriber already knows you, has given you permission to contact them and can click straight through to a product, booking page or offer. Automated emails, such as a welcome series or a reminder after an abandoned basket, can keep working in the background. Our ecommerce email marketing guide covers the flows online shops tend to use.

Social media can drive sales too, particularly through shoppable posts, direct messages and promotions. But a social post competes with everything else in the feed, and people often scroll past even when they are interested. Many businesses find social media starts the relationship and email or a direct enquiry finishes it.

Cost and time

Both channels can be started cheaply, but neither is free once you count time. Email platforms usually charge based on list size or sending volume, and you will need time to plan and write each email. Social media profiles are free to set up, but posting often enough to stay visible can take far more time each week than a monthly newsletter.

Rules and consent in the UK

Email marketing to individuals in the UK is covered by UK GDPR and the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR). In general, you need appropriate consent to send marketing emails to individuals, with a limited exception for existing customers in some circumstances, and every email should include an easy way to unsubscribe. Keep records of how and when people signed up, and explain this in your privacy policy. Our guide to website terms and privacy policies covers the basics. This is general information, so check the ICO's current guidance for your situation.

Social media has its own rules: each platform's terms and advertising policies, plus the CAP Code for marketing content, including clear labelling of paid partnerships and ads. Rules on promotions, competitions and influencer content can apply here, so check the current official guidance before running anything unusual.

Measuring which channel works

Email platforms report opens, clicks and unsubscribes, though privacy features in some email apps mean open rates are less reliable than they used to be. Clicks and the sales or enquiries that follow are better signals.

Social platforms report reach, engagement and follower growth, which are useful but can be misleading if they do not lead to visits or enquiries. The fairest comparison is to send both channels to your website with tagged links and see which brings visitors who buy, book or enquire. Our guides to UTM tracking links and website analytics for small businesses show how to set that up.

How to decide where to focus

  1. If you have past customers but no list, start collecting email consent now, at checkout, booking or enquiry.
  2. If nobody knows you yet, prioritise discovery: search, social media and local listings.
  3. If you sell visually, give social media more weight, and use it to drive sign-ups.
  4. If you rely on repeat purchases or bookings, give email more weight.
  5. If time is tight, choose one social platform where your customers are and a simple monthly email, rather than spreading thinly.
  6. Review every quarter using website visits, enquiries and sales from each channel.

FAQs

Email marketing vs social media: which is better for a small business?

Email is usually better for repeat sales and bookings because you reach people who opted in. Social media is usually better for discovery. Most small businesses benefit from using social media to grow an email list.

Is email marketing still worth it in the UK?

For many businesses, yes. It gives you a direct line to people who chose to hear from you, on a list you own, and it can run automatically for welcome and reminder messages.

Do I need consent to send marketing emails?

In general, UK GDPR and PECR require appropriate consent for marketing emails to individuals, with some limited exceptions for existing customers. Check the ICO's current guidance for your situation.

How often should I email my list?

Often enough to stay remembered, but only when you have something useful to say. Many small businesses start monthly and increase frequency if engagement holds up.

Which social media platform should I use?

Start with the one where your customers already spend time and where your content suits the format. One well-run platform usually beats several neglected ones.

Can I use social media to grow my email list?

Yes. Share a genuine reason to sign up, such as useful tips, early access or a helpful guide, and link to a simple sign-up page on your website.
