Web design services cover everything needed to turn your business goals into a working website: planning, design, development, search setup, testing, launch and support. Discovery and planning

The most valuable hour of any project is the first one. We ask who your customers are, what they need to know before they contact you, which competitors they compare you with and what a good enquiry looks like. From this we agree a sitemap, a list of features and a content plan. Everything is written down so the price and timeline are clear before design starts.

Design that works on a phone first

Most visitors to UK small business websites arrive on a mobile. We design for small screens first, then scale up, so buttons are easy to tap, text is readable and key information sits near the top. Our guide to mobile website design explains the principles we follow, and our post on landing page design covers pages built for ads and campaigns.

Development and platform choice

We build most service business sites on WordPress and most online shops on Shopify, with WooCommerce for content-heavy stores. The right choice depends on what you sell, who will edit the site and your budget for ongoing costs. Our WordPress agency guide and Shopify agency guide explain each platform in more depth.

Whichever platform we use, we keep the build lean. Fewer plugins and lighter pages mean faster loading, fewer security risks and lower maintenance costs over time.

Search setup at launch

Many cheap web design services treat SEO as an extra. We think the basics belong in every project: unique page titles and descriptions, a logical heading structure, internal links, image alt text, an XML sitemap, schema where useful and Google Search Console setup. If you are replacing an older site, a redirect plan is essential to keep the rankings you have already earned.

Ongoing SEO, such as content, link building and local optimisation, is a separate monthly service. Our SEO and GEO services page explains what that includes, and our guide to SEO for small businesses covers the essentials.

Extras worth paying for

Some add-ons are genuinely useful. Others are padding. These are the ones we usually recommend considering.

  • Copywriting help: clear, specific page copy usually makes a bigger difference than design details.
  • Booking or quote tools: worth it if customers book appointments or request quotes regularly.
  • Local SEO setup: Google Business Profile optimisation for businesses serving a local area. See our local SEO guide.
  • Accessibility review: making sure the site works for people using assistive technology.
  • Maintenance plan: regular updates, backups and security checks after launch.

Extras we are more cautious about include heavy animation, sliders on the home page and large numbers of plugins added for minor features. These often slow sites down without bringing in more enquiries.

How to compare web design services fairly

  1. Use the same brief: send each provider the same goals, pages and features so quotes are comparable.
  2. Check inclusions line by line: content help, revisions, search setup, analytics, training and support.
  3. Ask about ownership: your domain, hosting, files and accounts should be in your name.
  4. Ask about speed: how they test it and what they do if pages are slow.
  5. Ask about aftercare: how changes are charged and how quickly they respond.

The cheapest quote is often cheapest because it leaves out search setup, tracking or support, which you then pay for later.

Common gaps in cheap web design quotes

A low price is not a problem in itself. The problem is when it hides work you will need anyway. These are the gaps we see most often when businesses show us quotes from elsewhere.

  • No redirect plan: replacing a site without mapping old URLs to new ones can lose the search visibility you already have.
  • No analytics: without GA4 and conversion tracking, you cannot tell whether the new site is bringing in more enquiries.
  • Template-only design: a theme with your logo dropped in may look fine, but it rarely fits your customers' questions or your sales process.
  • Locked-in hosting: some providers keep the site on their own systems, which makes it hard and expensive to move later.
  • No content help: you are left to write every page yourself, which is often the single biggest cause of delays.
  • Unclear aftercare: small changes after launch are charged at high hourly rates with no agreed response times.

When you compare quotes, add up what each gap would cost to fix separately. The cheapest headline price often ends up costing more over the first year.

Signs it is time for new web design services

Not every business needs a full rebuild. Sometimes a few targeted improvements are enough. A new website is usually worth considering when the current one is slow on mobile, hard for your team to update, missing features customers expect, such as online booking or clear pricing, or no longer reflects what the business actually does. If enquiries have dropped while traffic has stayed steady, the problem may be the content and calls to action rather than the design, and a smaller project could fix it. We are always happy to tell you if a lighter piece of work would do the job, because a rebuild that is not needed rarely pays for itself.
