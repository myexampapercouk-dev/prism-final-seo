Marketing Agency for Startups in the UK: Budgets, Channels and First Steps

A UK startup should fix positioning, tracking and its website before paying for growth. Here is what to do first, sensible budgets and channels by stage.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Get positioning, a clear website and working analytics in place before spending heavily on ads or SEO.
  • Match channels to your stage: validate demand first, then build compounding channels such as search and content.
  • Keep every account in the company's name and agree a small, measurable scope you can scale up later.

The first thing a UK startup should do with a marketing agency is not "run ads": it is to sharpen positioning, build a clear website and set up tracking so every pound spent afterwards can be measured. Once those foundations are in place, choose one or two channels that fit your stage and budget, test them properly, and only then scale.

Startups have less money, less time and more uncertainty than established firms, so the way you work with an agency should be different too. This guide covers what to do first, realistic budgets, which channels suit each stage, and how to avoid paying for activity before you are ready to benefit from it. If you are comparing suppliers in general, our guide on how to choose a digital marketing agency covers the selection process in detail.

Do startups need a marketing agency?

Not always, and not for everything. Many founders handle early marketing themselves, and that is often right while you are still learning who buys and why. An agency becomes useful when you need skills you do not have in-house (technical SEO, paid media, web development), when founder time is better spent elsewhere, or when you need to move faster than a first hire allows.

The best early relationships are narrow. Rather than a broad "do our marketing" brief, give an agency a defined problem: launch a website that converts, set up tracking properly, or test whether paid search can bring in customers at an acceptable cost.

What to do first: the startup marketing foundations

1. Positioning and message

If you cannot say in one sentence who you help and why you are different, no channel will fix it. A short piece of positioning work, covering audience, problem, proposition and proof, saves money on every campaign afterwards. Our brand strategy service and the guide to working with a brand strategy agency explain what that involves.

2. A website that does one job well

Early-stage sites do not need dozens of pages. They need a clear headline, a single primary action (book a demo, join a waitlist, buy), fast loading on mobile and enough proof to build trust. A small website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes 3 to 5 weeks with us; see our guide to small business website costs.

3. Tracking from day one

Set up analytics, conversion tracking and Search Console before launch, in accounts the company owns. Without this you cannot tell which channel brought which customer, and every later decision becomes guesswork. Under UK GDPR and PECR you will also need a proper cookie consent approach for non-essential tracking.

4. The basics of search

Even before investing in SEO, make sure your site is indexable, has sensible page titles and loads quickly. Our SEO for small business guide covers the essentials.

Startup marketing budgets in the UK

There is no correct percentage of revenue for a pre-revenue company, so think about budget in terms of experiments. Decide what you want to learn, how much it costs to learn it, and what result would justify spending more.

Channels by stage

Pre-launch and early traction

Your aim is learning, quickly. Paid search can be a fast way to test whether people are actively looking for what you offer, because you see search terms and conversion data within days. Paid social suits products that need to be shown rather than searched for. Keep tests small, set a clear success threshold before you start, and switch off what does not work. Founder-led content on LinkedIn often works well for B2B startups at this stage.

Product-market fit

Once you know who buys and why, start building channels that compound. SEO and content take time, typically 3 to 6 months for meaningful results, but they lower acquisition costs over the long run. B2B and software companies should read our guide to SaaS SEO and see how we approach SaaS marketing.

Scaling

With repeatable acquisition, you can add digital PR for authority, conversion rate optimisation to make each visitor worth more, and broader paid campaigns. This is where a larger retainer can make sense, as long as reporting ties activity to revenue.

Common startup marketing mistakes

  • Scaling ads before the website converts. More traffic to a weak page just costs more.
  • Spreading across every channel. Five half-funded channels teach you less than one well-run test.
  • Letting the agency own the accounts. Ad accounts, analytics and the domain should belong to the company.
  • Judging SEO in month one. Search work needs time; set milestones that reflect that.
  • No agreed definition of success. Decide on leads, sign-ups or sales targets before work starts.

Startup agency checklist

  • Positioning is written down in one or two sentences.
  • The website has one clear primary action and loads quickly on mobile.
  • Analytics, conversion tracking and Search Console are live and company-owned.
  • The agency scope is narrow, written and measurable.
  • Each test has a budget, a time limit and a success threshold.
  • Ad spend is paid directly by the company to the platform.
  • Reporting links activity to sign-ups, leads or revenue.
  • There is a plan for what happens if a channel works, and if it does not.

How to brief an agency as a startup

A good brief saves weeks. Keep it to one or two pages and cover the essentials: what the product does, who the ideal customer is, what you have already tried and what happened, your budget for fees and for ad spend, and the single outcome you most want in the next three months. Include any constraints, such as a launch date, a funding milestone or a regulated product claim that needs careful wording.

Share what you know about competitors and what customers say in their own words. Agencies can research a market, but they cannot invent the insight that comes from your first fifty sales conversations. The clearer the brief, the more of the agency's time goes on doing the work rather than working out what you need.

Working with an agency as a small team

Founders are busy, so agree a simple rhythm: a short monthly review, a shared document of priorities and one named decision-maker on your side. Give the agency access to customer conversations or sales notes where possible; the best marketing ideas usually come from what customers actually say. And be honest about runway. A good agency will shape the plan around the money you really have rather than the budget it would like you to have.

FAQs

What should a marketing agency for startups in the UK do first?

Start with positioning, a focused website and working tracking. Those foundations make every later campaign measurable and stop money being wasted on traffic that cannot convert.

How much should a startup spend on marketing?

Think in experiments rather than percentages. Fund one or two tests with a clear success threshold, then increase spend only on channels that prove they can acquire customers.

Should a startup do SEO or paid ads first?

Paid ads usually teach you faster, while SEO builds value over 3 to 6 months and beyond. Many startups test demand with paid search and start SEO foundations alongside.

Can a startup afford an agency retainer?

Our retainers start from £350 a month, with ad spend separate. Some startups begin with a one-off website project and add a retainer once a channel is proven.

Who should own the ad and analytics accounts?

The company should. Give the agency access rather than letting it create accounts in its own name, so your data stays with you if suppliers change.

When should a startup hire in-house instead?

When marketing becomes a constant, central function with enough work for a full role. Many teams keep specialist work such as technical SEO or paid media with an agency even then.
