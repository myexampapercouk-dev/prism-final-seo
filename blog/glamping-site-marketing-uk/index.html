Glamping Site Marketing in the UK: How to Win More Direct Bookings

Glamping site marketing in the UK works when great photos, unit-by-unit pages, simple direct booking and local search turn browsers into confirmed stays.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Glamping guests buy an experience, so every unit needs its own page with honest photos, facilities, sleeping arrangements and what makes it special.
  • A fast direct booking journey with clear prices and policies reduces reliance on listing platforms and their commission.
  • Search, social media, email and well-timed paid campaigns help fill shoulder seasons and midweek gaps, not just peak summer weekends.

Glamping site marketing in the UK works when you show guests exactly what each stay feels like, then make booking directly with you quicker and clearer than going through a listing platform. The sites that do well usually pair strong photography and unit-by-unit pages with simple online booking, local search visibility and campaigns aimed at the dates that are hardest to fill.

This guide is for owners of glamping sites with bell tents, shepherd's huts, yurts, pods, safari tents, treehouses or cabins, whether you run a handful of units on a farm or a larger site. We are a studio that builds websites and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so we focus on the digital journey from first search to confirmed booking.

How guests choose a glamping site

Glamping is rarely a last-minute need in the way a hotel room can be. It is a treat, a celebration or an escape, and guests often browse for a while before booking. They search for things like "glamping with hot tub Cornwall", "dog friendly shepherd's hut Wales" or "glamping near the Lake District", look at lots of photos, read reviews and compare prices.

The details that decide a booking are often small and practical. Is there a proper bed? Is the toilet inside the unit or a short walk away? Is there electricity, heating, a kitchen and Wi-Fi? Can the dog come? Is it suitable for young children? Guests who cannot find these answers quickly tend to book somewhere that spells them out.

Common guest types include:

  • Couples looking for a romantic break, often with a hot tub or wood-fired bath.
  • Families wanting outdoor space, safe surroundings and activities nearby.
  • Dog owners searching specifically for dog friendly stays.
  • Groups booking several units for birthdays, hen parties or reunions.
  • Walkers, cyclists and nature lovers who choose a location first and accommodation second.

Glamping site marketing UK: build the website around each unit

Your website is your shop window and your booking desk. The most important pages are the individual unit pages, because that is where guests decide. Each unit deserves its own page with:

  • A gallery of honest, well-lit photos inside and out, including the bathroom, kitchen and view.
  • Sleeping arrangements, maximum guests and whether children and dogs are welcome.
  • Facilities listed clearly: heating, electricity, hot tub, cooking, toilet and shower location, parking distance.
  • Prices or a live availability calendar, minimum stay and changeover days.
  • A clear book now button that goes straight to the right unit's availability.

Beyond unit pages, include a page about the site and its setting, a local area guide, a dog friendly page if relevant, and a page for group bookings. Direct booking versus listing platforms

Many glamping sites rely on large listing platforms and specialist glamping marketplaces. These bring visibility, but they take commission and you have less control over the guest relationship. Most sites benefit from using platforms to be found while gradually moving more guests to book direct.

Local SEO and Google Business Profile

A Google Business Profile helps your site appear in map results for searches like "glamping near me" or "glamping in the Peak District". Choose accurate categories, add plenty of photos, set your check-in information and link to your booking page. Our Google Business Profile guide covers setup and ongoing management.

Reviews carry a lot of weight. Ask guests for a review after their stay, ideally in a friendly follow-up email, and reply to every one. On your website, build pages that match how people search: unit types, features such as hot tubs or wood-fired stoves, dog friendly stays, and the area you are in. A local area guide with walks, beaches, pubs and rainy-day ideas can attract planners early in their search. Our SEO and GEO service helps with this, including making your pages clear enough for AI search tools to describe accurately.

Social media and content

Glamping is visual, which makes Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest and TikTok natural channels. Short videos of a morning view, a lit fire pit or a dog stretched out on a rug often do more than polished adverts. Show the changing seasons so people can imagine an autumn or winter stay, not just summer.

Keep posting consistent but realistic. A few strong posts a week, with a clear link to book, is better than daily posts that run out of steam. Encourage guests to tag you, and ask permission before sharing their photos.

Paid campaigns for glamping sites

Paid ads work best when aimed at specific gaps, such as midweek stays in spring, autumn half term or the winter months. Search ads catch people actively looking for glamping in your area. Meta ads suit visual storytelling and can target people interested in outdoor holidays and short breaks in nearby cities. Remarketing reminds people who looked at a unit page but did not book.

Keep campaigns tightly linked to the right unit page and dates, and track bookings properly so you know what each campaign returns. Our paid media service plans and manages campaigns like these, with ad spend paid separately.

Filling shoulder seasons and midweek gaps

Summer weekends often sell themselves. The real work is filling the rest of the calendar. Ideas that tend to help include:

  • Shorter midweek stays with a lower minimum night requirement.
  • Seasonal packages such as stargazing breaks, autumn walking weekends or winter hot tub escapes.
  • Offers for past guests to return out of season.
  • Group and exclusive-use bookings for celebrations.
  • Partnerships with local attractions, food producers or activity providers.

Email is especially useful here. Past guests already trust you, and a well-timed message about autumn availability can fill dates without any ad spend. Our guide to building an email list explains how to collect consent properly; check current ICO guidance on UK GDPR and PECR for your own setup.

Practical and legal points to keep in view

Your marketing should match what you are permitted to offer. Glamping sites can be affected by planning permission, site licensing, fire safety and other rules, and these vary by area. Do not advertise units, seasons or facilities that are not covered by your permissions, and check current guidance from your local authority and the relevant official bodies. Be accurate about accessibility, distances and facilities so guests arrive with the right expectations.

FAQs

What does glamping site marketing UK owners need most?

Strong photos, a separate page for each unit with honest details, a simple direct booking journey and a well-managed Google Business Profile. Once those are in place, SEO, social media, email and paid campaigns have something solid to send guests to.

Should we stay on listing platforms if we have our own website?

Usually yes, at least at first. Platforms bring visibility, while your own website lets you take commission-free bookings. Over time, aim to move repeat guests and those who find you directly onto your own booking system.

How can we get more glamping bookings out of season?

Create reasons to visit in quieter months, such as cosy winter breaks, stargazing or walking packages, flexible midweek stays and offers for past guests. Promote them through email, social media and targeted paid campaigns aimed at those dates.

Do glamping sites need a booking system on the website?

It makes direct booking much easier. A good system shows live availability, takes payment and syncs with any platforms you list on, which reduces the risk of double bookings and saves time on enquiries.

Which social media platform works best for glamping?

Visual platforms such as Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest and TikTok tend to suit glamping. The best choice depends on where your guests spend time, so start with one or two and post consistently rather than spreading yourself thin.

How long does SEO take for a glamping site?

SEO typically builds over three to six months, depending on your location, competition and the current state of your website. Clear unit pages, local content and steady reviews help you appear for the searches that matter.
