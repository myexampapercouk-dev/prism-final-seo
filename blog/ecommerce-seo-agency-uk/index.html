Choosing an Ecommerce SEO Agency in the UK: Services, Costs and Red Flags

An ecommerce SEO agency in the UK grows organic sales from category and product pages. What is included, what it costs and how to choose the right one.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good ecommerce SEO agency focuses on category pages, product pages and technical health, because that is where most organic revenue comes from.
  • Expect a monthly retainer, with meaningful movement in rankings and organic sales typically showing within 3 to 6 months.
  • Choose an agency that reports on revenue and orders from organic search, not just rankings, and that can explain its plan for your catalogue in plain English.

An ecommerce SEO agency helps an online shop earn more sales from Google without paying for every click, by fixing technical problems, improving category and product pages and building the authority the site needs to outrank competitors. For most UK online shops, that work is sold as a monthly retainer, and results typically build over 3 to 6 months rather than weeks.

This guide explains what an ecommerce SEO agency actually does, what should be included, what it costs in the UK and how to tell a capable agency from one that will send you a ranking report and little else.

What does an ecommerce SEO agency do?

Ecommerce SEO is different from SEO for a service business. A brochure site might have twenty pages; an online shop can have thousands of URLs created by products, variants, filters, sorting options and pagination. Most of the opportunity sits on category pages (for example "women's waterproof walking boots"), because those match how people search when they are ready to compare and buy. Product pages then capture more specific searches for brands, models and sizes.

A specialist agency therefore spends its time on four things: making sure Google can crawl and index the right pages and ignore the wrong ones, matching category pages to real search demand, making product pages more useful than the manufacturer description every other retailer copies, and earning links and mentions that build trust. Increasingly, it also means making product and category information clear enough to be quoted by AI search tools, often called generative engine optimisation.

Technical work is usually the first priority

On an online shop, technical problems compound quickly. A faceted navigation that lets Google crawl every colour, size and price combination can create more junk URLs than real pages. Out-of-stock and discontinued products handled badly create soft 404s and lost link equity. Slow, script-heavy themes hurt Core Web Vitals on mobile, where most UK shoppers browse. A good agency fixes these before writing a single blog post, because content cannot rank on a site Google struggles to crawl.

Category pages carry the commercial weight

Someone searching "oak dining table 6 seater" wants a choice of tables, not one product. That is why category pages are the backbone of ecommerce SEO. Improving them usually means a clear H1 that matches the search, a short helpful introduction, sensible filters, internal links to related categories and a few FAQs further down the page that answer delivery, sizing or care questions.

One-off audits versus retainers

If you have an in-house developer, a one-off technical audit can be a sensible first step: you get a prioritised list of fixes and implement them yourself. Most shops, though, benefit from ongoing work, because new products, seasonal ranges, platform updates and competitor activity keep changing the picture.

How to choose an ecommerce SEO agency

Plenty of agencies say they do ecommerce SEO. Fewer understand faceted navigation, product feeds and how organic search fits with Google Shopping. Use these questions to separate them.

  • Do they know your platform? Shopify, WooCommerce and Magento each have their own quirks with URLs, canonicals and apps. Our comparison of Shopify and WooCommerce covers some of the differences.
  • Do they talk about revenue? Rankings and traffic are useful, but the agency should agree to report organic orders and revenue from your analytics.
  • Can they explain their first 90 days? A credible plan starts with an audit and quick technical wins, then category priorities, then content and links.
  • Who owns the work? Content, accounts and analytics should belong to you, not the agency.
  • Are they honest about timelines? Anyone promising page one in a few weeks, or guaranteeing rankings, is a red flag.
  • Do they understand paid search? Organic and Google Shopping ads often target the same searches, so the two should be planned together.

Ecommerce SEO agency checklist

Before you sign, check that the proposal covers each of these:

  • A technical audit covering crawl, indexation, duplicate URLs, redirects and site speed
  • A keyword map tying search terms to specific category and product pages
  • A plan for out-of-stock, discontinued and seasonal products
  • Product and breadcrumb structured data, with a plan to test it
  • Category page copy and internal linking improvements
  • A content plan linked to commercial pages, not standalone blog posts
  • A realistic approach to links, such as digital PR, rather than bought links
  • Reporting on organic revenue and orders, plus rankings for priority categories
  • Clear contract terms, notice period and ownership of all assets

When an agency is worth it, and when it is not

An ecommerce SEO agency tends to make sense once you have a working shop, products people search for and the margin to wait several months for organic growth. If your site is very new, has a handful of products or sells something few people search for, you may get more from paid ads or social first, and add SEO once the basics are proven. Equally, if your platform makes basic fixes impossible, it can be cheaper to rebuild than to keep patching; our website and ecommerce development service covers that route.

Seasonality also matters. UK retail has clear peaks around Black Friday, Christmas and the January sales. Because SEO takes months to build, category pages for peak season need to be improved well before the season starts, not in the same month.

FAQs

What does an ecommerce SEO agency UK businesses hire actually do?

It improves how an online shop ranks in Google, mainly by fixing technical issues, optimising category and product pages, creating supporting content and earning links. The aim is more organic orders and revenue, not just more traffic.

How long does ecommerce SEO take to work?

Results typically start to show within 3 to 6 months. Technical fixes can help sooner, but competitive categories and new sites usually take longer to build authority.

How much should I pay for ecommerce SEO?

Our retainers start at £350, £750 and £1,500 a month depending on scope. Larger catalogues and more competitive markets need more work, so they usually sit at the higher end.

Should I focus on category pages or product pages?

Usually category pages first, because they match broader buying searches and tend to drive more revenue. Product pages matter for specific model and brand searches, and both need unique, helpful content.

Can an agency guarantee first-place rankings?

No. Nobody controls Google's rankings, so a guarantee is a warning sign. A good agency commits to the work, explains its reasoning and reports transparently on results.

Do I need SEO if I already run Google Shopping ads?

Often, yes. Shopping ads stop when the budget stops, while organic rankings keep working. Running both lets you cover more of the search results and reduce reliance on paid clicks over time.
