What Salzburg businesses need from a website

Salzburg is a compact city with an outsized international profile. Tourism, culture, music, hospitality, retail, professional services and regional manufacturing all sit side by side, and the wider state stretches into ski and lake areas with very different seasonal patterns. That mix shapes what a website has to do.

  • Serve two audiences at once. Many Salzburg businesses talk to local Austrian customers and to visitors from Germany, the UK, the USA and further afield. The site needs a clear language structure rather than a half-translated page here and there.
  • Handle seasonal demand. Festival periods, Advent markets, ski season and summer lake trips bring sharp spikes in traffic. Pages, offers and booking paths should be easy to update before each peak.
  • Load quickly on phones. Visitors often browse on mobile data while travelling. Heavy galleries and slow scripts lose bookings.
  • Build trust fast. Clear contact details, an imprint page, transparent prices where possible and straightforward policies help both local and international visitors feel confident.

Bilingual and multilingual structure

Language is the single biggest design decision for most Salzburg sites. A tidy structure helps visitors and search engines understand which version they are reading.

  • Use a separate URL path for each language, such as a German root with an English folder, or the reverse if most of your customers are international.
  • Translate menus, buttons, forms, error messages and email confirmations, not just body copy.
  • Add hreflang tags so search engines show the right language version.
  • Avoid automatic redirects based on browser language; offer a visible switcher instead.

Hospitality and tourism websites

Many of the businesses that search for web design in Salzburg are in hospitality. Hotels, guesthouses, holiday apartments, restaurants and tour providers compete with large booking platforms for the same visitors. A well-built website gives them a direct channel they control.

For these businesses we focus on:

  • Clear room, menu or tour pages with honest photos and prices where you choose to show them.
  • Prominent booking buttons that link to your existing booking engine or reservation tool.
  • Seasonal landing pages for festival weeks, winter sports, Christmas markets and summer breaks.
  • Location pages that explain how to arrive by train, car or airport transfer.

Speed, accessibility and legal basics

Austrian and EU visitors expect a site that loads quickly and works for everyone. We build to good performance and accessibility standards from the start rather than patching them later.

  • Speed. We compress images, limit third-party scripts and test against Core Web Vitals. Our Core Web Vitals guide explains the main measures.
  • Accessibility. We use readable contrast, clear headings, labelled forms and keyboard-friendly navigation. The European Accessibility Act raises expectations for many businesses that sell to consumers.
  • Legal pages. Austrian sites typically need an imprint, a privacy policy and cookie consent that meets EU rules. We build the pages and consent tools; the legal wording itself should come from your own adviser, and you should check official Austrian and EU guidance for your situation.

Designing pages that convert

A beautiful site that does not produce enquiries is a cost, not an asset. Every page we design for a Salzburg client has a clear purpose and a clear next step.

  • One primary call to action per page, repeated at sensible points on longer pages.
  • Short forms that ask only for what you need to respond.
  • Trust signals you can actually support, such as clear opening hours, address details, policies and photos of your real premises or products.
  • Pages structured for scanning, with headings that match what people search for.

How to brief a web design project

A clear brief saves time and money. Before your first call, it helps to gather:

  • Your main goal for the site and how you measure success.
  • Three to five competitor or inspiration sites, with notes on what you like.
  • Your current site, logins and any content you want to keep.
  • Which languages you need and who will write or translate them.
  • Booking, payment or CRM tools the site must connect to.
  • Your budget range and any fixed launch date, such as the start of a season.

With this in hand, we can give you a fixed price and timeline quickly.
