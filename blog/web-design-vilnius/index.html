Why Vilnius businesses are investing in better websites

Vilnius has become one of the most active business cities in the Baltic region. It has a strong technology and fintech scene, a growing number of shared service centres, a lively startup community, universities and a busy tourism and hospitality sector in and around the old town. Many Vilnius companies sell to customers across the EU, the UK and beyond.

For these businesses, the website is often the first and most important impression. International buyers and investors judge a company quickly by its site, and a dated or slow website can undermine trust even when the product is excellent. Local service businesses face the same pressure from competitors with better-designed sites.

Language versions: Lithuanian, English and beyond

Most Vilnius websites need Lithuanian and English, and some also need Polish, Russian, German or other languages depending on the market. Prism writes in English. Lithuanian and other language copy should come from your own team or a professional translator, and we build the site so each version has its own URL, translated metadata, correct hreflang tags and a clear language switcher.

It is better to launch with fewer, well-translated languages than many half-finished ones. Our guide to multilingual website design explains how to plan language versions without hurting search performance.

Types of websites we build for Vilnius businesses

  • Tech and SaaS websites: clear product pages, pricing, documentation links and sign-up journeys for software companies selling internationally.
  • Fintech and professional services: trust-focused sites for consultancies, accountants and financial firms. In regulated sectors, all claims and disclosures need approval from your own compliance team.
  • Hospitality and tourism: hotels, guesthouses, restaurants and tour operators wanting more direct bookings from visitors.
  • Ecommerce: online shops on Shopify or WordPress selling across Lithuania and the EU.
  • Local services: clinics, studios, trades and training providers that rely on local search and enquiries.

We build most brochure sites on WordPress or a lightweight static setup, and most online shops on Shopify, depending on how you want to manage content.

Accessibility and EU rules

The European Accessibility Act applies to many businesses selling certain products and services to consumers in the EU, including some ecommerce and booking services. Good accessibility also improves usability for everyone. We design with readable text, strong contrast, keyboard-friendly navigation and proper labels. Check how the rules apply to your business with official guidance or your own adviser. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act and websites gives a general overview.

Privacy rules matter too. Your cookie banner and consent setup should follow current guidance from the Lithuanian data protection authority and your adviser. Our guide to cookie banner design explains how to make consent clear without harming the user experience.

The website process, step by step

Most brochure websites take 3 to 5 weeks, and ecommerce sites 5 to 12 weeks. Our guide on how long it takes to build a website explains what affects timing. A typical project looks like this:

  1. Discovery: a video call to agree goals, audiences, languages and features.
  2. Structure: a sitemap and page plan, with SEO targets for key pages.
  3. Design: homepage and key page designs, reviewed and refined with you.
  4. Build: development, content entry, forms, tracking and speed work.
  5. Translation and testing: language versions added, then testing across devices and browsers.
  6. Launch: going live, redirects if replacing an old site, and a short handover.

The pace often depends on how quickly content and translations arrive, so we agree a content plan early.

SEO and paid results typically build over 3 to 6 months.

Common website mistakes we fix

Many of the Vilnius websites we review share the same problems, and most are easy to avoid with good planning.

  • English as an afterthought: an English version that is incomplete or machine-translated, which undermines trust with international buyers.
  • Slow pages: large images, heavy sliders and too many plugins that make the site slow on mobile.
  • Unclear calls to action: visitors cannot easily see how to book a demo, request a quote or make an enquiry.
  • Missing basics for search: duplicate titles, no descriptions, messy URLs and language versions that compete with each other.
  • Hard-to-edit sites: websites the internal team cannot update without paying a developer for every change.

Fixing these issues often improves enquiries before any extra marketing spend. When we replace an older site, we also map old URLs to new ones so existing search rankings are protected. We also hand over a short written guide so your team can update pages, add news and change images confidently without needing a developer for routine edits.
