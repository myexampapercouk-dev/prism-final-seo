Multi-Location SEO UK: How to Rank Every Branch in Local Search

Multi-location SEO UK guide for businesses with several branches: location pages, Google Business Profiles, reviews, site structure and reporting by branch.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Each branch needs its own accurate Google Business Profile and its own genuinely useful location page.
  • A clear site structure and consistent business details help search engines understand how branches relate.
  • Report by branch, because a strong average can hide locations that are struggling.

Multi-location SEO in the UK is the work of helping every branch, clinic, shop or office appear in local search for the people nearby. A business with one location can focus on one map listing and one set of local searches. A business with five, twenty or fifty locations has to do that for each one, while keeping the brand, website and data consistent across all of them.

This guide is for marketing leads and owners of businesses with several physical locations, such as clinics, gyms, estate agency branches, retailers, restaurants or service businesses with regional offices. It explains what to set up, how to structure the website and how to measure progress branch by branch.

Why multi-location SEO is different

The basics of local SEO are the same for one location or many: relevance, proximity and prominence. What changes is scale and consistency. With many branches, small mistakes multiply. A wrong phone number on one profile, a closed branch still showing as open or ten near-identical location pages can quietly cost visibility and trust.

There is also an internal challenge. Branch managers may have their own ideas about listings, reviews and social media, while head office controls the website. Without clear ownership, profiles drift out of date and duplicates appear. Good multi-location SEO is as much about process and governance as it is about rankings.

If you are new to local search, start with our local SEO guide, which covers the fundamentals this article builds on.

Google Business Profile for every branch

Each physical location that serves customers face to face should usually have its own Google Business Profile. Managing them well is often the single biggest lever in multi-location SEO.

  • One profile per real location. Avoid duplicates and do not create profiles for places where you do not have a staffed presence; that can breach Google's guidelines and risk suspension.
  • Consistent naming. Use your real business name. Adding keywords or locations that are not part of the actual name can cause problems.
  • Accurate categories and hours. Set the right primary category for each branch and keep hours current, including bank holidays and temporary closures.
  • Branch-specific photos and details. Show the actual premises, parking, accessibility and services available at that branch.
  • Link to the right page. Point each profile's website link to that branch's location page rather than the homepage.

Google offers ways to manage many profiles together, which makes bulk updates and access control easier. Our Google Business Profile guide covers profile optimisation in more depth.

Location pages that deserve to rank

Location pages are where many multi-location sites go wrong. Copying one template and swapping the town name creates thin, near-duplicate pages that rarely perform well. A useful location page gives someone in that area a reason to choose that branch.

Good location pages usually include:

  • The branch name, full address, phone number and opening hours, matching the Google Business Profile.
  • An embedded map and clear directions, parking and public transport information.
  • The services actually offered at that branch, which may differ from others.
  • Information about the team at that branch, where staff are happy to be featured.
  • Genuine local detail, such as nearby landmarks, areas served or branch-specific events.
  • Reviews or testimonials from that branch, used only with permission.
  • A clear call to action, such as booking or calling that branch directly.

Adding LocalBusiness structured data to each page helps search engines read the details. Our schema markup guide explains how.

Site structure for many locations

A clear, logical structure helps both people and search engines. A common approach is a locations hub page that lists every branch, often grouped by region, with each branch on its own page beneath it. Larger businesses sometimes add regional pages between the hub and the branches.

Consistent business details across the web

Your name, address and phone number appear in many places: your website, Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Bing Places, social profiles and directories. Inconsistencies across these, especially after a move or a phone system change, can confuse customers and weaken trust signals.

Keep a single master list of every branch's details and treat it as the source of truth. When a branch moves, changes hours or closes, update the master list first, then the website and every listing. For closed branches, mark the profile as permanently closed rather than leaving it live or deleting it without care, and redirect the old location page to the most relevant alternative.

Reviews at scale

Reviews influence local rankings and, more importantly, whether people choose a branch. With many locations, review performance often varies widely. Practical steps include:

  • Give each branch a short review link and a simple, consistent way to ask customers.
  • Set clear rules about who replies and how quickly, with a tone guide for responses.
  • Escalate negative reviews that mention serious issues to the right person.
  • Never offer incentives for reviews or filter who is asked based on expected sentiment.

UK rules on fake and incentivised reviews have tightened in recent years, so check current guidance and take advice if unsure.

Local links and content

Each branch can earn its own local signals. Sponsoring a local club, supporting a community event, joining a local business network or featuring in local news can produce relevant links and mentions for that branch's page. Branch-level content, such as a guide to an area or local event coverage, can help too if it is genuinely useful.

Paid search alongside local SEO

Local SEO takes time, and new branches often start with little visibility. Location-targeted Google Ads can support a new opening or a branch in a competitive area while organic visibility builds. Keep campaigns organised by branch so you can see cost per enquiry for each one. We manage these campaigns through our paid media service, and ad spend is paid separately from any management fee.

Reporting by branch

An overall traffic number can hide a lot. One strong branch may mask several weak ones. Report on each location separately where you can:

  • Google Business Profile views, calls, direction requests and website clicks per branch.
  • Organic visits and conversions to each location page.
  • Review count and average rating per branch.
  • Local ranking checks for priority searches in each area.

FAQs

What is multi-location SEO?

Multi-location SEO is local SEO for businesses with more than one physical location. It covers Google Business Profiles for each branch, location pages, consistent business details, reviews and reporting by branch.

Do I need a separate page for each location?

Usually yes. A dedicated page for each branch with its own details, services and local information gives search engines and customers a clear destination. Each Google Business Profile can then link to its matching page.

Should each branch have its own website?

Generally no. One website with a clear locations section is easier to maintain and keeps authority in one place. Separate sites usually mean more work and weaker overall visibility.

How do I avoid duplicate content on location pages?

Write each page with genuine local detail: branch-specific services, team, directions, parking, local reviews and nearby areas served. Shared information such as company policies can be summarised and linked rather than repeated in full.

What should I do when a branch closes?

Mark the Google Business Profile as permanently closed, update other listings, and redirect the location page to the nearest relevant branch or the locations hub so visitors and links are not lost.

How long does multi-location SEO take?

Fixing profiles and data can have an effect relatively quickly, while location page rankings and local authority typically take several months to build. Progress often varies by branch and competition.
