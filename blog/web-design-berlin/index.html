Web Design for Berlin Businesses: Remote Websites from a UK Studio

Looking for web design in Berlin? What a good Berlin business website needs, German and English pages, legal pages, timelines and our fees in pounds.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Berlin business website usually needs clear German and English versions, fast mobile pages, a proper legal notice and privacy page and a simple path to enquiry.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Germany; we design and build remotely, write in English and work with your team or a translator on German copy.
  • Small websites are £1,000 to £2,000 and online shops £1,500 to £3,500, quoted in pounds with other currencies on request; most sites take three to five weeks.

Web design for a Berlin business should produce a fast, clear website that turns visitors into enquiries or sales, in German, English or both. The right studio plans the structure around your buyers, builds pages that load quickly on mobile, handles the legal pages German visitors expect and hands you a site you fully own.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Berlin or anywhere in Germany. Our website and ecommerce development service runs by video call, shared designs and staging links, one hour behind Berlin time. We write in English and work with your team or a translator on German pages.

What Berlin businesses need from a website

Berlin has a large start-up and technology scene, creative and media businesses, hospitality, tourism, professional services and a growing number of online brands. Many companies hire international staff and sell to customers outside Germany, so their websites often have to work for two audiences at once:

  • Bilingual from the start. Many Berlin firms operate in English internally but sell to German-speaking customers, so both versions need equal care.
  • Credibility for investors and partners. Start-ups and scale-ups are often judged by their website before any meeting.
  • Clear company details. German visitors tend to look for a legal notice, privacy information and transparent contact details before getting in touch.
  • Speed on mobile. A large share of visits come from phones, often on the move.

If your site will serve several countries or languages, our multilingual website design guide covers language switchers, URL structures and keeping each version manageable.

Designing for German and English at the same time

German text is often longer than English, and compound words can break narrow layouts. We design buttons, menus and headings with that in mind, and test real German copy rather than placeholder text before launch. Each language gets its own URLs, page titles and meta descriptions, with hreflang tags so search engines show the right version to the right searcher.

Prism writes English copy in-house. For German pages we give your team or a native-speaking translator a clear brief for each page, including structure, headings and search terms, and we check the finished pages in the build. Our guide to website copywriting explains how we approach page copy that converts.

Choosing a platform

We build on the platform that suits the business rather than one we always use:

  • WordPress for content-led sites, service businesses and blogs that need flexible editing.
  • Shopify for online shops, including stores selling across several European countries.
  • Static builds for small, very fast sites that rarely change.

If you are weighing up options, our comparison of website builders versus custom websites sets out the trade-offs. For online shops selling into several EU markets, our guide to Shopify Markets for Europe covers currencies, languages and domains.

Legal pages, consent and accessibility

German websites usually need a legal notice page and a privacy policy, and analytics or marketing cookies normally need consent under EU and German rules. Online shops also need clear terms, delivery and returns information. Accessibility rules have also become more important for many consumer-facing businesses, and our European Accessibility Act guide explains the basics.

We design and build these pages and the consent banner, but the legal wording should come from your legal adviser, who has final approval. Requirements change, so check current official guidance before launch.

Performance, SEO and handover

A new website should be fast and ready for search from day one. Every build includes:

  • Compressed, correctly sized images and lean templates for quick loading on mobile.
  • Clean headings, titles and meta descriptions for each page and language.
  • An XML sitemap, sensible internal links and redirects from old URLs.
  • Analytics and consent set up so data is collected properly.
  • Full ownership: domain, hosting, code and content stay in your name.

If you are replacing an existing site, our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings explains how we protect the search traffic you already have.

Common problems we see on Berlin websites

When businesses come to us for a redesign, the same issues tend to appear, whatever the sector:

  • An English version that was added later. Pages are missing, menus point to German pages, and the switcher sends visitors to the home page instead of the matching page.
  • Machine-translated copy left unchecked. It reads awkwardly, uses inconsistent terms and can undermine trust with both audiences.
  • Heavy templates. Large sliders, autoplay video and too many scripts slow pages down on mobile.
  • Unclear calls to action. Visitors cannot see what to do next, whether that is booking a call, requesting a quote or buying.
  • Consent banners that block the page. Poorly built banners frustrate visitors and can break analytics data.

Each of these can be fixed with planning rather than a bigger budget. We set out the language structure, page templates and calls to action before design starts, so the finished site is consistent in both languages and easy for your team to maintain.
