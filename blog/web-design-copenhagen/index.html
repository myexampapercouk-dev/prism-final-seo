Web Design for Copenhagen Businesses: Remote Website Design from a UK Studio

Looking for web design in Copenhagen? Danish and English sites, accessibility, what a remote UK studio builds, typical timelines and how our prices work.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Copenhagen websites often need Danish and English versions, clean design, strong accessibility and fast mobile pages.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Denmark; we design and build websites remotely, write in English and work with your team or a translator on Danish.
  • Small business websites are £1,000 to £2,000 and usually take three to five weeks; ecommerce sites are £1,500 to £3,500, with other currencies quoted on request.

Web design for a Copenhagen business should produce a fast, clear website that works in Danish and often English, looks credible to local and international visitors and makes it easy to get in touch or buy. The best results come from a clear plan, honest content and a build your team can update without help.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Copenhagen or anywhere in Denmark. Our website and ecommerce development service runs by video call and shared project boards, one hour behind Copenhagen time. We write in English and work with your team or a translator on Danish content.

What Copenhagen businesses expect from a website

Copenhagen has a strong design culture, a large international workforce and a lot of businesses in technology, life sciences, shipping, architecture, food and drink, hospitality and sustainability-focused sectors. That shapes what visitors expect:

  • Clean, uncluttered design. Simple layouts, good typography and plenty of space tend to suit Danish audiences better than busy pages.
  • Danish and English. Many Danes are confident in English, but local customers often prefer Danish, and international buyers and job seekers want English.
  • Mobile first. Many visits come from phones, so pages need to load quickly and work well on small screens.
  • Honest, direct content. Clear information about what you do and what it costs usually works better than marketing language.

Design style matters, but it should support the content rather than replace it. If your brand itself needs work first, our brand strategy service can help define the message before the website is built.

Danish and English versions

A bilingual website works best when each language is treated as a proper version rather than a quick translation. Each needs its own page titles, descriptions and URLs so search engines can show the right version to the right person, and a clear language switcher so visitors can move between them.

Prism writes English content in-house. For Danish, we prepare the page structure and briefs and work with your team or a native-speaking writer or translator. We then check the finished pages for layout, spacing and technical setup. Danish words can be long, so navigation, buttons and headings are designed with room to spare. Our multilingual website design guide explains language switchers, URL structures and how to keep two versions up to date.

Accessibility and the European Accessibility Act

Accessibility is part of good design, and EU rules now place specific requirements on some businesses. The European Accessibility Act applies to certain products and services, including many online shops selling to consumers, with exemptions for some small businesses. We design with accessibility in mind from the start: readable text sizes, strong colour contrast, keyboard navigation, alt text for images and clear form labels.

Our European Accessibility Act guide explains the basics in general terms. Requirements and exemptions vary, so check current official guidance and take legal advice on your own obligations.

Privacy, cookies and legal pages

Danish websites fall under the GDPR and EU cookie rules, so analytics and marketing cookies generally need consent before they run. A cookie banner should be clear and give visitors a genuine choice. Our cookie banner design guide covers how to make consent clear without damaging the experience. We can design and place privacy and cookie pages, but the wording should come from your legal adviser, who has final approval.

Fast, mobile-friendly websites

A website that loads slowly or is awkward on a phone loses visitors before they read anything. We build pages that are light, with properly sized images, sensible fonts and minimal scripts. Layouts are designed for mobile first and then adapted for larger screens, rather than squeezed down afterwards. Our mobile website design guide explains what this looks like in practice.

Speed and structure also help search visibility. Each site we build includes clean page titles, descriptions, headings and a sitemap, so search engines can understand and index every page in each language.

Types of websites we build for Copenhagen businesses

  • Service business websites for consultancies, agencies, clinics and professional firms, focused on clear services and easy enquiries.
  • Ecommerce websites on Shopify or WooCommerce for brands selling in Denmark and across Europe.
  • Startup and SaaS websites that explain a product clearly to investors, customers and recruits.
  • Hospitality and food websites with menus, bookings and opening information that are easy to update.
  • Redesigns of existing websites, protecting search rankings with careful redirects.

Choosing a content management system

The right platform depends on what your team needs to do after launch. For most service businesses, WordPress gives flexible pages, a familiar editor and good multilingual options. For online shops, Shopify offers a reliable checkout, built-in hosting and straightforward product management. Some smaller sites suit a simpler builder, while larger or more complex projects may need a custom approach.

We recommend a platform based on your content, budget and how often you will update the site, not on what is easiest for us. Whatever we choose together, we set it up so that adding a page, changing text or swapping an image in either language is something your team can do in minutes. We also explain hosting, backups and updates so you know who is responsible for each once the site is live.

Web design Copenhagen costs and timelines

Our prices are shown on our pricing page. Small business websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000 and take three to five weeks. Ecommerce websites typically cost £1,500 to £3,500 and take five to twelve weeks. We quote in pounds, and quotes in other currencies are available on request. Outside translation, hosting, domains, paid plugins or apps are paid separately.

Timelines depend heavily on content. A site with Danish and English versions takes longer if translations arrive late, so we agree a content schedule at the start. Our article on how long it takes to build a website explains what affects timings.

You own your website, domain and content. At handover we provide logins, training and documentation, so your team can make everyday changes without relying on us.
