YouTube Ads vs TikTok Ads: Choosing a Video Ad Platform in the UK

YouTube Ads reach viewers across long and short video with Google targeting; TikTok Ads reward native, creator-style clips. Compare audience, creative and fit.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • YouTube Ads run inside Google Ads, reach a wide range of viewers across long-form video, Shorts and partner placements, and can use Google audience signals.
  • TikTok Ads reward native, fast, creator-style vertical video and can work well for visual consumer products, but need a steady supply of fresh creative.
  • Choose based on your audience, the video you can realistically produce and whether you want to explain a product in depth or grab attention in seconds.

YouTube Ads usually suit businesses that want to reach a broad range of viewers, explain a product or service in more depth and use Google's audience and intent signals, while TikTok Ads usually suit consumer brands that can produce fast, native, creator-style vertical video. Both can build awareness and drive sales, but they reward different creative and fit different audiences. The right choice depends on who you sell to and what video you can realistically make, week after week.

This guide compares YouTube Ads vs TikTok Ads on audience, formats, targeting, creative, cost and measurement, with a practical way to decide where to test first. Both platforms change their ad formats, policies and reporting regularly, so check the current official help centres before you launch.

Audience: who you can reach

YouTube is watched by a very wide range of ages and interests in the UK, on phones, laptops and smart TVs. People use it for entertainment, but also for how-to videos, reviews and research before buying. That makes it useful for products and services that benefit from explanation, as well as for broad brand awareness.

TikTok's audience has broadened over time, but it remains strongly associated with younger adults and with discovery driven by trends and creators. People are scrolling quickly and expect content that entertains or teaches something in seconds. Brands with visual products, a clear personality and something to show tend to fit naturally.

Platform audience data changes, so check each platform's current official figures and, more importantly, look at where your own customers say they spend time.

Formats and creative

YouTube offers a wider range of formats. In-stream ads play before or during videos and can be skippable after a few seconds; bumper ads are very short and non-skippable; in-feed ads appear in search results and the home feed; and Shorts ads appear between vertical short videos. You can run a two-minute product demo or a six-second reminder, depending on the goal. Our YouTube Ads management guide covers these formats in more detail.

TikTok is built around vertical, sound-on video. Ads that look like normal TikTok posts, often with a person talking to camera, quick cuts, captions and a hook in the first second or two, tend to work best. Polished TV-style adverts often feel out of place. Spark Ads, which promote existing organic posts from your account or from creators with permission, are a common way to keep ads native.

Targeting and intent

A key advantage of YouTube is that it sits inside Google Ads. Depending on the campaign type, you can use audience signals based on what people have searched for on Google, people who visited your website, customer lists where you have the right permissions, and topics or specific channels. That link to search behaviour can help you reach people who are actively researching a purchase.

TikTok relies heavily on content signals: what people watch, like and share. Its algorithm is good at finding interested viewers when the creative is right, and you can add interest, behaviour and custom audiences. In practice, on TikTok the creative itself does much of the targeting work.

Both platforms restrict targeting for some categories and have ad policies for sensitive products, so check current official guidance before you start.

Cost and budget

We do not quote typical prices because costs vary with audience, competition, season, format and creative quality. On both platforms, strong creative usually lowers costs because people engage with it, and weak creative raises them.

The bigger cost difference is often production. YouTube can run a small number of well-made videos for a long time. TikTok usually needs a regular supply of new videos to keep performance up, which means ongoing time with a phone, a presenter or creators. When comparing platforms, budget for creative as well as ad spend. Our TikTok Ads vs Meta Ads comparison covers the creative demands of TikTok in more depth.

Measurement and tracking

YouTube campaigns report inside Google Ads and can use the same conversion tracking as your search and Shopping campaigns, which makes it easier to compare channels side by side. Many video ads influence people who buy later rather than clicking immediately, so look at view-through and assisted conversions as well as direct clicks. Your GA4 setup should be in good order before you judge results.

TikTok uses its own pixel and events API. Our TikTok Pixel setup guide walks through the basics. In the UK, cookie consent under UK GDPR and PECR affects what can be tracked on your website, so make sure your consent setup follows current ICO guidance.

Both platforms also work well for remarketing, showing ads to people who have visited your site or watched your videos.

Which businesses suit which platform

YouTube Ads often suit: products or services that need explaining, considered purchases, local and national service businesses, B2B brands with useful video content, and businesses already running Google Ads that want to add video.

TikTok Ads often suit: visual consumer products such as fashion, beauty, food, home and gifts, brands with a clear personality, and businesses that can produce or source short, native videos regularly. Many online shops fall into this group; our ecommerce sector page explains how we approach paid video for retailers.

Both can work for brands with a broad audience and the resources to make different creative for each. Simply reusing one video on both platforms rarely performs as well as adapting it.

How to decide where to test first

  1. Start with your customers. Ask where they watch video and look at your own analytics.
  2. Be honest about creative. If you can make a few strong explainer videos, YouTube is often easier. If you can film quick, natural clips every week, TikTok becomes realistic.
  3. Consider your existing setup. If you already use Google Ads, YouTube slots into the same account and tracking.
  4. Set up tracking before spending. Check conversions record properly and consent is handled.
  5. Run a focused test. Give one platform enough budget and time to learn, then judge it on sales or leads.

FAQs

YouTube Ads vs TikTok Ads: which is better?

Neither is better for every business. YouTube suits a broad audience and products that benefit from explanation, while TikTok suits visual consumer brands that can make native, creator-style videos regularly.

Is TikTok advertising cheaper than YouTube?

Costs on both vary with audience, competition and creative quality, so there is no fixed answer. Remember to include the cost of producing fresh creative, which is often higher on TikTok.

Can I use the same video on YouTube and TikTok?

You can, but it rarely performs as well as adapting it. TikTok favours vertical, native-looking clips with a fast hook, while many YouTube placements suit horizontal video.

Do YouTube Ads work for small businesses?

They can, especially for businesses already using Google Ads, as YouTube runs in the same account. A small number of clear, useful videos can run for a long time.

Are TikTok Ads only for young audiences?

No. TikTok's audience has broadened, although it remains strongly associated with younger adults. Check current official audience data and your own customer insights.

How do I measure YouTube and TikTok ad results?

Set up conversion tracking on both, with correct consent handling, and look at sales or leads alongside view-through and assisted conversions, since many viewers buy later.
