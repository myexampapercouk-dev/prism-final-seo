Website Design Cost for Small Businesses in the UK: What Drives the Price and Hidden Costs

Website design cost for UK small businesses: typical price ranges, what pushes the price up or down, the hidden running costs, and how to compare quotes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Our small business websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and online shops £1,500 to £3,500, typically delivered in 3 to 5 weeks and 5 to 12 weeks respectively.
  • Page count, custom design, content, integrations and SEO set-up drive most of the price difference between quotes.
  • Budget for hosting, domains, software, maintenance and content updates, not just the build.

Website design cost for a UK small business depends mainly on how many pages you need, how custom the design is and what the site has to do. At Prism Works Studio, a small business website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and an ecommerce site £1,500 to £3,500, but the build is only part of the total cost of owning a website.

This guide explains what drives the price, compares the main options, lists the hidden costs that catch people out and gives you a checklist for comparing quotes. If you sell online, our dedicated guide to ecommerce website cost in the UK goes deeper into shops.

What drives the price of a website?

Number and type of pages

A five-page brochure site is very different from one with dozens of service and location pages. Each unique page layout needs designing and building; repeated templates are cheaper than one-off designs.

Design approach

Adapting a well-built theme is quicker than designing everything from scratch. If you do not yet have a logo, colours or tone of voice, brand work adds time; our brand strategy service covers that stage.

Content

Content is the most common cause of delays. Copywriting, photography and video can be supplied by you or produced for you. If you write it yourself, budget your own time realistically.

Functionality and integrations

Booking systems, CRM connections, payment, membership areas, multilingual content and calculators all add work. List what you need before asking for quotes, so every supplier prices the same thing.

SEO foundations

A site that is fast, mobile-friendly, properly structured and ready for search engines costs a little more to build but avoids expensive fixes later. If you are replacing an existing site, protecting current rankings is essential; see our guide to a website redesign without losing rankings.

Ecommerce

Product catalogues, payment gateways, delivery rules for Royal Mail, Evri or DPD, VAT settings and options such as Klarna or Clearpay push an online shop above a brochure site. Our comparison of Shopify vs WooCommerce in the UK helps you pick a platform.

Hidden costs of a small business website

The build quote is rarely the whole story. Budget for these ongoing or one-off costs:

  • Domain name, renewed annually.
  • Hosting, which ranges from basic shared plans to managed hosting with backups and security.
  • Platform subscriptions such as Shopify plans, premium themes or plugins.
  • Business email on your own domain.
  • Maintenance: updates, backups, security patches and small edits.
  • Legal pages and cookie consent: a privacy policy and a consent banner that reflect UK GDPR and PECR. Wording should be checked by you or a solicitor, not copied from another site.
  • Payment fees for online shops, charged per transaction.
  • Marketing: a new site does not bring visitors on its own. SEO, ads or content are usually needed to generate enquiries.
  • VAT on supplier invoices if you are not VAT-registered.

Cheap website vs good value website

The lowest quote can become the most expensive option if the site is slow, hard to edit, poorly built for search or locked to a supplier. Value comes from a site that loads quickly, explains what you do clearly, makes it easy to get in touch and can be updated without calling a developer every time. Ask every supplier the same practical questions:

  • Will I own the domain, the hosting account and the website files?
  • Can I edit text and images myself?
  • What happens if I want to move the site to another provider?
  • Is basic on-page SEO, such as titles, descriptions and redirects, included?

Typical website project stages

Understanding how a project runs helps you see where the money goes and where delays usually happen. Most small business website projects follow a similar path, whoever builds them.

  1. Discovery. Agreeing goals, audience, pages, features and what success looks like. A good brief here prevents expensive changes later.
  2. Structure and content plan. Mapping out the pages, deciding which search terms each page should target and listing the content needed.
  3. Design. Producing layouts for key pages on desktop and mobile, with an agreed number of revision rounds.
  4. Build. Developing the site, adding content, setting up forms, analytics and consent, and connecting any integrations.
  5. Testing. Checking speed, mobile layouts, forms, links, redirects and basic accessibility across common browsers.
  6. Launch and handover. Going live, submitting the sitemap to Google Search Console and showing you how to edit the site.

The stage that most often extends the timeline is content. If text and images are ready before the build starts, a small site can comfortably fit our typical 3 to 5 week window. If content is still being written during the build, allow longer.

It is also worth deciding early whether you need any regulated wording. Businesses in sectors such as healthcare or financial advice have rules about what they can claim on their website, and final sign-off of that wording rests with your own compliance team or adviser rather than the web designer.

Finally, think about accessibility. Clear headings, readable contrast, descriptive link text and forms that work with a keyboard help every visitor and are increasingly expected by customers and public sector buyers alike. Building them in from the start costs far less than retrofitting them later.

Checklist: comparing website design quotes

  • Write a short brief: goals, number of pages, must-have features and examples you like.
  • Decide who supplies copy and images.
  • Ask each supplier to price the same scope.
  • Check what is included: design rounds, mobile layouts, SEO set-up, analytics and training.
  • Confirm ownership of domain, hosting and site files.
  • Ask about the timeline and what you need to deliver, and when.
  • List ongoing costs: hosting, licences, maintenance and support.
  • Agree how changes after launch are charged.

FAQs

What is the website design cost for a small business in the UK?

It varies by scope and supplier. Our small business websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites £1,500 to £3,500, with hosting, domains and marketing budgeted separately.

How long does it take to build a small business website?

Our small business websites typically take 3 to 5 weeks and ecommerce sites 5 to 12 weeks, depending on content and feedback.

What ongoing costs should I expect?

Domain renewal, hosting, any platform or plugin subscriptions, business email, maintenance and payment fees for shops, plus marketing to bring visitors.

Should I use a website builder or hire a designer?

A builder can suit a very early-stage business on a tight budget. A designer or studio is usually better when you need a professional look, search-ready structure and support.

Will I own my website?

You should. Make sure the domain, hosting account and site files are in your name, or that the contract clearly transfers them to you.

Does a new website include SEO?

Good builds include technical and on-page foundations such as titles, structure and speed. Ongoing SEO, content and link building are usually a separate service.
