Web Design for Madrid Businesses: Remote Website Builds from a UK Studio

Need web design in Madrid? How Spanish and English sites are planned, built and launched remotely, plus consent, accessibility and our website fees in pounds.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

  • Web design for a Madrid business usually means Spanish pages for local buyers, English pages for international ones, fast mobile templates and clear routes to enquire or buy.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Spain; we work remotely, write in English and work with your team or a translator on Spanish copy.
  • Small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and e-commerce sites £1,500 to £3,500, quoted in pounds with other currencies on request, and most sites take three to five weeks.

Web design for a Madrid business should give you a fast, clear website that wins the trust of Spanish and international visitors and turns them into enquiries or sales. That means a structure built around what your customers search for, pages that work well on a phone, copy in the right languages and a site your team can update without calling a developer.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Madrid or anywhere in Spain. Our website and e-commerce development service runs by video call, shared documents and staged previews, with Madrid one hour ahead of UK time. We write in English and work with your team or a translator on Spanish pages.

What Madrid businesses need from a website

Madrid is the Spanish capital and the country's largest business centre. It is home to corporate headquarters, professional services firms, technology companies, property businesses, hospitality and tourism, education providers and a large number of independent shops and online brands. Many sell to customers in Spain and abroad at the same time, so their websites often serve several audiences:

  • Spanish-speaking customers. Local buyers who expect Spanish pages, prices, delivery terms and contact details that feel local.
  • International clients and partners. Companies elsewhere in Europe, the UK and Latin America who look for suppliers and partners in English or Spanish.
  • Visitors and newcomers. Tourists, students and people relocating to Madrid who search in English for services they need.
  • Candidates. Growing firms that need careers pages to attract staff.

Each group arrives with different questions, so we plan pages for each audience before we choose a design.

Spanish and English pages

Most Madrid businesses need Spanish as their main language, with English for international buyers, visitors or investors. A good bilingual site gives each language its own clean URLs, a language switcher that keeps visitors on the matching page and hreflang tags so search engines show the right version. Machine-translated pages rarely read well and can harm trust, so we plan for proper Spanish copy from the outset.

Prism writes English copy in-house. For Spanish, we supply the page structure, headings, search intent and draft English text, then a native speaker on your team or a translator writes the final Spanish. We load and check it before launch. Our multilingual website design guide explains the structure we use for any combination of languages.

Platforms we build on

We choose the platform around how you sell and who will update the site:

  • WordPress. A strong fit for service businesses, professional firms and content-led sites. Multilingual plugins handle Spanish and English versions, and editors can update pages easily.
  • Shopify. Our usual choice for online shops. Shopify Markets supports several languages and currencies from one store. Our post on Shopify for Spanish brands covers the e-commerce side in more depth.
  • Custom builds. For sites with unusual functions, booking flows or integrations that off-the-shelf platforms handle poorly.

Spanish shoppers are used to card payments and several local and digital payment options. Bizum is widely used in Spain, and many Spanish card payments run through Redsys. Whether these are available on your site depends on your payment provider and bank, so we confirm options with you before the build.

Design that converts on mobile

Most visitors will see your site on a phone first. We design mobile layouts before desktop ones, keep navigation short and put the most important action on every page within easy reach. For Madrid businesses, that usually means:

  1. Clear service or product pages. One page per main service or category, each with its own heading, summary and call to action.
  2. Fast loading. Compressed images, minimal scripts and lean templates so pages load quickly on mobile connections.
  3. Trust signals. Business details, clear contact options, delivery and returns information and anything you are happy to publish about how you work.
  4. Simple forms. Short enquiry and checkout forms with clear error messages in each language.

Consent, privacy and accessibility

Spanish websites fall under the GDPR for personal data, and the EU ePrivacy rules mean analytics and marketing cookies normally need consent before they load. We build a clear consent banner and set up analytics so it respects visitors' choices. Our guide to cookie banner design covers the approach in more detail.

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing products and services, including a wide range of online shops. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but building to WCAG AA, the level referenced by the harmonised standard EN 301 549, is good practice for any site. Our European Accessibility Act guide explains what it means for websites. Legal wording, privacy notices and decisions on compliance should come from your legal adviser, and official guidance should be checked before relying on any summary.

Search visibility from launch

A new website should be ready to rank from day one. We set up clean URLs, page titles, meta descriptions, headings, image alt text, an XML sitemap and structured data where it helps. If you are replacing an existing site, we map every old URL to its new equivalent so you keep the rankings and links you have already earned. Our guide to website redirects when relaunching explains why this matters.

Ongoing search work is a separate service. If you want to grow organic traffic after launch, our post on SEO for Barcelona businesses covers how we approach Spanish and English search for a similar brief.

Website fees and timelines

Our website prices are listed on our pricing page. A small website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000, and an e-commerce site typically costs £1,500 to £3,500, depending on the number of pages, products, languages and integrations. We quote in pounds, and quotes in other currencies are available on request. Spanish translation is arranged and paid separately if you do not have an in-house writer.

Most websites take three to five weeks, and e-commerce builds typically take five to twelve weeks. Bilingual sites can take longer if translated copy arrives late, so we agree content deadlines at the start. Our guide on how long a website takes to build sets out what affects the schedule.

FAQs

Do you have a web design office in Madrid?

No. Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Spain. We work remotely by video call, shared documents and staged previews, one hour behind Madrid time.

Can you build a website in Spanish and English?

Yes. We plan and build both language versions with matching pages, a language switcher and hreflang. English copy is written in-house, and Spanish copy comes from your team or a native-speaking translator.

How much does web design in Madrid cost with your studio?

A small website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 and an e-commerce site £1,500 to £3,500. We quote in pounds, with other currencies on request. Translation is a separate cost.

How long does a website take to build?

Most websites take three to five weeks, and e-commerce sites typically take five to twelve weeks. Timing depends on scope and how quickly content and feedback arrive.

Can you add Spanish payment options to an online shop?

Often, yes. Options such as Bizum depend on your payment provider and bank, so we check what your chosen platform and provider support before the build starts.

Will my website meet GDPR and accessibility rules?

We build in consent, privacy pages and WCAG AA accessibility practices. Your legal adviser should approve privacy wording and confirm how the GDPR and the European Accessibility Act apply to your business.
