Getting tracking right first

Google's bidding systems learn from the conversions you feed them. If tracking is missing or counts the wrong things, the account optimizes towards noise. Before we scale anything, we check that forms, calls, bookings and purchases are tracked accurately, and that we are not double-counting.

For lead generation, we often recommend importing offline outcomes such as qualified leads or closed deals, so the algorithm learns which clicks actually turn into revenue. For online stores, we make sure purchase values pass through correctly so return on ad spend is reliable.

Keywords and targeting for Austin

Austin searchers often include a neighborhood or nearby city, such as Round Rock, Cedar Park, Georgetown, Pflugerville or San Marcos. Others search with "near me" and rely on Google to work out location. We set location targeting to match where you can actually serve customers, and we review search terms weekly to block irrelevant traffic.

We split campaigns by service and intent so budget goes to the searches most likely to convert. Brand terms, high-intent service terms and research terms each get their own budget and bids. That makes reporting clearer and stops one expensive term from draining the whole account.

For B2B and SaaS, we pair search with LinkedIn when job titles matter. Our guide to LinkedIn Ads for US B2B companies covers that combination, and our post on SaaS marketing in the USA covers the wider funnel.

Landing pages that convert

A good ad sends people to a page that matches what they searched. We review your landing pages for speed, clarity, trust signals and a single clear call to action. If your current pages are holding the campaign back, we can build focused landing pages or recommend changes for your team. Our post on web design in Austin covers what a strong local page looks like.

Google Ads and SEO together

Paid search brings traffic quickly, while SEO builds visibility you do not pay for per click. Running both lets you use search term data from ads to shape content, and lets you pull back on paid spend for terms where you rank well organically. See our post on SEO in Austin for the organic side, and our guide to AI-powered marketing in Austin for how we use AI tools in analysis and testing.

Ad copy, assets and testing

Responsive search ads let Google mix headlines and descriptions, but the quality of what you give it still decides the result. We write headlines that match the search phrase, state a clear benefit, and give a reason to act now. We add sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, call assets and location assets where they fit, because they make ads larger and more useful.

We test one idea at a time where possible, such as a different offer, a stronger guarantee or a new angle on price. Small, steady tests give clearer answers than rewriting everything at once. We also watch ad strength and asset performance reports, but we judge success on conversions and cost per result, not on Google's labels.

For Shopping and Performance Max, the product feed is the ad. We check titles, descriptions, product types, images and custom labels, because a clean feed often lifts results more than any bidding change. If you sell on marketplaces too, the same product data discipline helps there.

Budgets, bidding and wasted spend

New accounts usually start with conservative bidding while the system gathers data. Once conversions are flowing reliably, we move to strategies such as maximize conversions or target cost per acquisition, and for stores, target return on ad spend. We change bidding strategies carefully, because each change resets some of the learning.

Wasted spend usually hides in three places: broad search terms that do not match your offer, locations you cannot serve, and hours when nobody answers the phone. We tackle all three with negative keyword lists, precise location settings and ad schedules. We also check device performance, since some Austin service businesses convert far better on mobile calls than on desktop forms, and the reverse is common for B2B software.

Seasonality matters too. Home services see demand shift with Texas summer heat and storm seasons, while events and hospitality follow the city's festival calendar. We plan budget changes ahead of those peaks rather than reacting after costs have already risen.

How Austin compares with other Texas markets

Austin, Dallas and Houston all have competitive Google Ads auctions, but the mix of advertisers differs. Austin skews towards tech, startups and home services for a growing population, which means some terms are crowded by well-funded advertisers. Careful keyword selection and strong landing pages matter more than raw budget. If you advertise across Texas, see how we approach Google Ads in Dallas and Google Ads in Houston.

Our process

  1. Audit. We review your current account, tracking, landing pages and competitors.
  2. Plan. We agree goals, budgets, campaign structure and targeting.
  3. Build. We set up or rebuild campaigns, ads, extensions and audiences.
  4. Optimize. We review search terms, bids, ads and budgets weekly.
  5. Report. Monthly reports cover spend, conversions, cost per result and next steps.

Paid search can bring enquiries soon after launch, but stable performance usually takes a few months of learning and refinement. Most accounts settle within three to six months.

Sales tax, accessibility and privacy notes

We keep these points general. Sales tax rules vary by state and depend on what you sell, so confirm how they apply to your business with your accountant. Landing pages should follow accessibility good practice, and questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act are best taken to your own legal adviser. Tracking tags, cookies and remarketing lists involve personal data, so review official guidance on privacy rules in the states where your customers live and make sure your privacy notice reflects how you use data.
