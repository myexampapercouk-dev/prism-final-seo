Shopify SEO for UK Stores: Collections, Duplicate URLs, Schema and Speed

Shopify SEO for UK stores: how to optimise collections, fix duplicate product URLs, add schema, speed up your theme and avoid app bloat that slows sales.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Collections are usually your most valuable Shopify pages for SEO, so give each one a clear title, helpful copy and strong internal links.
  • Shopify creates duplicate product URLs through collection paths; make sure your theme links to the canonical /products/ URL.
  • Every app adds code, so audit apps regularly and remove anything that slows the store without earning its keep.

Shopify SEO in the UK comes down to five things: well-optimised collection pages, clean product URLs, accurate structured data, a fast theme and a lean set of apps. Shopify handles a lot of the basics for you, but it also has quirks that quietly hold stores back if nobody fixes them.

This guide walks through each area in plain English, with a table of common Shopify issues and a checklist you can work through yourself or hand to a developer.

Shopify SEO UK: what the platform does for you

Shopify is a solid starting point for search. It generates an XML sitemap automatically, adds canonical tags to most pages, gives you editable title tags and meta descriptions, uses HTTPS by default and lets you create URL redirects from the admin. For a small UK store, that removes many of the technical headaches that self-hosted platforms bring.

What Shopify does not do is decide which pages deserve to rank, write useful copy, control how your theme links between pages or stop apps loading heavy scripts. Those decisions are where most Shopify stores win or lose organic traffic. If you are still choosing a platform, our comparison of Shopify and WooCommerce for UK businesses covers the trade-offs.

Optimise collections first

Collections are Shopify's category pages, and they usually match the searches with the most buying intent. Someone searching "linen bedding sets" wants a range to compare, which a collection page delivers and a single product page does not.

What a strong collection page includes

  • A title tag and H1 that use the phrase people actually search for, written naturally
  • A short introduction above or near the product grid explaining the range, materials or who it suits
  • Further helpful copy lower down: buying advice, sizing notes and a few FAQs on delivery or care
  • Links to related collections and to relevant buying guides
  • A meta description that sells the click, mentioning UK delivery or returns where relevant

Avoid creating dozens of near-identical collections that target the same phrase. Two collections called "mens trainers" and "trainers for men" compete with each other. Pick one, make it excellent and redirect the other.

Tag-filtered collection pages

Shopify lets shoppers filter collections by tag, which can create URLs like /collections/dresses/red. Some themes let Google crawl and index every combination, producing large numbers of thin pages. Depending on your theme and filter setup, you may want to keep these out of the index or, where a filtered view has real search demand, turn it into a proper standalone collection with its own copy.

Fix duplicate product URLs

This is the best-known Shopify SEO issue. A product can be reached at /products/blue-linen-shirt and also at /collections/shirts/products/blue-linen-shirt. Shopify adds a canonical tag pointing to the /products/ version, which helps, but many themes still link internally to the collection-based path. That sends mixed signals and wastes crawl activity.

The fix is usually a small theme edit so that product links in collection grids point directly to the /products/ URL. A developer can do this in an hour or two on most themes. It is worth checking after any theme change, because new themes sometimes reintroduce the problem.

Structured data on Shopify

Structured data helps search engines understand your products: name, price in GBP, availability, brand and reviews. Many modern Shopify themes include basic product schema, but quality varies. Common problems include missing GTINs, prices that do not update for variants, review markup that does not match visible reviews and duplicate schema blocks when an app adds its own on top of the theme's.

Test a handful of product pages with Google's Rich Results Test and check the enhancement reports in Search Console. Aim for one clean, accurate block of product schema per page, plus breadcrumb and organisation markup. Accurate data also helps AI search tools describe your products correctly, which we explain in our guide to generative engine optimisation. If you run Google Shopping ads, keep schema and your Merchant Center feed consistent.

Speed and Core Web Vitals

Most UK shoppers browse on their phones, so mobile speed matters for both rankings and conversion. On Shopify, the biggest speed problems usually come from heavy themes, oversized images, autoplay video, third-party chat or review widgets and apps that inject scripts on every page.

  • Use a well-built, lightweight theme and keep customisations tidy
  • Compress images and use sensibly sized hero banners
  • Avoid sliders and autoplay video above the fold unless they genuinely help sales
  • Load chat, review and pop-up tools only where they are needed
  • Check Core Web Vitals in Search Console, not just a single lab test

Apps: helpful, but audit them

The Shopify app store makes it easy to add features, and just as easy to slow a store down. Each app can add scripts, styles and sometimes extra pages or duplicate schema. When you uninstall an app, code it injected into the theme can be left behind. Review apps every few months: does each one earn its keep, and is anything left over from apps you no longer use? SEO apps can be useful for bulk editing titles, alt text and redirects, but they cannot replace good collection copy or a sound site structure.

Migrating to Shopify without losing rankings

Moving to Shopify from WooCommerce, Magento or another platform almost always changes URLs, because Shopify uses fixed /products/ and /collections/ paths. Without a full redirect map, you risk losing rankings you have built up over years. Map every old URL to its closest new equivalent, carry over titles and copy, and monitor Search Console closely for several weeks after launch. Shopify SEO checklist

  • Key collections have unique titles, H1s, intro copy and FAQs
  • Product links in the theme point to /products/ URLs
  • Tag and filter URLs are controlled, not indexed by default
  • Products have unique descriptions, not copied manufacturer text
  • One accurate product schema block per page, tested without errors
  • Images compressed and given descriptive alt text
  • Unused apps removed and leftover code cleaned up
  • Redirects set for deleted products and changed URLs
  • Sitemap submitted and coverage checked in Search Console
  • Organic revenue tracked in analytics, not just traffic

FAQs

Is Shopify good for SEO in the UK?

Yes. Shopify covers sitemaps, canonicals, HTTPS and editable titles out of the box. Stores still need good collection content, clean internal links and a fast theme to rank well.

What is the most important part of Shopify SEO UK stores should fix first?

Usually collection pages and duplicate product URLs. Collections match the strongest buying searches, and fixing theme links to /products/ URLs removes a common source of confused signals.

Can I change Shopify URL structure?

Not fully. Shopify uses fixed /products/, /collections/ and /pages/ paths. You can edit the handle after the prefix, but always add a redirect when you change one.

Do I need a Shopify SEO app?

Not necessarily. Some apps save time on bulk edits, but they add code and cannot replace good content or structure. Install only what you will use and review regularly.

How long does Shopify SEO take?

Technical fixes can show benefits within weeks, but meaningful growth in organic traffic and sales typically takes 3 to 6 months, depending on competition and the state of the store.

Will apps slow down my Shopify store?

They can. Many apps load scripts on every page, and some leave code behind after uninstalling. Regular app audits are one of the simplest ways to protect speed.
