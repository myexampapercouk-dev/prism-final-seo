How clients look for a lawyer in Montreal

Montreal's legal market is broad. Finance, aerospace, technology, life sciences, gaming, transport and a large port generate corporate, commercial, intellectual property, employment and regulatory work. A diverse population creates steady demand for family law, immigration, real estate, wills and estates, criminal defence and civil litigation, from Downtown and the Plateau to Saint-Laurent, Laval and the South Shore.

Quebec's civil law tradition also shapes how people search. Clients may look for a notary for property, estates or marriage contracts, or for an avocat for disputes and litigation, and the vocabulary differs between French and English searchers. Private clients usually start with a search; business clients also check LinkedIn, ask for referrals and study a firm's website before getting in touch.

Language matters too. Quebec has rules about the use of French in business, and most Montreal firms need strong French pages. We write in English only. French content should be written by your own lawyers or a professional legal translator, and you should check your language obligations with official guidance or your own adviser. Our role is to build a site where both language versions are clearly linked, correctly tagged and easy for search engines to index.

A website that works in two languages

For a Montreal firm, the website has an extra job: it must serve French and English readers equally well without confusing search engines. That means separate, properly linked language versions rather than automatic translation widgets, and practice-area pages that are written for each audience rather than copied word for word.

We build law firm websites with:

  • A clear page for each practice area and main sub-topic, in each language your firm provides.
  • Correct language tags so search engines show the right version to the right searcher.
  • Lawyer and notary profiles written or approved by your firm.
  • Contact forms that ask visitors not to send confidential details before a mandate is agreed.
  • Fast, accessible, mobile-first layouts with secure hosting.
  • A content management system your team can update.

A small firm website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks. Read our guides to web design in Montreal and WordPress in Montreal for detail on our builds.

SEO for Montreal law firms

SEO is often a law firm's strongest long-term channel, because people look for answers before they look for a lawyer. Our work covers:

Professional rules and confidentiality

Lawyers and notaries in Quebec are regulated professionals, and their advertising is subject to rules on accuracy, claims, testimonials, comparisons and fees. Confidentiality and privacy law also affect how forms, tracking and data are handled.

We give general marketing information only and are not legal advisers. Your firm should check all marketing against your professional order's guidance, and approval of every page, ad and post rests with the firm. We help by writing carefully, avoiding outcome promises, keeping forms minimal and recording approvals.

Common mistakes we see on Montreal law firm websites

Many firm websites in the city share the same avoidable problems. Fixing them is often the quickest way to win more enquiries before any new content or advertising is added.

  • One page for everything: a single services page listing ten practice areas gives search engines little to rank and clients little to read. Each main practice area deserves its own page.
  • Machine-translated pages: automatic translation reads poorly to clients and can introduce legal inaccuracies. Both language versions should be written or checked by people who know the law and the language.
  • Mismatched language versions: when the French and English sites have different structures, search engines struggle to pair them and visitors land on the wrong version.
  • No clear first step: visitors should know whether to call, book a consultation or send a short enquiry, and what happens next.
  • Slow, dated mobile pages: most private clients arrive on a phone, often in a stressful moment, and a slow page loses them.
  • Ads sent to the home page: paid clicks should land on the page for the matter the person searched about, not a generic home page.
  • No tracking: without call and form tracking, a firm cannot tell which practice areas or campaigns bring real enquiries.

We usually address these points in the first month or two, then move on to new content and campaigns once the foundations are sound.
