Amazon SEO in the UAE: how marketplace search works

Marketplace search engines have one main goal: to show shoppers the products they are most likely to buy. They look at how closely a listing matches the search, then at signals that suggest people choose and are happy with it, such as clicks, sales, reviews, price, availability and delivery options.

That means listing SEO has two jobs. First, make sure the right words appear in the right places so your product is eligible to show for relevant searches. Second, make the listing persuasive enough that shoppers click and buy, which in turn helps it keep its position. Marketplaces do not publish exactly how their ranking works, and it changes over time, so good practice focuses on relevance and conversion rather than tricks.

Keyword research for UAE shoppers

Shoppers in the UAE search in English, Arabic and sometimes a mix of both, and the words they use may differ from those used in the UK or US. Product names, sizes, spellings and even the way people describe a problem can vary.

  • English searches often include product type, brand, size and use, and may follow British or American spelling.
  • Arabic searches can use several spellings and transliterations, so we look at variants rather than a single translated term.
  • Seasonal demand changes around Ramadan, Eid, back to school, national holidays and major sales events, so listings and stock plans should reflect this.

We recommend that any Arabic copy is written or reviewed by a fluent speaker. Machine translation can miss the natural phrasing shoppers actually use.

Selling on Amazon.ae and other Gulf marketplaces

Many UAE sellers list on more than one marketplace. Each has its own listing format, content rules, review systems and search behaviour, so copying the same listing everywhere rarely gets the best result. We adapt titles, bullets and images to each marketplace's requirements while keeping your brand and product information consistent.

If you also sell in Saudi Arabia or other Gulf countries, keyword research and listings may need adjusting for each market. Delivery promises, pricing and stock levels also affect how listings perform, and these sit with your operations team or fulfilment partner. Marketplace rules, fees and seller requirements change over time, so please check each marketplace's current official guidance.

Marketplaces and your own website

Marketplaces give you access to shoppers who are ready to buy, but you are working inside someone else's platform. Your own online store lets you control the brand experience, keep customer relationships and build repeat sales. Most growing brands use both.

Images, content and conversion

Good listings look professional on a small screen. Shoppers often decide from the main image and the first few lines of text, so we pay close attention to both.

  • A clean main image that follows marketplace rules and shows the product clearly.
  • Supporting images showing scale, detail, use and what is in the box.
  • Bullet points that lead with benefits and answer the questions shoppers ask most.
  • Consistent sizes, variants and specifications across every listing.

Many of the same principles apply to product pages on your own site. Our guides to writing product descriptions and product page SEO go into more detail.

How we work on marketplace listings

Our process is simple and transparent, so you always know what is changing and why.

  1. Review. We look at your current listings, the searches your products should appear for and the competing listings shoppers see alongside yours.
  2. Prioritise. We pick the products where better listings are likely to matter most, usually best sellers, new launches and products with clear search demand.
  3. Rewrite and refresh. We prepare new titles, bullets, descriptions and backend terms, plus an image brief where photos need improving, and share everything for your approval.
  4. Update and record. Changes are made, or handed to your team to upload, with a written log of what changed and when.
  5. Measure and repeat. We compare traffic, conversion and sales data before and after, then move on to the next group of products.

Your seller account always stays in your name, and you decide what access we have.
