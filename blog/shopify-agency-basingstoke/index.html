Why Basingstoke retailers choose Shopify

Basingstoke has a strong commercial base, with technology, finance, logistics and distribution firms alongside independent retailers, makers and growing product brands. Its position on the M3 and close to the A34 has made it a natural home for warehousing and fulfilment, which suits online sellers shipping across the South of England and beyond.

Shopify is popular with these businesses because it handles the hard parts of selling online:

  • Payments through Shopify Payments, PayPal and buy-now-pay-later options such as Klarna or Clearpay.
  • Delivery settings for Royal Mail, Evri, DPD and click and collect.
  • Stock and orders in one admin, with apps for inventory and fulfilment.
  • Security and hosting managed by Shopify, so you do not maintain servers or plugins.

Planning your store structure

The structure of your store affects how easily customers find products and how well Google understands your range. Before we design anything, we plan:

  • Collections that match how customers shop and search, such as by product type, use or audience.
  • Product data including titles, variants, images, descriptions and specifications.
  • Navigation that keeps key collections one or two clicks from the homepage.
  • Filters so customers can narrow large ranges quickly.

Good product data takes time. Many Shopify projects run longer because product information is not ready, so we agree early who supplies what. Our Shopify store setup guide lists the settings and content you will need.

Speed and conversion

Shopify stores can slow down as apps, large images and extra scripts build up. A slow store loses mobile shoppers and can hurt rankings. We choose themes carefully, keep apps to those you actually need and compress images. Our article on Shopify speed optimisation explains the main fixes.

Conversion matters just as much. Clear product photos, honest delivery information, visible returns policies, trust signals and a short checkout all help. Our e-commerce CRO guide covers the changes that usually make the biggest difference.

Getting traffic to a new store

Launching a store is only the first step. Most Basingstoke retailers grow through a mix of channels:

  • Shopify SEO to rank collections and products for buying searches. See our Shopify SEO guide.
  • Google Shopping and Performance Max to show products with images and prices in search results.
  • Meta and TikTok Ads to reach new customers through social feeds.
  • Marketplace SEO if you also sell on Amazon or eBay and want listings that rank.
  • Email marketing to bring past customers back.

Our e-commerce sector page explains how we combine these for online retailers.

UK settings to get right

Before launch, we check the settings that often cause problems for UK stores: VAT display and tax settings, delivery zones and rates, cookie consent, privacy and returns pages, and order notification emails. We keep regulatory notes general, so for VAT treatment or consumer law questions we recommend checking official GOV.UK guidance or speaking with your accountant or adviser.

Our Shopify project process

  1. Discovery. We talk through your products, customers, competitors, current sales channels and what you want the store to achieve.
  2. Plan. We agree the collection structure, page list, apps, integrations and who supplies each piece of content.
  3. Design. We customise a well-supported theme around your brand, focusing on the homepage, collection pages and product pages where most buying decisions happen.
  4. Build and import. We set up products, variants, payments, delivery, tax and legal pages, then import or migrate your data.
  5. Test. We place test orders, check emails, discount codes, mobile layouts and speed, and review everything with you on the staging store.
  6. Launch and support. We connect your domain, analytics and Google Merchant Centre, submit your sitemap and monitor the first weeks closely.

Because we work remotely, every stage is written down and shared, so you can review progress whenever suits you rather than waiting for a meeting.

Apps: fewer is usually better

Shopify's app store makes it tempting to add a new app for every feature. Each app can add scripts, monthly fees and potential conflicts, and many duplicate features already built into modern themes. Before launch we review every app against a simple question: does it clearly help customers buy or save you real time? If not, we leave it out. This keeps the store faster, cheaper to run and easier to maintain as it grows.
