Care Home Marketing UK: A Practical Guide for Providers

Care home marketing UK guide: local SEO, websites families trust, careful paid ads and enquiry handling, with general notes on CQC and advertising rules.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Families choose care homes carefully, so honest, detailed information builds more trust than slogans.
  • Local search and a clear website do most of the work; paid ads support specific vacancies.
  • Keep claims accurate and inspection references current, with sign-off resting with the provider.

Care home marketing in the UK is about helping families find your home, understand the care you provide and feel confident enough to visit. For most homes, that means strong local search visibility, a website with honest, detailed information, careful paid campaigns for specific vacancies and a warm, quick response to every enquiry.

This guide is for owners and managers of independent residential, nursing and dementia care homes. It is general marketing guidance only, not regulatory or legal advice; responsibility for compliance and approval of all claims rests with the provider.

How families choose a care home

Choosing a care home is one of the most emotional decisions a family makes. It often happens under time pressure, for example after a hospital stay, and frequently involves several relatives. The person searching is often an adult son or daughter, sometimes living some distance away, looking for a home near a parent or near themselves.

Families usually want to know:

  • What types of care you provide, such as residential, nursing, dementia or respite.
  • Where you are and what the surroundings are like.
  • What daily life looks like: activities, food, visiting and rooms.
  • Your latest inspection rating and report.
  • How fees and funding work, in general terms.
  • Whether there is a room available and how quickly someone could move in.

Marketing that answers these questions clearly, without pressure, tends to work better than glossy slogans.

Local search and Google Business Profile

Many families start with searches like "care home near [town]" or "dementia care home [area]". Your Google Business Profile should have accurate categories, address, phone number and opening times for visits, plus real photos of rooms, communal areas and gardens. Reviews from families can help, but never pressure anyone to leave one, and respond to every review with care and discretion, without sharing personal or care details.

A website families can trust

Your website is often where a shortlist becomes a visit. A strong care home website usually includes:

  • A clear page for each type of care you provide, written in plain English.
  • Real photos of the home, with consent from anyone identifiable.
  • Information on daily life, activities, meals and visiting arrangements.
  • Your current inspection rating, with a link to the official report.
  • A simple explanation of how fees and funding generally work, with an invitation to discuss individual circumstances.
  • An easy way to arrange a visit or call, prominent on every page.

Paid ads for vacancies

Paid search can help when you have a specific room to fill or want to promote a care type such as respite. Target the area families search from, which may be wider than your town, and use specific search terms. Write ad copy that is accurate and calm; avoid urgency tactics or claims you cannot evidence. Meta ads can reach adult children in the area with information-led messages, such as an open day or a guide to choosing a home.

Content that helps families

Helpful articles can answer the questions families search for, such as the difference between residential and nursing care, what to look for on a visit or how to prepare for a move. Keep this content general and point people to official sources and their local authority for funding questions. Our content strategy guide explains how to plan topics around real questions.

Regulation and claims: general points

Care homes in England are registered with and inspected by the Care Quality Commission (CQC), with other regulators covering Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. As a general point, regulators may expect ratings to be displayed accurately, so marketing should reference ratings correctly and be updated when a new report is published. Advertising claims should be honest and evidenced in line with the CAP Code, and consumer law guidance on care home fees and terms may apply to how you present pricing information.

This is general information only. Prism Works Studio is not a regulator or a legal adviser, and the provider is responsible for checking current requirements and signing off all marketing content. Our healthcare marketing guide covers related points for other care and health businesses.

Handling enquiries well

Families often contact several homes at once. A quick, kind, informative reply makes a big difference. Make sure someone can answer calls during the day, reply to web forms promptly and offer a visit at a time that suits the family. Keep a simple log of enquiries, sources, visits and admissions so you know which marketing works. Handle any personal or health information in line with UK GDPR.

Open days, visits and the local community

A visit is usually the moment a family decides. Make visits easy to book and consistently good: a welcoming greeting, a calm tour, time to ask questions and a clear summary of next steps afterwards. Offer visits at different times, including evenings or weekends where possible, as many relatives work.

Open days and community events can also help, such as coffee mornings, seasonal events or links with local schools and community groups. These build a local reputation over time and give you genuine news to share. Always protect residents' privacy and dignity, and only feature people who have given clear consent. Common care home marketing mistakes

  • Stock photos. Families want to see the real home. Generic images can reduce trust rather than build it.
  • Out-of-date information. An old inspection rating, old menus or out-of-date activity schedules suggest the website is not looked after.
  • Vague care descriptions. Saying you offer "all types of care" is less useful than explaining exactly what you provide and who you can support.
  • Slow responses. Families often contact several homes at once. A delayed reply can mean a lost visit.
  • Pressure tactics. Urgency claims about limited rooms can feel uncomfortable in such a sensitive decision. Be honest about availability instead.

Recruiting staff through the same channels

Many homes also need to attract carers and nurses. A careers page on your website, honest descriptions of the role and working environment and targeted local social ads can support recruitment alongside your resident-focused marketing. Keep the two audiences on separate pages so families and job seekers each find what they need quickly.

FAQs

What is the best care home marketing UK approach for an independent home?

Focus on local search, a detailed and honest website, prompt enquiry handling and careful paid ads for specific vacancies. Families value clear information and a warm response over slogans.

Should a care home show its CQC rating on its website?

As a general point, providers in England may be expected to display their rating accurately, including on their website. Check current CQC guidance and confirm requirements yourself.

Do Google Ads work for care homes?

They can help promote specific vacancies or care types to families searching locally. Keep copy accurate and calm, and check platform policies that apply to healthcare-related ads.

Can we share residents' stories in marketing?

Only with clear, informed consent from the resident or their legal representative, and with care for dignity and privacy. The provider should approve every story.

Who searches for care homes online?

Often adult children and other relatives, sometimes living away from the area, as well as social workers and hospital discharge teams.

How long does care home marketing take to work?

Paid ads can bring enquiries soon after launch. SEO and website improvements typically take three to six months to show clear results.
