Restaurant websites that make booking easy

A restaurant website has a simple job: answer the questions diners have and get them to book, order or visit. A good site should:

  • Load quickly on a phone, even on a weak signal.
  • Show the menu as real text, not a slow PDF, so it can be read and found in search.
  • Display opening hours, address, parking notes and a map clearly.
  • Link to your booking or online ordering system in one tap.
  • Include pages for private dining, catering or events if you offer them.
  • Use strong, real photos of your food and space.

Our restaurant website design guide sets out these principles in detail. Small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and usually take three to five weeks, built through our website development service.

Local SEO for Memphis restaurants

Many diners search for terms like barbecue near me or brunch in Midtown and choose from the map results. Local SEO helps your restaurant appear there.

  • Google Business Profile. We help you keep categories, hours, menu links, photos and holiday hours up to date.
  • Consistent details. Your name, address and phone number should match on your website, your profile and major directories.
  • Reviews. We help you set up a simple, honest way to ask happy guests for reviews and reply to them. We never write or buy reviews.
  • Menu and cuisine content. Clear text about your dishes and cuisine helps search engines and AI tools understand what you serve.

Our local SEO guide covers these steps in more depth. The approach is the same in Memphis as in the UK. Local SEO results typically build over three to six months through our SEO and GEO service.

Building a direct relationship with diners

Third-party delivery apps and booking platforms can bring new customers, but they also take a share of every order and keep much of the customer data. Many Memphis restaurants want a better balance, with more guests booking and ordering directly. A few practical steps help:

  • Make direct ordering obvious. Put an order online button at the top of every page and on your Google Business Profile, so diners do not default to an app.
  • Collect emails with permission. A simple sign-up form for news, events and new menus lets you reach regulars without paying for every message. Keep it short and explain what people will receive.
  • Use events to bring people back. Live music nights, tasting menus and seasonal specials give you a reason to post, email and run small ad campaigns to people who already know you.
  • Keep social profiles current. Updated photos, hours and menu highlights on Instagram and Facebook help diners who check social before they choose.
  • Link everything together. Your website, profile, social accounts and ads should all point to the same booking and ordering links, so tracking stays clear.

We set up tracking so you can see which channels bring bookings and orders, rather than guessing. That helps you decide where to spend time and budget each month. For restaurant groups, we can report by location, so each manager sees how their venue is performing and which pages, searches and ads lead guests to their door.

None of this needs a large team. Most restaurants we support spend a short time each week sending us photos, menu changes and event dates, and we handle the website updates, profile posts, ad changes and reporting from there. Email and privacy rules apply to customer data, so the consent wording on any sign-up form should be checked by you and your own adviser.
