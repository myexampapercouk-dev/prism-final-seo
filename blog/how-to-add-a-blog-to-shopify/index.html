How to Add a Blog to a Shopify Store (and Make It Useful)

How to add a blog to a Shopify store: creating the blog, writing and formatting posts, navigation, SEO settings, linking to products and what to write.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify includes a built-in blog, so you can add one without extra apps or a separate website.
  • A shop blog earns its place by answering questions your customers search for and linking them to relevant products and collections.
  • Set titles, descriptions, URLs and image alt text for every post, and keep the blog easy to find in your navigation.

Many Shopify store owners do not realise their store already has a blog built in. You do not need a separate website or an add-on to start publishing articles. The harder part is making that blog worth having: posts that people actually search for, that help them choose and that lead them towards your products.

This guide explains how to add a blog to a Shopify store step by step, then covers how to make it useful for customers and search engines. For broader search advice for your store, see our guide to Shopify SEO.

Why a shop needs a blog at all

Product and collection pages are built for people who already know roughly what they want. A blog helps you reach people earlier, while they are still researching. For example, someone searching how to care for a wool jumper, which candle wax burns longest or how to choose a running backpack may not be ready to buy yet, but they are clearly interested in your category.

A good shop blog can:

  • Answer questions customers ask before buying.
  • Bring in search traffic that product pages alone would not attract.
  • Give you content to share by email and on social media.
  • Build trust by showing expertise in your products.
  • Reduce customer service questions by explaining things once, clearly.

A blog is not a quick win. It works best as a long-term habit. If you are not sure a blog suits your business, our guide to content strategy covers how to decide what content is worth producing.

Step 1: Find the blog section in Shopify

In your Shopify admin, go to the online store section and open blog posts. Shopify usually creates a default blog, often called News, for every store. You can use it as it is, rename it, or create a new blog.

Step 2: Create or rename your blog

A Shopify store can have more than one blog. Each blog is a container that holds posts. Most stores only need one. Give it a clear name, such as Journal, Guides or Advice, and check its URL.

Shopify blog URLs follow a fixed pattern that includes "blogs" and the blog's handle. You can change the handle, such as from news to guides, but do it before you publish posts. If you change it later, set up redirects from the old URLs. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings explains why redirects matter.

You can also choose whether comments are allowed. If you enable them, decide whether they need approval before appearing, since spam comments are common. Many shops leave comments off or require moderation.

Step 3: Write your first post

Create a new blog post and choose which blog it belongs to. The editor lets you add:

  • Title: the headline people see on the page.
  • Content: your article, with headings, lists, links, images and video.
  • Excerpt: a short summary that may appear on the blog listing page, depending on your theme.
  • Featured image: the main image shown on listing pages and often when the post is shared.
  • Author and tags: used for organising posts and, in some themes, filtering them.
  • Visibility: publish now, schedule for later or save as hidden.

Use proper headings to structure the article rather than making text bold. Headings help readers scan and help search engines understand the content. Keep paragraphs short, especially for mobile readers.

Step 4: Set the SEO fields

At the bottom of each post, Shopify has a search engine listing section where you can edit:

  • Page title: the title shown in search results. Aim for something clear and specific, ideally including the main phrase people search for.
  • Meta description: the short summary that may appear under the title in search results. Write it to encourage clicks.
  • URL handle: the end part of the post's address. Keep it short, readable and descriptive.

Also add alt text to images, describing what each image shows. This helps people using screen readers and gives search engines context. Our guide to website image optimisation covers image file sizes and alt text in more detail.

Step 5: Add the blog to your navigation

A blog nobody can find will not do much. Add it to your menus through the navigation settings in Shopify. Common places include:

  • The main menu, if content is central to how customers shop with you.
  • The footer, which suits many stores.
  • Links from relevant product and collection pages.

Our guides to navigation structure and footer design explain how to decide what goes where.

Step 6: Check your theme's blog templates

Your theme controls how the blog listing page and individual posts look. In the theme editor, open the blog and blog post templates and review options such as:

  • Whether featured images, dates, authors and excerpts are shown.
  • Layout of the listing page, such as grid or list.
  • Whether you can add sections below posts, such as featured products or a newsletter sign-up.

Adding a product or collection section beneath blog posts can help readers move from reading to shopping. If your theme lacks the options you need, our guide to Shopify theme customisation explains what can be changed.

What to write about

The most useful shop blog posts answer real questions from people likely to buy your products. Good sources of ideas include:

  • Customer questions: emails, live chat and questions asked in person.
  • Search research: the questions and phrases people search for around your products. Our keyword research guide explains how to find them.
  • Product reviews and returns: what confuses or disappoints people points to content that would help.
  • Your own expertise: how products are made, materials, care and use.

Types of post that tend to suit shops include:

Post typeExample
How-to guidesHow to care for, use, fit or assemble a product.
Buying guidesHow to choose the right size, material or type.
ComparisonsThe differences between two materials or product types.
Gift guidesIdeas for occasions or types of recipient.
Behind the scenesHow products are made or sourced.
InspirationWays to style, use or combine products.

Avoid posts written only to fill the blog, such as thin news updates or articles that repeat what is on product pages. Fewer, better posts are more useful than many weak ones.

Linking posts to products and collections

Internal links are what turn a blog into part of your shop. In each post, link naturally to the products and collections you mention. A care guide should link to the products it covers. A buying guide should link to the collection where readers can compare options.

Link the other way too. A product page can link to a relevant guide, such as a size guide or care article, for customers who want more detail. Our guide to size guides shows how helpful content can support buying decisions.

Use descriptive link text, such as "our merino jumper collection", rather than "click here".

Writing for readers and search engines

  • Lead with the answer, then explain. People want to know quickly whether the post solves their problem.
  • Use the words customers use, not internal jargon.
  • Be specific and practical. Generic advice is easy to find elsewhere.
  • Add your own photos where they help, rather than relying only on stock images.
  • Include a short FAQ section where it fits, answering related questions directly.
  • Keep posts up to date, especially when products or advice change.

If you want help with writing, our guide to website copywriting covers tone and structure, and our guide to tone of voice helps keep the blog consistent with the rest of your shop. Content that answers questions clearly also stands a better chance of being used in AI-generated search answers, as our guide to AI Overviews explains.

Keeping it going

A blog with one post from two years ago can look like an abandoned shop. Set a realistic publishing rhythm you can keep, whether that is weekly, monthly or less often. Plan topics in advance around your seasons and product launches.

Review older posts from time to time. Update facts, refresh links to current products, and merge or remove posts that no longer serve a purpose. Our content audit guide explains how.

Track how posts perform in Google Search Console and your analytics: which bring in visitors, which lead to product views and which lead to sales. Our guide to website analytics for small businesses can help you set this up.

FAQs

Does Shopify have a built-in blog?

Yes. Every Shopify store includes blogging features, and most stores start with a default blog that you can rename or replace.

Can I change my Shopify blog URL?

You can change the blog's handle, but the overall URL structure is fixed by Shopify. If you change a handle after publishing, add redirects from the old URLs.

Is a Shopify blog good for SEO?

It can be, if posts answer questions people search for and link to relevant products. A blog alone does not guarantee rankings.

How often should I post on my shop blog?

Choose a rhythm you can keep. Consistent, useful posts are more valuable than a burst of posts followed by silence.

Can I add products to Shopify blog posts?

You can link to products in any post, and many themes let you add product or collection sections to the blog post template.
