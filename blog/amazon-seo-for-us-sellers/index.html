Amazon SEO for US Sellers: How to Build Listings That Rank and Sell

Amazon SEO for US sellers means research on Amazon.com, clear titles, bullets, images and backend terms. Here is how listing optimisation works.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Amazon SEO for US sellers starts with keyword research on Amazon.com itself, then turns that research into titles, bullets, images and backend search terms.
  • Rankings on Amazon depend on relevance and on sales performance, so listing copy, images, price, stock and reviews all work together.
  • Always check Amazon's current style guides and policies for your category; an outside team can do the research and writing while you keep control of Seller Central.

Amazon SEO for US sellers is the work of making your listings appear for the searches shoppers type on Amazon.com and then persuading those shoppers to buy. It combines keyword research on the US marketplace with well-structured titles, bullet points, descriptions, images and backend search terms, all within Amazon's current rules for your category.

We are a UK-based studio offering marketplace listing SEO, and we work remotely with sellers in the USA. This guide explains how Amazon search works in practice, how to optimise each part of a listing, and what to look for if you outsource the work.

How Amazon search differs from Google

Google tries to answer a question. Amazon tries to sell a product. That difference shapes everything about how you optimise. On Amazon, two broad things decide where you appear:

  • Relevance: whether the words in your listing and backend fields match what the shopper searched for.
  • Performance: how well the listing converts when people see it, which is influenced by price, images, reviews, stock and delivery speed.

Amazon does not publish the full detail of its ranking system, so treat anyone who claims to know an exact formula with caution. What is clear is that a listing needs to be both findable and convincing. If you are still deciding whether Amazon should be a main channel at all, our comparison of selling on Amazon vs your own website sets out the trade-offs.

Amazon SEO for US sellers: start with US marketplace research

Keyword research for Amazon should be done on Amazon.com, with US data, not borrowed from Google or another marketplace. American shoppers use different words from shoppers elsewhere, and the difference matters: think of terms such as pants, sneakers, cookie or diaper, which are spelled or named differently in the UK.

Good research usually includes:

  • Search suggestions on Amazon.com as you type your main product terms.
  • The wording used in top-selling competitor listings in your category.
  • Search term reports from your own Sponsored Products campaigns, which show the real searches that led to clicks and sales.
  • Customer questions and reviews, which reveal the words buyers use to describe problems and features.

Our general keyword research guide explains the principles of grouping terms by intent, which apply on Amazon too.

Optimising each part of a listing

Each listing field has a different job. Amazon sets character limits and style rules that vary by category and change from time to time, so check the current style guide in Seller Central before you publish.

Price, stock and deliveryPerformanceCompetitive pricing, steady stock and fast delivery promises

Titles

Lead with the words that define the product. Avoid keyword stuffing, promotional phrases and capital letters throughout, which can breach Amazon's rules and put shoppers off. A clear title that a person can read in one glance usually performs better than a crammed one.

Bullet points and description

Write for a buyer comparing three tabs. Start each bullet with the benefit, then give the specific detail that proves it. Our guide to writing ecommerce product descriptions covers the same principles for your own store.

Images

Images often do more selling than words. Follow Amazon's main image requirements, then use the other slots for scale, detail, use and comparison. See our ecommerce product photography guide for briefing ideas.

Reviews, ratings and compliance

Reviews influence both conversion and visibility, but Amazon is strict about how sellers can request them. Incentivised or manipulated reviews can lead to listing or account action. Use Amazon's own tools for requesting reviews and check its current policies before trying anything else. Our article on ecommerce product reviews covers good practice for your own site.

Product claims matter too. Advertising and labelling rules, including FTC guidance on claims and endorsements, apply to what you say in listings, and some categories have extra requirements. Check current official guidance and take legal advice for regulated products such as supplements or cosmetics.

Ads and organic rankings work together

Sponsored Products and other Amazon ads are not the same as organic SEO, but they support each other. Ads put new listings in front of shoppers, generate sales history and reveal search terms worth adding to your copy. Organic rankings then reduce how much you rely on ads over time. Plan both together rather than treating them as separate projects.

Common Amazon listing mistakes

When we review listings, the same problems come up again and again. Most are quick to fix once you know to look for them:

  • Copy written for another market: UK or other non-US spelling and terms on an Amazon.com listing, which can hurt both relevance and trust.
  • Keyword lists instead of sentences: bullets crammed with search terms that no shopper wants to read.
  • Repeating words in backend fields: wasting space on terms already used in the title or bullets.
  • Weak secondary images: several near-identical photos instead of showing scale, detail, use and what is in the box.
  • Ignoring customer questions: the same question asked repeatedly is a sign the listing is missing information.
  • Set and forget: listings never updated after launch, even as competitors, search terms and seasons change.

A simple listing review routine

Listings improve most when they are reviewed on a schedule rather than only when sales drop. A practical routine for a small or mid-sized range looks like this:

  1. Monthly: check ad search term reports for new phrases, and scan new reviews and questions for recurring themes.
  2. Quarterly: compare your top listings with the leading competitors on title clarity, images and price, and refresh copy where you fall behind.
  3. Before peak seasons: confirm stock levels, update seasonal terms and make sure images and A+ Content are current.
  4. After any policy change: recheck the category style guide in Seller Central and fix anything that no longer complies.

Keep a simple change log so you can link any shift in sales or ranking to what was changed and when.

Should you outsource Amazon SEO?

Many sellers handle listings themselves at first, then bring in help as the range grows or sales plateau. Outsourcing makes sense when:

  • You have many listings and no time to research and rewrite them properly.
  • Listings were written quickly at launch and have never been revisited.
  • You are launching new products and want them right from day one.
  • You also sell on your own store or other marketplaces and want consistent product content everywhere.

Whoever you hire, keep Seller Central in your name, grant user permissions rather than sharing your login, and agree which changes need your approval. Ask how they research US search terms, how they check Amazon's style rules for your category, and how they will report progress. FAQs

What is Amazon SEO for US sellers?

It is the process of researching how shoppers search on Amazon.com and optimising your titles, bullets, descriptions, images and backend terms so listings appear for those searches and convert.

Is Amazon SEO different from Google SEO?

Yes. Amazon focuses on products and buying behaviour, so sales performance, price, stock and reviews matter alongside the words in your listing.

Can a UK agency optimise listings for Amazon.com?

Yes, provided research is done on the US marketplace and copy uses US spelling and terms. You keep Seller Central in your name and grant user access.

How long does Amazon listing optimisation take to work?

Some changes show quickly, but SEO results typically take 3 to 6 months, depending on competition, reviews, stock and advertising.

Do Amazon ads help organic rankings?

They can support them by generating sales history and showing real search terms, but they do not replace a well-optimised listing.

Can I pay for Amazon SEO in US dollars?

Our published prices are in pounds sterling. Other currencies, including US dollars, are quoted on request.
