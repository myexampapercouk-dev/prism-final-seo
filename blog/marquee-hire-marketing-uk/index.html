Marquee Hire Marketing in the UK: How to Win More Event Enquiries

Marquee hire marketing in the UK works when your website answers sizing and pricing questions, local search covers your area and enquiries are followed up fast.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Marquee hire is a considered, high-value purchase, so your website must answer sizing, layout, site and timing questions before people will enquire.
  • Local SEO should reflect the area you actually cover, with service area pages that are genuinely useful rather than copied town lists.
  • Fast, organised follow-up on enquiries and site visits often matters as much as the marketing that produced the enquiry.

Marquee hire marketing in the UK is about being visible when people start planning an outdoor event, then giving them enough confidence to send an enquiry and book a site visit. The marquee companies that stay booked usually have a website full of real photos and practical detail, strong local search coverage for the area they serve, a simple enquiry form and quick, organised follow-up.

This guide is written for marquee, tipi, stretch tent and event structure hire businesses, from small family firms to companies running several crews in peak season. It covers the website, local SEO, content, paid media, reviews and enquiry handling. We are a studio that builds websites and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so we focus on what helps you win more of the right enquiries.

How people choose a marquee hire company

Booking a marquee is rarely an impulse decision. A couple planning a garden wedding, a family organising a milestone birthday, a school planning a summer fair or an events team running a corporate day will usually research for weeks or months. They search for terms like "marquee hire near me", "wedding marquee hire Kent" or "how big a marquee for 100 guests", look at photos, compare two or three companies and then send enquiries.

Their biggest worries tend to be practical. Will it fit the space? What happens if it rains or is windy? What is included? How early do they need to book? A website that answers those questions clearly will win more enquiries than one that simply says "get in touch for a quote".

Your audiences typically include:

  • Couples planning weddings at home, on farmland or at venues without enough indoor space.
  • Private households celebrating birthdays, anniversaries and garden parties.
  • Venues and estates that need seasonal structures or extra capacity.
  • Corporate and event organisers running hospitality, product launches and staff days.
  • Community groups, schools and councils organising fêtes, festivals and shows.

Marquee hire marketing UK: website foundations

Your website does most of the selling before anyone speaks to you. It needs to be quick on a phone, look good, and lead people towards an enquiry or a site visit without making them hunt.

Show your structures properly

Give each type of structure its own page: traditional pole marquees, frame marquees, tipis, stretch tents, pagodas or whatever you offer. Each page should explain what it suits, typical guest capacities for seated dinners and standing receptions, the ground it can go on and the features available. Use real photos of your own equipment at real events, with permission from the hosts, rather than stock images.

Help people with sizing and layout

A simple sizing guide is one of the most useful pages a marquee company can publish. Explain how capacity changes with round tables, long banquet tables, a dance floor, a bar and a band, and show a few example layouts. Make it clear that final sizing is confirmed at a site visit. This kind of page answers a common search and builds confidence at the same time.

Be open about what is included

Even if you do not publish full prices, explain what a hire usually includes: delivery, erection, flooring options, lighting, heating, furniture, toilets, power and dismantling. If you offer packages, describe them in plain language. Our guide to website trust signals covers other ways to reassure visitors, such as accreditations you genuinely hold, insurance information and clear contact details.

Make enquiring easy

Your enquiry form should ask for the essentials: event date, location or postcode, event type, approximate guest numbers and the best way to contact the person. Too many fields will put people off; too few will leave you chasing information. Our guide to website contact forms explains how to strike the balance.

Local SEO for marquee hire

Most marquee companies serve a region rather than a single town, which makes local SEO slightly different from a shop with a front door. Our local SEO guide explains the fundamentals; these points apply to marquee hire in particular.

Set up your Google Business Profile as a service area business if customers do not visit your yard, and list the counties or towns you genuinely cover. Add photos from real events through the season and keep your categories accurate.

Area pages can work well, but only when each one is genuinely useful. A page about marquee hire in a particular county might mention the types of venue you often work at, the ground conditions you tend to deal with, access considerations for rural sites and any local events you supply. Copying the same text and swapping the town name rarely helps and can make the whole site look thin.

Reviews carry a lot of weight for a purchase this size. Ask hosts for a review a few days after the event, once the structure is down and the invoice is settled, with a direct link to make it easy.

Content that answers planning questions

People planning an outdoor event have lots of questions, and answering them builds visibility and trust. Useful topics include:

  • How far in advance to book a marquee for a summer wedding.
  • What size marquee you need for different guest numbers.
  • Whether a marquee can go on a slope, gravel or hard standing.
  • Heating and lighting options for spring, autumn and winter events.
  • Planning permission and landowner permission questions, explained in general terms with a note to check current local guidance.
  • What happens on the day, from delivery to dismantling.

Planning and safety topics should stay general. Rules for temporary structures can vary by location and event type, so encourage people to check current guidance with their local council and venue, and explain what you handle as the supplier.

Paid media for marquee hire companies

Paid search can bring in enquiries while organic visibility builds, especially in the months when couples and organisers are booking for the following season. The key is tight targeting by area and careful keyword choice. Our guide to Google Ads for small businesses covers budgets and setup, and our article on negative keywords explains how to filter out searches for cheap gazebos, camping tents and party tent purchases.

Social ads on Facebook and Instagram suit the visual nature of the business. Carousel ads showing a structure from bare field to finished dinner layout can work well, targeted to people within your service area. Make sure conversion tracking records form submissions and calls, not just clicks, so you can see which campaigns produce real enquiries. Our paid media service can manage search and social campaigns together.

Wedding fairs, venues and partnerships

Many marquee companies win work through relationships with planners, caterers, venues and other suppliers. Your website can support that. A page for event professionals that explains how you work with planners, your lead times and how to get technical details helps partners recommend you. Our guide to wedding business marketing covers the wider wedding market, and our article on event planner website design shows what your partners are likely to be looking for.

Handling enquiries and site visits

Marketing only works if enquiries are handled well. In peak booking periods, a slow response can mean losing a date to a competitor. A few practical habits help:

  • Acknowledge every enquiry quickly, even if a full quote takes longer.
  • Offer site visit slots online or in your first reply.
  • Send quotes in a consistent, clear format with what is included and what is optional.
  • Follow up politely if you do not hear back, then stop.
  • Record where each enquiry came from so you know which channels work.

With consent, past hosts can also join a simple email list for seasonal reminders and early booking for the next year, following PECR rules on marketing emails.

Measuring what works

Track enquiries by source, site visits booked, quotes sent, bookings won and average booking value. Compare by season rather than by month, as demand is strongly seasonal. If one area or event type consistently converts better, put more of your content and ad budget behind it.

FAQs

What is the best marquee hire marketing UK companies can start with?

Start with a website that shows your structures clearly, a sizing guide, a simple enquiry form and a complete Google Business Profile for your service area. These foundations make every other channel more effective.

Should a marquee company publish prices online?

That is your decision. Many companies prefer to quote after a site visit, but explaining what a hire includes and what affects the price helps people enquire with realistic expectations.

Do area pages help marquee hire SEO?

They can, if each page is genuinely useful and specific to the area. Pages that copy the same text with a different town name rarely help and can weaken the overall site.

When do people book marquees for summer events?

Many weddings and larger events are booked well in advance, often in the autumn and winter before. That is why it helps to have your website and campaigns ready before the main booking period.

Are Google Ads worth it for marquee hire?

They can be, especially while organic visibility builds. Use tight location targeting, negative keywords for unrelated products and conversion tracking on enquiries so you only pay for useful searches.

How long does marquee hire SEO take to work?

Some improvements in local results can show within a few months, but meaningful gains typically take three to six months, depending on competition in your area and how much useful content you publish.
