Shopify for UK Supplement Brands

A guide for UK supplement brands on Shopify: product pages, health claims caution, labelling information, subscriptions, payments, ads and SEO.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Food supplements are regulated in the UK, and health claims in particular are tightly controlled, so product copy needs careful checking.
  • Shopify can run a supplement store well, but some payment providers and ad platforms apply extra restrictions to this category.
  • Clear ingredient, dosage and warning information on every product page builds trust and supports compliance.

Supplements are a popular category for direct-to-consumer brands. Products are small, light and consumable, which suits online selling and repeat purchases. They are also one of the more regulated categories in UK ecommerce. What you can say about a product, what must appear on the label and how you advertise are all subject to rules, and getting them wrong can lead to regulatory action, removed adverts or suspended payment accounts.

This guide looks at how UK supplement brands can build a Shopify store that presents products clearly and approaches compliance with appropriate caution. It is general information, not legal or regulatory advice. Rules change and depend on your specific products, so take professional advice and check current guidance from the relevant regulators.

Understand the regulatory landscape first

Before thinking about design, it helps to understand the broad areas of regulation that can affect a supplement website. In the UK these may include, among others:

  • Food supplement rules, covering matters such as which vitamins and minerals may be used, labelling requirements and notification or registration obligations. Guidance is available from the Food Standards Agency and, in Scotland, Food Standards Scotland.
  • Nutrition and health claims rules, which generally mean only authorised claims may be made, using permitted wording and conditions. Claims are a frequent area of difficulty for supplement brands.
  • Medicines rules. A product presented as treating, preventing or curing a disease, or with certain ingredients, may be treated as a medicine. The Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) oversees this boundary.
  • Novel food rules, which can apply to some ingredients not widely consumed before a particular date.
  • Advertising rules, enforced by the Advertising Standards Authority, which apply to marketing claims on your own website as well as in paid adverts.

Your local authority trading standards or food team may also be relevant. We cannot tell you whether a specific product or claim is permitted. That is a question for a regulatory specialist. What we can do is make sure the website is structured so that approved information is presented clearly and consistently.

Be very careful with health claims

Claims are where many supplement websites run into trouble. A product page, a blog post, a customer review you have chosen to feature, a social post embedded on the site or even a product name can all be treated as making a claim. Points to consider include:

  • Only using claims you have confirmed are authorised for the ingredient and amount in your product, in wording that matches the permitted conditions.
  • Avoiding language that implies treating, curing or preventing any disease or medical condition.
  • Being cautious about before and after imagery, testimonials and influencer content that make implied claims.
  • Reviewing blog content as well as product pages, since educational content about a condition next to a product can be read together.
  • Keeping a record of where each claim on the site came from and when it was checked.

When in doubt, leave it out and take advice. A plainer product page is better than one that creates regulatory risk.

Product pages: information first

A good supplement product page gives shoppers the information they need to make an informed decision. Depending on your products and the rules that apply, that may include:

  • A clear product name and what the product is.
  • The full ingredients list and the amounts of active ingredients per serving.
  • Recommended daily dose and how to take it.
  • Required warning statements, such as not exceeding the recommended dose and keeping out of reach of children, as applicable.
  • Allergen information.
  • Suitability notes, such as whether the product is vegan or suitable during pregnancy, only where you have confirmed these are accurate and appropriate to state.
  • Pack size, number of servings and storage instructions.

Shopify metafields are well suited to this. Instead of typing everything into one description box, you can create structured fields for ingredients, dosage, warnings and allergens, and display them in a consistent layout on every product page. This reduces the chance of a product missing required information and makes updates easier when a formulation or label changes. Make sure the website information matches the physical label.

Images of the label itself can supplement the text, but text versions are better for accessibility and search engines, so do not rely on images alone. Our guide to website accessibility explains why.

Catalogue structure

Supplement shoppers browse in a few different ways: by product type, such as capsules, powders or gummies; by ingredient, such as a particular vitamin; or by lifestyle and audience. Be careful with collections named after health benefits or conditions, since a collection title can itself act as a claim. Neutral groupings by format, ingredient or range are generally lower risk. Our guide to ecommerce category page design covers layout and filtering.

Payments and platform policies

Shopify allows supplements to be sold, but supplements are an area where platforms and payment providers may apply additional policies. Some ingredients or product types may be restricted, and payment providers can review businesses in categories they consider higher risk. Before launching, read the current acceptable use and restricted business policies for Shopify and any payment provider you plan to use, and make sure your products and claims fall within them. Having a backup payment option in mind can also be prudent.

Subscriptions and repeat orders

Many supplements are taken daily, so subscriptions are a natural fit. Shopify supports subscriptions through compatible apps. Good practice includes clear information on price, frequency and how to cancel, along with easy self-service controls to pause, skip or cancel. UK rules around subscription contracts have been changing, so check current requirements. Our ecommerce subscriptions guide covers the main considerations, and our article on transactional emails explains the messages customers should receive.

Advertising supplements

Paid advertising for supplements can be more restricted than for many product categories. Google and social platforms have their own policies on health-related products and claims, and some ingredients may be prohibited from advertising altogether. Adverts must also comply with UK advertising rules. Check each platform's current policies before planning campaigns, and expect adverts to be reviewed. Our guides to Google Shopping ads and Meta ads explain how those channels work in general.

Trust and transparency

Supplement shoppers often look for signs that a brand is legitimate and careful. Helpful trust signals include clear company details and contact information, a transparent returns and refunds policy, honest and accurate product information, and genuine customer reviews that you moderate for claims. Never invent reviews, endorsements or certifications. If you mention testing or quality standards, make sure you can substantiate them. Our guides to website trust signals and product reviews cover this.

SEO without overclaiming

Search can be a valuable channel for supplement brands, but content needs the same care as product pages. Some practical points:

  • Target product and ingredient searches with clear, factual collection and product pages.
  • Write educational content about ingredients and how to use products, keeping it factual and avoiding disease-related claims.
  • Consider having health-related content reviewed by someone suitably qualified.
  • Add product structured data so search engines understand price and availability.

Search engines tend to scrutinise health-related content for expertise and trustworthiness, so accurate, well-sourced and carefully reviewed content matters. Our Shopify SEO guide and content strategy guide explain the wider approach.

FAQs

Can I sell supplements on Shopify in the UK?

Shopify generally allows supplements, but some products and ingredients may be restricted. Check Shopify's and your payment provider's current policies before launching.

What health claims can I make about supplements?

In the UK, health claims are generally limited to authorised claims used under specific conditions. Check current guidance and take regulatory advice before publishing claims.

Do customer reviews count as claims?

Reviews you choose to display can be treated as part of your marketing. Moderate them carefully and avoid featuring reviews that make medical claims.

What information should a supplement product page include?

Typically ingredients, amounts per serving, dosage, warnings, allergens and pack size, matching the physical label. Check current labelling requirements.

Can I advertise supplements on Google and social media?

Often yes, but policies on health products are strict and some ingredients may be prohibited. Check each platform's current policies and UK advertising rules.
