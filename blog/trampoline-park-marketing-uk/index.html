Trampoline Park Marketing in the UK: How to Fill Sessions and Parties

Trampoline park marketing in the UK works best when local search, quick online booking, party pages and targeted ads all focus on your quieter sessions.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most trampoline park bookings start with a parent searching locally, so Google Business Profile, reviews and a fast mobile booking journey matter most.
  • Parties, toddler sessions, school groups and fitness classes each need their own page and their own marketing, because they fill different times of the week.
  • Paid social and search work best when aimed at specific sessions and audiences, with clear offer terms and conversion tracking on completed bookings.

Trampoline park marketing in the UK comes down to being the obvious choice when parents search for something to do nearby, then letting them book a session or a party in a couple of minutes on their phone. Busy parks tend to combine strong local search visibility, a clear page for every session type, an easy booking flow, lots of recent reviews and targeted ads that fill weekday and term-time slots.

This guide is for independent trampoline parks and small groups of sites. It covers the website, booking, local SEO, social media, paid campaigns, email and measurement. We are a studio that builds websites and runs SEO and paid media, so we focus on the digital side of filling sessions rather than operations on the floor.

How families choose a trampoline park

Most visits start with a parent or carer searching for things like "trampoline park near me", "soft play and trampolines open today" or "kids party venues in Nottingham". They look at the map results, check the rating and photos, see whether there is a slot today or this weekend and look at the price. If the booking page is slow, confusing or asks them to create an account before showing times, many will simply try the next option.

Several different groups use trampoline parks, and each one searches and decides in its own way:

  • Families looking for weekend and school holiday activities, often booking at short notice.
  • Parents of toddlers wanting quieter, younger sessions during the week.
  • Party bookers planning a child's birthday several weeks ahead, comparing packages carefully.
  • Teenagers booking with friends, often influenced by social media.
  • Schools, clubs and youth groups booking group sessions in term time.
  • Adults attending fitness classes or adult-only evening sessions, where offered.

Trampoline park marketing UK: website foundations

Your website is where every ad, social post and search result sends people, so it needs to be quick on a phone and focused on booking. Put session times and a booking button at the top of the homepage, and keep navigation simple: Book, Parties, Sessions, Visit and Safety.

Give every session type its own page

Open jump, toddler sessions, SEN or sensory-friendly sessions, dodgeball, fitness classes and adult nights all serve different people. A single page listing everything makes it hard to rank for specific searches and hard for parents to find what they need. Each session page should include:

  • Who the session is for, including age and height guidance.
  • Duration, typical times and how busy it usually is.
  • What to bring, such as grip socks, and whether they are sold on site.
  • Rules for supervision and whether adults need to jump or watch.
  • A booking button that opens that specific session.

Make booking effortless

Most parks use a dedicated booking platform. Aim to show live availability without forcing an account, take payment securely, and collect any waivers or declarations online before arrival so check-in is quick. Our guide to online booking systems for websites explains how to embed booking while keeping visitors on your domain. Confirmation emails should explain arrival times, parking and what to wear.

Be clear about safety

Parents want reassurance before booking. A dedicated safety page that explains your rules, supervision, briefings, age zones and first aid arrangements helps. Safety standards and guidance for trampoline parks are set outside your website, so check current industry and government guidance, follow it, and describe what you do accurately rather than making broad claims.

Party pages that sell

Birthday parties are often among the highest-value bookings and they help fill weekend slots well in advance. Give parties their own section rather than a single paragraph, with:

  • Each package explained in plain language, including jump time, party room time, food and party bags if offered.
  • Minimum and maximum guest numbers and age guidance.
  • How deposits, guest number changes and cancellations work.
  • Real photos of the party room, shared with permission.
  • An online booking or enquiry form for the exact package.

Gift vouchers are another strong product, especially around Christmas and birthdays. Selling them through your website extends your reach to relatives who may never visit themselves. Our guide to selling gift cards and vouchers online covers the setup.

Local SEO for trampoline parks

Local search is where much of your traffic will begin. Our local SEO guide covers the fundamentals, and these points apply to trampoline parks in particular.

Complete your Google Business Profile carefully: accurate categories, opening hours including holiday hours, photos of the arena, party rooms and café, and regular updates for new sessions and holiday timetables. Our Google Business Profile guide walks through each section.

Reviews influence parents strongly. Ask at the right moment, such as in a follow-up email the day after a visit or party, with a direct link. Reply to reviews calmly and specifically, especially critical ones about cleanliness, queues or safety. Our guide on how to respond to Google reviews covers tone and wording. Never offer incentives for reviews, as that breaks platform rules.

On your website, a visit page with your address, parking, public transport, accessibility and nearby landmarks helps both visitors and search engines. If you run several sites, give each location its own page with its own timetable.

Social media and paid campaigns

Trampoline parks are energetic and visual, which suits short video on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok. Content that works well includes new features, holiday timetables, party room set-ups, staff tips and short clips from sessions, with clear consent from parents for any child who is identifiable. Under UK GDPR, photos and video of identifiable people are personal data, so record consent and make it easy to withdraw.

Paid social is often the quickest way to fill specific quiet slots, such as weekday toddler mornings or term-time after-school sessions. Target parents within a sensible drive time and promote one clear session or offer per ad. Our article on Meta ads costs in the UK explains how budgets work. Google Search ads can capture party searches and "near me" terms while organic visibility builds. Our paid media service can run both together, with tracking set up on completed bookings rather than just clicks.

Any offers should clearly state their terms, such as which sessions and dates they apply to, in line with the CAP Code that governs UK advertising. Avoid claims you cannot back up, such as being the biggest or safest park in a region.

Email and repeat visits

Families who enjoyed a visit are your most likely repeat customers. With marketing consent captured at booking, separate from booking confirmations as PECR requires, you can send a short welcome message, holiday timetables, party reminders ahead of birthdays if customers chose to share them, and news of new sessions. Our guide to building an email list in the UK explains how to grow your list without annoying people.

Memberships or multi-visit passes, if you offer them, can also be promoted by email to families who visit often.

Measuring what works

Focus on a small set of figures: online booking conversion rate, bookings by source, occupancy by session and day, party bookings per month, voucher sales and review volume. If a particular session regularly runs below capacity, focus offers, ads and email on that slot rather than discounting the whole week.

FAQs

What is the best trampoline park marketing UK operators can start with?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile, a fast mobile booking journey and separate pages for parties and each session type. These foundations influence most bookings and make any later advertising more effective.

How can a trampoline park fill weekday sessions?

Create sessions with a clear audience, such as toddler mornings, school groups or adult fitness, give each its own page and promote them with targeted social ads and email rather than general discounts.

Should parties have their own pages?

Yes. Party bookers compare packages carefully, so dedicated pages with clear inclusions, guest numbers, deposits and photos help them decide and book.

Can we post photos of children on social media?

Only with clear consent from a parent or guardian. Photos of identifiable children are personal data under UK GDPR, so record consent and remove images promptly if asked.

Is TikTok worth it for a trampoline park?

It can be, especially for reaching teenagers and younger parents. Short, energetic clips of new features and events tend to work best, provided anyone identifiable has given consent.

How long does trampoline park SEO take?

Improvements in local map results can show within a few months, but meaningful gains typically take three to six months, depending on competition nearby and how many reviews you collect.
