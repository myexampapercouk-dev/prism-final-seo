Why Malmo needs its own Google Ads plan

Malmo is Sweden's third-largest city and the hub of Skane. It is linked to Copenhagen by the Oresund Bridge, so many local firms serve customers on both sides of the strait. The economy includes logistics, food production, retail, life sciences, technology start-ups, construction and a busy services sector, with Lund's university and research park nearby.

That geography affects paid search in practical ways:

  • Location targeting needs care. A Malmo clinic may only want people in Malmo, Lund, Helsingborg and nearby towns, while a logistics or software firm may want buyers across Sweden, Denmark and the wider Nordics.
  • Language varies. Most searches are in Swedish, but English is common for technology, recruitment and business services, and Danish matters if you sell across the bridge.
  • Currency and pricing on landing pages should match the market you target, with kronor shown for Swedish buyers.

Tracking first, then budget

Before we raise spend, we make sure tracking is accurate. That means conversion tracking for forms, calls, bookings and purchases, linked to Google Analytics, with consent handled in line with your cookie banner. Privacy and data rules apply in Sweden and the EU, so we keep set-up notes general and recommend you check official guidance or your own adviser on consent wording.

Without reliable tracking, automated bidding has nothing useful to learn from, and you cannot tell which keywords are producing customers.

How we run a remote Google Ads account

  1. Audit or set-up: we review any existing account, or build a new one with a clear structure.
  2. Keyword and competitor research: we map the searches that show buying intent in Malmo and Skane, plus wider Sweden where relevant.
  3. Landing pages: we check each campaign sends people to a page that answers their search, loads quickly and has a clear call to action.
  4. Launch: we start with controlled budgets, strict negative keywords and close monitoring.
  5. Optimisation: weekly checks on search terms, bids, ads and budgets, with tests run one at a time.
  6. Reporting: a monthly report and call covering spend, conversions, cost per lead or return on ad spend, and next steps.

Paid results typically settle within three to six months, as the account gathers data and weak keywords are cut.

Choosing a Google Ads agency in Malmo

Whichever agency you pick, ask:

  • Will the Google Ads account be in my name, and do I keep it if we part ways?
  • How is conversion tracking set up, and how do you check it?
  • Who writes the Swedish ad copy?
  • How often do you review search terms and add negative keywords?
  • What will the monthly report show?

Be cautious of any agency that guarantees a fixed number of leads or keeps the ad account in its own name.

Common Google Ads mistakes we see in Swedish accounts

When we review an existing account, the same problems come up again and again. Most are simple to fix, and fixing them often frees budget for the searches that matter.

  • Broad keywords without negatives. Broad match can find useful new searches, but without a regularly updated negative keyword list it also spends money on job seekers, students, competitors' brand names and unrelated topics.
  • Wrong location settings. By default, Google can show ads to people who are interested in a location as well as people who are in it. For a local Malmo service that only works in Skane, that can waste a large share of the budget.
  • One landing page for everything. Sending every ad to the home page makes visitors hunt for the answer. A page for each main service or product group usually converts better and improves ad quality.
  • Tracking page views instead of results. Counting a visit to a contact page as a conversion tells the bidding system the wrong thing. We track submitted forms, calls of a useful length, bookings and purchases.
  • Mixing languages in one ad group. Swedish and English searches should sit in separate campaigns or ad groups, each with ads and landing pages in the matching language.
  • Too many changes at once. Changing bids, budgets, ads and keywords in the same week makes it impossible to see what worked. We change one thing at a time and give it enough data to judge.
