Vineyard Marketing in the UK: How to Sell More Wine, Tours and Tastings

Vineyard marketing in the UK works when your website sells bottles and tours, local search brings visitors, and email turns one visit into years of orders.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • A UK vineyard website has three jobs at once: selling bottles online, booking tours and tastings, and taking enquiries for events.
  • Local search and a strong Google Business Profile bring in day visitors, while content about your site, grapes and wines builds trust with buyers further away.
  • Email and wine clubs turn a single cellar door visit into repeat orders, provided marketing consent is captured properly and age checks are in place.

Vineyard marketing in the UK is about turning interest in English and Welsh wine into three kinds of revenue: bottles sold online, visits booked for tours and tastings, and repeat orders from people who have already tasted your wines. The vineyards that do this well usually have a website that sells and books in one place, solid local search visibility, a reason for people to sign up to email, and content that tells the story of their land and their wines.

This guide is written for small and mid-sized vineyards and wineries, from estates with a cellar door shop to newer plantings preparing their first releases. It covers the website, ecommerce, local SEO, content, paid media and email, with a practical view of what tends to matter most. We are a studio that builds websites and online shops and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so the focus is on the digital side rather than viticulture.

Why vineyard marketing is different

A vineyard is part producer, part retailer and part visitor attraction. Most other businesses have one main conversion; a vineyard often has four or five. Someone might discover you on a weekend walk, book a tour a month later, buy a mixed case at the cellar door, join your wine club and then order online at Christmas. Each step needs a clear path on your website and a reason to take the next one.

Seasonality matters too. Visitor numbers usually peak in the warmer months and around harvest, while online wine sales often lean towards the run-up to Christmas and other gifting moments. Good marketing evens out those peaks by moving visitors into online buyers and club members.

The audiences you serve typically include:

  • Day visitors and tourists looking for something to do nearby, often searching on the day or the week before.
  • Wine enthusiasts who follow English and Welsh wine and want to know about grape varieties, vintages and methods.
  • Gift buyers looking for a bottle, a case or a tour voucher as a present.
  • Couples and event planners enquiring about weddings, private tastings and corporate days, if you host them.
  • Trade buyers such as local restaurants, pubs and independent shops.

Vineyard marketing UK: website foundations

Your website is where search, social, ads and printed materials all send people, so it needs to handle every one of those audiences without becoming cluttered. A clear top-level structure helps: Visit, Shop, Our Wines, Events and About. Each section then gets the detail it needs.

An online shop that suits wine

Selling wine online has a few practical requirements beyond a standard shop. You will want mixed case options, clear bottle sizes and vintages, tasting notes, food pairing suggestions and delivery information that explains couriers, breakages and who needs to be present to receive an order. Our guide to food and drink ecommerce in the UK covers the wider points. Cases of wine are heavy and fragile, so price delivery carefully and test your packaging before launch.

Alcohol sales come with legal responsibilities, including licensing and age verification at checkout and on delivery. The detail depends on your licence and how you sell, so check current guidance from your licensing authority and the relevant government pages before launching, and keep your checkout wording in line with it. We build the checkout steps; the licensing decisions stay with you.

Booking tours and tastings

Tours and tastings should be bookable online, with live availability, clear timings, what is included, how long it lasts, accessibility notes, parking and whether children or dogs are welcome. Visitors are far more likely to commit when they can see a date and pay straight away. Our guide to online booking systems for websites explains the options and how to keep the experience on your own domain.

Wine club and subscriptions

A wine club gives loyal customers a reason to keep ordering and gives you predictable revenue. Typical formats include quarterly mixed cases, member pricing in the shop and early access to new releases. The mechanics of recurring orders are covered in our guide to ecommerce subscriptions in the UK. Keep sign-up simple, explain exactly what members receive and how to pause or cancel.

Individual wine pages are often overlooked. A single shop grid with tiny descriptions makes it hard to rank and hard for buyers to choose. Give each wine room to breathe, with a good bottle photo, honest tasting notes and suggestions for when to drink it.

Local SEO for vineyards and wineries

Many visits start with searches such as "vineyard tours near me", "wine tasting in Sussex" or "things to do in the Cotswolds this weekend". Winning those searches depends heavily on your Google Business Profile and on clear, location-specific content.

Choose accurate categories, add opening hours for the shop and tours separately if they differ, and upload photos of the vines through the seasons, the tasting room and the view. Keep hours updated for bank holidays and harvest closures. Our Google Business Profile guide walks through each setting.

Reviews matter a great deal for visitor attractions. A short note after each tour, with a direct review link, gives happy visitors an easy way to share their experience. Never offer incentives in exchange for reviews, as that breaks platform rules.

On your website, a dedicated visit page that names your village, nearest town, county and wine region helps search engines understand where you are. If you sit on a wine trail or near other attractions, explain how a day out might fit together, linking only to pages you control.

Content that tells your story

Wine buyers enjoy learning, and well-written content helps both search visibility and conversion. Ideas that work well for vineyards include:

  • A guide to the grape varieties you grow and why they suit your site.
  • A plain-English explanation of how your sparkling or still wines are made.
  • A harvest diary, published each autumn with photos.
  • Food pairing ideas for each wine, ideally using seasonal British ingredients.
  • A guide to visiting, including the best times of year to come.

Content also gives journalists and local publications something to reference. If you want a more structured approach to stories and coverage, our digital PR and content service can plan and write it alongside your other marketing.

When writing about sustainability or awards, only state what you can evidence. If you hold a certification or have won a competition, name it accurately and link to your own page about it rather than making broad claims.

Paid media for vineyards

Advertising alcohol has extra rules in the UK. The CAP Code includes specific sections on alcohol, and social platforms have their own policies and age targeting requirements. Before running any ads, check current guidance and platform policies, and keep messaging focused on taste, place and experience rather than effects of drinking.

Within those rules, paid media can work well for:

  • Search ads for tour and tasting searches in your area, especially when you are still building organic visibility.
  • Social ads to adults within driving distance, promoting seasonal events, new releases and gift vouchers.
  • Shopping campaigns for online wine sales, where platform policies and your licence allow.

Track completed bookings and orders as conversions, not just clicks, so you can see what actually drives revenue. Our SEO and GEO service often works alongside paid campaigns so that organic visibility gradually takes over from ad spend for core searches.

Email: turning visitors into regular buyers

Your email list is likely to be one of the most valuable things your vineyard owns. Every tour booking, cellar door sale and online order is a chance to invite people to join, with clear consent captured separately from transactional messages, as PECR requires for most marketing emails to individuals.

A simple programme might include a welcome series about your vineyard and wines, a note when new vintages are released, an invitation to events, and gift reminders ahead of Christmas, Father's Day and other occasions. Our guide to ecommerce email marketing covers automations and segmentation in more depth.

Segment where you can. Visitors who came for a tour but have never ordered online need different messages from wine club members, and trade contacts should be on a separate list entirely.

Weddings and private events

If your vineyard hosts weddings, celebrations or corporate days, treat this as its own marketing channel. Event enquiries are high value and slow to decide, so they need dedicated pages with real photos, capacities, seasons and a short enquiry form. Our guide to wedding business marketing covers the wider approach, including how couples research venues.

Measuring what works

Keep measurement simple and tied to revenue. Useful figures include online orders and average order value, tour bookings by source, email sign-ups per month, wine club joins and cancellations, and event enquiries. Compare months year on year rather than month to month, because seasonality is strong.

FAQs

What is the best vineyard marketing UK producers can start with?

Start with a website that sells wine and books tours in one place, a complete Google Business Profile and a way to capture email sign-ups. These three foundations support every other channel and usually deliver more than early advertising spend.

Can a small vineyard sell wine online?

Yes, provided you have the right licence and processes for alcohol sales, including age verification. Check current guidance with your licensing authority before launching, then set up a shop with mixed cases, clear delivery information and secure payment.

Should each wine have its own product page?

Yes. Separate pages let you describe grape varieties, vintage, style and food pairings properly, which helps buyers choose and gives search engines more useful content to rank.

Is a wine club worth setting up?

For many vineyards it is, because it creates repeat revenue and keeps customers engaged between visits. Keep the offer simple, explain exactly what members receive and make pausing or cancelling straightforward.

Can vineyards advertise on social media?

Usually, but alcohol advertising has specific rules in the CAP Code and on each platform, including age targeting. Check current guidance and platform policies before launching any campaign.

How long does vineyard SEO take to work?

Local visibility for tours and tastings can improve within a few months, but meaningful gains typically take three to six months, depending on competition in your region and how much useful content you publish.
