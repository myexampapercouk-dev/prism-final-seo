Shopify Agency for Spanish Brands: Remote Builds and Growth

Looking for a Shopify agency in Spain? What a Spanish Shopify build should include, languages, payments, timelines, pricing and how a remote UK studio works.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Shopify store for a Spanish brand should be built for mobile shoppers, local payment habits, Spanish and English content, and selling across Europe if that is the plan.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Spain; we design, build and migrate Shopify stores remotely, writing English copy and working with your team or a translator on Spanish.
  • Ecommerce builds are £1,500 to £3,500 and typically take five to twelve weeks, quoted in pounds with euro quotes on request.

A Shopify agency for a Spanish brand should give you a fast, mobile-friendly store that makes buying easy for customers in Spain and, if you want, across Europe. That means a clear design, well-organised products, the payment and delivery options shoppers expect, Spanish and English content where needed, and solid foundations for search and advertising.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs, builds and improves Shopify stores remotely for brands across Europe. We have no office in Spain. Our website and ecommerce development service runs by video call and shared documents, one hour behind mainland Spain, from planning through to launch and handover.

What Spanish brands need from a Shopify store

Spain has a strong ecommerce scene across fashion, footwear, food and wine, olive oil, cosmetics, homeware and lifestyle brands, many based in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia and Andalusia. Whatever the sector, most stores need the same things done well:

  • Mobile-first design. A large share of shoppers browse and buy on their phones.
  • Clear product pages. Good photos, sizes, delivery times and returns information close to the buy button.
  • Familiar payments. Card payments plus the wallets and methods Spanish shoppers trust.
  • The right languages. Spanish for the home market, English for international buyers, and possibly Catalan or other languages depending on your audience.
  • Room to grow. A structure that makes it easy to add European markets later.

Payments, delivery and returns for Spanish shoppers

Checkout is where many stores lose sales. Spanish shoppers expect familiar ways to pay, clear delivery costs before they reach the final step and simple returns. Shopify supports card payments, digital wallets and a range of local and buy-now-pay-later options depending on your payment provider and country settings, so the choice should be made around what your customers use rather than what is easiest to switch on.

  • Payment methods. Offer cards and wallets as a minimum, then add local methods your customers ask for. Check which options your payment provider supports for Spain.
  • Delivery promise. Show realistic delivery times for the peninsula and islands on product pages and in the cart.
  • Free delivery thresholds. If you offer them, make the threshold visible so shoppers can add an item to qualify.
  • Returns. A clear returns page in Spanish and English reduces questions and builds confidence before purchase.
  • Order emails. Confirmation, shipping and delivery emails should be translated and branded, not left in default wording.

We set these up in Shopify during the build and test the full checkout on mobile before launch, including discount codes, shipping zones and order notifications.

Product content and photography

Strong product pages sell more and rank better. Each product should have clear photos from several angles, a description written for your customers rather than copied from a supplier, accurate sizes or specifications and the key details people need to decide. For fashion and footwear brands, size guides that reflect European sizing reduce returns. For food, wine and cosmetics, ingredients, origin and usage information matter.

If you sell in Spanish and English, plan product content in both languages from the start. It is far easier to translate a tidy, consistent product catalogue than to fix hundreds of inconsistent descriptions later.

Selling across Europe from Spain

Many Spanish brands want to sell to France, Portugal, Italy, Germany and beyond. Shopify Markets lets one store handle several countries, currencies and languages. Our guide to Shopify Markets for Europe covers how to set it up, including local pricing and domains.

Each new language needs proper translation, not machine output left unchecked. Prism writes in English. For Spanish, Catalan or other languages we work with your in-house team or a native-speaking writer or translator, while we handle the structure, theme, translation tools and technical setup. Our multilingual website design guide explains how to keep language versions clean for shoppers and search engines.

Migrating to Shopify from another platform

Many Spanish shops start on WooCommerce, PrestaShop or a marketplace and move to Shopify as they grow. A careful migration moves products, customers and order history where possible, keeps URLs or redirects them, and protects search visibility.

  • Product data. Clean up titles, variants and images before moving them.
  • Redirects. Map every old product and category address to its new one.
  • Customer accounts. Plan how customers will reactivate accounts.
  • Integrations. Check stock, accounting and courier connections early.

Our guide to WooCommerce to Shopify migration walks through the process. For larger brands we also handle Shopify Plus migrations, covered in our Shopify Plus migration guide.

Search and ads after launch

A new store needs traffic. Strong collection pages, unique product descriptions and structured data give organic search a head start, and a clean product feed supports Google Shopping campaigns. Our ecommerce SEO checklist lists what to have in place, and our paid media service can run Google, Meta, TikTok or YouTube campaigns once you are ready.

Search and paid results typically build over three to six months. We do not promise specific sales figures.
