Preparing Your Online Shop for Black Friday and Christmas: Collections, Site Readiness and Offers

How UK online shops can prepare for Black Friday and Christmas, from seasonal collections and gift guides to site speed, stock, delivery cut-offs and offers.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Seasonal preparation works best when it starts well ahead of the busy period, leaving time to build, test and fix.
  • Seasonal collections, gift guides and clear delivery information help shoppers find the right products and buy in time.
  • Offers must be genuine and clearly explained, and the site needs to cope with extra traffic and orders.

For many UK online shops, the weeks between late November and Christmas are among the busiest of the year. Black Friday, Cyber Monday and the run-up to Christmas bring shoppers looking for deals and gifts, and they often have firm deadlines. A store that is ready can make the most of that interest; one that is not may struggle with slow pages, confused customers or stock problems.

This guide covers how to prepare an online shop for the seasonal peak, focusing on collections and merchandising, site readiness, delivery and stock, offers and the period after Christmas. Not every store sees a peak at this time, so adapt the advice to your own trading pattern.

Start early

Changes to a website take time to plan, build and test. Making significant changes in the middle of the busiest period increases the risk of something breaking when it matters most. Many stores set a point, sometimes called a code freeze, after which only essential fixes are made until the peak has passed.

Working backwards from your busiest dates helps set a realistic timetable for:

  • Deciding which products and offers to promote.
  • Building seasonal collections, landing pages and gift guides.
  • Testing the site, checkout and integrations.
  • Preparing emails, adverts and social content.
  • Agreeing stock levels, packing capacity and delivery cut-offs.

Our guide to staging and testing explains how to make changes safely before they go live.

Review last year

If you traded through a previous peak, your analytics and order data are a useful starting point. Look at which products sold, which pages people visited, where they came from, when traffic peaked, how many abandoned their baskets and what customers contacted you about. Our GA4 setup guide covers getting reliable data.

If this is your first peak, look at your own sales patterns through the year and think about which products make natural gifts.

Planning seasonal collections

Seasonal collections group products in ways that match how shoppers think at this time of year. Rather than browsing your normal categories, many people search with a recipient or budget in mind.

Types of seasonal collection

  • By recipient, such as gifts for him, her, children, teenagers or pet owners.
  • By budget, such as gifts under a certain price, which helps people with a set amount to spend.
  • By occasion, such as stocking fillers, Secret Santa or corporate gifts.
  • Black Friday offers, a single place for discounted products during the sale period.
  • Last-minute gifts, such as products that can still arrive in time, gift cards and digital products.

Avoid creating so many collections that they overwhelm visitors or become hard to maintain. A focused set, linked clearly from the navigation and homepage, usually works better.

Using persistent URLs

Seasonal pages can build search visibility over time. Rather than creating a new Black Friday or Christmas gifts page each year with a different address, consider keeping the same URL year after year and updating the content. This means any links and search visibility the page has gained can carry over. Outside the season, the page can be updated to explain when offers return or point to other collections, rather than being deleted.

Gift guides

A gift guide is an editorial page that recommends products for particular people or budgets, with short explanations of why each item makes a good gift. Guides can work well on the website, in emails and on social media. Keep them honest: recommend products based on their genuine qualities, not just margin.

Merchandising for the season

Seasonal shoppers often arrive with less time and more urgency. Small merchandising changes can help them:

  • Homepage. Update the hero and featured sections to point to seasonal collections and key delivery dates.
  • Navigation. Add a seasonal link to the main menu during the period. Our navigation guide covers menu structure.
  • Product pages. Add gift-related information, such as gift wrapping options, gift messages and whether the item is suitable as a present.
  • Bundles. Gift sets and bundles can make choosing easier. Our variants and bundles guide covers setting them up.
  • Gift cards. Promote gift cards, especially as delivery deadlines approach. See our gift cards guide.

Getting the site ready

Speed and stability

More visitors can put extra load on your site, apps and integrations. Hosted platforms such as Shopify manage server capacity for you, but heavy themes, many apps and large images can still slow pages. Self-hosted stores, such as many on WooCommerce, may need hosting reviewed to make sure it can cope. Our guides to website speed optimisation and WordPress hosting cover this.

Checkout and payments

Test the full purchase journey on desktop and mobile, with different payment methods, discount codes and delivery options. Check that confirmation emails send correctly and that orders flow into any fulfilment or stock systems.

Discount logic

Discounts can interact in unexpected ways. Test whether codes stack, whether they apply to already discounted products, whether minimum spend thresholds work and whether free delivery is triggered correctly.

Search and filters

Make sure site search returns seasonal terms such as "gift" or "stocking filler" and that filters work on seasonal collections. Our site search guide covers search set-up.

Tracking

Check that analytics and advertising conversion tracking work before the peak, so you can see what is performing and adjust while it matters.

Stock, packing and delivery

Running out of best sellers, or selling products you cannot dispatch in time, frustrates customers. Plan stock with suppliers early and check that stock levels on the site update accurately. Our guide to stock availability display covers showing stock clearly.

Packing capacity matters too. Consider how many orders you can realistically pack and send each day, and whether you need extra help or simpler packaging during peak.

Delivery cut-off dates are one of the most important pieces of information for Christmas shoppers. Carriers publish their own last posting dates, which vary by service, so confirm yours and add a buffer for your own processing time. Show the cut-offs clearly on the homepage, product pages, basket and delivery page, and update messaging once standard delivery dates pass. Our guide to delivery and returns pages covers this.

Many stores extend their returns window for gifts bought during the season. If you do, state the terms clearly. Customers also have statutory rights for distance purchases regardless of your own policy, so check current guidance.

Planning offers fairly

Black Friday offers are common, but they need to be genuine. UK consumer protection law prohibits misleading pricing, including inflated "was" prices, fake urgency and offers that are not what they seem. The Competition and Markets Authority has published guidance on pricing practices and has powers to enforce consumer law directly, so check current guidance or take advice before planning reference prices, countdown timers or limited-stock messages.

Practical steps include:

  • Only showing a previous price if the product was genuinely sold at that price for a meaningful period.
  • Only using countdowns or "ends soon" messages if the offer really ends then.
  • Explaining any conditions, such as minimum spend or excluded products, clearly near the offer.
  • Including compulsory charges in prices shown to consumers.

Discounting is also a commercial decision. Deep discounts can attract buyers but affect margin, and some stores choose value-based offers, such as gifts with purchase, bundles or free gift wrapping, instead of cutting prices.

Marketing the season

Seasonal marketing usually combines email, paid advertising, organic search and social media. Building your email list ahead of the peak gives you a way to reach interested customers without paying for every click. Our guides to ecommerce email marketing, Google Shopping ads and remarketing cover the main channels.

Advertising costs can rise during busy periods as more businesses compete for attention, so plan budgets and landing pages in advance. Make sure every advert leads to a relevant page, such as the right collection or offer page.

Customer service during the peak

More orders usually mean more questions about delivery, stock and returns. Update your FAQ page with seasonal answers, make contact options and response times clear and consider adding order tracking information to dispatch emails. Our guides to FAQ pages and transactional emails can help.

After Christmas

The season does not end on Christmas Day. Gift card redemptions, returns, exchanges and January sales follow. Plan for:

  • Updating seasonal pages once offers end, rather than deleting them.
  • Processing returns promptly and clearly.
  • Following up with new customers to encourage a second purchase.
  • Reviewing what worked and what did not, while it is fresh.

Recording what you learned makes next year's preparation easier.

FAQs

When should I start preparing for Black Friday?

Ideally well before November, so there is time to build pages, test the site and plan stock and marketing without rushing.

Should I create a new Black Friday page every year?

Keeping the same URL and updating the content each year can help the page build search visibility over time.

Do I have to take part in Black Friday?

No. Some stores choose not to discount and focus on gifting, value-added offers or their own events instead.

How do I show Christmas delivery cut-offs?

Check your carriers' last posting dates, add your processing time and show the dates clearly on key pages, updating them as they pass.

Can I show a "was" price on Black Friday deals?

Only if it reflects a genuine previous price. Misleading reference prices can breach consumer law, so check current guidance.
