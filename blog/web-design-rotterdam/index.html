Web design Rotterdam: what businesses usually need

Rotterdam is home to one of Europe's largest ports, so logistics, shipping, freight forwarding, maritime services, trade, engineering and industrial suppliers are a big part of its economy, alongside architecture, creative studios, retail, hospitality and a growing technology scene. These businesses often share some needs.

  • Dutch and English. Many Rotterdam companies deal with international partners, so English pages are common alongside Dutch.
  • Clear services and capabilities. B2B buyers want to quickly see what you handle, where, for whom and how to start.
  • Quote and enquiry forms that collect the right details without being too long.
  • Trust signals such as company details, accreditations you genuinely hold, case information you are allowed to share and clear contact routes.
  • Fast mobile pages for buyers checking suppliers between meetings.

Planning Dutch and English versions

Before design starts, decide which language is the main one and which pages each language needs. Some companies need every page in both languages; others only need English versions of key service pages for international buyers. Each version should have its own URLs, translated navigation and metadata, and hreflang tags so search engines show the right version. A language switcher should keep visitors on the equivalent page. Our guide to multilingual website design covers these choices.

Prism writes in English. Dutch content is written by your team or a professional translator and approved by a fluent speaker on your side. We set the site up so each language can be edited easily after launch.

Websites for logistics, B2B and industrial firms

Business buyers rarely buy on a first visit. They compare suppliers, share links with colleagues and come back later. A good B2B website helps that process with clear service pages, sector pages where you serve distinct industries, downloadable specifications where useful, and a quote form that asks for the details your sales team needs. We keep forms short and add optional fields rather than forcing everything up front.

For logistics and trade businesses, pages explaining the services, routes, regions and cargo types you handle, written in plain language, usually work better than general claims. Our B2B marketing page explains how we approach websites and campaigns for business buyers.

Cookies, privacy and accessibility for Dutch websites

Dutch websites must follow the GDPR, and the ePrivacy rules on cookies mean non-essential cookies, such as most analytics and advertising tags, generally need consent before they load. A good cookie banner blocks those tags until the visitor chooses and makes rejecting as easy as accepting. Your privacy statement should describe what you actually collect, including data from quote forms.

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many businesses selling products or services to consumers online, including e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, and purely business-to-business sites may fall outside its scope, but accessible design still helps every visitor. The usual technical reference is the harmonised standard EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act for websites explains the basics. These notes are general; check official Dutch and European guidance and ask your own adviser what applies to you.

Forms, integrations and lead tracking

For most B2B websites, the quote or contact form is the most important page element. We make sure every form submission reaches the right inbox or CRM, sends a clear confirmation to the visitor and is recorded in analytics, with consent, so you can see which pages and campaigns lead to enquiries. Where you use tools for scheduling calls, live chat or newsletter sign-ups, we connect them cleanly and check that their cookies are covered by your consent banner.

We also add simple automations where they save time, such as routing enquiries by service or language, or tagging leads by source. AI-powered tools can help draft replies or summarise long enquiries, but we keep a person in charge of what is sent to customers.

Speed and mobile performance

Buyers often check suppliers on their phones, sometimes on slow connections in warehouses, ports or on the road. We keep pages light, resize and compress images, avoid unnecessary scripts and test on real devices before launch. Faster pages are easier to use and also support search visibility, so speed is planned in from the start rather than treated as an afterthought.

Redesigning an existing Dutch website

Many Rotterdam companies have websites that grew over years, with outdated pages, mixed languages and slow loading. A redesign is a chance to fix the structure, not just the look. We review which pages bring visitors and enquiries, keep and improve the content that works, and redirect old URLs to their new equivalents so existing search visibility is protected. Search engine optimisation from day one

A new website is the best time to set up SEO properly. We plan one clear page per service, sector or product category, write descriptive titles and headings, add structured data and make sure every language version can be crawled and indexed.
