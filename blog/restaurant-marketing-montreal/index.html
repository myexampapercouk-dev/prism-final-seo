Restaurant marketing in Montreal is about being found when people decide where to eat, then turning that interest into bookings and orders you own. For most restaurants, that means a complete Google Business Profile, a fast mobile website with clear menus and direct booking, steady local SEO and social ads that reach diners nearby at the right time.

Who this guide is for

Montreal has one of the most varied food scenes in North America, with neighbourhood bistros, fine dining, cafés, bakeries, bars, food halls and seasonal terraces. Competition is strong, visitors and locals both search heavily, and diners expect to see menus, photos and booking options instantly. This guide is for:

  • Independent restaurants and bistros wanting more direct bookings.
  • Cafés and bakeries that rely on footfall and repeat visits.
  • Bars and venues promoting events, private hire and late-night trade.
  • Small groups running several locations across the city.
  • Caterers and meal brands selling online or for events.

Google Business Profile and local search

For a restaurant, the Google Business Profile is often seen before the website. Accurate hours, including holiday and terrace season changes, an up-to-date menu link, good photos, booking links and a steady flow of honest reviews all help diners choose you. Responding to reviews politely, in the language the reviewer used where your team can do so, shows that you care.

Montreal searches often include a neighbourhood, a cuisine or an occasion, such as brunch in the Plateau or a group dinner downtown. Your website and profile should reflect where you are and what you are known for. Our guide to local SEO in Canada covers the details, and our SEO agency in Montreal guide looks at the wider search market in the city.

A website that drives direct bookings

Third-party booking and delivery platforms bring reach, but commission and fees add up. A fast website that makes booking and ordering simple helps you keep more of each sale. The essentials are a mobile-first design, menus as readable web pages rather than only PDFs, a clear booking button, private dining and events pages, location and parking details and gift cards where relevant.

French and English content

Quebec's language rules, including the Charter of the French Language, affect how businesses present information to the public, and French is expected to be prominent. Prism writes in English. French menus, website copy, ads and review replies should be written or checked by your own team or a professional translator. We handle the design, structure, language tags and campaign setup so that both versions work well and can be found in search.

This is general information only. Please check official guidance from the Office québécois de la langue française and speak to your own adviser about your obligations.

Social ads for restaurants

Food is visual, which makes Meta and TikTok strong channels for restaurants. Short videos of dishes being made, the room filling up, a new seasonal menu or a weekend brunch can reach thousands of nearby diners for a modest budget. We set up location targeting around your restaurant, test creative regularly and track bookings or orders where possible.

Our guides to a Meta ads agency in Montreal and TikTok Ads in Canada go deeper into how these campaigns are run.

Seasonality in Montreal

Montreal's seasons shape restaurant demand. Terrace season, summer festivals and tourist peaks bring very different trade from the colder months, when comfort food, private events and holiday parties matter more. We plan campaigns and content around these cycles, pushing terrace and festival messaging when it counts and switching to events, group bookings and gift cards as the weather turns.

Privacy, consent and email rules

Booking systems, email lists and SMS campaigns collect personal information. In general terms, PIPEDA is the federal privacy law covering many private-sector businesses, CASL sets rules for commercial electronic messages such as marketing emails and texts, and Quebec's Law 25 adds specific requirements for businesses dealing with people in Quebec, including transparency about how personal information is used. Other provinces have their own laws too. These notes are general only. Please check official guidance from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and the Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec, and speak to your own adviser.

Reviews and reputation

Reviews influence where people eat more than almost any other factor. A steady flow of recent, honest reviews on Google helps diners trust you and supports your visibility in Maps. The simplest approach is to ask at the right moment, such as on the receipt, on a table card or in a follow-up email where guests have agreed to hear from you. Never offer incentives for reviews or post fake ones, as platforms prohibit this.

Replies matter too. Thank guests for positive reviews and respond calmly to negative ones, explaining what you will do differently. Future diners read these replies and judge how you treat people. Where reviews are written in French, replies should come from someone on your team who writes French well, or be checked by a translator.

Measuring what works

Restaurant marketing is easier to improve when you can see what it produces. We set up tracking for booking clicks, calls, direction requests, online orders and gift card sales where your systems allow it. Monthly reports then show which channels and campaigns brought bookings, so budget moves towards what works and away from what does not.
