A real estate website built for buyers and sellers

Buyers want to browse homes and understand neighbourhoods. Sellers want to know what their home might be worth and why they should trust you. A good real estate website serves both. We design sites that include:

  • Neighbourhood pages that describe areas, property types and local amenities in plain language.
  • Valuation request forms that are short and easy to complete on a phone.
  • Listing links or feeds connected to the listing tools your brokerage already uses, where those tools allow it.
  • Agent profiles with photos, contact details and areas covered.
  • Clear calls to action on every page, such as book a call or request a valuation.

We build on WordPress or a custom static setup, depending on how often you update content and which listing tools you use. For more on design choices, read our guide to estate agent website design. Although written for the UK, the principles apply in Edmonton too.

Local SEO for Edmonton neighbourhoods

Most property searches are local. People search for a specific neighbourhood, a school area or a type of home in a suburb. Local SEO helps you appear for those searches. Our work usually includes:

  • Setting up and improving your Google Business Profile with the right categories, photos and service areas.
  • Writing neighbourhood and community pages that answer real buyer questions.
  • Making sure your name, address and phone number are consistent across directories.
  • Adding schema markup so search engines understand your business and services.
  • Encouraging genuine client reviews through a simple, compliant request process.

Our guide to SEO agencies in Edmonton and our Google Business Profile guide go into more detail on these steps.

Google Ads for real estate in Edmonton

Google Ads put you in front of people actively searching for help buying, selling or renting. Useful campaigns for Edmonton real estate include:

  • Seller campaigns targeting searches about home valuations and selling a house.
  • New build campaigns for builders promoting specific communities and show homes.
  • Property management campaigns targeting landlords looking for help.
  • Brand campaigns so your own name appears first when people search for you.

Housing ads on Google fall under special policies that limit some targeting options, such as age and postcode-level targeting in some cases. We set up campaigns within those rules. Our article on Google Ads agencies in Edmonton explains how we manage campaigns for local businesses.

Meta and Instagram ads for listings and sellers

Meta ads are strong for showing homes visually and for building awareness among people who are not yet searching. We use them for:

  • Showcasing new listings and open houses with photo carousels.
  • Seller lead campaigns offering a valuation or market update.
  • Retargeting people who visited your website but did not get in touch.
  • Promoting new communities for builders and developers.

Meta treats housing as a special ad category, which restricts targeting by age, gender and narrow locations. We plan creative and messaging to work well within those limits. You supply photos and any property tour clips; Prism does not film or edit video. Our guide to Meta ads in Edmonton covers campaign setup in more detail.

Privacy, consent and marketing emails

Real estate businesses collect a lot of personal information through valuation forms, viewing requests and newsletters. In Canada, a few areas of law are worth knowing about at a general level:

  • PIPEDA is the federal privacy law that applies to many private-sector businesses handling personal information in commercial activity.
  • Alberta's provincial privacy law may apply to some private-sector organisations in the province instead of, or alongside, federal rules.
  • CASL sets rules for commercial emails and texts, including consent and unsubscribe requirements.

Real estate advertising is also covered by provincial industry rules. We build consent checkboxes, privacy page templates and clear opt-in flows, but the final responsibility for compliance rests with your brokerage. We recommend checking guidance from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, Alberta's privacy commissioner and the Real Estate Council of Alberta, or speaking with your own adviser.

Common mistakes in real estate marketing

We see the same issues on many real estate websites and campaigns:

  • Generic home pages that do not mention specific Edmonton neighbourhoods.
  • Long valuation forms that put people off before they finish.
  • Ads that send people to a home page instead of a relevant landing page.
  • No follow-up process for enquiries, so leads go cold.
  • Slow websites loaded with large, uncompressed listing photos.

Fixing these basics often makes a bigger difference than adding new channels. Our landing page design guide explains how to build pages that turn ad clicks into enquiries.
