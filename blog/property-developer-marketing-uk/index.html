Property Developer Marketing in the UK: Websites, Ads and Off-Plan Sales

Property developer marketing in the UK means a dedicated development website, early registration of interest, targeted ads and accurate, approved materials.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start building a register of interest well before launch, using a dedicated development site and targeted ads.
  • Paid social and search campaigns should follow the sales stages: awareness, registration, launch and final plots.
  • CGIs, specifications and claims must be accurate and approved by the developer, as property advertising rules apply.

Property developer marketing in the UK works best when it starts early: a dedicated development website, a register of interest built before launch, targeted social and search advertising, and accurate, developer-approved sales materials that carry buyers from first click to reservation. The marketing plan should follow the build programme, with different messages for pre-launch, launch, build and final plots.

This guide covers how to plan development marketing, what a good development website needs, how to use paid media at each stage, and what to keep in mind about property advertising rules. It is general marketing information. Prism Works Studio is a marketing agency and is not regulated; legal and compliance sign-off of sales materials rests with the developer and its advisers.

Who property developers are marketing to

Development audiences vary a lot depending on the scheme. A block of city-centre apartments, a rural scheme of family homes and a build-to-rent development need different approaches.

Knowing your likely buyer mix early shapes everything from the site copy to the ad creative and the budget split between channels.

Property developer marketing UK: planning by stage

Pre-launch

The months before launch are the best time to build demand. A simple holding page with a strong image, a short description and a "register your interest" form can go live as soon as planning and branding allow. Social ads targeting the right areas and audiences feed that list. By launch day you want a warm database of people who have asked to hear first.

Launch

The full development site goes live with plots, floor plans, specifications and pricing (when the developer is ready to release it). Registered contacts get early access or an invitation to a launch event. Search ads on the development name, the location and relevant property terms help capture people researching the area.

Build and sales

Regular updates (construction progress, photography of finished show homes, local area features) keep interest alive. Retargeting ads reach people who visited but have not booked an appointment. Email nurtures registrants who are not ready yet.

Final plots

The last homes often need a more focused push, with specific plot-level messaging and tighter targeting. Clear, accurate information about what is left and when it will be ready helps buyers decide.

What a development website needs

Many developers use a dedicated site or microsite for each scheme, alongside a corporate site. Either way, each development page or site should include:

  • Strong visuals: CGIs clearly labelled as illustrative, photography as it becomes available, and video.
  • Plot availability with house types, floor plans and status, kept up to date.
  • Specification, approved by the developer, with notes where items may vary.
  • Location information: transport, schools, amenities and maps.
  • Buying information: reservation process, any schemes the developer offers, and contact details for the sales team.
  • Register of interest and appointment booking, with clear privacy information under UK GDPR.
  • Fast loading, despite heavy imagery. Our website speed guide explains how.

SEO for property developers

Developments have a limited life, which makes SEO different from most businesses. The corporate site benefits from long-term SEO: pages for each location you build in, a clear history of completed schemes and content about buying new homes. Individual development pages benefit most from strong on-page basics (the development name, location and house types), a Google Business Profile for the sales office or show home, and links from local sources. When a development sells out, redirect or keep the page as a completed-scheme case study rather than deleting it, so you keep any search value it built. Our SEO service and local SEO guide cover the detail.

Property advertising rules: a general overview

Advertising new homes is covered by consumer protection law and the CAP Code, which broadly require that materials are accurate and do not mislead, and that significant information is not left out. CGIs, specifications, distances, timescales and any incentives should all be accurate and properly qualified. Many developers also follow an industry consumer code for new homes, which sets expectations about sales information.

We cannot advise on legal requirements. Our process includes a review stage so that the developer, and its legal advisers where needed, approve all copy, imagery captions, specifications and offers before publication. We avoid claims about investment returns, price growth or rental yields unless the developer has supplied and approved them, and any figures that touch on financial outcomes should be checked carefully by the developer's own advisers.

Working with agents and the sales team

Many developers sell through a mix of an in-house sales team and one or more local estate agents. Marketing works best when everyone is using the same information. A shared sales pack (approved plot details, specification, floor plans, location information and current incentives) avoids conflicting messages. Leads from the website and ads should reach the right person quickly, ideally within the same working day, with the source recorded so you can see which channels lead to reservations.

Portal listings remain important for new homes, and they should match the development website exactly. If an incentive ends or a plot is reserved, update every channel at the same time. Out-of-date information frustrates buyers and can create compliance problems.

Show home and launch events also benefit from digital support: an event page, booking form, reminder emails and follow-up after the visit. Small details, such as sending the brochure and floor plans the same day, often make the difference between a visit and a reservation.

Measuring development marketing

Useful measures include registrations of interest by source, appointment bookings, show home visits, reservations and the cost of each. Tracking registrations in GA4 and passing source information into the sales team's CRM lets you see which channels lead to reservations, not just clicks. FAQs

How should property developer marketing in the UK be planned?

Plan it around the build programme: build a register of interest before launch, launch with a full development site and targeted ads, keep interest going during the build, then focus on final plots. Make sure every stage has approved, accurate materials.

When should a developer start marketing a new scheme?

As early as the developer is comfortable, usually once branding and basic details are confirmed. A holding page and register of interest can start building demand months before launch.

Does each development need its own website?

Not always. A dedicated page on the corporate site can work well, but a separate microsite can give a larger scheme more space and a distinct brand. The choice depends on the scheme size and budget.

Can Facebook ads be used to market new homes?

Yes, and they are often effective for registrations of interest. Housing ads are a special ad category on Meta, which limits some targeting options, so campaigns must be set up within those rules.

Who approves CGIs and specifications in ads?

The developer, with its legal advisers where needed. Prism Works Studio prepares and places materials, but sign-off on accuracy and compliance rests with the developer.

What happens to the development page after it sells out?

Keep it as a completed-scheme case study or redirect it to a relevant page, rather than deleting it, so any search value it built is kept.
