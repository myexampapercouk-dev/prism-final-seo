A Shopify agency in Basel should build an online store that sells well to Swiss customers and, often, to buyers in Germany and France just across the border. Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely. We have no office in Basel or anywhere in Switzerland. Why Basel brands choose Shopify

Basel sits on the Rhine where Switzerland, Germany and France meet. Its economy is known for life sciences, but the city also has a strong base of design, art, food, specialist retail and independent brands. Many local businesses sell to customers across Switzerland and into the EU, which brings extra questions about currency, tax, shipping and language.

Shopify handles much of this well. It supports several currencies and languages from one store, has a large app ecosystem, offers reliable hosting and checkout, and is easy for a small team to run day to day. For most independent Basel brands it is quicker to launch and simpler to maintain than a custom platform.

Selling across Switzerland, Germany and France

Cross-border selling is often the main reason Basel brands need specialist help. Switzerland is outside the EU, so goods moving between Switzerland and EU countries can face customs declarations, import VAT and duties. Shopify Markets lets you set prices, currencies and languages for each market, but the commercial and tax decisions behind it need input from your accountant or customs adviser.

Common decisions include whether to show prices in Swiss francs and euros, whether to include duties at checkout, which carriers to use and whether to hold stock inside the EU. Once those decisions are made, we configure the store to match. Our guide to Shopify Markets for Europe explains the main options.

Payment methods matter too. Swiss shoppers often expect familiar local options alongside cards and wallets, so check which methods your chosen payment provider supports in Switzerland and add the ones your customers use most.

Languages: German, French and English

Basel is German-speaking, but many brands also need French for western Switzerland and France, and English for international customers. Prism writes in English. German and French copy should come from your own team or a professional translator, and we set up the language structure, URLs, hreflang and navigation so each version works properly.

Product data needs the same care. Titles, descriptions, sizes, care instructions and legal pages should all be translated, not only the homepage. Our guide to multilingual website design explains how to plan this without creating duplicate content problems.

  • New store builds: independent brands launching online for the first time, usually on a well-supported theme customised to their brand.
  • Migrations to Shopify: moving from WooCommerce, Magento or an older platform, with careful redirects to protect search rankings.
  • Shopify Plus migrations: larger brands moving to Shopify Plus for advanced checkout, automation and multi-store needs. Our guide to Shopify Plus migration covers the process.
  • Redesigns: refreshing an existing Shopify store to improve speed, navigation and conversion.

Speed, design and trust

Swiss shoppers tend to expect quality, clarity and precision. A store that loads slowly, shows unclear delivery costs or hides returns information will lose sales. We focus on fast-loading themes, compressed images, a limited number of well-chosen apps, clear product pages and transparent shipping, duty and returns information.

Speed also affects search rankings and ad performance. Our guide to Shopify speed optimisation explains the main fixes we apply to new and existing stores.

Timelines

Most Shopify builds take 5 to 12 weeks. A single-language store with a modest catalogue sits at the shorter end, while a store with several markets, languages, migrated data and integrations takes longer. A typical project runs like this:

  1. Weeks one and two: discovery, market decisions, sitemap and design direction.
  2. Weeks three to six: theme build, product import, apps and Markets setup.
  3. Weeks seven onwards: translations added, testing across devices and currencies, redirects and launch.

Translation turnaround from your team or translator is often the factor that decides the final launch date, so plan it early.

Bringing traffic after launch

A new store needs visitors. Most Basel brands combine SEO for long-term traffic with Google Ads for faster sales. Our guides to an SEO agency in Zurich and a Google Ads agency in Zurich explain how both channels work in the Swiss market. Results from SEO and paid campaigns typically build over 3 to 6 months.

Marketplaces and other sales channels

Many Basel brands sell through more than their own store. Shopify can connect to Google Shopping, Meta and Instagram shops, and to selected marketplaces through apps, so stock and orders stay in one place. Larger Swiss marketplaces and EU platforms such as Amazon in Germany and France can extend your reach, but each has its own listing rules, fees and fulfilment options.

We can help plan which channels make sense, keep product data consistent and optimise listings for marketplace search. The key is to avoid spreading a small team too thinly. Most brands do best by getting their own store working well first, then adding one extra channel at a time and measuring the results before adding more.

Working with a remote Shopify team

We share a project board, hold regular video calls and send written updates. Switzerland is one hour ahead of the UK for most of the year, which makes scheduling easy. Product photography and local tasks stay with your team, and we advise on what you need.
