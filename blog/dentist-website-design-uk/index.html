Dentist Website Design: Treatments, Fees and Patient Bookings

Website design for UK dental practices: treatment pages, NHS and private fees, new patient registration, online booking, emergency info and GDC-aware content.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Dental websites should make it immediately clear whether the practice takes NHS patients, private patients or both, and how to register.
  • Treatment pages should explain what is involved in plain language without promising outcomes, in line with GDC standards and advertising rules.
  • Emergency information, opening hours and online booking are among the most-used features, so keep them prominent and accurate.

For many people, choosing a dentist starts with a search and a quick look at a few practice websites. They want to know whether the practice is taking new patients, whether it offers NHS treatment, what private treatments cost, where it is, and how to book. A dental practice website that answers those questions clearly, and presents treatments responsibly, does a lot to turn visitors into patients.

This guide covers the website itself for general and specialist dental practices. For wider promotion, including local search, reviews and advertising, see our guide to dental marketing in the UK. If you also offer non-surgical aesthetic treatments, our guide to private clinic marketing may be relevant.

Answer the first questions immediately

The home page should make several things clear without scrolling far:

  • Whether you are accepting new patients, and whether that is NHS, private, plan-based or a mix.
  • Your location, opening hours and phone number.
  • How to book or register.
  • What to do in a dental emergency.

NHS availability is a common source of confusion and frustration for patients. If you are not currently accepting new NHS adult patients, say so clearly rather than leaving people to phone and find out. Keep this information updated as your capacity changes.

Structuring a dental practice website

  • Home.
  • New patients, explaining how to register, what to expect at a first visit and what to bring.
  • Treatments, grouped into general, cosmetic, restorative, orthodontic and other categories as relevant.
  • Fees, covering NHS charges, private prices and any membership or payment plans.
  • Emergencies.
  • Meet the team.
  • Contact and find us.

Practices with several sites should give each location its own page, with its own address, hours, team and services, rather than a single page listing everything.

Treatment pages

Treatment pages help patients understand their options and help the practice appear in searches for specific treatments. A useful treatment page explains:

  • What the treatment is and who it may be suitable for.
  • What happens at each stage and roughly how many appointments are usually involved.
  • Aftercare and what to expect afterwards.
  • General risks or considerations, explained in plain language.
  • Whether it is available on the NHS or privately only, and where to find fees.

Write the content in your own words. Generic copy supplied by manufacturers or reused from other sites is unlikely to stand out and may not reflect how you actually work. Keep clinical claims measured and avoid suggesting results are guaranteed.

Advertising standards and GDC guidance

Dental professionals in the UK are regulated by the General Dental Council, which sets standards that cover how dental professionals communicate, including advertising. Website content is also subject to advertising rules overseen by the Advertising Standards Authority, and some treatments, such as botulinum toxin, are prescription-only medicines that cannot be advertised to the public. On a dental website this generally means:

  • Information should be accurate, clear and not misleading.
  • Do not promise outcomes or use claims you cannot substantiate.
  • Only describe team members as specialists if they are on the relevant GDC specialist list.
  • Show registration details for clinicians in line with current GDC guidance.
  • Take care with before-and-after images, using genuine photos with consent and avoiding misleading editing.

Rules and guidance change, so check the current GDC standards and advertising guidance, and take advice if you are unsure. This article gives general information, not regulatory advice.

Showing fees clearly

Patients appreciate knowing costs in advance. A fees page might include:

  • Current NHS charge bands, if you provide NHS treatment, with a link to official information on charges and exemptions. NHS charges differ between England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, so show those that apply to you.
  • Private prices, either as fixed fees or "from" prices with an explanation of what affects the final cost.
  • Details of any practice membership plan or payment options.

If you offer finance for treatment, consumer credit rules apply, and promoting credit may require appropriate authorisation. Take advice before advertising finance on your site.

New patient registration

A clear new patient journey reduces phone calls and paperwork. Explain how to register, whether there is a waiting list, and what happens at the first appointment. Online forms for medical history and contact details can save time at the practice, but they collect health information, which is special category data under UK GDPR. Use a secure system designed for this purpose, collect only what you need, and explain how data is handled in your privacy notice.

Online booking

Many patients prefer to book online. Practice management systems often include an online booking feature that can be linked or embedded on your site. If you offer it, make sure it shows clearly which appointment types can be booked online, works smoothly on a phone, and sends confirmations and reminders. If booking is phone-only, display the number prominently and make it tappable on mobile. Our guide to online booking systems covers general considerations.

Emergency information

People with toothache or a broken tooth often search urgently. Your emergency page should explain how to get an emergency appointment at your practice, what to do outside opening hours, and where to go for urgent dental care, such as NHS 111 in England and Wales or the relevant service elsewhere in the UK. Keep this information simple, accurate and easy to find from the home page.

Meet the team

Patients, especially nervous ones, like to know who they will see. Team pages with real photographs, names, roles and short professional backgrounds help build familiarity. Include GDC registration numbers for clinicians where appropriate. Only include team members who currently work at the practice.

Supporting nervous patients

Dental anxiety is common. If your practice offers approaches that may help nervous patients, such as longer appointments or sedation options, describe them factually. A calm, reassuring tone throughout the site, clear explanations of what happens during visits, and photos of the practice can help nervous patients feel more comfortable taking the first step.

Reviews and testimonials

Genuine patient reviews help prospective patients decide. Display reviews accurately and do not edit them to change their meaning. Be careful that testimonials do not make claims about treatment outcomes that would breach advertising rules. Respond to feedback professionally and avoid disclosing patient information in replies.

Accessibility and practical details

Describe physical access to the practice, such as step-free entry, ground-floor surgeries and parking. The website itself should be accessible, with clear headings, good contrast, alt text and forms that work with assistive technology. See our guide to website accessibility.

Local search and practice details

Most patients look for a dentist near home or work, so your website should make your location unmistakable. Use the full practice address in text rather than only in an image, embed a map, and describe parking, public transport and nearby landmarks. Keep your name, address and phone number consistent between the website and your Google Business Profile. If you have several practices, each location page should carry its own details, hours and team so search engines and patients can tell them apart.

Opening hours deserve particular care. Update them for bank holidays and any temporary changes, and make sure the hours on your site match those shown elsewhere. A patient who arrives at a closed door because the website was wrong is unlikely to return.

Content for children and families

If you see children, a short page for parents helps. Explain from what age you see children, what a first visit involves, whether NHS treatment for children is available at your practice, and tips for preparing a child for an appointment. Keep the tone friendly and practical, and avoid making clinical claims you cannot support.

Common mistakes on dental websites

  • Unclear NHS availability, leading to frustrated phone calls.
  • Out-of-date fees or NHS charges.
  • Treatment pages that promise outcomes or use overstated claims.
  • Emergency information that is hard to find.
  • Team pages listing clinicians who have left.
  • Booking routes that do not work well on phones.

FAQs

Should I say whether I am taking NHS patients?

Yes. Make your current NHS and private availability clear and keep it up to date.

Can I publish before-and-after photos?

Generally yes, if they are genuine, used with consent and not misleading. Check current GDC and advertising guidance.

Do I need to show GDC registration numbers?

Current GDC guidance covers the information that should be shown. Check it and include registration details accordingly.

Can patients fill in medical history forms online?

Yes, using a secure system designed for health data, with a clear privacy notice.

Should I publish private prices?

It helps patients plan. Fixed or "from" prices with an explanation of what affects the cost work well.
