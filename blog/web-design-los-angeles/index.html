Web Design Los Angeles: How a Remote Studio Builds Websites for LA Businesses

Looking for web design in Los Angeles? A remote studio can plan, design and build your site by video call. Here is how it works, the cost and what to check.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Web design for a Los Angeles business does not need an LA office; it needs a clear brief, a fixed scope and a site built for US customers.
  • We are a UK-based studio working remotely with US clients; our project prices are set in pounds, with other currencies quoted on request.
  • Most small business websites take three to five weeks and ecommerce builds five to twelve, depending on content and integrations.

If you are searching for web design in Los Angeles, the real question is whether a studio can build a fast, clear website that turns your visitors into enquiries or sales. A remote studio can plan, design and build that site entirely by video call and shared documents, as long as the process is structured and the site is shaped around your US customers.

We are a UK-based studio and we work remotely with US businesses. We do not have a Los Angeles office or any US office. This guide explains what good web design for an LA business involves, how a remote project runs, what it typically costs and how to compare your options fairly.

What web design in Los Angeles should deliver

Los Angeles is crowded with creative businesses, studios, restaurants, wellness brands, trades and online stores. Visitors there are used to polished brands, so a website has to look good, but looks alone do not win business. A good site should do five things well:

  • Explain what you do in seconds: a clear headline, a short supporting line and an obvious next step.
  • Load quickly on phones: most visitors arrive on mobile, often on the move, so speed and layout matter more than animation.
  • Build trust: real photos, clear contact details, genuine reviews and plain information about how you work.
  • Be found in search: sensible page structure, titles and content so Google can understand each service or product.
  • Be easy to update: a content management system your team can use without calling a developer for every change.

Our guides to homepage design and website trust signals go into these points in more detail, and they apply to US sites just as much as UK ones.

Businesses in Los Angeles usually choose between a local agency, a remote studio, a freelancer or a website builder they manage themselves. Each can work; the right choice depends on budget, the complexity of the site and how much contact you want.

How a remote web design project works

Web design is planning, writing, design and development, and all of it happens in shared tools. Remote work changes how meetings are arranged, not the quality of the work. A typical project with us runs like this:

  1. Discovery call: a video call to understand your business, customers, competitors and goals, followed by a written summary.
  2. Scope and quote: a fixed list of pages, features and integrations, so you know exactly what you are paying for.
  3. Sitemap and content plan: the pages you need and what each one must say, agreed before design starts.
  4. Design: layouts for key pages, shared as links you can comment on in your own time.
  5. Build: the site is developed on a private staging link, so you can review it on your own phone and laptop.
  6. Testing and launch: forms, speed, mobile layout, tracking and redirects checked before the site goes live.
  7. Handover: logins, ownership and a short guide to editing your own content.

Our post on how long it takes to build a website explains the stages in more depth, and the guide to website ownership and handover lists what you should own at the end.

Working across time zones

Los Angeles is usually eight hours behind the UK, which sounds awkward but can work in your favour. Our working day overlaps with your early morning, so a call at the start of your day is easy to arrange. Feedback you leave during your afternoon is often picked up and acted on before you start the next morning.

To make this run smoothly, we agree a regular call slot, use written updates for decisions and keep one shared place for feedback. You are never waiting for a reply to a vague email, because each stage has a clear list of what we need from you and when.

Launch timing is planned the same way. We usually schedule go-live for a quiet point in your week, with someone on our side checking forms, tracking and redirects as soon as the site is live. If anything needs fixing, it is often done before your working day begins, so your customers see a finished site rather than a work in progress.

What a website for an LA business typically costs

Our project prices are set in pounds, and we can quote other currencies, including US dollars, on request. As a guide:

  • Small business website: £1,000 to £2,000, typically delivered in three to five weeks.
  • Ecommerce website: £1,500 to £3,500, typically five to twelve weeks depending on products and integrations.
  • Ongoing marketing retainers: Launch £350 a month, Growth £750 a month and Scale £1,500 a month, if you want SEO or paid media after launch.

The final figure depends on the number of pages, how much content you supply, booking or ecommerce features and any third-party tools you need connected. Our pricing page sets out what each level includes, and our website and ecommerce development service explains the build process in full.

US-specific points to plan for

A website for an LA business should feel American to American visitors. That sounds obvious, but it is easy to get small details wrong. We plan for these from the start:

  • Spelling and wording: your site copy can use US English for your customers, even though we write our own documents in UK English.
  • Formats: dates, phone numbers, addresses, currency and measurements shown the way US visitors expect.
  • Accessibility: US businesses face expectations around accessible websites. We build to recognised accessibility guidelines, but the legal position varies, so check current official guidance or ask your lawyer about your own obligations.
  • Privacy: California has its own privacy rules for some businesses. We can set up cookie notices and privacy page layouts, but the wording and compliance decisions should come from your lawyer.
  • Bilingual content: if many of your customers prefer Spanish, a bilingual site may be worth planning in from the start rather than bolted on later.

If you do need more than one language, our guide to multilingual website design covers structure, translation and SEO.

Is a remote studio right for your LA business?

A remote studio tends to suit businesses that want a clear process and a fixed scope, are happy to work by video call and shared documents, and care more about the finished site than about meeting in person. It suits less well if you need someone on site regularly, for example to photograph your premises every month or to run in-person workshops.

Whoever you choose, ask to see how they handle discovery, who writes the content, what happens after launch and whether you will own the site and its accounts outright. Our guide on how to choose a web designer includes a list of questions that works for any studio, local or remote.

FAQs

Do you have an office for web design in Los Angeles?

No. We are a UK-based studio and we work remotely with US clients, including businesses in Los Angeles. All meetings take place by video call, and the work itself happens in shared design and project tools.

How much does web design in Los Angeles cost with a remote studio?

Our small business websites are £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce websites are £1,500 to £3,500. Prices are set in pounds and we can quote other currencies, including US dollars, on request after a discovery call.

How long does a website take to build?

A small business website typically takes three to five weeks and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks. The biggest factor is usually how quickly content, photos and feedback arrive, not the build itself.

Can you write my website copy in US English?

Yes. Website copy can be written for your US customers using American spelling and wording, while our own proposals and project documents stay in UK English.

Will my website meet US accessibility and privacy rules?

We build to recognised accessibility guidelines and can set up privacy and cookie page layouts. Legal requirements vary by business and state, so check current official guidance or speak to your lawyer about what applies to you.

Can you help with SEO and ads after the site launches?

Yes. Our monthly retainers cover SEO and paid media, starting at £350 a month. Many clients launch the site first and then decide which channel to invest in once they have tracking and early data in place.
