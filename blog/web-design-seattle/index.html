Web Design Seattle: How a Remote Studio Builds Websites for Seattle Businesses

Looking for web design in Seattle? A remote studio can plan, design and build fast sites for Seattle firms by video call. Here is the process and the cost.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Web design for a Seattle business needs a clear brief, a fixed scope and a site written for US buyers, not a local office.
  • Tech firms, local services and online shops in Seattle need different websites, so planning should start from how your customers decide.
  • We are a UK-based studio working remotely with US clients; project prices are in pounds, with other currencies quoted on request.

If you are searching for web design in Seattle, what you need is a studio that can build a fast, clear website that turns visitors into enquiries, bookings or sales. A remote studio can plan, design and build that site for a Seattle business entirely through video calls and shared tools, provided the process is well organised and the site is written for your US customers.

We are a UK-based studio and we work remotely with US businesses. We do not have a Seattle office or any US office. This guide covers what a good website for a Seattle business should do, how needs differ by business type, how a remote project runs across the time difference and what it typically costs.

What web design in Seattle should deliver

Seattle's economy mixes large technology and cloud companies with a deep base of smaller firms: software start-ups, coffee roasters, outdoor brands, restaurants, clinics, architects, trades and professional services. Many local buyers work in tech and expect a polished, fast experience. Whatever your sector, a good website should:

  • Make the offer obvious: what you do, who it is for and the next step, visible without scrolling.
  • Load quickly on every device: speed affects both search visibility and how many visitors stay.
  • Build trust: genuine reviews, clear contact details, real photography and plain explanations.
  • Support search: a logical structure, descriptive titles and a page for each main service or product.
  • Measure what matters: tracking for form submissions, calls, bookings and purchases.
  • Be easy to update: a content management system your team can use without calling a developer.

Designing for Seattle customers

Your website should speak to the people you actually serve. A few points are worth building into the brief from the start:

  • US English and conventions: on-site copy for US buyers should use US spelling, date formats and units, even though we write our own guides in UK English.
  • Local detail: if you serve particular neighbourhoods or nearby cities such as Bellevue, Redmond or Tacoma, say so clearly on the relevant pages.
  • Mobile first: many visitors will arrive on a phone, often from a map or local search result.
  • Accessibility: clear contrast, readable text, keyboard navigation and descriptive image text help every visitor. Accessibility expectations and legal duties vary, so check current official guidance or ask your lawyer about your own obligations.
  • Privacy: forms, cookies and analytics should be set up thoughtfully. US privacy rules differ between states, so ask your lawyer which apply to you.

Our guides to mobile website design and website accessibility explain the design principles behind these points.

How a remote web design project runs

Website projects are planned, designed and built in shared tools, which makes them well suited to remote delivery. A typical project with us follows these stages:

  1. Discovery call: we learn about your business, customers, competitors and goals, and agree what the site must achieve.
  2. Scope and plan: a written scope covering pages, features, content responsibilities and timeline.
  3. Structure: a sitemap and page outlines so everyone agrees on the content before design starts.
  4. Design: page designs shared for your feedback, with agreed rounds of changes.
  5. Build: development on a private staging site you can review at any time.
  6. Testing and launch: checks on devices, forms, speed and tracking before going live.
  7. Handover: training on updating the site, with all accounts and logins in your name.

Seattle is usually eight hours behind the UK. Our late afternoon overlaps with your morning, which works well for review calls, and feedback you send at the end of your day can often be acted on before you are back. Web design costs for Seattle businesses

Our website projects are priced in pounds, and we can quote other currencies, including US dollars, on request:

  • Small business website: £1,000 to £2,000, typically for a service business with a focused set of pages.
  • Ecommerce website: £1,500 to £3,500, depending on the number of products, features and integrations.

A small business website typically takes three to five weeks, and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks, depending on scope and how quickly content and feedback arrive. Hosting, domains, paid apps and ongoing marketing are separate costs. Our pricing page sets out what is included, and our website and ecommerce development service page explains the work in more detail.

Choosing a platform for your Seattle website

The platform behind your site affects how easy it is to update, how it performs and what it costs to run. We recommend a platform based on what the site needs to do, not on habit:

  • WordPress: a flexible choice for service businesses, professional firms and content-heavy sites that publish regularly.
  • Shopify: usually the simplest route for online shops and consumer brands that want reliable checkout and stock management.
  • Custom builds: worth considering when you need unusual features or tight integration with your own product or systems.

Whatever the platform, the site should be fast, secure and easy for your team to edit. Ask how updates, backups and security will be handled after launch, because a website needs care long after it goes live. Our comparison of a website builder vs a custom website explains the trade-offs in more depth.

Comparing options for web design in Seattle

Seattle businesses usually weigh up a local agency, a remote studio, a freelancer or a website builder. Think about the level of support you need after launch as well as the build itself. A local agency offers in-person meetings but may carry higher overheads. A freelancer gives direct contact but limited cover. A website builder is cheap to start but can become restrictive as you grow. A remote studio offers a structured process and a full team at a price that reflects lower overheads, with meetings by video.

Whichever route you choose, insist on owning your domain, hosting account and content from day one. Our guide to website ownership and handover explains exactly what you should receive at the end of a project.

Questions to ask any Seattle web design studio

  • What exactly is included in the price, and what would cost extra?
  • Who writes the content, and who sources the photography?
  • How many rounds of design changes are included?
  • Will the site be built on a platform we can update ourselves?
  • What support is available after launch, and how is it charged?
  • Will all accounts, files and logins be in our name?

Clear, written answers to these questions are a good sign. Vague answers about scope or ownership are worth probing before you sign anything.

FAQs

Do you have an office for web design in Seattle?

No. We are a UK-based studio working remotely with US businesses, including companies in Seattle. Projects run through video calls, shared documents and a private staging site you can review at any time.

Small business websites are £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce websites £1,500 to £3,500. Prices are in pounds, and we can quote other currencies, including US dollars, on request.

How long does a website project take?

A small business website typically takes three to five weeks and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks. Timelines depend on scope and how quickly content and feedback are ready.

Can you write the website copy in US English?

Yes. Copy for your site should match your customers, so we can use US spelling and conventions on your pages. Our own guides are written in UK English because we are a UK studio.

Yes. We set up domains, hosting and key accounts in your name wherever possible, and hand over logins and files at launch so you are never locked in.

Can you build a website for a Seattle tech start-up?

Yes. Start-up sites usually need clear product, feature and pricing pages plus a simple sign-up or demo path. We plan the structure around how your buyers evaluate the product before design begins.
