Shopify Agency for Montreal Brands: Building Bilingual Shopify Stores Remotely

Looking for a Shopify agency in Montreal? How we build and improve bilingual Shopify stores for Quebec brands, with French content, timelines and costs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Shopify agency for a Montreal brand should plan French and English versions of the store from the start, with French content written by your team or a professional translator.
  • Quebec has specific rules on French-language commerce and privacy, so check official guidance and treat compliance as part of the project plan rather than an afterthought.
  • Our ecommerce builds typically cost £1,500 to £3,500 and take five to twelve weeks, with Canadian dollar quotes available on request.

A Shopify agency for a Montreal brand should build a store that works properly in both French and English, sets up products, payments, shipping and taxes correctly for Canadian customers, and is fast and easy to buy from on a phone. Because Shopify is a hosted platform, design, development and ongoing improvements can all be done remotely. Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that builds and improves Shopify stores for Canadian businesses online; we have no office in Montreal or anywhere else in Canada.

This guide explains what to expect from a Shopify project for a Montreal business, how bilingual stores are set up, the Quebec rules worth knowing about, what our builds cost and what to ask before choosing an agency.

What a Shopify agency in Montreal should do

Montreal has a strong independent retail and creative scene, from fashion labels and designers in the Mile End and Plateau to food and drink producers, cosmetics and wellness brands, furniture makers, bookshops and specialty grocers. Many of these businesses sell in shops and markets as well as online. A good Shopify partner should cover:

  • Store design that reflects your brand and makes products easy to find and buy.
  • Bilingual set-up using Shopify's language features, with separate, properly written French and English content.
  • Product data including variants, collections, images and descriptions that help customers and search engines.
  • Payments, shipping and taxes configured for Canadian provinces and any international markets you serve.
  • Apps and integrations chosen carefully, so the store stays fast and maintainable.
  • Analytics and tracking so you can see where sales come from.

Our website and ecommerce development service covers each of these, and our guide to Shopify agencies in Canada explains how we work with brands across the country.

Building a bilingual Shopify store

For most Montreal brands, French is not an optional extra. Customers in Quebec expect to shop in French, and many brands also sell to English-speaking customers in Montreal and across Canada. Shopify supports multiple languages within a single store, so you can manage one set of products, inventory and orders while showing each customer the right language.

The quality of the French matters as much as the technical set-up. Automatic translation tools often produce awkward product names, sizes and instructions that put customers off. We write in English; French content is written with your team or a professional translator, and we build the store so both versions are complete, consistent and indexed properly by search engines. That includes translated navigation, checkout notifications, policy pages and email templates, which are easy to overlook.

Payments, shipping and sales taxes

Getting the practical settings right is just as important as the design. Shopify lets you set up shipping rates by province or region, local pickup for customers who want to collect from a Montreal shop or studio, and separate rates for the United States or other countries if you ship abroad. Clear delivery times and costs shown before checkout reduce abandoned carts.

Canadian sales taxes vary by province, and Quebec has its own provincial sales tax alongside the federal tax. Shopify includes tools to calculate and display taxes, but your accountant should confirm your registration and settings, because tax obligations depend on your business and where you sell. We configure the store to match the settings you and your accountant agree, and test orders from different provinces before launch.

Quebec rules worth knowing about

Quebec's Charter of the French Language sets requirements for the use of French in commerce, which can include websites, product information and customer communications for businesses operating in the province. The Office québécois de la langue française publishes official guidance on what applies. We build stores that make full French versions straightforward, but deciding what your business must do is a matter for you and your advisers.

Privacy is another area to plan for. Quebec's privacy law, often called Law 25, sets rules for how businesses collect and use personal information, including requirements around consent and tracking technologies. Outside Quebec, the federal Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) generally applies, and Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) governs marketing emails, so your newsletter sign-up and abandoned cart emails need proper consent. If you sell into Ontario, its accessibility law may also be relevant depending on your organisation's size. These notes are general information, not legal advice, so refer to official guidance from the relevant regulators.

Migrating to Shopify or rebuilding a store

Many Montreal brands start on marketplaces such as Etsy, or on another platform, before moving to Shopify for more control over branding, customer data and margins. Moving a store involves products, customers, order history where possible, and redirects from old URLs so search rankings are protected. Our guide to moving from Etsy to Shopify covers the main steps.

If you already have a Shopify store that feels slow, cluttered or hard to manage, a rebuild or theme refresh can help. We often find too many apps, inconsistent product data and confusing navigation; tidying these up usually improves both speed and the buying experience. Our guide to briefing a Shopify developer explains how to prepare, whoever you choose.

How a remote Shopify project works

Montreal is on Eastern Time, so the UK is usually five hours ahead. UK afternoons overlap with Montreal mornings for calls, and most of the work happens in a shared staging store where you can review progress at any time.

  1. Discovery. We agree goals, products, markets, languages and must-have features.
  2. Design. We design key pages, including home, collection, product and cart, for your approval.
  3. Build. We set up the theme, products, payments, shipping, taxes and apps.
  4. Content. French and English content is added, with French written by your team or a translator.
  5. Testing and launch. We test orders, emails and language switching on phones and desktops, then launch.

You own the Shopify store, domain and all content throughout. After launch, we can continue with improvements, SEO or advertising on a monthly retainer.

Questions to ask any Shopify agency

  • How will you set up French and English versions of the store?
  • Who writes the French content, and how is it reviewed?
  • Which apps do you recommend, and what do they cost each month?

FAQs

How do I choose a Shopify agency in Montreal?

Look for an agency that plans bilingual content from the start, explains its app choices and costs, protects rankings during migrations and leaves you owning everything. Ask how French content will be written and reviewed.

Can a UK agency build a Shopify store for a Montreal business?

Yes. Shopify is a hosted platform, so the whole project can be delivered online. Prism has no Canadian office; we work remotely and schedule calls in the overlap between UK afternoons and Eastern Time mornings.

How much does a Shopify store cost with Prism?

Ecommerce builds typically cost £1,500 to £3,500, with Shopify's own fees paid separately. We can quote in Canadian dollars on request.

Does my Shopify store need to be in French?

If you sell to customers in Quebec, French-language requirements may apply. Check the official guidance from the Office québécois de la langue française and take advice on what applies to your business.

How long does a Shopify build take?

Our ecommerce builds typically take five to twelve weeks, depending on the number of products, languages and custom features involved.

Can you move my store from another platform to Shopify?

Yes. We migrate products, customers and content where the platforms allow it, and set up redirects so existing search rankings are protected as far as possible.
