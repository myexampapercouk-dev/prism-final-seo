Getting the foundations right

Most underperforming accounts we review share the same problems. We fix these first.

  • Conversion tracking. Every form, call and purchase should be tracked accurately, or the bidding algorithms optimise towards the wrong things. Our conversion tracking guide explains the setup.
  • Location targeting. Settings that target people actually in your service area, rather than people who merely show interest in it. See our location targeting guide.
  • Negative keywords. Removing searches that waste budget, such as jobs, free or unrelated terms. Our negative keywords guide covers the process.
  • Account structure. Clear campaigns and ad groups so budgets go to what matters.
  • Landing pages. Pages that match the ad, load quickly and make the next step obvious.

Targeting Dublin and beyond

Dublin businesses often serve the city, the wider Leinster area, all of Ireland or the UK as well. We set up targeting to match:

  • Radius or county targeting for local services that cover set areas.
  • Country-level campaigns for national services and e-commerce.
  • Separate UK campaigns where you sell across the border, with their own budgets and currency settings.
  • Ad scheduling so ads run when your team can answer calls.

Choosing keywords that bring business

Good keyword choice is the difference between a profitable account and one that burns budget. We build keyword lists around how your buyers actually search.

  • High-intent phrases first. Searches that include words such as price, quote, near me, hire or buy usually signal someone ready to act.
  • Service-specific groups. Each service gets its own ad group with matching ads and a matching landing page, which tends to improve relevance and cost per click.
  • Sensible match types. We use phrase and exact match for tight control, and broad match only where conversion data is strong enough to guide it.
  • Brand protection. A small campaign on your own business name stops competitors and directories taking clicks from people already looking for you.
  • Competitor terms with care. Bidding on competitor names can work, but it is often expensive and needs careful ad wording.
  • Regular search term reviews. We check the actual searches triggering your ads every week and add negatives or new keywords as needed.

Shopping and Performance Max for Irish e-commerce

If you sell products online, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are usually the core of your Google Ads account. They depend on a healthy product feed in Google Merchant Center. We check that product titles are descriptive, images meet Google's requirements, prices and availability match your site, and shipping and returns information is set up correctly for Ireland and any other countries you sell to.

We then structure campaigns so that best-sellers, high-margin products and new lines can be given different budgets and targets. Performance Max can perform well, but it needs accurate conversion values and clear brand exclusions, so we set those up before scaling spend. GDPR, cookie consent and accessibility

Google Ads depends on tracking, so privacy and consent rules matter. These notes are general; please check official guidance, such as from the Irish Data Protection Commission, or your own legal adviser.

  • GDPR. Enquiry forms and customer lists hold personal data, which needs a lawful basis, a privacy notice and secure handling. Uploading customer lists for audience targeting needs particular care.
  • Cookie consent. ePrivacy rules generally require consent before non-essential cookies, including advertising tags, are set. We set up Google's Consent Mode alongside your consent banner so tags respect the visitor's choice.
  • European Accessibility Act. From June 2025, the EAA applies to many consumer-facing digital services, including much of e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt. The harmonised standard EN 301 549 refers to WCAG AA.

Consent rules can reduce the conversions you see, so we read reports with that in mind. Our cookie banner design guide shows how to ask for consent clearly. In regulated sectors such as finance or health, ad copy approval rests with your compliance team.

FAQs

What does a Google Ads agency in Dublin do?

It plans, builds and manages your Google Ads campaigns, including tracking, keywords, ads, landing pages, budgets and reporting, so your spend turns into enquiries or sales.

Do you have an office in Dublin?

No. Prism Works Studio is UK-based and works remotely. We have no office in Dublin or elsewhere in Ireland.

How much does Google Ads management cost?

Our retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month. Ad spend is paid separately to Google, and euro pricing is quoted on request.

Will I own my Google Ads account?

Yes. The account belongs to you and we work in it with your permission, so you keep all data and history if you ever move on.

How quickly will Google Ads work?

Ads can bring traffic within days, but campaigns typically take 3 to 6 months to settle as conversion data builds and we refine targeting and bids.

Do cookie consent rules affect Google Ads?

Yes. Advertising tags generally need consent before they run. We set up Consent Mode so tracking respects choices, though you may see fewer recorded conversions as a result.
