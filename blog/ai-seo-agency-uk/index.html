AI SEO Agency UK: What They Really Do and How to Choose One

What an AI SEO agency does for UK businesses, where AI helps and where human judgement matters, plus how to compare providers and the questions to ask.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • An AI SEO agency uses artificial intelligence to speed up research, analysis and content planning, while people stay responsible for strategy, accuracy and final decisions.
  • AI SEO is about how the work gets done, while GEO is about being visible and cited in AI-generated answers, so check which of the two a provider is offering.
  • Choose a provider by asking how it checks AI output, how it protects your data and how it links the work to enquiries or sales rather than volume.

An AI SEO agency uses artificial intelligence tools to help with search engine optimisation: analysing search patterns, finding content gaps, grouping keywords and speeding up technical and content work. The useful version combines those tools with human judgement on strategy, accuracy and brand. For a UK business, the real question is not whether an agency uses AI, because many now do, but whether the way it uses AI leads to better pages, better decisions and more of the right enquiries.

This guide explains what the service normally includes, how it differs from related services, what it cannot do and how to compare providers before you sign anything.

What an AI SEO agency does

The label covers a range of work, so ask for specifics. A well-run service will usually include most of the following.

  • Search analysis: using AI to sort large sets of queries by topic and intent, so patterns become visible more quickly than they would in a spreadsheet.
  • Content gap research: comparing the subjects your customers search for with the subjects your site covers, then deciding which gaps are worth filling.
  • Content briefs: preparing outlines that cover the questions a buyer asks, with a person deciding the angle and what your business can honestly say.
  • Technical support: helping to review crawl data, spot patterns in errors and draft items such as structured data, with changes checked before release.
  • Existing page improvement: finding pages that already earn impressions but few clicks, then improving their titles, openings and coverage.
  • Reporting support: summarising performance data so that the discussion can focus on what to do next.

Notice what sits behind each item: a decision made by a person. AI can suggest a keyword group, but someone who understands your margins and customers has to decide whether it is worth pursuing.

AI SEO, GEO and AI marketing: what is the difference?

These terms are often used interchangeably, which makes proposals hard to compare. A simple way to separate them is to ask what each one is aimed at.

TermMain focusTypical question it answers
AI SEOUsing AI to do search optimisation workHow can we find and fix ranking opportunities more effectively?
GEOVisibility inside AI-generated answersIs our brand mentioned or cited when people ask AI tools for help?
AI marketingUsing AI across marketing channelsWhere can AI help across ads, content, email and analysis?

In practice they overlap. Clear, well-structured and accurate pages tend to help in both ordinary search results and AI answers. Where AI helps most in SEO

AI is at its strongest on tasks that are large, repetitive and easy for a person to check afterwards.

  • Clustering a long keyword export into topics and likely search intent.
  • Drafting several title and description options for a person to choose from and refine.
  • Summarising what the current top results cover, as a starting point for a brief.
  • Spotting patterns in technical crawl exports, such as groups of similar pages with the same fault.
  • Finding internal linking opportunities across a large site.

Each of these saves time without handing over responsibility. If the result is wrong, a reviewer has a realistic chance of noticing before anything reaches your website.

Where human judgement still matters

Some parts of SEO depend on knowledge a tool does not have, or on a level of care you cannot delegate.

  • Commercial priorities: a query can look attractive and still bring buyers you cannot serve profitably.
  • Accuracy: AI tools can state incorrect information confidently. Facts, prices, legal points and product details all need checking.
  • First-hand knowledge: your real experience, examples and opinions are what make a page different from everything else on the subject. A tool cannot supply them for you.
  • Brand voice: unedited output tends to sound like everyone else's unedited output.
  • Regulated subjects: health, finance and legal content needs review by someone qualified to judge it.

Google's published guidance focuses on whether content is helpful and made for people, rather than simply how it was produced. It also warns against producing large amounts of low-value content to manipulate rankings. Check the current guidance yourself, and treat any plan built mainly on publishing volume with caution.

Warning signs when comparing providers

  • Promises of guaranteed rankings: nobody controls Google's results, with or without AI.
  • Output counted as success: a promise of a large number of articles each month tells you little about whether those articles will be useful.
  • No review step: if the provider cannot explain who checks AI output, assume that nobody does.
  • Vague methods: "our proprietary AI" is not an explanation. Ask what it actually does and what is done by people.
  • Ignoring the basics: AI will not rescue a site that search engines cannot crawl, or pages that fail to answer the query.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. Which parts of the work use AI, and which are done by people?
  2. Who checks AI output for accuracy, and what do they check it against?
  3. What happens to any customer data, analytics or documents we share? Are they entered into third-party tools?
  4. Who owns the content and reports produced during the contract?
  5. How will you prioritise work in the first months, and why?
  6. What will you report on, and how does it connect to enquiries or sales?
  7. Does the service include GEO and AI answer visibility, or is that separate?
  8. What access do you need, and what happens to it if we stop working together?

The data question deserves particular care. If personal data could be entered into an AI tool, you should understand how it will be handled. Your contract and privacy arrangements should reflect this, and it is sensible to check current ICO guidance or take advice if you are unsure.

What to prepare before speaking to an agency

You will get a more useful conversation, and more comparable proposals, if you gather a few things first.

  • Your most profitable services or products, and the customers you most want.
  • Access to Google Search Console and your analytics account, or at least a recent export.
  • A note of any previous SEO work and any sudden changes in traffic.
  • The competitors you meet most often in real sales conversations.
  • Any rules your sector places on what you can claim in marketing.
  • Your own view on AI-written content, including what must always be reviewed by your team.

Is an AI SEO agency right for your business?

This kind of service tends to make most sense when you have a meaningful amount of search data or content to work through, or when your team understands the business but lacks the time to analyse and act on what search is telling you. A larger catalogue or content library usually gives AI-assisted analysis more to work with.

It may matter less if you have a very small site in a narrow local market. There, getting the basics right and building trust locally may do more than any sophisticated analysis. Either way, the label matters less than the plan. An honest provider should be willing to tell you when a simple fix is the better first step.

FAQs

Is AI-written content bad for SEO?

Not automatically. The bigger risk is publishing inaccurate, thin or generic pages, however they were produced. Have a knowledgeable person review anything before it goes live, and add the first-hand detail only your business can provide.

Will AI SEO get results faster?

AI can speed up research and preparation, but rankings still depend on factors such as competition, your site's strength and how search engines respond. Be wary of anyone who promises a fixed timescale for rankings.

Is AI SEO the same as GEO?

No. AI SEO generally means using AI to carry out search optimisation, while GEO aims to make your brand visible and cited in AI-generated answers. Some providers offer both, so ask which is included.

Do I still need ordinary SEO?

Yes. Crawlable pages, clear structure, useful content and a trustworthy reputation remain the foundation. AI changes how some of the work gets done rather than removing the need for it.

Can my own team do this with AI tools?

Often, for some tasks. A capable team can use AI for drafting and research. An agency may add value through technical experience, a wider view of search and the time to keep the work moving.

How do I measure whether it is working?

Look at the search performance of the pages you care about, then at the enquiries or sales they bring in. Output figures, such as the number of pages produced, show activity rather than results.

Next steps

List the services or products that matter most to your business, then check how they perform in Search Console. That will show you where better analysis could make a real difference.
