Google Business Profile Posts: How UK Small Businesses Can Use Them

Google Business Profile posts let you share updates, offers and events on Search and Maps. Here is how to write them, what to post and how often in the UK.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Posts add fresh, useful information to your profile where local customers already look.
  • Use updates, offers and events with a clear photo and a single call to action.
  • A short, regular routine beats bursts of activity followed by months of silence.

Google Business Profile posts are short updates, offers or event notices that you publish from your profile and that can appear on your listing in Google Search and Maps. They are a simple, free way to show customers what is new, highlight a service or promote an offer, right where people are already comparing local businesses.

This guide explains the types of post, how to write one that gets noticed, what to post about and how to fit it into a small business week. Google changes post types and features from time to time, so check the current Google Business Profile help pages for what is available on your profile.

What Google Business Profile posts are

Posts sit within your Business Profile, alongside your hours, reviews, photos and contact details. A post usually combines a short piece of text, an image or video and an optional button, such as Learn more, Book or Call. Depending on how Google displays your profile, posts may show in an updates section or tab.

They are not a replacement for a website or for the core profile work of choosing the right categories, writing a good description and collecting reviews. Our Google Business Profile optimisation guide covers that foundation. Posts are the regular top-up that keeps the profile looking alive.

Types of post

Post typeWhat it is forExample for a UK small business
UpdateGeneral news, tips or highlightsA photo of a finished garden project with a short note on the plants used
OfferA promotion with dates and optional termsTen per cent off winter boiler services booked before the end of the month
EventSomething happening on specific datesA late-night Christmas shopping evening in your shop

Some business types, such as restaurants and hotels, may see slightly different options. Check what your own profile shows.

How to create a post

  1. Sign in with an account that manages the profile and open it from Google Search, Maps or the profile manager.
  2. Choose the option to add an update, offer or event.
  3. Add a photo or video. Use a real, well-lit image of your work, products or premises rather than generic stock.
  4. Write the text. Keep the important point in the first sentence, as longer text may be cut off in previews.
  5. Add a button and link it to the most relevant page on your website.
  6. Preview and publish. Posts may be reviewed before they appear, and some can be rejected if they break content policies.

Writing posts that get noticed

A good post reads like a helpful note from a local business, not an advert from a national chain. Some practical tips:

  • Lead with the useful bit. Opening times, the offer, the date or the result should come first.
  • Be specific and local. Mention the area or type of job in natural language where it helps the reader.
  • One message per post. Do not cram three offers into one update.
  • Use a clear button that matches the action, such as Book for appointments and Call for urgent services.
  • Avoid phone numbers in the text. Google's content rules have restricted this, and your number already appears on the profile.
  • Keep claims honest. Offers must be real, with clear dates, and must follow advertising rules such as the CAP Code.

What to post about

Running out of ideas is the main reason posting stops. Keep a list you can dip into:

  • Recent jobs or projects, with permission from the customer and no private details.
  • New products, services or seasonal ranges.
  • Changes to opening hours, especially around bank holidays and Christmas.
  • Answers to questions customers often ask, linking to a fuller page on your site.
  • Behind-the-scenes photos of your premises or process, without naming individual staff if they prefer.
  • Local events you are attending or hosting.
  • Genuine offers with clear dates and terms.

Your website is the natural home for the fuller version. A post about a recent bathroom fit can link to your bathroom service page; a post about a seasonal range can link to the collection. If your service pages are thin, our guide to service page design explains what they should include.

How often to post

There is no official ideal frequency. For most small businesses, a sustainable rhythm is more valuable than a burst of activity. A realistic routine might be:

Business situationSuggested rhythmFocus
Owner-run, very busyEvery two weeksRecent work and hours changes
Small team with some marketing timeWeeklyMix of updates, tips and offers
Seasonal businessMore often in peak monthsEvents, offers and availability

Batch the work. Take photos as you go, then spend half an hour once a fortnight writing and scheduling. Some third-party tools can schedule posts; check that any tool you use is reputable and that you keep control of your profile.

Do posts help local SEO?

Google has not said that posts directly improve rankings, and it would be unwise to promise they do. What posts can do is make your profile more useful and more complete, give people a reason to click through to your website, and show that the business is active. Those things support the wider local picture described in our local SEO guide.

To see whether posts drive visits, add UTM parameters to the button links so the traffic is labelled in analytics. Our guide to website analytics for small businesses explains how to read that data.

A simple post template

If a blank box puts you off, use a repeatable structure. Most good posts fit this pattern:

  1. Headline sentence: the news, offer or result in plain words.
  2. One or two supporting sentences: who it is for, when it applies and any useful detail.
  3. A clear next step: the button, linked to the right page.

For example, a dog groomer might write that weekend slots are now available for the next month, explain that the new slots suit owners who work weekdays, and add a Book button linked to the booking page. A garden centre might announce that Christmas trees have arrived, mention sizes and delivery to nearby villages, and link to the relevant collection.

Keep a shared folder of good photos and a note of past posts. Looking back at what you posted a year ago is an easy way to plan seasonal updates, and it stops you repeating the same message too often. If you also post on social media, many updates can be adapted rather than written from scratch, though keep each version suited to where it appears.

Before publishing, read the post on a phone. Most people will see it on a small screen, so the first line and the image need to do the work on their own.

Common mistakes

  • Posting once and stopping, leaving an offer from last year visible.
  • Using the same stock photo on every post.
  • Keyword stuffing with lists of towns and services.
  • Linking every post to the homepage instead of the most relevant page.
  • Ignoring rejected posts. If a post does not appear, check the profile for a notice and review the content policy.

FAQs

What are Google Business Profile posts?

They are short updates, offers or events you publish from your Business Profile, usually with an image and a button. They can appear on your listing in Google Search and Maps.

How long do Google Business Profile posts last?

This has changed over time. Event and offer posts are tied to their dates, while updates may remain visible longer. Check the current help pages for how your profile shows older posts.

Can I schedule Google Business Profile posts?

Scheduling options inside Google have been limited, but some third-party social and listings tools offer it. Check the tool is reputable and that you keep ownership of the profile.

Why was my post rejected?

Posts can be rejected for breaking content policies, such as including phone numbers in the text, misleading claims or inappropriate images. Review the policy, edit the post and try again.

Do posts improve my Google Maps ranking?

Google has not confirmed a direct ranking effect. Posts make your profile more useful and active, which can support visits and enquiries, but the core profile, reviews and website matter more.

What size should a post image be?

Use a clear, good-quality landscape image and keep key details away from the edges, as crops vary by device. Check Google's help pages for current recommended sizes.
