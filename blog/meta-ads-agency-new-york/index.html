Meta Ads Agency New York: How Remote Facebook and Instagram Ads Work for NYC Brands

Looking for a Meta Ads agency in New York? A remote team can set up tracking, creative testing and Facebook and Instagram campaigns for NYC brands. See how.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good Meta Ads agency for a New York business starts with reliable tracking, then tests creative and audiences steadily rather than chasing quick wins.
  • New York is crowded and expensive to advertise in, so sharp creative, careful targeting and landing pages that match the ad do most of the heavy lifting.
  • We are a UK-based studio managing Meta Ads remotely, with retainers from £350 a month, ad spend paid separately and other currencies quoted on request.

If you need a Meta Ads agency in New York, the right choice is the team that can turn Facebook and Instagram spend into sales, bookings and leads you can measure, wherever that team is based. A remote team can set up tracking, plan creative and manage campaigns for New York fashion and beauty brands, restaurants, studios, clinics, professional firms and online stores, as long as the work is built on your margins and your customers.

We are a UK-based studio that manages Meta Ads remotely for US businesses. We have no New York office and no US office. This guide explains what Meta Ads management for a New York business should include, how to approach targeting and creative in a crowded market, how a remote engagement works and what it costs.

What a Meta Ads agency in New York should do

New York has one of the busiest advertising markets anywhere. Fashion, beauty, food and drink, fitness, hospitality, media, finance and professional services all compete for the same feeds, so attention is expensive and creative wears out quickly. Good Meta Ads management usually covers:

  • Tracking: the Meta pixel and Conversions API set up so purchases, leads and bookings are recorded as accurately as possible.
  • Account structure: a simple set of campaigns for prospecting, retargeting and retention, rather than dozens of tiny ad sets.
  • Audiences: broad targeting where the algorithm has enough data, supported by customer lists and lookalikes where they help.
  • Creative: a steady pipeline of images, short videos and copy variations to test.
  • Offers and landing pages: clear offers and pages that continue the message of the ad.
  • Budget management: spend moved towards what works, with clear rules for scaling and pausing.
  • Reporting: a monthly summary of spend, results, cost per result and what we are testing next.

Tracking comes first, because Meta's delivery system learns from the conversions you send it. Our Meta pixel setup guide walks through the essentials, and our guide to lookalike audiences explains when they still help and when broad targeting does better.

Targeting New York without wasting spend

The New York metro area is enormous, and the right targeting depends entirely on what you sell. A restaurant in the West Village or a gym in Williamsburg may only want people within a few miles. A dental practice in Midtown might target commuters from New Jersey and Long Island as well as Manhattan residents. An online fashion brand based in Brooklyn may sell across the whole country and treat New York as just one market. Useful questions to answer before launch include:

  • Where can you actually serve? Local businesses should target the boroughs, towns or radius they genuinely cover.
  • Who are you trying to reach? Residents, commuters, visitors and national buyers behave differently and may need separate campaigns.
  • What is the conversion? A booking, a purchase, a lead form or a store visit each needs a different set-up.
  • How much data do you have? Smaller accounts usually do better with fewer, broader campaigns so the system can learn.

Seasons matter too. Holiday gifting, summer events, back-to-school and the run-up to the new year all shift demand and costs, so creative and budgets should be planned ahead rather than rushed in the week before.

Creative is the main lever

With broad targeting now common, creative does much of the work that detailed targeting used to do. The ad itself tells the system who it is for. For a New York audience used to polished brands and constant noise, that means:

  • A strong first second: show the product, the result or the problem straight away in video.
  • Native formats: vertical video for Reels and Stories, square or portrait images for feeds.
  • Several angles: test different benefits, objections and offers rather than tiny wording changes.
  • Real footage where possible: genuine product, venue or team footage tends to feel more credible than stock imagery.
  • Regular refreshes: new creative before performance drops, rather than after.

We use AI-powered tools to help with copy variations, creative briefs and reporting, but every ad and every account change is reviewed by a person. The landing page matters just as much as the ad: it should load fast on mobile, repeat the offer clearly and make the next step obvious. Our guide to landing page design covers the essentials. How remote Meta Ads management works for a New York business

New York is on Eastern Time, usually five hours behind the UK. That gives a comfortable overlap from your morning into our afternoon for calls, creative reviews and approvals. Campaign changes can be made before your day starts and checked when you log on. A typical engagement looks like this:

  1. Discovery call: products or services, margins, audiences, past results and what success looks like.
  2. Audit or set-up: a review of your existing account, pixel and events, or a clean build.
  3. Plan: campaign structure, budgets, creative needs and testing priorities, agreed in writing.
  4. Creative: briefs and assets prepared with you, using your footage, photos and brand guidelines.
  5. Launch and optimisation: close monitoring early on, then regular testing and budget changes.
  6. Reporting: a monthly summary of spend, results and next steps.

You own your Meta Business account, ad account and pixel, and you pay Meta directly. Our guide to working with a remote PPC agency for US businesses covers access, billing and communication in more detail.

FAQs

How do I choose a Meta Ads agency in New York?

Look for a team that checks tracking first, explains its testing plan in writing, talks about creative as much as targeting and lets you own every account. Be wary of guaranteed returns or accounts held in the agency's name.

Do you have an office in New York?

No. We are a UK-based studio and work remotely with US businesses. We have no New York office or any other US office, and we work through shared access, video calls and written reports.

Do you run both Facebook and Instagram ads?

Yes. Meta Ads covers Facebook, Instagram and other Meta placements from one ad account. We choose formats and placements based on where your audience responds best.

Is ad spend included in your fee?

No. Ad spend is paid separately and directly to Meta from your own account. Our retainer covers management, testing, creative direction and reporting.

How much does a Meta Ads agency in New York cost?

Our management retainers are Launch at £350 a month, Growth at £750 and Scale at £1,500, plus your ad spend. Prices are in pounds, and we can quote US dollars or other currencies on request.

Can you produce video ads if we are in New York?

We can plan concepts, write scripts and edit footage you or a local creator capture. We do not film in person in New York, as we work remotely from the UK.
