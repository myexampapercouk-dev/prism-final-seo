Wix to WordPress Migration: How to Move Your Website Without Losing Traffic

Wix to WordPress migration guide for UK businesses: when to move, what transfers, redirects, SEO protection, content, forms and planning a smooth switchover.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Wix has no simple one-click export to WordPress, so a migration is really a careful rebuild plus a content move.
  • Redirecting every old URL to its new equivalent is the most important step for protecting search visibility.
  • Plan, build and test on a staging site before switching the domain, then monitor closely after launch.

A Wix to WordPress migration is a common step for UK businesses that have outgrown a website builder. Wix is quick to start with, but some businesses eventually want more control over design, content structure, SEO, integrations or ecommerce. WordPress offers that flexibility, but moving across is not a simple copy and paste. Done carefully, a migration can give you a stronger platform without losing the visibility and enquiries your current site already earns.

This guide explains when a move makes sense, what can and cannot be transferred, how to protect your search rankings and how to plan the switchover. It is aimed at business owners and marketing teams rather than developers, so it focuses on decisions and risks rather than code.

Why businesses move from Wix to WordPress

There is nothing wrong with Wix for many small sites. People usually start thinking about WordPress when they run into limits. Common reasons include:

  • More control. WordPress lets you shape templates, content types and site structure to suit your business rather than working within a builder's options.
  • Ownership. With self-hosted WordPress, you choose your hosting and can move it whenever you like.
  • Content and SEO depth. Businesses that publish a lot of content often want finer control over URLs, categories, structured data and technical settings.
  • Integrations. WordPress has a large ecosystem of plugins and can connect to many CRMs, booking tools and marketing platforms.
  • Ecommerce growth. Some shops want to move to WooCommerce for more flexibility. Our Shopify vs WooCommerce guide may help if you are weighing up ecommerce platforms.

Flexibility comes with responsibility. A WordPress site needs hosting, updates, backups and security care that Wix handles for you. Make sure you are comfortable with that, or have someone to help, before you commit. Our website maintenance guide explains what is involved.

Is migrating the right move for you?

Before starting, be clear about what problem you are solving. If your Wix site works well, brings in enquiries and you simply want a fresh look, a redesign within Wix might be enough. If you are repeatedly hitting limits, a move may be worthwhile. Questions to ask include:

  • What can we not do on Wix that we need to do?
  • Who will look after the WordPress site once it is live?
  • How much traffic and how many enquiries does the current site generate, and from which pages?
  • Is this a good time, or would a busy season make a switch risky?

What transfers and what does not

Wix does not offer a full export that WordPress can import directly. In practice, a migration usually involves rebuilding the design in WordPress and moving the content across. Here is how the main elements tend to work:

  • Blog posts. Wix blogs can often be exported through an RSS feed and imported into WordPress, although formatting, images and categories usually need checking and tidying.
  • Pages. Static pages generally need to be recreated. This is a good chance to improve the copy and structure rather than copying everything as it is.
  • Images and files. Media needs to be downloaded and re-uploaded, ideally with sensible file names and alt text.
  • Design. Wix templates cannot be moved to WordPress. The design is rebuilt, either from a theme or as a custom build.
  • Forms, bookings and apps. Wix apps do not transfer. Each one needs a WordPress equivalent or an external tool.
  • Products and customers. Store data may be exportable in spreadsheet form, but it needs mapping to the new platform's format and checking carefully.

Make a full inventory of everything on your current site before you begin, so nothing is forgotten.

Protecting your SEO during migration

The biggest risk in any platform move is losing search visibility. Search engines have learned to trust your existing URLs. If those URLs disappear without a proper redirect, rankings and traffic can drop. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings covers the principles in depth. The key steps are below.

Crawl and record your current site

Before changing anything, make a list of every URL on your Wix site, along with its page title, meta description and main heading. Note which pages attract the most traffic and links, using your analytics and Google Search Console. These are the pages you most need to protect.

Map old URLs to new ones

Wix often uses URL patterns that differ from WordPress, particularly for blog posts and product pages. Create a redirect map that pairs each old URL with the most relevant new page. Avoid sending everything to the homepage, as that tends to lose the value of individual pages.

Set up 301 redirects

Permanent 301 redirects tell search engines and browsers that a page has moved. They should be in place the moment the new site goes live. Test a sample of old URLs straight after launch to make sure they land on the right pages.

Keep what works

Carry across titles, headings and content that already perform well, improving them where it helps but not discarding them unnecessarily. Keep internal links pointing to the correct new URLs rather than relying on redirects.

Technical checks

Make sure the new site is indexable, has an XML sitemap, uses HTTPS throughout and loads quickly on mobile. Remove any setting that blocks search engines once the site goes live, since staging sites are usually hidden. Our technical SEO audit checklist is a useful reference.

Planning the build

A migration is a good opportunity to improve your website, but scope creep can delay it. Agree the essentials first: the pages you need, the features that must work on day one and what can wait for later.

Decide whether a well-built theme will meet your needs or whether a custom design is worth it. Keep plugins to the minimum you genuinely need, as each one adds maintenance. Choose reliable hosting suited to WordPress and make sure the hosting and domain accounts are in your business's name.

Build the new site on a staging environment so you can review it properly before anyone else sees it. Check every page on mobile and desktop, test forms and bookings, and confirm that emails from the site actually arrive.

Domains and email

If you bought your domain through Wix, you can usually either transfer it to another registrar or point it at your new hosting while leaving it registered where it is. Either way, take care with DNS settings. If your business email uses the same domain, changing DNS records carelessly can interrupt email. Note your existing records before making changes, and plan the switch for a quieter time.

Remember to cancel your Wix plan only after the new site is live, stable and fully checked, and after you have saved any data you need.

Launch day and the weeks after

On launch day, switch the domain, confirm redirects are working, submit the new sitemap in Search Console and check analytics and conversion tracking are recording correctly. Test the contact form and any checkout one more time on the live site.

In the weeks after launch, keep an eye on:

  • Crawl errors and pages reported as not found in Search Console.
  • Traffic to your most important pages compared with before the move.
  • Enquiries, bookings or sales.
  • Page speed and mobile usability.

Some fluctuation in rankings after a migration is normal while search engines process the changes. A sustained drop on particular pages is a sign to check redirects and content for those URLs.

Common Wix to WordPress migration mistakes

  • Launching without a complete redirect map.
  • Redirecting all old pages to the homepage.
  • Forgetting to remove the search engine block from the staging site.
  • Breaking business email through careless DNS changes.
  • Losing form, booking or tracking functionality that worked on Wix.
  • Installing too many plugins and slowing the new site down.
  • Cancelling the Wix plan before everything has been saved and checked.

FAQs

Can I export my Wix site directly to WordPress?

Not fully. Blog posts can often be moved using an RSS export, but pages, design, apps and forms generally need to be rebuilt in WordPress.

Will I lose my Google rankings if I move from Wix?

There is always some risk, but careful planning reduces it. Mapping and redirecting every old URL, keeping strong content and checking technical SEO are the most important steps.

Can I keep my domain name?

Yes. You can transfer the domain away from Wix or point it to your new hosting. Take care with DNS settings, especially if your email uses the same domain.

How long does a Wix to WordPress migration take?

It depends on the size of the site, the number of features and how much content needs rewriting. A small brochure site is simpler than a large blog or online shop.

Is WordPress harder to manage than Wix?

Day-to-day editing can be straightforward, but WordPress needs updates, backups and security care that Wix handles for you. Plan who will take responsibility for that.

Should I move my Wix store to WooCommerce?

It can make sense if you need more flexibility. Compare options first; our WooCommerce agency guide explains what to look for in support.
