Ecommerce Delivery and Returns Pages: How to Write Clear Shipping Information

A UK guide to writing ecommerce delivery and returns pages: delivery options, costs, timescales, international shipping, returns steps and where to show them.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shoppers often check delivery and returns before they buy, so unclear information can stop a purchase.
  • State options, costs, timescales and conditions plainly, and show key points on product, basket and checkout pages too.
  • Returns information must reflect consumer law and your own terms accurately, so check it carefully and keep it current.

Delivery and returns pages rarely get much design attention, yet they answer some of the most important questions a shopper has. How much will delivery cost? When will my order arrive? What happens if it does not fit or is not right? If those answers are hard to find or unclear, a hesitant shopper may simply leave and buy elsewhere.

This guide explains how to plan and write clear delivery and returns information for a UK online shop, and where that information should appear across your site. It is practical guidance rather than legal advice; returns in particular are shaped by consumer law, so it is worth checking your wording against current guidance or with a legal adviser.

Why delivery and returns information matters

Unexpected costs and uncertainty are common reasons shoppers abandon a basket. If delivery costs only appear at the final step of checkout, or delivery times are vague, shoppers may lose confidence. Clear returns information helps too, particularly for first-time customers, clothing and footwear, and higher-value items, because it reduces the perceived risk of buying.

Good information also helps after the sale. Customers who know what to expect are less likely to contact you asking where their order is, and returns are more straightforward when the process is explained clearly.

What to include on a delivery page

Delivery options

List each option you offer, such as standard, express, named-day or click and collect. For each, explain what it means in plain language. Avoid internal or courier-specific terms that customers may not understand.

Costs

Show the cost of each option clearly. If you offer free delivery above a certain order value, state the threshold. If costs vary by weight, size or location, explain how, and consider a simple table. Shoppers should be able to work out what they will pay before they reach checkout.

Timescales

Explain how long each option usually takes, and be clear about what the timescale covers. For example, does "two to three working days" start from when the order is placed or when it is dispatched? State order cut-off times for same-day dispatch, and explain how weekends and bank holidays affect delivery.

Delivery areas

Be clear about where you deliver. Some areas of the UK, such as the Scottish Highlands and islands, Northern Ireland, the Isle of Man and the Channel Islands, may have different costs, timescales or restrictions depending on your couriers. If this applies, say so plainly rather than leaving shoppers to discover it at checkout.

International delivery

If you ship outside the UK, explain which countries you deliver to, costs and timescales. Mention that customers may need to pay import duties, taxes or handling fees in their country, and be clear about who is responsible for these. Rules for shipping to the EU and elsewhere have changed in recent years and can be complex, so check current requirements for the destinations you serve.

Tracking and what to expect

Explain whether orders are tracked and how customers will receive tracking information. Describe what happens if nobody is home, such as safe-place delivery, a neighbour or a collection point, depending on your couriers.

Problems with delivery

Tell customers what to do if an order is late, damaged or missing, and how to contact you. A simple, reassuring explanation reduces anxiety and helps you handle issues consistently.

What to include on a returns page

Customers' rights and your policy

In the UK, consumers generally have legal rights when buying online, including a cancellation period for most distance purchases and rights when goods are faulty or not as described. There are exceptions, for example for some personalised or perishable items. Your returns page should not suggest customers have fewer rights than the law gives them. Many shops also offer a more generous policy, such as a longer returns window. Be clear about which parts are your own policy and keep the wording consistent with your terms of sale. Because the details matter, it is sensible to check current official guidance or take legal advice when writing this section.

How to return an item

Set out the steps clearly, ideally as a numbered list. For example: how to tell you about the return, whether a returns form or online portal is used, how to package the item and where to send it. The simpler the process, the fewer questions you will receive.

Return costs

Explain who pays for return postage in different situations, such as change-of-mind returns compared with faulty items. If you provide a returns label, explain how to get one.

Condition of returned items

Explain any reasonable expectations about the condition of returned goods, such as unworn items with tags, while staying consistent with consumer rights.

Refunds and exchanges

Explain how and when refunds are made, and whether exchanges are available. Be careful that any timescales you state are consistent with your legal obligations and that you can meet them.

Items that cannot be returned

If certain items are excluded from change-of-mind returns, list them clearly and explain why. Make sure any exclusions are permitted for that type of product.

Writing style for delivery and returns pages

These pages work best when they are calm, plain and easy to scan:

  • Use headings for each topic so shoppers can jump to what they need.
  • Put the most common answers first.
  • Use tables for costs and timescales.
  • Use numbered steps for processes.
  • Write in your normal brand voice, but prioritise clarity.
  • Avoid long blocks of legal text; link to full terms where needed.

Where to show delivery and returns information

A dedicated page is important, but shoppers should not have to find it themselves. Show key points throughout the buying journey:

  • Header or announcement bar: a short message such as your free delivery threshold, if you offer one.
  • Product pages: a brief summary of delivery options and returns near the add-to-basket button, with a link to full details.
  • Basket: estimated delivery costs, and how far the shopper is from any free delivery threshold.
  • Checkout: clear delivery choices, costs and expected dates before payment.
  • Order confirmation and emails: what happens next, tracking information and how to return.
  • Footer: links to delivery and returns pages on every page.

Keeping information consistent

Delivery and returns details often appear in several places: the dedicated pages, product pages, checkout, emails, terms of sale and FAQs. If they disagree, customers will notice. Keep one source of truth, update every location when something changes and review the pages regularly, especially before busy periods such as Christmas when cut-off dates and returns windows may change.

Seasonal information, such as last order dates for Christmas delivery, is worth publishing in good time and removing promptly once it no longer applies.

Platform considerations

Platforms such as Shopify and WooCommerce let you configure delivery zones, rates and rules, and display them at checkout. The settings in your shop should match what your delivery page says. If you use apps or plugins for returns or delivery date estimates, check that they show accurate information and do not slow the site down. Delivery and returns pages and search

Shoppers sometimes search for delivery and returns information directly, such as your brand name plus "returns". Clear, indexable pages help them find the answer. Structured data for shipping and returns can also give search engines more information about your policies; our schema markup guide explains structured data in general.

Common mistakes

  • Hiding delivery costs until the final checkout step.
  • Vague timescales with no explanation of what they cover.
  • Not mentioning areas with different delivery costs or restrictions.
  • Returns wording that understates customers' legal rights.
  • Complicated return processes with unclear steps.
  • Inconsistent information across pages and emails.
  • Out-of-date seasonal cut-off dates.

A simple checklist

  • Every delivery option explained with cost and timescale.
  • Free delivery threshold stated, if offered.
  • Delivery areas and any exceptions explained.
  • International delivery, duties and taxes covered, if relevant.
  • Returns steps set out clearly.
  • Return costs, refunds and exclusions explained.
  • Wording checked against consumer rights and your terms.
  • Key points shown on product, basket and checkout pages.
  • A review schedule, especially before busy periods.

FAQs

Do I need separate delivery and returns pages?

Many shops use two pages so each is easy to find, while smaller shops sometimes combine them. Either works if the information is clear and easy to locate.

Should I offer free delivery?

It depends on your margins and products. A free delivery threshold is a common approach. Whatever you choose, state it clearly.

Can I refuse returns?

Customers generally have legal rights to cancel most online orders and to remedies for faulty goods, with some exceptions. Check current guidance before writing any restrictions.

Where should delivery costs appear?

Ideally before checkout: on product pages, in the basket and on a dedicated delivery page, so there are no surprises.

How often should I update these pages?

Whenever couriers, costs or policies change, and before seasonal peaks when cut-off dates and returns windows may differ.
