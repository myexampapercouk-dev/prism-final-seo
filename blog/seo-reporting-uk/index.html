SEO Reporting for UK Businesses: What a Good Monthly Report Should Show

Good SEO reporting links rankings and traffic to enquiries and sales. Learn which metrics to track, how to set up reports and what to ask your agency.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A useful SEO report starts with business outcomes such as enquiries, sales and revenue, then explains the search metrics that led to them.
  • Use Google Search Console for search visibility and GA4 for what visitors did next. Neither tells the full story alone.
  • Every report should end with what was done, what was learned and what happens next, not just a page of charts.

SEO reporting means showing how organic search is contributing to your business: how visible your site is, how many people arrive from search, and how many of them enquire or buy. A good monthly SEO report for a UK business leads with conversions and revenue from organic search, then explains the visibility, traffic and technical changes behind them, and finishes with the work planned for next month.

Many reports do the opposite: pages of ranking tables and traffic graphs with no link to enquiries, and no explanation of what changed or why. This guide explains what to measure, which tools to use, how to structure a report, and the questions to ask if you are paying an agency for SEO.

Why SEO reporting goes wrong

SEO is slow and noisy. Rankings move daily, search engines change their results pages, seasonality shifts demand, and AI Overviews can reduce clicks even when your visibility rises. That makes it easy to cherry-pick numbers that look good or to drown the reader in data. The common problems are:

  • Vanity metrics. Total impressions or a ranking for a term nobody buys from.
  • No context. A traffic drop shown without noting that the same month last year also dropped.
  • No commercial link. Traffic reported, but not leads or sales.
  • No narrative. Charts without explanation of what caused the change or what will happen next.

Group your metrics into four layers, from business results down to technical health. The higher layers are what decision makers need. The lower layers explain them.

Setting up the data properly

Reporting is only as good as the tracking behind it. Before you judge any SEO campaign, check these foundations:

  1. Google Search Console verified as a Domain property, so it covers every version of your site, with your sitemap submitted.
  2. GA4 set up with key events for the actions that matter: form submissions, calls from the site, bookings, purchases. Our GA4 setup guide walks through this.
  3. Consent mode configured correctly. Under UK GDPR and PECR, visitors who decline analytics cookies will not be fully tracked, so GA4 numbers will understate real traffic. Reports should say so rather than treat the figures as complete.
  4. Call tracking and CRM links where phone and offline sales matter, so organic leads are not lost once they leave the website.
  5. Annotations for site changes, migrations, algorithm updates and campaigns, so movements can be explained later.

If the site has been rebuilt or moved recently, compare like for like with care. Our SEO migration checklist explains what to benchmark before and after a move.

How to structure a monthly SEO report

A good report can be read in five minutes by a business owner and still be useful to a marketing manager. We suggest this structure:

  1. Summary. Three to five sentences: what happened, why, and what it means for the business.
  2. Outcomes. Organic enquiries or revenue this month, compared with last month and the same month last year.
  3. Visibility. Clicks and impressions from Search Console, with the pages and query groups that moved most.
  4. Work completed. Pages published or improved, technical fixes, links earned.
  5. Insights. What the data suggests: new queries appearing, pages close to the first page, content that is underperforming.
  6. Next month. The specific actions planned and why.

Always compare year on year as well as month on month. Many UK sectors are seasonal: garden products in spring, accountancy around the January self-assessment deadline, retail around Black Friday and Christmas. Month-on-month comparisons alone can be misleading.

Reporting on AI search visibility

AI Overviews and chat-based search tools are changing how people find information, and standard tools only show part of the picture. Search Console includes AI Overview appearances within normal web search data, but does not separate them out. A practical approach is to track a set of priority questions manually or with specialist tools, note whether your brand is cited, and watch for referral traffic from AI tools in GA4. Reading SEO data without fooling yourself

Even with good tracking, it is easy to draw the wrong conclusion. A few habits make reports more honest and more useful:

  • Segment branded and non-branded searches. Searches for your company name mostly reflect brand awareness, offline marketing and word of mouth. Non-branded searches, such as "accountant in Nottingham", show whether SEO is winning new customers who did not know you before. Report them separately.
  • Look at pages and query groups, not single keywords. One keyword dropping two places means little. A whole group of related queries falling for one page is worth investigating.
  • Check average position with care. Search Console's average position can rise or fall simply because you start appearing for new, lower-ranking queries. Read it alongside clicks and impressions.
  • Separate cause from coincidence. A spike after publishing a page may come from a seasonal trend or a press mention. Use annotations and year-on-year data before claiming credit.
  • Watch click-through rate. If impressions grow but clicks do not, the issue may be your titles and descriptions, or AI Overviews answering the question before anyone clicks.

These checks take a few minutes each month and stop a report turning into a list of good news.

How often should you report?

FrequencyBest forContent
Live dashboardDay-to-day monitoringTraffic, conversions, technical alerts
Monthly reportMost businessesOutcomes, visibility, work done, next steps
Quarterly reviewStrategy and budget decisionsTrends, return on investment, priorities for the next quarter

Monthly reports work for most businesses because SEO changes take weeks to show. Quarterly reviews are where you step back, look at the trend and decide whether to change direction. SEO results typically take three to six months to become clear, so judging a new campaign on its first month rarely makes sense.

Questions to ask your SEO agency about reporting

  • Which business outcomes will you report on, and where does that data come from?
  • Do I own the Search Console, GA4 and dashboard accounts, or do you?
  • Will the report explain why numbers changed, not just show them?
  • How will you show the work you did and link it to results?
  • How do you handle seasonality and consent-related tracking gaps?

You should always own your own analytics and Search Console accounts, with the agency given access. Our guides on choosing a digital marketing agency and marketing agency contracts cover this in more detail.

FAQs

What should SEO reporting in the UK include?

Good SEO reporting in the UK should include organic enquiries or revenue, Search Console clicks and impressions, progress on priority keywords, technical health, work completed and plans for next month, with year-on-year comparisons to account for seasonality.

Which tools do I need for SEO reporting?

Google Search Console and GA4 cover most needs. A rank tracker, crawl tool and a dashboard tool are useful additions for larger sites.

Why do Search Console and GA4 show different numbers?

They measure different things. Search Console counts clicks from search results, while GA4 counts sessions on your site and is affected by cookie consent, ad blockers and tracking setup.

Are rankings still worth reporting?

Yes, for a focused set of priority keywords, but they should support the story rather than lead it. Results pages vary by location and device, so treat positions as a guide.

How do I know if SEO is working?

Look for growing organic enquiries or sales over several months, rising impressions for your target topics and more pages ranking on the first page. Judge the trend, not one month.

Should my SEO report include AI Overviews?

Ideally, yes. Track whether your brand is cited for key questions and watch AI referral traffic. If you would like help setting up reporting that ties SEO to results, get in touch.
