WordPress Website Cost UK: What a Business Site Really Costs

WordPress website cost UK: our small sites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000. See what drives the price and why hosting, themes and plugins are extra.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A small WordPress business website from Prism Works Studio typically costs £1,000 to £2,000; a WooCommerce shop typically £1,500 to £3,500.
  • Hosting, premium themes and paid plugins are third-party costs that vary, so we quote them separately.
  • WordPress needs regular updates, backups and security checks, so plan for maintenance as well as the build.

A WordPress website for a UK small business typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 to build with Prism Works Studio, and a WordPress shop using WooCommerce typically costs £1,500 to £3,500. On top of the build you will have running costs for hosting, your domain and any premium theme or paid plugins; those vary with your choices, so we quote them separately.

This guide explains what goes into that price, what makes it rise, and what you need to budget after launch. If you are weighing up website costs more generally, our small business website cost guide is a useful companion.

WordPress itself is free software, which is why people sometimes assume a WordPress website is free. The cost lies in planning, design, building, content, hosting and keeping the site secure.

What you get for the build price

A typical small business WordPress website from us includes:

  • Planning. Agreeing the pages you need, who they are for and what you want visitors to do, such as call, book or enquire.
  • Design. A clean, mobile-first design in your brand, usually built on a lightweight theme rather than a heavy page builder.
  • Core pages. Typically home, about, services, contact and supporting pages, with a blog if you plan to publish.
  • Forms and tracking. Contact forms, GA4 and Search Console so you can see enquiries and traffic.
  • SEO foundations. Sensible URLs, title tags, meta descriptions, headings, an XML sitemap and fast loading.
  • UK compliance basics. Privacy and cookie notices set up with UK GDPR and PECR in mind; final wording rests with you and your advisers.
  • Launch and handover. Testing on mobile and desktop, then showing you how to edit pages yourself.

Our builds typically take 3 to 5 weeks for a small website, depending on how quickly content and feedback arrive.

What makes a WordPress website cost more

Number and type of pages

A five-page brochure site is quicker than a 30-page site with service pages for every location. Each unique template adds design and build time.

Custom functionality

Booking systems, member areas, calculators, directories and integrations with a CRM or accounting system all add work, and some need paid plugins.

Ecommerce

Adding WooCommerce turns a website into a shop, with products, payments, VAT, delivery and stock to configure. That is why shop builds sit in a higher price band. Our WooCommerce agency guide explains what a well-built store needs.

Content

If you provide finished copy and photos, the build moves faster. If we need to help shape the words, that takes more time, but it is often where the real value is, because content drives enquiries and rankings.

Redesigns and migrations

Rebuilding an existing site means mapping old URLs to new ones and keeping what already ranks. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings covers this. Redirects are planned before launch, not after, because lost rankings are much harder to win back than to protect in the first place.

Running costs after launch

A WordPress website is not finished at launch. Plan for these ongoing costs:

Hosting

Good hosting matters more on WordPress than on hosted platforms, because you are responsible for performance. Cheap shared hosting can make a site slow and unreliable. Prices vary widely by provider and by how much traffic and storage you need, so we recommend options and quote them separately.

Themes and plugins

Many sites run happily on a free theme and free plugins. Others benefit from premium licences for forms, SEO, backups or security. Licences are usually annual and their prices vary, so we list them separately and only recommend what you need.

Maintenance

WordPress, themes and plugins release updates regularly, often for security reasons. Someone needs to apply them, check nothing breaks and keep backups. You can do this yourself or have it handled for you; our website maintenance guide explains what is involved.

WordPress vs other platforms on cost

WordPress is a strong choice when you want ownership, flexibility and a good platform for content. Hosted website builders bundle hosting into a subscription and need less maintenance, but limit what you can change. For shops, Shopify offers a hosted alternative to WooCommerce; our Shopify agency guide explains how Shopify projects are scoped.

There is no universally cheaper option. A WordPress website can cost less to run if it is lean and well hosted, or more if it relies on many paid plugins and needs frequent fixes.

Who owns what

Ownership is easy to overlook when comparing prices. With a WordPress website you should hold the domain registration, the hosting account and the admin login in your own name, with your provider given access rather than the other way round. That way you can change suppliers later without losing the site or its content.

Premium theme and plugin licences are worth checking too. Some providers use their own developer licences, which can lapse if you move on. Ask for licences to be registered to your business, or at least for a clear note of which licences the site depends on and when they renew.

Planning a realistic first-year budget

A sensible first-year budget for a WordPress website has three parts. First, the one-off build at the price you have agreed. Second, the running costs for hosting, domain and any licences, which we list separately in our quotes. Third, the time or money you will spend on content and marketing, because a site that nobody finds will not pay for itself. It is also worth keeping a small contingency for changes you only discover you need once real visitors start using the site.

Many small businesses spend most of their budget on the build and leave nothing for getting found. If your budget is tight, a simpler site with a few strong pages and some ongoing SEO usually performs better than an elaborate site with no promotion. Our SEO for small business guide explains where to start.

Cheap WordPress websites: what to watch for

Very low quotes often save money in ways that cost you later:

  • A heavy multipurpose theme that makes the site slow.
  • No redirects when replacing an old site, so rankings drop.
  • No analytics or Search Console, so you cannot see what works.
  • Plugins installed and never updated, creating security risks.
  • Copy written without any thought for search or conversion.

Ask any provider how they handle speed, SEO, updates and ownership of the site. You should own your domain, hosting account and content. Our WordPress agency guide lists more questions to ask.

How to get the most from your budget

  1. Be clear on the main job of the site: enquiries, bookings or sales.
  2. Prepare your content early, including photos and service descriptions.
  3. Start with the pages that matter most to customers and add more as your business grows.
  4. Choose hosting for reliability and speed, not just the lowest price.
  5. Budget for marketing so the site gets found once it is live.

FAQs

What is the WordPress website cost UK small businesses can expect?

With Prism Works Studio, a small business WordPress website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 to build, and a WooCommerce shop £1,500 to £3,500. Hosting, domain, premium themes and paid plugins are separate, variable costs.

Is WordPress free?

The WordPress software is free. You still pay for hosting, a domain, design and build, and any premium themes or plugins you choose.

How long does it take to build a WordPress website?

Our small website builds typically take 3 to 5 weeks. Ecommerce builds typically take 5 to 12 weeks, depending on catalogue size and integrations.

Why is hosting quoted separately?

Hosting is a third-party service and prices vary by provider, traffic and storage. Quoting it separately lets you see exactly what you pay for and keeps the account in your name.

Do WordPress websites need maintenance?

Yes. WordPress, themes and plugins need regular updates, and the site needs backups and security checks. Skipping maintenance is a common cause of hacked or broken sites.

Can I edit my WordPress website myself?

Yes. We hand over sites so you can update text, images and blog posts yourself, and we show you how at launch.
