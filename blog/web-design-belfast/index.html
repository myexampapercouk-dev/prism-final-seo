Web Design in Belfast: Websites for Tech Startups and Northern Ireland Businesses

Web design for Belfast tech startups and NI businesses: product messaging, investor-ready sites, and ecommerce that handles GB, NI and EU customers clearly.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Belfast tech startups need sites that explain technical products simply, support hiring and stand up to scrutiny from customers and investors.
  • Northern Ireland businesses selling online may face different delivery, VAT and customs arrangements for GB, NI and EU customers, so check current rules and present them clearly.
  • Prism Works Studio works with Belfast businesses remotely, without an office in Northern Ireland.

Web design in Belfast for a tech startup or a growing Northern Ireland business brings a mix of familiar and distinctive challenges. Startups need to explain a technical product in plain language, look credible to customers and investors and support hiring. Businesses that sell online may need to handle customers in Great Britain, Northern Ireland, the Republic of Ireland and the wider EU, where delivery, pricing and tax arrangements can differ. Prism Works Studio works with Belfast businesses remotely, so planning and reviews take place over video calls and shared drafts.

Belfast has a lively tech and software community, with activity around areas such as the Cathedral Quarter, the Titanic Quarter, the city centre and near Queen's University, alongside established firms in professional services, manufacturing and food and drink across Northern Ireland. This guide looks first at tech startups, then at practical considerations for NI businesses trading online.

Explaining a technical product simply

Many Belfast startups build software, cyber security, fintech or data products. Founders understand them deeply, which can make it hard to explain them simply. Visitors, including buyers, investors and potential hires, usually decide within moments whether a site is relevant to them.

A useful test for a startup homepage is whether a non-specialist could answer these questions after a quick look:

  • What problem does this solve?
  • Who is it for?
  • What makes it different from the obvious alternative?
  • What should I do next?

Technical depth belongs further into the site: product pages, documentation, security information and integration details. The homepage should earn the right to show that depth. Our guide to SaaS website design covers product page structure in more detail.

Security and data pages for B2B buyers

Business buyers, particularly larger ones, often want to understand how a product handles data before they take a call. A clear security page answers common questions: where data is stored, how it is protected, who can access it and what certifications or frameworks you follow. Only list certifications you have actually achieved, and keep dates current.

Your privacy notice should reflect how your website and product handle personal data under UK GDPR. If you serve customers in the EU, the EU GDPR may also apply. Take advice on which rules apply to your situation, and see the Information Commissioner's Office for UK guidance.

Careers pages that help you hire

Hiring is often as pressing as selling for a growing tech company. A careers page that explains the work, the tech stack in general terms, how the team works, benefits and the hiring process helps candidates decide to apply. Keep role listings current and remove filled roles. If you offer remote or hybrid work across the UK and Ireland, say so clearly.

Investor-ready without the hype

Investors will look at your website. They are not looking for bold claims; they want clarity on the problem, the product and the team. Avoid inflated user numbers, unverifiable logos or vague claims about AI or disruption. If you have customers who have agreed to be named, featuring them can help. If not, a clear product demonstration and honest founder story are stronger than invented social proof. Our guide to marketing for startups covers how the website fits into early growth.

Selling online from Northern Ireland

For Northern Ireland businesses selling physical goods online, there are practical issues that do not affect sellers in Great Britain in the same way. Arrangements under the Windsor Framework mean that goods movements between Great Britain, Northern Ireland and the EU can involve different processes, and VAT and customs treatment can depend on where goods start and finish their journey.

These rules are detailed and change over time, so the specifics should come from GOV.UK guidance, HMRC and your accountant or customs adviser, not from a web designer. What a website can do is present whatever applies clearly:

  • Delivery pages that set out where you ship, how long it takes and any extra charges, separated by destination where they differ.
  • Checkout messaging that tells customers about any duties or fees that might apply before they pay, rather than after.
  • Shipping zones in Shopify or WooCommerce configured to match your actual delivery options for GB, NI, the Republic of Ireland and further afield.
  • Returns information that reflects consumer rights for the customers you sell to.

Pricing in pounds and euros

Some NI businesses sell to customers in the Republic of Ireland as well as the UK. Showing prices in euros as well as pounds can make that easier. Shopify, for example, supports selling in multiple currencies. Think about whether prices should be converted automatically or set manually, how rounding works and how VAT is shown, and confirm the tax treatment with your accountant.

Spelling, terminology and contact details matter too. Show phone numbers in a format that works from both sides of the border, and consider whether your delivery promises differ for each market. Our guide to ecommerce website design covers store structure more broadly.

SEO for Belfast and Northern Ireland businesses

Search considerations depend on who you sell to:

  • Tech startups selling to businesses across the UK, Ireland or further afield often benefit more from topic-led content and product pages than from local search terms.
  • Local service businesses should keep a consistent Google Business Profile and make sure their address and service area are clear. Our local SEO guide covers the basics.
  • Cross-border sellers should consider how pages are targeted at UK and Irish audiences, especially if prices or delivery terms differ.

If you are moving from an older site, our guide to redesigning without losing rankings covers redirects and launch checks.

Platforms for growing businesses

Tech startups often keep the marketing site separate from the product, using WordPress or another content platform so marketing pages can change without touching the app. Product businesses usually choose Shopify or WooCommerce. Whatever you choose, make sure the business owns the domain, hosting and admin accounts.

FAQs

What should a tech startup homepage explain?

The problem you solve, who it is for, what makes you different and the next step a visitor should take. Technical detail can sit on deeper product pages.

Do Northern Ireland online shops have different shipping rules?

Goods moving between Great Britain, Northern Ireland and the EU can involve different processes and tax treatment. Check current GOV.UK and HMRC guidance and take advice, then set out the result clearly on delivery pages and at checkout.

Can my online store show prices in euros?

Yes. Shopify and some WooCommerce set-ups support multiple currencies. Decide whether to convert automatically or set prices manually, and confirm how VAT should be shown with your accountant.

Should a startup include a security page?

If you sell to businesses, usually yes. Explain how data is stored and protected and list only certifications you genuinely hold.

Does a careers page really help hiring?

It can. Candidates often check a company website before applying, and a clear page about the work, team and process helps them decide.
