Estate Agent Marketing in the UK: How to Win More Vendor and Landlord Instructions

Estate agent marketing in the UK should focus on winning instructions: local SEO, valuation landing pages, useful area content and well-targeted social ads.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Portals bring buyers and tenants; your own marketing should focus on winning vendors and landlords.
  • Valuation landing pages, local SEO and area guides give owners a reason to contact you directly.
  • Property advertising must be accurate and include required information, so check listings and ads before they go live.

Estate agent marketing in the UK should focus mainly on winning instructions from vendors and landlords, because the property portals already deliver most buyer and tenant interest. The agents who grow tend to combine strong local SEO, valuation-focused landing pages, genuinely useful area content and targeted social advertising, all backed by a quick, professional response to every valuation request.

This guide explains where instructions come from, how to structure your website, which paid channels work, and what to keep in mind about property advertising rules. It is general marketing information. Prism Works Studio is a marketing agency, not a property professional or a regulated business, and checking listings and ads against the rules that apply to your agency is the agency's responsibility.

Where estate agent instructions come from

Most agencies win instructions from a mix of sources. Understanding which are growing and which you control helps decide where to spend time and budget.

The aim is not to replace portals but to reduce dependence on them for vendor and landlord leads, which are the lifeblood of the business.

Estate agent marketing UK: building your own lead sources

Local SEO

Homeowners search for "estate agents in [town]", "letting agents near me" and "house valuation [area]". To rank, you need a complete Google Business Profile for each branch, consistent details across directories, and branch or area pages that say something specific about each location. Our local SEO guide and Google Business Profile guide cover the essentials, and our SEO service explains how we handle it for multi-branch businesses.

Valuation landing pages

The valuation request is the most valuable action on an agent's website. Give it a dedicated page (or one per area) that explains what happens during a valuation, how long it takes, what the owner needs to prepare and what happens next. Keep the form short. Online "instant valuation" tools can capture interest, but be clear that any figure is an estimate and that a proper appraisal follows. Our landing page design guide covers the principles.

Area guides and market content

Well-written area guides (schools, transport, amenities, types of property, what the market is like) help with both buyer trust and SEO. Monthly or quarterly local market commentary, based on figures you can source and cite, gives vendors a reason to visit and positions your team as the local experts. Avoid invented figures; if you quote statistics, use ones you can verify and date them.

Landlord and lettings content

Landlords face a steady stream of rule changes, from safety certificates to deposit protection and energy efficiency standards. Clear, dated guides on these topics, reviewed by your lettings team, attract landlords looking for help and support your lettings management service. Present these as general information and encourage landlords to take advice where appropriate.

Paid advertising for estate agents

Social ads

Meta (Facebook and Instagram) ads can reach homeowners in specific postcodes with messages about valuations, recent sales in their area or your marketing approach. Video tours and "just sold" content often perform well as awareness, with a valuation offer as the follow-up. Housing ads on Meta fall under a special ad category with targeting restrictions, so campaigns need to be set up accordingly. Our Meta Ads guide explains more, and TikTok can work for agencies with good video content; see our TikTok Ads guide.

Search ads

Google Ads on terms like "estate agent [town]" and "sell my house [town]" can produce valuation requests quickly, though competition varies a lot by area. Bid on your own brand name if competitors do, filter out irrelevant searches with negatives, and send traffic to the valuation page rather than the home page. Our paid media service covers set-up and management.

Website essentials for estate and letting agents

  • Property search that works well on mobile, with fast-loading images and accurate, up-to-date listings.
  • Clear routes for each audience: sellers, buyers, landlords and tenants.
  • Valuation calls to action on every relevant page, not just the home page.
  • Branch pages with real details, opening hours and local team information approved by the agency.
  • Fees pages, particularly for lettings, where fee information has specific display requirements.
  • Redress scheme and client money protection details displayed where required.

If your website is slow or hard to update, our website development service can help, and our website redesign cost guide explains budgets.

Property advertising rules: a general overview

Property listings and ads in the UK are subject to consumer protection law and the CAP Code, which broadly require that information is accurate and that material facts a buyer or tenant needs are not left out. Guidance from trading standards bodies sets out the kinds of information expected in listings, and lettings ads carry additional requirements around fees. Rules can change, and how they apply depends on the property and the agency.

We cannot advise on legal requirements, but we build practical safeguards into our work:

  • Ad and landing page copy is reviewed by your team before launch.
  • We avoid claims such as "best agent in town" or "we sell faster" unless your agency confirms they are accurate and can be substantiated.
  • Any client feedback used is genuine and approved by the agency.
  • Templates leave space for material information and required disclosures.

Measuring estate agent marketing

Track valuation requests, phone calls and email enquiries by source, then follow them through to instructions and completions. GA4 and call tracking cover the first part; your CRM covers the rest. Our GA4 setup guide explains how to track form submissions and calls properly. A monthly review of how many valuations turned into instructions, by source, usually tells you where to put next quarter's budget.

Email, social content and staying in touch

Most homeowners are not ready to sell when they first come across your agency. Someone who requests an online valuation estimate may not move for a year or more. A simple email programme keeps you in front of them without being pushy: a short monthly update on local market activity, new instructions and sales in their area (described accurately), and practical guides on preparing a home for sale. Past buyers are also future vendors, so keeping in touch after completion is worth the effort, provided you have a lawful basis for contacting them under UK GDPR and PECR.

Organic social media works in a similar way. Short videos of new listings, behind-the-scenes content from the branch, and local community involvement all build familiarity. Consistency matters more than polish: a steady rhythm of useful, local posts usually beats occasional bursts of activity. Paid ads can then amplify the posts that people respond to most.

For multi-branch agencies, a shared content calendar with room for each branch to add local stories keeps the brand consistent while letting each office sound like part of its community.

FAQs

What is the best estate agent marketing in the UK?

Combine local SEO and a strong Google Business Profile with valuation landing pages, area content and targeted social ads to homeowners. Use portals for buyer reach, but build your own channels to win vendor and landlord instructions directly.

Do estate agents still need their own website if they use portals?

Yes. Portals bring buyers and tenants, but your website is where vendors and landlords check your credibility and request valuations. It is also the only channel you fully control.

Do Facebook ads work for estate agents?

They can work well for valuation leads and brand awareness in specific areas. Housing ads have targeting restrictions on Meta, so campaigns need to be set up within those limits.

How can letting agents attract more landlords?

Publish clear, dated guides on landlord obligations, show your management service and fees transparently, rank locally for lettings searches and follow up enquiries quickly.

Can Prism Works Studio check our listings for compliance?

No. We are a marketing agency and cannot give legal or regulatory advice. We build a review step into our work so your team can confirm listings and ads meet the rules that apply to you.

How long does SEO take for an estate agent?

Usually three to six months to see a clear trend, depending on local competition and the state of your current site and Google Business Profile.
