Arabic and English campaigns

Search behaviour in Riyadh is mixed. Many people search in Arabic, others in English, and plenty switch between the two or use transliterated terms. We usually split campaigns or ad groups by language so that each keyword set, ad and landing page matches. Mixing languages in one ad group makes it hard to see what is working and often leads to the wrong ad showing.

The Arabic keywords, ad copy and landing pages need a fluent writer. Your team or a translator provides them, and we handle structure, match types, bidding, testing and reporting. We also check that language targeting in the campaign settings reflects how your customers actually use their phones and browsers.

Location targeting across Riyadh

Riyadh is a large, spread-out city, and travel time affects whether people will visit or book. For businesses with a physical location, we target by radius or district rather than the whole region, and we adjust bids for areas that convert well. For businesses that serve the whole Kingdom, we use wider targeting but watch performance by region and adjust budgets accordingly.

We set location options to target people in or regularly in your chosen area, rather than people who are simply interested in it, unless there is a good reason to reach visitors planning a trip.

Landing pages decide your cost per lead

Ads can only send people to your website. What happens next depends on the page. A good landing page for Riyadh traffic:

  • Loads quickly on mobile data.
  • Matches the ad's language and promise.
  • Shows a clear call, WhatsApp or form option above the fold.
  • Uses a proper right-to-left layout for Arabic pages.
  • Explains your service simply, with real photos and accurate details.

If your site is not ready, we can build or improve landing pages through our website and ecommerce development work. Our web design guide for Saudi Arabia explains how bilingual sites should be planned.

Tracking that shows what works

Without accurate tracking, Google's automated bidding has nothing reliable to learn from. Before we scale any campaign, we set up:

  • Google Analytics 4 with key events for forms, calls and purchases.
  • Google Ads conversion tracking, including enhanced conversions where suitable.
  • Call tracking for click-to-call ads and calls from the website.
  • WhatsApp click tracking if that is how customers contact you.
  • Consent settings that suit your privacy approach, which you should confirm with your own adviser.

We report on leads, sales and cost per result, not just clicks and impressions.

AI-powered tools, with human checks

We use AI-powered tools to speed up keyword research, draft English ad variations and spot patterns in search term reports. Every suggestion is reviewed by a person before it goes live, and Arabic copy still comes from a fluent writer. Google's own automation, such as Smart Bidding and Performance Max, works best when it has clean conversion data and clear exclusions, so most of our effort goes into feeding it good signals.

Common Google Ads mistakes in Riyadh

  • Broad match with no negatives. Spend drifts onto irrelevant searches quickly.
  • One campaign for both languages. It hides what works and muddles the ads shown.
  • Sending all traffic to the home page. Specific landing pages convert better.
  • No call tracking. Many Saudi customers prefer to call or message, so form tracking alone misses leads.
  • Changing too much at once. Automated bidding needs time and stable settings to learn.
