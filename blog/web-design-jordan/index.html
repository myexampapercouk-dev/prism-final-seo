Web Design for Jordan: Building Websites for Businesses in Amman and Beyond

Web design for Jordan businesses: how we plan and build fast, bilingual Arabic and English websites remotely from the UK, the timelines and what they cost.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Good web design for Jordan means a fast, mobile-first site with properly built Arabic and English versions, clear calls to action and a structure search engines can understand.
  • We design and build websites remotely from the UK with no office in Jordan, and Arabic content is written with your team or a professional translator.
  • Small websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000 and take 3 to 5 weeks; ecommerce sites cost £1,500 to £3,500 and take 5 to 12 weeks; other currencies are quoted on request.

Web design for Jordan is the work of planning, designing and building a website that helps a Jordanian business win customers, whether they are in Amman, Irbid, Zarqa or Aqaba, or abroad. A good site for the Jordanian market is fast on mobile, works properly in Arabic and English, explains clearly what you offer and makes it easy for visitors to get in touch or buy.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio. We design and build websites as part of our website and ecommerce development service and work remotely with businesses in Jordan and across the Middle East. We have no office in Jordan. This guide explains how we approach web design for Jordanian businesses, what a project includes and what it costs.

What Jordanian businesses need from a website

Jordan has a well-educated, internet-savvy population and a strong base of service businesses, technology companies, clinics, education providers, tourism operators and exporters. Many of them compete not just locally but with businesses across the region and beyond, so their website often has to work for several audiences at once.

When we plan a site for a Jordanian business, we usually start from these needs:

  • Two languages done properly. Many visitors prefer Arabic, while international clients, partners and some local customers expect English.
  • Mobile performance. Most browsing happens on phones, often on mobile data, so pages must load quickly and work with a thumb.
  • Clear trust signals. Visitors want to see who you are, what you do, where you operate and how to contact you.
  • Easy contact routes. Phone, WhatsApp, email and short forms should be obvious on every key page.
  • Room to grow. A site should be easy to update and ready for SEO and advertising later.

Web design Jordan: how we run a project

Every website project follows a clear process, so you know what happens at each stage and what we need from you. Working remotely makes structure even more important, and the small time difference between the UK and Jordan leaves plenty of overlap for calls during the working day.

  1. Discovery. We agree your goals, audiences, services, languages and the actions you want visitors to take.
  2. Sitemap and content plan. We map the pages you need in each language and agree who writes what.
  3. Design. We design key page layouts for mobile and desktop, including how each page will look in Arabic, right to left.
  4. Build. We build the site on a platform your team can manage, usually WordPress for content sites or Shopify for online stores.
  5. SEO foundations. We set up page titles, descriptions, headings, language tags, clean URLs, a sitemap and analytics.
  6. Testing and launch. We test forms, speed, mobile layouts and both languages, then launch and hand over with simple guidance.

If you have not worked with a studio abroad before, our guide to hiring an overseas web design agency explains what to expect and what to ask.

Arabic and English websites

A bilingual site is more than a translated copy. Arabic reads right to left, so menus, icons, sliders, forms and tables need to mirror correctly, and fonts must be chosen to display Arabic text clearly at small sizes. Each language version should have its own URLs and language tags, so search engines can show the right version to the right person.

We write English website content and build the Arabic version of the design. Arabic content is written with your team or a professional translator, and we recommend that a fluent speaker reviews every page before launch. Our guides to bilingual Arabic and English websites and multilingual website design cover structure, navigation and SEO in more depth.

Designing for mobile visitors

Most visitors to a Jordanian business website arrive on a phone. We design for small screens first, then scale up, rather than squeezing a desktop design down. That means short, clear headings, large tap targets, compressed images, readable fonts in both scripts and contact buttons that stay easy to reach. Our article on mobile website design explains the principles we follow.

Speed matters too. A fast site keeps visitors on the page, supports search rankings and reduces the cost of any advertising you run later. We keep plugins and scripts to what is needed, optimise images and use reliable hosting suited to your audience.

Websites for tourism, education and export businesses

Some Jordanian sectors have particular needs. Tourism operators, hotels and tour companies often serve visitors from Europe, the Gulf and further afield, so their sites need clear itineraries, prices or enquiry routes, strong photography and content in the languages their guests use. Education and training providers need course pages that are easy to compare, with clear entry requirements and application steps. Exporters and technology firms selling to clients abroad usually need English content that reads naturally to international buyers, with detailed service pages, clear contact details and fast enquiry forms. We plan the structure around these audiences from the start, so each type of visitor can find what they need within a few taps, rather than adding pages later without a plan.

Ownership, hosting and handover

You should own your website, domain and content outright. We set up hosting, domains and analytics in your name wherever possible, give you admin access and hand over the logins and guidance your team needs. If you ever move to another provider, you can take everything with you. Our guide to website ownership and handover lists what you should receive at the end of any project.

Rules and compliance in Jordan

Websites that collect personal data, take payments or sell regulated products need to consider data protection, consumer protection, e-commerce rules and any sector-specific requirements, such as those for healthcare, education or financial services. We build sites with clear privacy information, consent where it is needed and transparent contact details, but we are not legal advisers. Check official Jordanian guidance and ask your own adviser or compliance team to confirm what applies to your business.

What you need to provide

Because we work remotely, we rely on you for some inputs: information about your services, logos and brand assets, photos or a plan to produce them, approval of Arabic content and someone who can give feedback at each stage. We will also need access to your domain and any existing website. Clear, timely feedback is the single biggest factor in keeping a project on schedule.

FAQs

What does web design in Jordan cost?

Our small business websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites £1,500 to £3,500. Other currencies, including Jordanian dinars, are quoted on request.

Do you have an office in Jordan?

No. Prism Works Studio is based in the UK and works remotely with Jordanian businesses through video calls, shared documents and project tools.

Can you build a website in Arabic and English?

Yes. We design and build both language versions, and Arabic content is written with your team or a professional translator before launch.

How long does it take to build a website?

A small business website usually takes 3 to 5 weeks, and an ecommerce site 5 to 12 weeks, depending on content and features.

Yes. Every site includes SEO foundations such as page titles, descriptions, headings, language tags, clean URLs and a sitemap, ready for ongoing SEO work.
