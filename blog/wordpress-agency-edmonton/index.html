How we build WordPress sites

We keep WordPress builds lean. Heavy themes and stacks of plugins are the main reason so many sites are slow and fragile. Our approach is:

  • Block editor first. We use the native block editor and site editor where possible, so your team edits pages without learning a complex page builder.
  • Few, well-chosen plugins. Each plugin is a maintenance and security cost, so we only add what earns its place.
  • Clean page structure. One clear heading per page, logical sections and real text rather than text inside images.
  • Fast loading. Compressed images, sensible font loading and caching set up from day one.
  • Forms that reach you. Quote and contact forms tested end to end, with spam protection.

Rescuing slow or broken WordPress sites

Many Edmonton businesses already have a WordPress site that was built a few years ago and has since drifted. Common signs include pages that take several seconds to load, plugins that have not been updated in months, a theme that is no longer supported, or an admin area that nobody on the team understands. A rescue usually involves:

  1. Audit. We review the theme, plugins, hosting, speed and security, and list what needs fixing.
  2. Backup. We take a full copy of files and database before we change anything.
  3. Clean-up. We remove unused plugins, update what stays and replace anything abandoned.
  4. Speed fixes. We compress images, tidy scripts and set up caching.
  5. Handover. We write a short summary of what changed and what to watch.

Security is often the biggest worry. Our guide to WordPress security explains the basics, most of which apply to any WordPress site wherever it is hosted.

Privacy and accessibility basics

Canadian businesses should think about privacy and accessibility when they launch a website. At a general level:

  • PIPEDA is the federal privacy law that applies to many private-sector organisations, and Alberta has its own private-sector privacy law, PIPA, that may apply to your business instead.
  • CASL, Canada's anti-spam law, covers consent for commercial emails, which matters if your site collects newsletter sign-ups.
  • Accessibility good practice, such as readable contrast, alt text and keyboard navigation, helps every visitor.

We build clear consent checkboxes, privacy page templates and accessible layouts, but which rules apply and how you comply is your decision. We recommend official guidance from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner of Alberta, or your own adviser.

Questions to ask any WordPress agency

  • Will we own the domain, hosting and all site files?
  • Which page builder or editor will our team use?
  • How many plugins will the site rely on, and who updates them?
  • What happens to our existing URLs and rankings during a rebuild?
  • What does ongoing care cost, and what does it include?

Clear answers to these questions protect you whichever agency you choose.
