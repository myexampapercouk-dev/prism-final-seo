Shopify Agency for Dutch Brands: What to Expect and What to Ask

Looking for a Shopify agency in the Netherlands? What a good Shopify build covers for Dutch shoppers, how a remote UK studio works and what it typically costs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Shopify store for the Netherlands should feel local: Dutch-language content, the payment methods Dutch shoppers expect, clear delivery and returns terms, and euro pricing.
  • A remote, UK-based Shopify studio can design, build and migrate stores for Dutch brands, with calls scheduled across the one-hour time difference.
  • Ecommerce builds typically take five to twelve weeks and are priced in pounds, with other currencies such as euro quoted on request.

A Shopify agency for a Dutch brand should build a store that feels native to shoppers in the Netherlands: Dutch-language pages, familiar payment options, clear delivery and returns information, and fast pages on mobile. It should also set the store up so it can grow into Belgium, Germany and other markets without a rebuild.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds Shopify stores for brands in the UK and across Europe, working remotely. We have no office in the Netherlands and will not claim one. What we provide is structured website and ecommerce development delivered online, with shared project boards, regular video calls and a working week that overlaps closely with Dutch business hours. This guide covers what to expect from any Shopify partner, the questions worth asking and how the work is usually priced.

What Dutch shoppers expect from an online store

The Netherlands is a mature ecommerce market. Shoppers are used to quick delivery, simple returns and smooth checkouts, and they compare stores quickly. A Shopify store that works well in the UK will not automatically meet those expectations.

  • Local payment methods. Many Dutch shoppers expect to pay with iDEAL, which links directly to their bank, alongside cards and buy-now-pay-later options. Leaving out the methods your customers trust can cost sales at the final step.
  • Dutch-language content. Many people in the Netherlands read English well, but product details, sizing, delivery terms and checkout messages in Dutch reduce doubt and support returns handling.
  • Clear delivery and returns. Delivery times, carriers, pickup points and the returns process should be easy to find before checkout, not hidden in the footer.
  • Euro pricing that looks deliberate. Converted prices ending in odd cents look unplanned. Rounded euro prices, set per market, look more trustworthy.
  • Trust signals. Visible contact details, clear company information, reviews and well-written policy pages all matter to cautious first-time buyers.

Language and multi-market setup

Shopify lets one store serve several countries and languages through Markets, with separate pricing, currencies and translated content. For a Dutch brand, the common patterns are a Dutch-first store with an English version for international visitors, or a store that serves the Netherlands and the Dutch-speaking part of Belgium together.

The technical setup is the easy part. The harder decisions are about content: who writes and checks the Dutch copy, whether product descriptions are translated or rewritten, and how updates are kept in sync. Machine translation is a reasonable starting point for long catalogues, but key pages should be reviewed by a fluent speaker. Our guide to Shopify Markets in Europe explains the options in more detail.

Payments, VAT and compliance

Payments and tax are where cross-border stores most often go wrong, so they deserve attention during the build rather than after launch.

  • Payment providers. We help you choose and connect a provider that supports the methods your Dutch customers use. Availability and fees vary by provider and change over time, so we check current terms during discovery. Our overview of payment gateways for online shops explains the main trade-offs.
  • VAT. Selling to consumers in several EU countries can bring rules such as the VAT One Stop Shop into play. Shopify can display and calculate tax, but the correct settings depend on where your business is registered and how you sell. Confirm them with your accountant and current official guidance.
  • Consumer rules. EU consumer law sets expectations around pricing, withdrawal rights and returns information. Your policy pages should reflect your actual terms, reviewed by someone qualified to advise on them.
  • Privacy and cookies. GDPR and the ePrivacy rules generally mean non-essential tracking waits for consent. We set up consent tools in line with common practice, but we are not lawyers.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act affects many online stores selling to consumers, so we build with accessible patterns from the start. Check the current requirements for your business.

Moving an existing Dutch store to Shopify

Many Dutch brands come to Shopify from WooCommerce, Magento or a local platform. A migration is more than copying products: it needs a plan for URLs, redirects, customer accounts, order history and SEO so you do not lose the rankings you have already earned.

We map every important URL to its new home, set up redirects, move product and customer data, and test thoroughly on a staging copy before switching over. Our guide to moving from WooCommerce to Shopify covers the process step by step, and the same principles apply from other platforms.

Performance and search visibility

Dutch shoppers browse mostly on phones, and slow stores lose them. A good build keeps the theme lean, limits apps to the ones that earn their place and compresses images properly. Our Shopify speed optimisation guide covers what we check.

Search visibility should be designed in from the start: clear collection structure, unique product descriptions in the target language, sensible URL handles, and correct language and country signals when you serve more than one market. A store that launches with these basics in place is much easier to grow through SEO later.

How a remote Shopify studio works with Dutch brands

Almost all Shopify work happens in the admin, the theme code and shared documents, so working remotely is straightforward when the process is clear.

  1. Discovery call. We agree goals, markets, catalogue size, integrations and your launch date.
  2. Proposal and plan. You receive a written scope, timeline and fixed price for the agreed work.
  3. Design and build. Designs are shared for feedback, then built in a development theme you can preview at any time.
  4. Content and testing. You supply or approve Dutch content; we test checkout, payments, emails and mobile layouts.
  5. Launch and support. We go live, monitor the first weeks and train your team on day-to-day management.

The Netherlands is one hour ahead of the UK, so calls are easy to schedule during the working day. The store, apps and accounts are set up in your name, so you keep full ownership. How much does a Shopify store cost for a Dutch brand?

Our published price for an ecommerce site is £1,500 to £3,500, depending on catalogue size, design complexity, integrations and the number of languages and markets. Projects typically take five to twelve weeks. Prices are in pounds by default, and other currencies such as euro can be quoted on request. Shopify subscription fees, paid apps, payment fees and any paid theme are separate costs.

Ongoing support, SEO or paid media can follow as a monthly retainer. Full details are on our pricing page, and our guide to what a Shopify website costs breaks down where the money goes. If you would like a straight view on your project, get in touch.

FAQs

How do I choose a Shopify agency in the Netherlands?

Look for a clear written scope, experience with multi-language and multi-currency setups, a sensible approach to payments and VAT, and a plan for SEO and redirects if you are migrating. Ask who owns the store and accounts after launch. Location matters less than a clear process.

Do you have an office in Amsterdam?

No. Prism Works Studio is based in the UK and works remotely with brands in the Netherlands and across Europe, using video calls, shared project boards and direct access to your Shopify admin.

Can you set up iDEAL on Shopify?

We can help you choose and connect a payment provider that supports iDEAL and the other methods your customers use. Availability and fees depend on the provider and your business, so we confirm current terms during discovery.

Will my store be in Dutch?

It can be. We set up the languages you need and the structure to manage them. You can supply Dutch copy, approve translations or work with a translator, and we recommend a fluent review of key pages before launch.

Do you quote in euro?

Our published prices are in pounds, and other currencies, including euro, can be quoted on request. Please check the VAT treatment of cross-border services with your accountant.

How long does a Shopify build take?

Ecommerce builds typically take five to twelve weeks. Simpler single-language stores sit at the shorter end, while larger catalogues, several markets or a migration from another platform take longer.
