Handyman Marketing in the UK: How to Get More Local Jobs and Better Customers

Handyman marketing in the UK works when a clear list of jobs, local search, honest pricing guidance and reviews win repeat customers, not one-off odd jobs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Customers search for the job, not the trade, so pages for flat-pack assembly, shelf and TV mounting, small repairs and door fitting win more searches than a single handyman page.
  • Clear pricing guidance, such as how you charge for time and minimum call-outs, filters out poor-fit enquiries and builds trust.
  • Repeat customers, landlords and letting agents are the backbone of a steady handyman business, so make rebooking easy.

Handyman marketing in the UK is about being the person locals think of for the small jobs nobody else wants, then turning one-off visits into repeat customers. The handymen who build steady businesses usually combine a strong Google Business Profile, a website that lists the specific jobs they do, clear guidance on how they charge and a growing collection of reviews.

This guide is for sole-trader handymen, small property maintenance firms and multi-skilled tradespeople who want more direct work without relying on paid lead sites. We cover how people search, how to present your services, pricing, trust and how to win landlord and agent work.

How people find a handyman

Most handyman searches start with a specific task. Someone has bought a wardrobe they cannot face assembling, wants a TV mounted on the wall, needs a door that sticks fixed, or has a list of small repairs before selling a house. They search for "handyman near me", "TV wall mounting [town]", "flat pack assembly service", "fix a dripping tap" or "odd job man [town]".

Older customers and busy households often rely on recommendations from neighbours and community groups, then check reviews before calling. Landlords and letting agents need someone dependable for a steady flow of small maintenance tasks. Our guide to marketing for tradespeople covers the general principles; this post is about what is specific to handyman work.

Your Google Business Profile

For a handyman, the Google profile is often where people decide whether to call. Set it up as a service-area business, list the towns you genuinely cover and choose the most accurate category. Then add services one by one: furniture assembly, TV and shelf mounting, curtain pole and blind fitting, door hanging and adjustment, minor plumbing repairs, sealant and grout, small carpentry jobs, garden tidying if you offer it.

Upload photos of finished jobs, your tidy van and your tools. Show your face if you are comfortable doing so; people like to know who will be coming into their home. Our Google Business Profile guide covers setup in detail, and our article on responding to Google reviews explains how replies build trust with future customers.

Build pages around jobs, not just your trade

One page that says "handyman services, no job too small" gives search engines very little to work with. Customers search for the job, so create pages for the jobs you most want.

  • Flat-pack furniture assembly: wardrobes, beds, desks and garden furniture.
  • TV, shelf and mirror mounting: wall types you can fix to and how you hide cables.
  • Door hanging and adjustment: sticking doors, new internal doors, handles and locks.
  • Small repairs: dripping taps, sealant, loose fixtures, minor plaster patching.
  • Curtains, blinds and pictures: poles, tracks and gallery walls.
  • Property maintenance for landlords: between-tenancy fixes and ongoing jobs.

Be clear about what you do not do. Electrical and gas work often have specific legal and competence requirements, so do not advertise anything outside your qualifications, and tell readers to check current official guidance on which jobs need a registered or qualified tradesperson. Our guide to service page design explains a layout that works for each job page.

Be clear about how you charge

Pricing is one of the biggest sources of friction for handyman work. Customers worry about being overcharged for a small job; handymen worry about travelling across town for twenty minutes of work. Clear guidance on your website helps both sides. Explain:

  • Whether you charge by the hour, half day, day or by the job.
  • Any minimum charge or call-out fee.
  • Whether materials are included or charged separately.
  • How you handle parking, travel and waste removal.
  • How customers can save by grouping several jobs into one visit.

Encouraging a "job list" approach, where a customer books a half day for several tasks, often improves your earnings and their value for money. Our guide to website pricing page design covers how to present prices clearly without overwhelming people.

Make enquiries quick and easy

Most handyman enquiries come from phones. A short form asking for postcode, a brief description of the jobs and optional photos is usually enough. Photos are especially useful because they let you estimate time and materials before you visit. Show your phone number clearly too, as many older customers prefer to call. Our guide to website contact forms covers good practice for small business forms.

Trust: the deciding factor

Customers are inviting a stranger into their home, often while they are alone or with children around. Trust is the main thing you are selling. Ways to show it include:

  1. Genuine reviews that mention tidiness, punctuality and fair pricing.
  2. A short about section with a photo and how long you have been working locally.
  3. Public liability insurance stated clearly, if you hold it.
  4. Any background check or trade scheme membership you genuinely have.
  5. A simple promise about how you leave the property, such as clearing up and removing packaging.

Our guide to website trust signals explains how to present these without clutter.

Landlords, agents and repeat customers

Repeat work is what turns a busy week into a stable business. Letting agents and landlords need a dependable handyman for small repairs between tenancies, and households often come back with new jobs every few months. Create a page for property professionals explaining response times, how you send photos and invoices, and how keys and access are handled. Then contact local agents with a short introduction.

For households, a simple follow-up message a few months after a job, with a reminder that you are available for the next list of tasks, can bring a surprising amount of repeat work. If you collect contact details for this, check current ICO guidance on UK GDPR and PECR rules before sending marketing messages.

When paid ads help

Google Ads can work for higher-value or time-consuming jobs such as large flat-pack orders, multiple TV mounts or property maintenance contracts. Small one-off jobs rarely justify the click cost. Keep targeting tight, add negative keywords for job seekers, training courses and tool shopping, and point ads to the matching job page. Track where each job comes from and which services are most profitable for your time. Over time, shift your marketing towards those. A short monthly review of enquiries, booked jobs and reviews is usually enough to spot which pages and channels deserve more attention. FAQs

What is the best handyman marketing in the UK?

For most handymen, the best approach is a well-kept Google Business Profile, a website with pages for specific jobs, clear pricing guidance, plenty of reviews and steady relationships with landlords and letting agents.

How do I get more handyman jobs?

Make it easy to find you for the specific tasks people search for, show genuine reviews, explain how you charge, ask happy customers for referrals and stay in touch so past customers rebook.

Should a handyman list prices online?

Clear guidance on hourly or half-day rates, minimum charges and materials helps customers decide and reduces awkward conversations. You can still quote individually for larger or unusual jobs.

Do handymen need a website?

A simple website helps you rank for specific jobs, gives people somewhere to check your work and reviews, and makes it easy to send a list of tasks and photos for a quote.

How can a handyman get work from letting agents?

Create a page for property professionals covering response times, access, photos and invoicing, then introduce yourself to local agents and landlords. Reliability and clear communication win repeat work.

What jobs should a handyman not advertise?

Avoid advertising work you are not qualified or registered to do, particularly gas and certain electrical jobs. Check current official guidance on what requires a registered or qualified tradesperson.
