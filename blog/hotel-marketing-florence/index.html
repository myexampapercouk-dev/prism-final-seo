How guests search for hotels in Florence

Florence draws visitors from across Europe, North America, Asia and the rest of Italy, for art, architecture, food and wine, study trips, weddings and business events. Guests often plan weeks or months ahead, and they search in very specific ways:

  • By area: near the Duomo, Santa Maria Novella station, Oltrarno, Santa Croce, San Lorenzo or the hills around Fiesole.
  • By type: boutique hotel, family hotel, hotel with a terrace, hotel with parking, apartment with a kitchen, or a Tuscan villa outside the city.
  • By occasion: honeymoon, wedding guests, art weekend, university visit, conference or wine tour base.
  • By practical need: walking distance to the Uffizi, access for people with reduced mobility, or a property outside the limited traffic zone for those arriving by car.

Each of those searches can be matched with a page, an ad group or a listing detail. The more precisely you answer them, the more likely a guest books with you directly.

Online travel agencies still play a role for most Florence hotels. The aim is not to drop them, but to make your own website the easiest and most rewarding place to book.

Your website: the centre of direct bookings

A hotel website should load quickly on a phone, show rooms with clear photos and details, display prices without forcing guests through many steps, and link smoothly to your booking engine. It should also answer practical questions about check-in times, luggage storage, parking, traffic zones, breakfast, air conditioning and accessibility.

Accessibility matters for both guests and the law. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act and websites explains what it means for EU businesses; legal questions should go to your own adviser or official guidance.

If your current site is slow or hard to update, a rebuild may come before any marketing. A small website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks.

Hotel SEO in Florence

Hotel SEO focuses on searches that the big platforms do not cover well: specific areas, room types, occasions and practical needs. Useful pages include:

  • One page per room type, with real details and photos.
  • Area guides written from your location, such as walking routes to major sights or the nearest stops.
  • Occasion pages for weddings, study groups, families or longer stays, where you genuinely offer them.
  • Clear FAQs about arrival, parking, luggage and accessibility.

Our guide to hotel SEO sets out the full approach, and our local SEO guide covers map listings in detail. Results from SEO typically take three to six months, so it pays to start well before your busiest season.

Google Ads and Meta Ads for Florence hotels

Google Ads. Branded campaigns protect your name from travel agencies bidding on it. Non-branded campaigns target searches by area, type and occasion, with budgets raised around peak periods such as spring, early summer and autumn. Our guide to a Google Ads agency in Milan covers Italian paid search more broadly.

Meta Ads. Facebook and Instagram ads reach travellers in your main source countries while they are still dreaming and planning, using your existing photos and short clips. Remarketing reminds website visitors to book directly. We do not film or edit video, so we work with the images and clips you already have or commission.

Both channels need accurate booking tracking, so we connect your booking engine to Google Analytics and the ad platforms wherever the booking engine supports it. Our paid media services page explains how we manage campaigns.

Reviews, trust and the guest experience

Reviews on Google and travel platforms weigh heavily in hotel choices. We never write or buy reviews, and we advise against offering rewards in return for positive ones. Instead, we help you make it easy for genuine guests to leave a review after their stay, and to reply to every review politely and promptly, including the critical ones. A calm, helpful reply often reassures future guests more than a page of perfect scores.

Trust also comes from small details on your website: clear cancellation terms, real photos of every room type, honest descriptions of stairs, lifts and room sizes, and visible contact details. In a historic city, guests appreciate knowing about steep staircases, narrow streets or noise from a lively square before they arrive. Being upfront reduces complaints and improves the reviews you earn.
