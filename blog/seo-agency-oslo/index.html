SEO Agency for Oslo Businesses: Norwegian and English Search

Looking for an SEO agency in Oslo? How SEO works across Norwegian and English search, what a remote UK studio handles, what to ask and how pricing works.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Oslo businesses often need Norwegian pages for local customers and English pages for international buyers, with each language researched and written separately.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Norway; we lead technical SEO, English content and reporting remotely, working with your team or a translator on Norwegian pages.
  • SEO results typically build over three to six months, and retainers are priced in pounds, with Norwegian krone and other currencies quoted on request.

An SEO agency for an Oslo business should help you rank for the searches your buyers actually make, in Norwegian, in English or both, and turn that visibility into enquiries, bookings or sales. Good SEO combines technical health, language setup, keyword research, useful content, earned mentions and reporting that connects search to business results.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio working remotely with companies in Norway and across Europe. We have no office in Oslo. Our SEO and generative engine optimisation service is delivered online, one hour behind Oslo time, with video calls, shared documents and monthly reporting. We are strongest on English-language SEO and the technical foundations that every language version relies on.

Why Oslo businesses often need SEO in two languages

Oslo is a centre for energy, shipping and maritime, seafood, software, consumer brands, tourism and professional services. English is widely spoken, and many Oslo companies sell internationally from an early stage. Even so, Norwegian customers often search in Norwegian, particularly for local services, retail, healthcare and anything involving trust or money.

  • Norwegian-first businesses. Local services, shops and clinics serving mainly Norway need strong Norwegian pages written or reviewed by native speakers.
  • English-first businesses. Software, maritime, energy and B2B companies often lead with English to reach buyers in the UK, Europe and further afield.
  • Both. Many need both, with a structure that helps search engines show the right version to each searcher.

Typical problems include English pages ranking in Norway where Norwegian ones should, machine-translated pages that read awkwardly, and language versions competing for the same terms. Our international SEO guide explains the main ways to structure a multi-language site.

Keyword research in Norwegian and English

Translating an English keyword list into Norwegian rarely works. Norwegian joins many words into compounds, has two written standards, Bokmål and Nynorsk, and in some sectors uses English terms as standard. Most commercial searches use Bokmål, but your audience and sector decide what matters.

The reliable approach is to research each language separately, with data set to Norway, then map each group of terms to one clear page. The process in our keyword research guide applies directly. Prism writes in English. For Norwegian keyword checks and copy, we work alongside your in-house team or a native-speaking writer or translator, so Norwegian pages sound natural and match real searches.

SEO for Oslo software, maritime and consumer brands

  • Software and SaaS. Feature pages, use-case pages, integration pages and comparison content attract buyers researching a decision, mostly in English.
  • Maritime, energy and industrial B2B. Long sales cycles reward detailed service pages, technical guides and clear proof of expertise for international buyers.
  • Consumer and ecommerce brands. Category pages, product data and structured data help shoppers in Norway and abroad find products.
  • Hospitality and tourism. Visitors plan in many languages, so English pages answering practical questions can reach international guests.

Structured data helps search engines and AI tools understand your business and products. Our schema markup guide covers the main types to use.

Data protection and selling from Norway

Norway is not in the EU but is part of the European Economic Area, and the GDPR applies there through the EEA agreement. That affects cookie consent, analytics and forms on your site. Selling into the EU from Norway also raises customs and VAT questions for online shops.

We keep these points general. Check current official guidance and take advice from your own legal and tax advisers. We set up consent tools and tracking so they can follow the approach you decide on.

Search beyond Google: AI answers

More buyers now discover brands through AI-generated summaries and assistants. Clear answers to real questions, accurate business information and mentions from trusted sources all make a site more likely to be cited. Content that earns visibility for Oslo businesses

Ranking pages are rarely the ones that simply repeat a keyword. They answer the questions buyers ask at each stage of a decision. For an Oslo business, that often means a mix of page types:

  • Service and product pages that explain clearly what you offer, who it suits and how to get started.
  • Comparison pages that help buyers weigh options honestly, including when your offer is not the right fit.
  • Practical guides that answer detailed questions in your sector, which also give AI tools something worth citing.
  • Location or market pages for the countries you sell into, written for each audience rather than copied with the place name swapped.
  • Case pages built on facts about how you work, your process and what customers can expect, without overclaiming.

We plan content in a simple calendar agreed with your team, so English and Norwegian pages are scheduled together and translators have time to do their work properly. Quality matters more than volume: a few well-researched pages usually do more than many thin ones.
