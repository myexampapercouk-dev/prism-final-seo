Web Design in Krakow: Planning a Website That Brings Enquiries

Web design for Krakow businesses: fast, mobile-first WordPress and Shopify sites with Polish and English versions, built for SEO by a remote UK studio.

Web design in Krakow means building a website that explains your business clearly, loads quickly on phones and turns visitors into enquiries or sales. For many Krakow companies, it also means running Polish and English versions side by side, structured so that each one can be found in search.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely. We have no office in Krakow or anywhere in Poland. We work through video calls, shared documents and written updates. We do not take photos or film video, so your team or a photographer supplies images, and we plan, design, build and optimise the site.

Why web design matters for Krakow businesses

Krakow is one of Poland's largest cities and a major hub for technology, shared services, education, tourism and creative industries. Many international companies run offices there, and the city has a strong startup and software scene. Tourism also brings large numbers of visitors to the Old Town, Kazimierz and beyond.

This creates several types of website need. A software company may sell mostly in English to clients across Europe and North America. A hotel, restaurant or tour operator needs strong mobile pages in Polish and English with easy booking. A local clinic, law firm or accountant needs trustworthy Polish pages that rank in local search. Good web design starts by understanding who the site is for and what they need to do.

Planning Polish and English versions

Bilingual sites need planning before design starts. Each language should have its own URL path, its own titles and descriptions, and hreflang tags so Google knows which page to show to which searcher. Machine translation widgets are not a good substitute, because their output is not indexed well and quality varies.

We build the structure and language switcher, and your translator provides the Polish text. If budgets are tight, we can launch key pages in both languages first and add more later. Our guide to multilingual website design explains the choices.

Websites for technology and SaaS companies

Krakow's software and technology firms often need websites that explain complex products simply. That means clear product pages, feature explanations, pricing pages where appropriate, documentation links and strong demo or trial calls to action. Our SaaS marketing page explains how we approach this sector, and a well-structured pricing page is one of the most important pages on a SaaS site.

For software firms selling abroad, English is often the main language, with Polish pages supporting local recruitment and partnerships.

Choosing a platform

We build most business websites on WordPress, which is flexible and easy for your team to update. For online stores, we usually recommend Shopify, which handles payments, stock and shipping well. Polish shoppers often expect local payment options, and these can be set up through supported payment providers. Final checks on payments and tax setup rest with your business and its accountant.

If you sell on marketplaces too, our guide to Allegro SEO in Poland explains how to improve listings on the country's best-known marketplace, and our guide to Shopify Markets in Europe covers selling across borders.

SEO built into the build

A new website is the best time to set up SEO properly. We plan pages around real searches, write clear titles and headings, set up redirects from any old site, add structured data and check speed before launch. Our technical SEO audit checklist shows the checks we run, and our guide to SEO in Warsaw explains how search competition works in Poland.

After launch, many businesses add Google Ads to bring in enquiries sooner. Our guide to Google Ads in Krakow covers local campaigns, and our guide to web design in Warsaw compares approaches in the capital.

Accessibility and privacy

The European Accessibility Act brings accessibility requirements for many businesses selling to consumers in the EU. We design with readable fonts, good colour contrast, keyboard navigation and clear labels as standard. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act explains what it means for websites.

GDPR applies too. Your site needs a privacy notice and a cookie banner that respects visitors' choices. We set these up, but final legal approval rests with your business. For specific obligations, check official guidance or ask your adviser.

Common web design mistakes

  • Skipping redirects. Old URLs that break lose their rankings and links.
  • Translating only the homepage. Visitors leave when deeper pages switch language.
  • Heavy design elements. Large videos and sliders slow pages and distract.
  • Weak calls to action. Visitors need an obvious next step on every page.
  • No analytics. Without tracking you cannot improve what you cannot measure.

Your team supplies content, translations and images and approves each step, while we handle design, development and SEO. Many Krakow businesses already work with international teams, so this way of working is familiar.

Websites for tourism and hospitality

Krakow welcomes large numbers of visitors each year, so hotels, apartments, restaurants, tour operators and attractions compete hard for attention. Their websites need fast mobile pages, clear photos supplied by the business, simple booking paths and up-to-date opening hours and prices. Many visitors plan on their phones while already in the city, so a page that loads slowly or hides the booking button loses customers to the next result.

Direct booking is worth investing in. Booking platforms bring reach but take commission, so a well-designed site with a clear booking engine can shift some reservations back to you. Pages for different audiences, such as families, groups or business travellers, help both search visibility and conversions. We support hospitality businesses with both the website and the search and ads that follow.

Content that keeps working

A website is not finished at launch. The businesses that grow online keep adding useful content: new service pages, answers to common questions, guides and updates. We plan a simple content calendar with you and write in English, while your translator adapts pages into Polish. Clear headings and direct answers at the start of each section also help your pages appear in AI-generated search summaries.

We also recommend a quick review every few months. Checking which pages bring enquiries, which ones visitors leave quickly and which searches you appear for shows where small changes can make the biggest difference.
