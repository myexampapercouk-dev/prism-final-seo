Ice Cream Parlour Marketing in the UK: How to Bring In More Customers

Ice cream parlour marketing in the UK works when local search, great photos, social media and off-season ideas keep customers coming in all year round.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most ice cream visits start with a local search or a social post, so a complete Google Business Profile and great photos do much of the work.
  • The quieter months are the real marketing challenge, so desserts, hot drinks, takeaway tubs, parties and events help balance a seasonal business.
  • Clear allergen information, opening hours and menus online answer the questions families and visitors ask before they set off.

Ice cream parlour marketing in the UK is about being the place people think of, and can find in seconds, when they fancy a treat, then giving them enough reasons to come back in October as well as August. The parlours and gelaterias that grow usually combine strong local search, great photos, an active social presence and ideas that smooth out the seasons.

This guide is for independent ice cream parlours, gelato shops, dessert parlours, farm dairies with an ice cream shop and seaside kiosks. It covers how customers choose where to go, Google Business Profile, your website, social media, paid ads, the quieter months, parties and events, wholesale and online sales. We are a studio that builds websites and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so we focus on the digital side of bringing customers through the door.

How customers choose an ice cream parlour

Ice cream is usually an impulse decision. A family on a day out, friends after dinner, or a couple walking along the seafront search for "ice cream near me", "best gelato in York" or "dessert parlour open late", glance at photos and reviews, and head for whichever looks best and is closest.

That means three things matter most: showing up in local search, looking irresistible in photos, and having accurate opening hours. A great product will not help if Google says you are closed when you are open.

Typical customers include:

  • Families on days out, after school or at weekends.
  • Tourists and day visitors in seaside, countryside and historic towns.
  • Teenagers and young adults looking for somewhere to meet.
  • Couples and friends looking for an evening dessert spot.
  • Party organisers wanting a children's party or celebration venue.
  • Local businesses and event organisers ordering for events, weddings or catering.

Ice cream parlour marketing UK: get local search right

Your Google Business Profile is likely to bring more customers than any other channel. Make sure your primary category is accurate, your hours are correct, including seasonal and bank holiday hours, and your profile has plenty of recent photos of your counter, flavours, sundaes and seating. Our guide to Google Business Profile in the UK covers setup and ongoing management.

Posts on your profile can promote new flavours, seasonal specials and events. Our guide to Google Business Profile posts explains how to use them. Encourage happy customers to leave reviews, perhaps with a small sign at the till, and reply to reviews politely. Our local SEO guide covers the wider steps that help you rank in the map pack.

Your website and menu

Many ice cream parlours rely on social media alone, but a simple website still helps. It gives you a page that can rank for searches like "ice cream parlour in Whitby", holds your full menu and allergen approach, and lets you take party bookings and enquiries.

Pages that usually earn their place:

  • Menu, with flavours, sundaes, desserts, drinks and prices, kept up to date.
  • Visit us, with opening hours, location, parking, seating and accessibility.
  • Allergens and dietary options, explaining vegan, dairy-free and other options and how to ask staff.
  • Parties and events, with packages and an enquiry form.
  • Wholesale or catering, if you supply cafés, restaurants or events.
  • Shop, if you sell tubs, gift vouchers or merchandise.

Allergen rules for food businesses are important and can change. Keep website wording general, make clear that customers should speak to staff about allergies, and check current food safety and allergen guidance to make sure your information and in-store practices are right. For ideas on building a site that suits a small food business, our guide to café website design covers many of the same needs.

Photos and social media

Ice cream is one of the most photogenic products there is, which gives you a natural advantage on Instagram, TikTok and Facebook. Bright colours, new flavours, sundaes being built and happy customers all make strong content.

Good social content ideas include:

  • New and limited-edition flavours, with a short video of the first scoop.
  • Behind the scenes, such as making a batch or sourcing ingredients, if you make your own.
  • Seasonal specials, such as autumn spiced flavours or Christmas desserts.
  • Customer polls on which flavour to bring back.
  • Opening time updates on sunny days, holidays and during events.

Always ask permission before featuring customers, especially children. Paid social ads can help promote new flavours, events and parties to people nearby. Our guide to Facebook ads for small businesses explains how to start with a small budget.

Managing the quieter months

For many ice cream businesses, the biggest marketing challenge is the colder, darker months. Summer often looks after itself, while winter can be very quiet. Planning for the off-season is where marketing makes the biggest difference.

Promotions should always be accurate and clear about any conditions. If you are unsure about wording, check current advertising guidance such as the CAP Code.

Email and loyalty

An email list lets you reach regulars directly, without relying on social media algorithms. Collect addresses through party bookings, online orders and in-store sign-ups, with clear consent under UK GDPR and PECR. Send updates about new flavours, seasonal openings, events and offers. Our guide to email list building covers simple ways to grow a list.

Loyalty schemes, such as a stamp card or digital equivalent, can encourage repeat visits. Keep them simple and easy to understand. A single clear reward, such as a free scoop after a set number of visits, is usually easier to run and explain than a points system with tiers. Mention the scheme on your menu page, at the till and in your emails so regulars know it exists.

Parties, events and wholesale

Children's parties, celebrations and private bookings can be valuable, especially outside peak season. A clear parties page should explain packages, capacity, what is included and how to book. Wedding and event catering, such as an ice cream cart or dessert table, can also be marketed separately with its own page and photos.

If you make your own ice cream, wholesale to cafés, restaurants, farm shops and delis can add steady income. A wholesale page with flavours, pack sizes and an enquiry form helps trade buyers find you. FAQs

What is the best ice cream parlour marketing in the UK?

For most parlours, the most effective steps are a complete Google Business Profile with great photos and accurate hours, active social media, a simple website with your menu and allergen approach, and plans to keep customers coming in through the quieter months.

Does an ice cream parlour need a website?

It helps. A website can rank in local search, hold your full menu and allergen information, take party bookings and sell tubs or gift vouchers. Social media alone is harder to control and less likely to appear in search.

How can we get more customers in winter?

Add hot desserts and drinks, promote takeaway tubs and gift vouchers, run themed events, market parties and celebrations, and use email to stay in touch with regulars when footfall is lower.

What should we say about allergens online?

Explain your dietary options, ask customers to speak to staff about allergies, and keep wording general and accurate. Check current food safety and allergen guidance to make sure your website and in-store practices are right.

Which social media works best for ice cream shops?

Instagram and TikTok suit colourful, short video content, while Facebook is useful for local community groups and events. Choose the platforms your customers use and post regularly.

Can an ice cream business sell wholesale online?

Yes. A wholesale page with flavours, pack sizes and an enquiry form can help cafés, restaurants and farm shops find you. Delivery and food safety requirements apply, so check current guidance before expanding.
