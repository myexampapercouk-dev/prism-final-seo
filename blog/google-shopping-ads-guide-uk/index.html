Google Shopping Ads: How They Work and How to Set Them Up in the UK

How Google Shopping ads work, from product feeds and Merchant Center to campaign types, bidding and costs. A plain-English guide for UK online retailers.

By the Prism Works Studio team · Updated 7 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopping ads run on your product feed rather than ad copy and chosen keywords, so product data shapes when your items appear.
  • You need Merchant Center, a linked Google Ads account, accurate product data and conversion tracking before you start.
  • Begin with a focused campaign on profitable products, and judge it on margin rather than revenue alone.

Google Shopping ads are product listings that show an image, price and shop name in Google results and the Shopping tab. Rather than writing an ad or bidding on chosen keywords, you give Google a product feed through Merchant Center, and Google decides which of your products to show for each search based on that data, your bids and other auction factors. You generally pay when someone clicks.

This guide explains how Shopping ads work, what you need to set them up, how campaigns are structured and which mistakes can waste budget. It is written for retailers learning the channel or running it themselves. If you are deciding whether to hire help, read our separate guide to choosing a Google Shopping agency.

How Google Shopping ads work

Unlike search ads, you do not write Shopping ads or choose keywords. Instead, you send Google a product feed: a structured list of your products with titles, descriptions, prices, images, availability and identifiers such as GTINs. Google matches searches to products in your feed and decides when to show them, based on relevance and your bids.

In practice the process runs in a loop:

  1. Your store sends product data to Merchant Center, where Google checks it against its policies.
  2. Approved products become eligible for your Google Ads campaigns.
  3. When someone searches, Google works out which products are relevant and runs an auction.
  4. Winning products appear with their image and price. A click takes the shopper to your product page.
  5. Conversion tracking reports the sale back, which informs bidding and your next decisions.

That means two things drive performance: the quality of your feed, and how you structure campaigns and bids. Both need attention.

Shopping ads and free listings

Merchant Center can also make products eligible for unpaid listings on Google. These need no ad budget but give you less control over when products appear. Shopping ads are the paid route, with budgets, bids and campaign reporting. The same feed can support both, so accurate product data pays off twice.

What you need before you start

  • A Google Merchant Center account with your website verified and claimed. Our Merchant Center setup guide walks through this step by step.
  • A product feed, usually generated by your store platform or uploaded as a file.
  • Shipping and returns settings in Merchant Center that match your website, including UK delivery costs and times.
  • A Google Ads account linked to Merchant Center.
  • Conversion tracking that records purchases and their value, through Google Ads tags, GA4 or both.
  • A store that meets Google's policies: clear contact details, returns policy, secure checkout and accurate prices including VAT.

If your store is not yet in good shape, fix it first. A slow, confusing checkout can waste ad spend. Our website and ecommerce development service covers checkout and speed improvements.

Standard Shopping vs Performance Max

There are two main ways to run Shopping ads today.

Campaign typeHow it worksGood forWatch out for
Standard ShoppingShopping placements only; you control product groups, bids and negative keywordsControl, clear reporting, tight budgetsMore manual work to manage
Performance MaxAutomated campaigns across Shopping, Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail and DiscoverReach and automation with good conversion dataLess visibility into where money goes; needs strong tracking and assets

Some retailers use both: Performance Max for broad reach and Standard Shopping for specific products or tighter control. The right mix depends on your budget, data volume and margins. Our Performance Max guide covers that campaign type in depth.

Optimising your product feed

The feed is a sensible place to start because it shapes which searches you can appear for. Focus on the basics below, and see our feed and campaign management guide when you are ready for ongoing optimisation.

Product titles

Titles carry a lot of weight in how products are matched to searches. Put the most important words first, using the terms shoppers actually search: brand, product type, key attributes such as size, colour or material. "Women's Waterproof Hiking Boots, Leather, Size 6" tells Google and shoppers far more than "Explorer Boot".

Images

Use clear, high-quality images on a plain background for the main image. Avoid watermarks, promotional text and borders, which can cause disapprovals.

Identifiers and categories

Include GTINs (barcodes) for branded products where they exist, and accurate product categories and product types. These help Google match you to the right searches.

Price and availability

Prices and stock in the feed must match your website. Mismatches lead to disapprovals and, if repeated, account issues. Automated feed updates help prevent this.

Structuring campaigns around profit

A single campaign for every product treats a high-margin best seller the same as a low-margin accessory. A better structure groups products by what matters commercially:

  • Margin bands, so you can bid more where you make more.
  • Best sellers versus long tail, so proven products get the budget they deserve.
  • Seasonal or promotional products, so you can push them at the right time.
  • Brand searches separated where possible, as they behave very differently from generic searches.

Use the custom label fields in your feed to tag products, and build campaigns or listing groups around those labels.

How much Google Shopping ads cost

There is no set price. You pay per click, and what a click costs varies by product category, competition and how you bid. You control the budget, but there is no fixed minimum that guarantees results, and campaigns need enough data to learn. If your budget is small, focus it on your most profitable products rather than spreading it across the whole catalogue.

Automated bidding strategies such as Target ROAS (return on ad spend) depend on conversion data, so they suit campaigns with enough sales to learn from; before that, simpler strategies can be easier to read.

If someone manages the account for you, ad spend is separate from their fee. With our paid media service, advertising spend is paid directly to Google and is never marked up.

Tracking: what to check first

Google's bidding is only as good as the data it receives. Check that:

  1. Purchases are tracked once per order, with the correct revenue value.
  2. Consent is handled properly under UK cookie rules, with consent mode configured where you use it. Take advice if you are unsure what applies.
  3. Enhanced conversions are enabled where appropriate, to improve accuracy.
  4. GA4 and Google Ads numbers broadly agree; large gaps usually mean a tracking problem.

In our own paid media work, we check tracking and fix gaps before any budget is increased, because automated bidding relies on this data.

Reading the numbers: which metrics matter

Google Ads reports a lot of figures. For Shopping campaigns, a few matter more than the rest:

  • Return on ad spend (ROAS). Revenue divided by ad spend. Useful, but always compare it with your margins: a ROAS that looks healthy on a low-margin product can still lose money.
  • Cost per acquisition. What you pay in ads for each order. Compare it with your average order profit, not revenue.
  • Conversion rate. If clicks are high but orders are low, the issue may be on the website: price, delivery costs, stock or checkout friction.
  • Impression share. Shows how often your products appear when they could. Low impression share on profitable products is a sign the budget or bids are holding you back.
  • Search terms. Not a single number, but the most useful report for spotting waste and new opportunities.

A smaller campaign that is clearly profitable is worth more than a large one with impressive revenue and no profit behind it. Work out your break-even ROAS for each margin band before setting targets, so everyone knows what "good" looks like.

Seasonality and promotions

UK retail has clear peaks: Black Friday and Cyber Monday, the run-up to Christmas, January sales and category-specific seasons such as summer garden or back to school. Plan feed updates, promotions and budget increases ahead of these periods, and use Merchant Center promotions to show offers directly on your listings. Check delivery cut-off dates in your feed and on your website in December, so shoppers can see them.

After a peak, review what sold and what did not. Products that performed well at full price may deserve more budget during the rest of the year.

Google Shopping mistakes to avoid

  • Ignoring search terms. Review search term reports and add negative keywords for irrelevant searches.
  • Weak product titles. Supplier titles and internal codes rarely match what shoppers type.
  • Hidden delivery costs. Charges that appear late can put shoppers off, and must match what your feed says.
  • Judging too early. New campaigns need a learning period before results settle.
  • Running ads to a slow site. You pay for the click whether or not the page loads quickly, so check speed on mobile.

A small retailer with a clean feed and a modest catalogue can run a simple Shopping campaign. As budgets, product ranges and automation grow, so does the time needed for feed management, search term reviews and testing. FAQs

How do Google Shopping ads work?

You send product data to Merchant Center and link it to Google Ads. Google then matches your products to relevant searches and runs an auction. You generally pay when someone clicks.

Do I choose keywords for Shopping ads?

No. Google uses your feed to decide which searches match, although you can use negative keywords in Standard Shopping to exclude irrelevant ones.

How much do Google Shopping ads cost in the UK?

You pay per click, and costs vary by product category and competition. You control the daily budget, and management fees are separate from ad spend.

Do I need Performance Max for Shopping ads?

No. Standard Shopping campaigns give more control, and some retailers use both, depending on budget and data volume. Check Google Ads for the current options, as they can change.

Why are my products disapproved in Merchant Center?

Possible reasons include price or availability mismatches, missing identifiers, image issues, missing policies or shipping settings. Merchant Center diagnostics show the specific cause.

Can any online shop run Google Shopping ads?

Generally yes, if the shop sells eligible products, can supply accurate product data and meets Google's policies.

How long before Shopping ads become profitable?

It depends on margins, competition and tracking quality. Expect an initial learning and testing period before performance settles and can be scaled.
