Google Shopping Ads in the UK: A Practical Guide for Online Retailers

How Google Shopping ads work for UK retailers: Merchant Center feeds, Standard Shopping vs Performance Max, budgets, tracking and common mistakes to avoid.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopping ads are driven by your product feed, so feed quality (titles, images, prices, availability) matters as much as bidding.
  • Set up conversion tracking with revenue values before scaling spend; without it, Google optimises blind.
  • Start with a clear campaign structure around margins and best sellers, then expand once you know what is profitable.

Google Shopping ads show your products, with an image, price and shop name, directly in Google search results and the Shopping tab. For UK online retailers they are often the most profitable paid channel, because they reach people who are already searching for a specific product and ready to compare prices.

This guide explains how Shopping ads work, what you need to set them up, how to structure campaigns and the mistakes that most often waste budget.

How Google Shopping ads work

Unlike search ads, you do not write Shopping ads or choose keywords. Instead, you send Google a product feed: a structured list of your products with titles, descriptions, prices, images, availability and identifiers such as GTINs. Google matches searches to products in your feed and decides when to show them, based on relevance and your bids.

That means two things drive performance: the quality of your feed, and how you structure campaigns and bids. Both need attention.

What you need before you start

  • A Google Merchant Center account with your website verified and claimed.
  • A product feed, usually generated by your store. Shopify and WooCommerce both have official or well-supported integrations.
  • Shipping and returns settings in Merchant Center that match your website, including UK delivery costs and times.
  • A Google Ads account linked to Merchant Center.
  • Conversion tracking that records purchases and their value, through Google Ads tags, GA4 or both.
  • A store that meets Google's policies: clear contact details, returns policy, secure checkout and accurate prices including VAT.

If your store is not yet in good shape, fix it first. A slow, confusing checkout wastes ad spend. Our website and ecommerce development service covers checkout and speed improvements.

Standard Shopping vs Performance Max

There are two main ways to run Shopping ads today.

Campaign typeHow it worksGood forWatch out for
Standard ShoppingShopping placements only; you control product groups, bids and negative keywordsControl, clear reporting, tight budgetsMore manual work to manage
Performance MaxAutomated campaigns across Shopping, Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail and DiscoverReach and automation with good conversion dataLess visibility into where money goes; needs strong tracking and assets

Many retailers use both: Performance Max for broad reach and Standard Shopping for specific products or tighter control. The right mix depends on your budget, data volume and margins.

Optimising your product feed

Feed optimisation is often the quickest win. Focus on:

Product titles

Titles are the strongest relevance signal. Put the most important words first, using the terms shoppers actually search: brand, product type, key attributes such as size, colour or material. "Women's Waterproof Hiking Boots, Leather, Size 6" is far more useful than "Explorer Boot".

Images

Use clear, high-quality images on a plain background for the main image. Avoid watermarks, promotional text and borders, which can cause disapprovals.

Identifiers and categories

Include GTINs (barcodes) for branded products where they exist, and accurate product categories and product types. These help Google match you to the right searches.

Price and availability

Prices and stock in the feed must match your website. Mismatches lead to disapprovals and, if repeated, account issues. Automated feed updates prevent most problems.

Structuring campaigns around profit

A single campaign for every product treats a high-margin best seller the same as a low-margin accessory. A better structure groups products by what matters commercially:

  • Margin bands, so you can bid more where you make more.
  • Best sellers versus long tail, so proven products get the budget they deserve.
  • Seasonal or promotional products, so you can push them at the right time.
  • Brand searches separated where possible, as they behave very differently from generic searches.

Use the custom label fields in your feed to tag products, and build campaigns or listing groups around those labels.

Budgets and bidding

There is no fixed minimum budget, but campaigns need enough data to learn. If your budget is small, focus it on your most profitable products rather than spreading it across the whole catalogue. Smart bidding strategies such as Target ROAS (return on ad spend) work well once there are enough conversions to learn from; before that, simpler strategies give more predictable results.

Remember that ad spend is separate from management fees. With our paid media service, advertising spend is paid directly to Google and is never marked up.

Tracking: the part most retailers get wrong

Google's bidding is only as good as the data it receives. Check that:

  1. Purchases are tracked once per order, with the correct revenue value.
  2. Consent mode is set up correctly for UK cookie rules, so data is not lost unnecessarily.
  3. Enhanced conversions are enabled where appropriate, to improve accuracy.
  4. GA4 and Google Ads numbers broadly agree; large gaps usually mean a tracking problem.

We always fix tracking before increasing spend. It is the single biggest factor in whether automated campaigns succeed.

Reading the numbers: which metrics matter

Google Ads reports a lot of figures. For Shopping campaigns, a few matter more than the rest:

  • Return on ad spend (ROAS). Revenue divided by ad spend. Useful, but always compare it with your margins: a ROAS that looks healthy on a low-margin product can still lose money.
  • Cost per acquisition. What you pay in ads for each order. Compare it with your average order profit, not revenue.
  • Conversion rate. If clicks are high but orders are low, the issue is often on the website: price, delivery costs, stock or checkout friction.
  • Impression share. Shows how often your products appear when they could. Low impression share on profitable products is a sign the budget or bids are holding you back.
  • Search terms. Not a single number, but the most useful report for spotting waste and new opportunities.

We would always rather see a smaller campaign that is clearly profitable than a large one with impressive revenue and no profit behind it. Work out your break-even ROAS for each margin band before setting targets, so everyone knows what "good" looks like.

Seasonality and promotions

UK retail has clear peaks: Black Friday and Cyber Monday, the run-up to Christmas, January sales and category-specific seasons such as summer garden or back to school. Plan feed updates, promotions and budget increases ahead of these periods, and use Merchant Center promotions to show offers directly on your listings. Check delivery cut-off dates in your feed and on your website in December, as shoppers look for them.

After a peak, review what sold and what did not. Products that performed well at full price often deserve more budget all year round.

Common Google Shopping mistakes

  • Ignoring search terms. Review search term reports and add negative keywords for irrelevant searches.
  • Weak product titles. Supplier titles and internal codes rarely match what shoppers type.
  • Unrealistic delivery costs. High or hidden delivery charges reduce clicks and conversions.
  • Judging too early. New campaigns need a learning period before results settle.
  • Running ads to a slow site. Every second of load time costs conversions, especially on mobile.

Shopping ads and SEO work together

Free product listings mean your Merchant Center feed can also appear in unpaid Shopping results, so feed work benefits organic visibility too. Should you manage Shopping ads yourself?

Small retailers can run a simple Shopping campaign themselves, especially with a clean feed and a modest catalogue. As budgets, product ranges and automation grow, the time needed for feed management, search term reviews and testing grows too, which is when specialist help usually pays for itself.

FAQs

How much do Google Shopping ads cost in the UK?

You pay per click, and costs vary by product category and competition. You control the daily budget, and management fees are separate from ad spend.

Do I need Performance Max for Shopping ads?

No. Standard Shopping campaigns are still available and give more control. Many retailers use both, depending on budget and data volume.

Why are my products disapproved in Merchant Center?

Common reasons include price or availability mismatches, missing identifiers, image issues, missing policies or shipping settings. Merchant Center diagnostics show the specific cause.

Can Shopify and WooCommerce stores run Google Shopping ads?

Yes. Both platforms can generate a product feed for Merchant Center through official or well-supported integrations.

How long before Shopping ads become profitable?

It depends on margins, competition and tracking quality. Expect an initial learning and testing period before performance settles and can be scaled.
