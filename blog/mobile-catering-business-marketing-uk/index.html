Mobile Catering Marketing in the UK: How to Book More Events and Pitches

Mobile catering marketing in the UK works when event planners can find your menus, pitch requirements and prices quickly, then enquire for a date in minutes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Private events, weddings and corporate bookings are usually more profitable than casual street trading, so build your website around enquiries for specific dates.
  • Planners want menus, serving capacity, pitch size, power needs and hygiene information up front, and the caterer who answers those first usually wins the shortlist.
  • Social media shows where you are trading this week, but search and a strong website bring the higher-value private hire enquiries that fill the diary months ahead.

Mobile catering marketing in the UK is about being easy to find and easy to book when a couple, an office manager or an event organiser is looking for food for a specific date. The street food traders and mobile caterers who build a full diary usually combine a clear website with menus and packages, strong local search visibility and social media that shows the food and the setup at real events.

This guide is for food trucks, horsebox bars, pizza vans, street food traders, hog roast caterers, coffee carts and event caterers that want more private bookings and better pitches. We cover the different types of customer, what your website must include, how to rank for local searches and how to balance social media with search.

Who books mobile caterers

Mobile caterers usually serve several distinct audiences, each with different needs. Wedding couples want food that fits the style of the day and a caterer they can trust with the timings. Corporate clients want reliable service for staff parties, product launches and summer events, often with invoices and paperwork in order. Private party hosts want a fun option for birthdays and garden parties. Event organisers, festival managers and venues want traders who are reliable, well presented and compliant.

Each audience searches differently. A couple might search "wood fired pizza van wedding [county]", an office manager might search "food truck hire for corporate event London", and a venue might look for "approved street food traders". Your marketing should give each of these buyers a page that speaks directly to them. Our guide to wedding business marketing covers the wedding side in more depth.

Mobile catering marketing UK: what your website needs

Many mobile caterers rely on a social media page alone, which makes it hard for planners to compare options quickly. A simple, well-structured website gives you a place to answer their questions and collect proper enquiries. The table below shows the pages we would usually include.

PageWhat it should cover
Menus and packagesSample menus, dietary options, serving style and a price guide or minimum spend
WeddingsService timings, evening food options, photos from weddings, how booking works
Corporate eventsStaff numbers you can serve, invoicing, paperwork, branded options
Private partiesGarden and venue setup, minimum guest numbers, travel area
Technical detailsPitch size, access, power and water needs, setup and pack-down time
Enquiry formDate, location, guest numbers, event type and budget

The technical details page is often overlooked, but it is exactly what venues and event organisers need. Listing your vehicle size, power requirements and access needs saves a round of emails and makes you look professional. Show your food properly

Food is a visual product, and the quality of your photos will influence bookings more than almost anything else. Invest in good photography of your dishes, your vehicle or stall, your team serving guests and the setup at different types of event. Natural light, clean backgrounds and real guests enjoying the food work far better than overly staged shots.

Short videos of food being prepared work well on social media and can be embedded on your website too. Keep image file sizes small so pages load quickly on phones, since many planners will browse on mobile. Our post on website image optimisation covers the practical steps.

Get found in local search

A Google Business Profile helps you appear in map results for searches like "food truck hire near me". Set it up as a service-area business covering the towns and counties you travel to, choose accurate categories, and upload photos regularly. Post upcoming public trading dates and seasonal menus to keep the profile active.

Reviews matter a great deal. After every private event, send a short thank-you message with a link asking for a review. Recent reviews that mention the type of event and the food help both rankings and trust. Our guides to the Google Business Profile and getting more Google reviews walk through both in detail.

Beyond the profile, build pages targeting the event types and areas you most want to serve, such as weddings in your county or corporate events in your nearest city. Our SEO and GEO service covers this kind of local and event-focused optimisation, including content that can be picked up in AI search answers.

Hygiene, licensing and paperwork

Planners and venues increasingly ask for paperwork before confirming a booking. It helps to state clearly on your website that you are registered with your local authority as a food business, that you hold public liability insurance, and that you can provide risk assessments, allergen information and gas or electrical safety documents on request where relevant.

If you have a food hygiene rating you are proud of, mention it and explain where customers can check it. Rules for food businesses, street trading consents and event licensing vary between councils and change over time, so check current official guidance from the Food Standards Agency and your local authority rather than relying on general advice. Allergen information on menus should be accurate and kept up to date whenever recipes change.

Social media and search work together

Instagram, Facebook and TikTok are natural homes for mobile caterers. They show where you are trading this week, build a following among locals and showcase your food. But social media alone tends to bring casual customers rather than private bookings planned months ahead. Search and a good website capture the planners who are actively looking to book.

Plan for the season

Most mobile caterers are busiest from late spring to early autumn, with a second peak around Christmas parties and markets. Couples often book wedding caterers a year or more ahead, while corporate summer events are typically planned in the spring. Use the quieter months to refresh photos, update menus and packages, build new pages and contact venues about approved supplier lists.

Keep a simple list of past customers and enquiries, and email them when you open bookings for the next season. A short message with new menus and available dates can fill a surprising number of slots without any ad spend.

It also helps to keep an availability note on your website, even a simple line saying which months are filling up. Planners who can see that dates are limited are more likely to enquire promptly, and it saves you answering requests for weekends that are already booked.

FAQs

What is the best mobile catering marketing in the UK?

For most caterers, it combines a clear website with menus and event pages, a strong Google Business Profile with regular reviews, good food photography and social media that shows where you are trading. Measure success by private booking enquiries, not followers.

Do food trucks need a website?

It is strongly recommended. Social media is useful, but a website lets planners see menus, prices, technical details and paperwork in one place and send a proper enquiry for their date.

Should I show prices on my catering website?

A price guide or minimum spend helps planners shortlist you and filters out enquiries that will not fit your budget. Exact quotes can follow once you know the date, location and guest numbers.

How do I get more wedding bookings as a mobile caterer?

Create a dedicated weddings page with photos from real weddings, timings, evening food options and booking steps. Ask couples for reviews after the day and build relationships with venues that allow external caterers.

What paperwork should I mention on my website?

Mention food business registration, public liability insurance, allergen information and risk assessments where relevant. Requirements vary by council and event, so check current official guidance before you publish.

Are Google Ads worth it for food truck hire?

They can be in peak season, especially for weddings and corporate events. Keep targeting tight to the areas you travel to, block irrelevant searches such as buying a food truck, and send clicks to the relevant event page.
