Schema Markup Guide for UK Businesses: Structured Data That Actually Helps

Schema markup tells search engines and AI tools what a page is about. This UK guide covers which types to use, how to add JSON-LD and how to test it.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Schema markup is structured data that describes your business, pages and products in a format machines can read, usually added as JSON-LD.
  • Only mark up what is visible on the page and true. Markup that does not match the content can lose rich results or trigger a manual action.
  • Self-serving review markup about your own business is not eligible for review rich results, so do not rely on star ratings for your own services.

Schema markup is structured data you add to a web page so search engines and AI tools can understand exactly what it describes: your business, a product, an article, an event or a set of questions. For most UK businesses the practical starting point is Organization or LocalBusiness markup on the home page, Product markup on shop pages, Article markup on blog posts and BreadcrumbList markup across the site, all written in JSON-LD and tested before going live.

Structured data does not directly push a page up the rankings. What it does is remove ambiguity, make some pages eligible for richer search results, and give AI search systems cleaner facts to work with. This schema markup guide for UK businesses explains which types matter, how to add them without breaking anything and the mistakes we see most often in audits.

What schema markup is and why it matters

A normal web page is written for people. A search engine can read the words, but it has to infer that a string of numbers is a phone number, that a block of text is an opening-hours table, or that a price belongs to one product rather than another. Schema markup states those facts explicitly using a shared vocabulary of types (such as Product or LocalBusiness) and properties (such as price, address or openingHours).

That explicit labelling helps in three ways:

  • Understanding. Search engines can connect your brand, address, products and content with less guesswork, which supports how your business appears in knowledge panels and local results.
  • Eligibility for rich results. Some search features, such as product prices and stock status, breadcrumb trails and event listings, depend on valid structured data.
  • AI search. Generative answers draw on pages that are easy to parse. Clear entities and facts make your pages easier to cite. Our guide to generative engine optimisation explains how this fits into wider AI visibility work.

The schema types most UK businesses need

There are hundreds of schema types, but most sites only need a handful. The table below covers the ones we use most for service businesses, shops and publishers.

Schema typeWhere to use itWhat it describes
OrganizationHome page or about pageBrand name, logo, contact details, social profiles
LocalBusiness (or a subtype)Home page or location pagesAddress, phone, opening hours, area served
WebSiteHome pageSite name and, where relevant, internal search
BreadcrumbListEvery page below the home pageThe path from home to the current page
Article or BlogPostingBlog posts and guidesHeadline, publish date, update date, author organisation
Product with OfferProduct pagesName, image, price in GBP, availability, SKU, brand
ServiceService pagesThe service offered, provider and area served
FAQPagePages with a genuine question and answer sectionQuestions and their visible answers
EventEvent or course pagesDates, venue or online location, ticket details

If you run a physical business, LocalBusiness markup should match your Google Business Profile exactly: same name, same address format, same phone number. Inconsistent details weaken trust signals. Our Google Business Profile guide and local SEO guide cover keeping those details aligned.

Product schema for UK online shops

For ecommerce, Product markup is the most valuable type. Include the product name, images, description, brand, SKU or GTIN where you have one, and an Offer with price, priceCurrency set to GBP, and availability. Keep the price consistent with what shoppers see, including VAT if your displayed prices include it. If your structured data says one price and the page says another, search engines may ignore the markup.

Product data also overlaps with your shopping feed. If you run Google Shopping, mismatches between feed, markup and page can cause disapprovals. See our Google Shopping ads guide for how feeds and on-page data should line up, and our Shopify SEO guide for platform-specific points, since many themes already output some Product markup that may need fixing rather than duplicating.

A note on review and rating markup

Review and AggregateRating markup describe reviews and star ratings. They are legitimate for pages about a product or a third-party item, such as a retailer showing customer reviews of a product it sells. However, self-serving reviews are not eligible for review rich results. If a business adds markup to its own site that rates itself or its own services, search engines will not show stars for it, and marking up reviews that are not visible on the page, or that you wrote yourself, breaks the guidelines. We do not add review markup about our own services, and we advise clients to follow the same rule.

How to add schema markup: a step-by-step method

There are three formats for structured data: JSON-LD, Microdata and RDFa. JSON-LD is the format Google recommends and the one we use, because it sits in a single script block in the page head or body and does not have to be woven through your HTML.

  1. List your page templates. Most sites have a small number: home, service, location, product, category, blog post, contact. Decide which schema type belongs on each template rather than marking up pages one by one.
  2. Map properties to real content. If the page does not show a price, do not put a price in the markup.
  3. Generate JSON-LD from the template. On most platforms the markup should pull from the same fields as the page, so prices, dates and stock levels update automatically.
  4. Check for duplicates. Themes, plugins and apps often add their own structured data. Two conflicting Product blocks on one page cause confusion. Remove or disable one source.
  5. Test before release. Use the Rich Results Test for features Google supports, and a schema validator for general syntax. Test a live URL and the rendered code, not only a snippet.
  6. Monitor after release. Google Search Console reports structured data errors and warnings by type. Check them after every template change.

On WordPress, an SEO plugin will usually handle Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList and Article markup, and you add Product or Service markup where needed. On Shopify, the theme provides a base that often needs extending. On custom builds, markup should be generated server-side from your content data. If a rebuild is on the cards, it is far cheaper to plan structured data in the build than to retrofit it, which is one reason we include it in our website and ecommerce development work.

Common schema markup mistakes

Most structured data problems we find in a technical SEO audit fall into a few patterns:

  • Markup that does not match the page. Prices, ratings, FAQs or opening hours in the code that are not visible to visitors. This is the quickest route to losing rich results.
  • FAQ markup on every page. FAQPage markup is for genuine question and answer content. Adding it everywhere does not create extra search features, and Google now shows FAQ rich results only for a narrow set of authoritative sites.
  • Wrong or missing currency. UK shops sometimes leave a default currency from a theme. Set priceCurrency to GBP.
  • Out-of-date details. A changed phone number or address updated on the page but not in the markup.
  • Duplicate and conflicting blocks. Two plugins outputting two Organization entities with different names.
  • Markup on blocked or noindexed pages. Structured data cannot help a page that search engines are told not to index.

Schema markup and AI search

AI Overviews and chat-based search tools build answers from pages they can parse and trust. Structured data is not a guaranteed route into those answers, but it supports the things that are: clearly identified entities, consistent business details, accurate product facts and well-organised content. Treat schema as one part of making your site machine-readable, alongside clear headings, direct answers near the top of the page, and consistent facts across the web.

FAQs

What is a schema markup guide for UK businesses meant to cover?

A practical schema markup guide for UK businesses should cover which structured data types to use for your site, how to add them in JSON-LD, how to format UK details such as addresses and GBP prices, and how to test and monitor the markup after launch.

Does schema markup improve rankings?

Not directly. Structured data helps search engines understand your pages and can make them eligible for rich results, which may improve click-through. Rankings still depend mainly on content quality, relevance, links and technical health.

Which schema format should I use?

JSON-LD. It is the format Google recommends, it keeps markup separate from your visible HTML, and it is easier to generate from templates and maintain over time.

Can I add review stars for my own business?

Not through your own website markup. Self-serving reviews, where a business marks up reviews of itself or its own services on its own site, are not eligible for review rich results. Review markup is meant for pages reviewing a product or third-party item, and the reviews must be genuine and visible on the page.

How do I test my structured data?

Run the page URL through the Rich Results Test to check eligibility for Google features, use a schema validator for general syntax, and watch the enhancement reports in Google Search Console for errors across the whole site.

Does my Shopify or WordPress site already have schema?

Usually some. Most themes and SEO plugins output basic Organization, Article or Product markup. It is worth checking what is already there before adding more, because duplicated or conflicting markup is a common problem. If you would like us to look at yours, get in touch.
