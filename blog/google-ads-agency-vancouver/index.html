Google Ads Agency for Vancouver Businesses: How Remote PPC Works

Hiring a Google Ads agency in Vancouver? How remote PPC management works for BC businesses, what good setup looks like, fees and questions to ask first.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Vancouver business can run Google Ads well with a remote agency when it owns the account, conversion tracking is right and reporting is tied to enquiries or sales.
  • Location targeting across Metro Vancouver, sensible negative keywords and landing pages that match each search usually matter more than raw budget.
  • Management fees and ad spend are separate, so ask any agency exactly what you pay them and what goes straight to Google.

A Google Ads agency for a Vancouver business should turn ad spend into enquiries or sales you can measure, and explain every change in plain language. That work can be done remotely: Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that manages paid search for Canadian businesses online, with no office in Vancouver, because campaigns live in your Google Ads account rather than in a meeting room.

This guide covers what a Google Ads agency usually does for Vancouver and wider British Columbia businesses, how to set up campaigns properly, what management costs with us and how to judge any agency before you hand over your account.

What a Google Ads agency in Vancouver should do

Vancouver is an expensive, competitive search market. Real estate, legal, renovation, health and wellness, hospitality and ecommerce all bid hard for the same buyers, so clicks can be costly and waste adds up quickly. A good agency focuses on control before scale:

  • Conversion tracking first. Calls, forms, bookings and purchases tracked properly, so the account optimises for real results. Our conversion tracking setup guide explains how.
  • Campaign structure. Search campaigns grouped by service or product, with clear budgets for each priority.
  • Keywords and negatives. Targeting buyer-intent searches and excluding irrelevant ones. Our negative keywords guide shows how much waste this can remove.
  • Ads and assets. Clear headlines, honest offers and useful assets such as call and location details.
  • Landing pages. Pages that match the search and make the next step obvious.
  • Ongoing management. Search term reviews, bid strategy checks, budget shifts and testing every month.

Our paid media service covers Google Ads alongside Microsoft Ads, Meta, TikTok and YouTube, so channels can be planned together.

Working with a remote Google Ads agency from Vancouver

The UK is usually eight hours ahead of Vancouver. UK late afternoons overlap with Vancouver mornings, so a regular call can sit early in your day, and account checks can happen while your business is closed.

  • You own the account. Your business should own the Google Ads account and billing, with the agency given manager access.
  • Agreed approvals. New campaigns, offers and large budget changes signed off before they go live.
  • A fixed call slot. Weekly or fortnightly at a time that suits your team.
  • Shared reporting. A monthly report plus a live dashboard you can open any time.
  • Feedback on lead quality. Your team tells us which enquiries were good, so the account can be steered towards them.

What the first months of Google Ads usually look like

  1. Weeks one and two: audit and tracking. Reviewing any existing account, fixing conversion tracking and agreeing goals and budgets.
  2. Weeks three and four: build or rebuild. Structuring campaigns, writing ads, adding negatives and checking landing pages.
  3. Months two and three: learning and refining. Reviewing search terms, adjusting bids and budgets and pausing what does not work.
  4. Month three onwards: scaling carefully. Putting more budget behind what converts and testing new ads and pages.

Paid search can bring traffic from day one, but stable, efficient performance typically takes three to six months as data builds up and the account is refined. Be wary of anyone promising a fixed cost per lead before they have seen your data.

How to judge any Google Ads agency in Vancouver

  • Who owns the account? If the agency owns it, you may lose your history when you leave.
  • How is the fee worked out? Fixed fee, percentage of spend or a mix. Understand how it changes as spend grows.
  • What do they report on? Enquiries, sales and cost per result, not just clicks and impressions.
  • Do they review search terms? Regular reviews are where much of the waste is found.
  • Will they audit before building? Our PPC audit checklist shows what a proper review should cover.

Common Google Ads mistakes we see on BC accounts

When we review an existing account, the same handful of problems tend to come up. Fixing them is often the quickest early improvement, and none of them need a bigger budget.

  • Broad match with no negatives. Broad keywords left to run without regular search term reviews can spend heavily on searches that were never going to buy.
  • Every service in one campaign. When high-margin and low-margin services share a budget, the cheaper clicks usually win and the valuable work gets less exposure.
  • Sending all traffic to the homepage. A searcher looking for one specific service should land on a page about that service, with a clear way to get in touch.
  • Counting the wrong conversions. Page views or button clicks counted as leads make the account look healthier than it is and steer automated bidding the wrong way.
  • Ads running when nobody answers. Call-focused campaigns running overnight or at weekends can waste money if calls go unanswered.
  • Automated suggestions accepted without review. Some recommendations help, but others mainly increase spend. Each one deserves a second look.

Canadian points to raise before you launch

  • Privacy and consent. Tracking tags and forms should reflect federal privacy law and BC's private-sector privacy legislation where it applies. Check current guidance from the relevant privacy commissioners and take your own advice.
  • Advertising rules. Claims, pricing and offers should follow Canadian advertising standards and Google's own policies. Regulated sectors such as health, finance and real estate have extra restrictions; check current official guidance, and approval rests with your own compliance team.
  • Taxes on ad spend. How Google bills Canadian accounts and applies sales taxes is between your business and Google. Check current guidance with your accountant.
  • Language. We write ads and pages in English. If you target French-speaking audiences, French ads and pages are written with your team or a translator.
