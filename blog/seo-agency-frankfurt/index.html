Technical SEO first

We start with a technical audit because content cannot rank if search engines struggle to read the site. We check how pages are crawled and indexed, look for duplicate pages, broken links and redirect chains, and test page speed on mobile. Our guide to Core Web Vitals explains the speed measures we track and why they matter.

For sites with both German and English pages, we check that each language version has its own URL, that hreflang tags point to the right pages, and that the language switcher works without sending people back to the home page. Mistakes here are common and can cause the wrong language to appear in German search results.

We also add structured data where it fits, such as organisation, local business, product, article and FAQ markup. Structured data does not guarantee rich results, but it helps search engines understand what each page is about.

Local SEO across Frankfurt

If you serve customers in person or in a defined area, local SEO matters. We set up or tidy your business profile, check your name, address and phone number are consistent across listings, and build location pages only where you genuinely serve people. A firm working with clients across Sachsenhausen, Westend, Bornheim and the wider Rhine-Main area can describe that honestly, without creating thin copy-and-paste pages for every district.

Our local SEO guide covers the basics, and most of the principles apply in Germany too. Reviews matter, but we never write fake reviews or offer incentives that break platform rules.

Content that answers real questions

Frankfurt buyers tend to research carefully. A finance director choosing a software supplier, or a trade fair exhibitor booking a stand builder, will compare options before picking up the phone. We plan content around the questions they ask: what a service costs, how long it takes, what to look for in a provider and how to compare options.

We write the English versions and brief your team or translator on the German versions. We avoid machine-translating pages without review, because poor German copy damages trust quickly. If your audience is international, we can keep English as the main language and add German pages for your most important services first.

Privacy, cookies and accessibility

Any SEO work that touches tracking or site changes has to respect European rules. These notes are general, so please check official guidance or speak to your own adviser.

  • GDPR applies to personal data collected through forms, analytics and advertising tags. Your privacy notice should explain what you collect and why.
  • ePrivacy and cookie consent rules mean non-essential cookies, including most analytics and advertising cookies, generally need consent before they are set. We set up analytics to respect your consent banner.
  • The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing digital services, including a lot of e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt. The harmonised standard EN 301 549 points to WCAG at level AA, which is a sensible target for any site.

Accessible sites also tend to be easier for search engines to read. Our website accessibility guide covers the practical steps we follow.

Choosing an SEO agency in Frankfurt

Whoever you choose, ask clear questions. Who will do the work? How will they handle German content? What will they report each month? Avoid any agency that guarantees first-page rankings, because no one controls search results. Look for a plan that starts with technical fixes and then builds content and authority steadily.
