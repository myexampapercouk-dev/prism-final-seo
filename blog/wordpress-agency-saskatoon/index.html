Common WordPress problems we fix

  • Too many plugins. Each plugin adds code, risk and update work. Many sites run dozens where a handful would do.
  • Heavy page builders. Some builders add large amounts of code to every page. The modern block editor can do most of the same work more lightly.
  • No backups or untested backups. A backup you have never restored is a guess, not a plan.
  • Admin accounts shared by everyone. Each person should have their own login with the right role.
  • Missing redirects after a redesign. Changed URLs without redirects can lose rankings that took years to build.

WordPress for different Saskatoon organisations

WordPress is flexible enough to suit very different kinds of organisation. Here is how we typically approach the sites we are asked to build:

  • Trades and home services: clear service pages, a service area page for Saskatoon and nearby communities, strong calls to action and quote request forms that are quick to complete on a phone.
  • Professional firms: accountants, engineers, consultants and lawyers need credible team pages, service detail and insights or news sections their staff can update.
  • Agri-food and industrial suppliers: product or capability pages, downloadable specification sheets, dealer or distributor information and enquiry forms routed to the right person.
  • Clinics and health practices: treatment pages, booking links and careful privacy wording agreed with the practice.
  • Non-profits and community groups: event listings, donation links handled by a trusted payment provider and simple editing for volunteers.
  • Retailers: WooCommerce stores with product categories, shipping rules and tax settings configured with advice from your accountant.

Whatever the type, we keep the plugin list short and the editing experience simple, so the site stays fast and manageable long after launch.

Questions to ask any WordPress agency

Before you hire anyone, ask who will own the domain and hosting, which theme and plugins they plan to use, how updates and backups are handled after launch, whether you will receive training, and how redirects will be managed if you are replacing an existing site. Clear answers to these questions protect you whichever agency you choose. It is also worth asking how the agency measures success after launch, because a website should be judged by the enquiries, bookings or sales it brings, not only by how it looks on the day it goes live.
