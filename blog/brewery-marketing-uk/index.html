Brewery Marketing in the UK: How Independent Breweries Sell More Beer Direct and to Trade

Brewery marketing in the UK works when drinkers can find and buy your beer online, visit your taproom easily and pubs and shops can order without friction.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Independent breweries usually sell through several routes at once: an online shop, a taproom, local pubs and bottle shops, and events.
  • A fast online shop with clear delivery and age checks, plus a taproom page and a simple trade enquiry route, covers most needs.
  • Alcohol marketing has its own rules, so keep claims responsible, target adults only and check current official guidance.

Brewery marketing in the UK works when drinkers can find and buy your beer online, plan a visit to your taproom easily, and pubs, bars and bottle shops can see your range and order without friction. The independent breweries that grow tend to combine a well-run online shop, a strong local presence around the taproom, regular email to their own customers and a clear route for trade buyers.

This guide is for independent and craft breweries, microbreweries, brewpubs and cider makers selling to consumers and to the trade. We are a studio that builds websites and online shops and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so we focus on the digital side. Alcohol licensing, duty, labelling and advertising rules are matters for you, and you should always check current official guidance or take advice.

Brewery marketing UK: the foundations

Before running ads or chasing bigger stockists, make sure the basics are in place. They support every other channel.

  • A clear, fast website that tells people who you are, what you brew and where to buy it, in that order.
  • An online shop with good product pages, clear delivery costs and age checks.
  • A taproom page with opening days, food, dog and family policies, events and how to get there.
  • A Google Business Profile for the brewery and taproom, with current opening hours and photos. Our Google Business Profile guide explains the setup.
  • A stockist list or map, so people can find your beer locally.
  • A trade page for pubs and shops, with a simple enquiry form.

Running a brewery online shop

Selling direct gives you the best margin and a direct relationship with drinkers, but beer is heavy and fragile, and alcohol brings extra checks. A good shop handles these well.

  • Product pages with real detail: style, strength, tasting notes, allergens, pack sizes and best before guidance. Our guide to ecommerce product descriptions shows how to write them well.
  • Mixed cases and bundles, which suit both gift buyers and curious drinkers.
  • Clear delivery rules: which areas you deliver to, costs, minimum orders and packaging. Our guide to online shop shipping rates covers how to set these.
  • Age verification at checkout and on delivery, in line with your licence conditions and courier requirements. Check current official guidance for what applies to you.
  • Subscriptions or beer clubs for regular buyers, if you have the range to keep them interesting.

Both Shopify and WooCommerce can handle a brewery shop. Our guide to food and drink ecommerce covers platform choice, delivery and packaging in more detail, and our website and ecommerce service builds and looks after shops like this.

Filling the taproom

The taproom is often where people fall in love with a brewery, and it can drive online sales and trade interest later. Local marketing is what keeps it busy.

  • Keep opening times accurate everywhere, especially if you only open on certain days.
  • Publish an events calendar: new releases, tap takeovers, food trucks, quizzes, tours and seasonal events.
  • Make tour and group booking easy, ideally online. Our guide to online booking systems compares options.
  • Ask happy visitors for reviews and reply to them.
  • Collect email addresses at the bar and online, with clear consent, for release news and event invites.

Email and repeat customers

For most breweries, email to existing customers is one of the most valuable channels. Drinkers who already like your beer want to hear about new releases, limited batches and events.

  • Release announcements for new and limited beers, with a direct link to buy.
  • Taproom and event news for local subscribers.
  • Reorder reminders for people who bought a mixed case a while ago.
  • Abandoned cart emails, which recover orders from people who were nearly there.

Our guide to ecommerce email marketing covers the essentials. Under UK GDPR and PECR, marketing emails need proper consent or a valid soft opt-in, so check current ICO guidance.

Advertising alcohol responsibly

Alcohol marketing is more tightly controlled than most products. Advertising codes in the UK set rules on how alcohol can be promoted, including not appealing to under-18s, not linking drinking with social or sexual success, and not encouraging excessive drinking. Industry codes also cover naming, packaging and promotion. Platforms such as Google and Meta have their own alcohol advertising policies and restrict targeting to adults.

In practice, this means using imagery and wording that focus on flavour, craft and the brewery rather than effects of drinking, targeting adult audiences only and checking each platform's current policies before launching. We do not give legal or compliance advice, so check current official guidance or take advice if you are unsure.

Winning more trade accounts

  • A trade page with your core range, formats such as cask, keg and small pack, and how to order.
  • Downloadable product information such as spec sheets and images for retailers to use.
  • Quick replies to trade enquiries and simple ordering, whether by email, phone or a trade portal.
  • Support for stockists: share where your beer is available, which encourages venues to keep stocking it.

Telling your brewery story

Drinkers and trade buyers often choose an independent brewery because of the people, place and ideas behind the beer. A short, honest about page, notes on how each beer was developed and photos from brew days give people a reason to pick you over a bigger brand on the same shelf. Keep the story factual and avoid claims you cannot support, such as awards or ingredients you do not actually use. Our guide to tone of voice can help you write in a consistent, recognisable way.

FAQs

Brewery marketing UK: where should an independent start?

Start with a clear website that shows your beers and where to buy them, an online shop with good product pages and delivery details, a taproom page with accurate opening times, and an email list. Ads and bigger trade pushes build on these foundations.

Can a brewery advertise on Facebook and Google?

Yes, within their alcohol advertising policies and UK advertising codes. Ads must target adults only and avoid content that encourages excessive drinking or appeals to under-18s. Check each platform's current policies before launching.

Is Shopify good for a brewery online shop?

Shopify works well for many breweries because it handles bundles, subscriptions and shipping rules neatly. WooCommerce is a good choice if you already use WordPress. The right option depends on your range and how you deliver.

How do I get more people to visit my taproom?

Keep opening times accurate on Google and your website, publish a regular events calendar, make tour and group booking easy, ask for reviews and use email and social posts to tell locals what is on.

Do I need age verification on my brewery website?

Selling alcohol online usually involves age checks at checkout and on delivery, and your licence and courier may set specific requirements. Check current official guidance for what applies to your business.

How can a small brewery get stocked in more pubs and shops?

Make it easy for buyers to see your range, formats and prices, reply to trade enquiries quickly, provide product information they can use and show where your beer is already stocked.
