Amazon SEO Agency UK: Marketplace Listing SEO for Amazon and eBay Sellers

Amazon SEO agency UK guide: how marketplace listing SEO works on Amazon and eBay, from keywords and titles to images and A+ content, plus a checklist.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Marketplace search rewards relevance and sales performance, so keyword-rich listings only work when images and price convert.
  • Titles, bullet points, backend search terms, item specifics and A+ content each play a different role; treat them as one system.
  • Keep marketplace and website SEO joined up: the same keyword research and product data should feed both.

An Amazon SEO agency improves how your product listings rank and convert in marketplace search, mainly on Amazon and eBay, by researching keywords, rewriting titles and bullets, filling backend fields, improving images and building A+ content. For UK sellers it matters because most shoppers search inside the marketplace and rarely scroll past the first page of results.

This guide explains how marketplace search works, what to optimise on each listing, how Amazon and eBay differ, and what to look for in an agency. If you also sell on your own site, our ecommerce SEO agency guide covers the Google side.

How Amazon SEO works

Amazon's search aims to show the products a shopper is most likely to buy. Two broad groups of signals matter:

  • Relevance. Whether your listing contains the words people search, in the title, bullets, description, backend search terms and attributes.
  • Performance. Whether people click and buy: click-through rate, conversion rate, sales history, reviews, price, stock and delivery speed.

Relevance gets you considered. Performance decides how high you stay. That is why stuffing a title with keywords rarely works on its own: a listing that ranks but does not convert slips back down. eBay's search works on similar principles, with more weight on item specifics, listing quality and seller performance.

Keyword research for marketplaces

Marketplace keyword research starts with how shoppers describe the product, not how you describe it internally. Useful sources include the marketplace's own search suggestions, search term reports from your sponsored ads, competitor listings, customer questions and reviews, and Google keyword data. Group terms by meaning, choose a primary phrase for the title and spread supporting terms through bullets and backend fields.

UK spelling and vocabulary matter: trainers not sneakers, colour not color, duvet not comforter. Many UK listings copied from US catalogues lose relevance because of this.

Images and A+ content

Images often decide whether a shopper clicks and buys. Follow the main image rules for your category, then use the remaining slots to answer questions: size against a familiar object, what is in the box, materials close up, the product in use, and a simple infographic of key benefits. On mobile, which is where many marketplace purchases happen, images do more selling than text.

A+ content (available to brand registered sellers) adds modules below the listing. Use it to compare your range, explain features with images and tell a short brand story. It mainly helps conversion, which in turn supports ranking.

Marketplace SEO and advertising work together

Sponsored ads can help a new listing gain early sales and give you search term data to improve the organic listing. In turn, a better listing lowers advertising cost because it converts more clicks. Treat the two as one plan. The same logic applies to Google Shopping, where product data quality drives results.

Amazon and eBay listing checklist

  • Primary keyword chosen for each product and placed early in the title.
  • Titles follow category length and style rules.
  • Five clear bullet points, benefits first, in UK English.
  • Backend search terms filled with synonyms and spellings, no repetition.
  • Every relevant attribute and item specific completed.
  • Main image meets the rules; at least six supporting images.
  • A+ content built for best-selling products if brand registered.
  • Prices, stock and delivery options reviewed against competitors.
  • Reviews gathered only through approved methods.
  • Sponsored ad search terms reviewed monthly for new keywords.
  • Listing performance tracked per product, not just account-wide.

Keeping marketplace and website SEO joined up

Many UK sellers run their own store alongside marketplaces. Use the same keyword research and product data for both, but avoid copying marketplace descriptions word for word onto your site; write fuller pages that help Google understand each product. Common marketplace listing mistakes

Many UK listings underperform for the same handful of reasons:

  • Copying US catalogue content. American spellings, sizes and terms reduce relevance for UK shoppers and can look careless.
  • Keyword-stuffed titles. Long, repetitive titles may break category rules, look untrustworthy and get truncated on mobile, where only the first part of the title shows.
  • Empty attributes. Shoppers filter by size, colour, material and other fields. A product with blank attributes can disappear from filtered results entirely.
  • Weak secondary images. Many sellers upload a compliant main image and little else. Supporting images answer the questions that otherwise turn into returns or negative reviews.
  • Ignoring customer questions and reviews. These show the exact words buyers use and the doubts they have. Feed both back into bullets, images and A+ content.
  • Running out of stock. Stock-outs can damage sales history and ranking, and recovery can take time. Plan stock around peak periods such as Black Friday and Christmas.
  • Treating listing work as a one-off. Competitors change their listings, prices and advertising. Review best sellers at least quarterly.

Measuring marketplace listing performance

Track each product rather than only the account total, because account-wide numbers hide which listings need work. Useful measures include sessions or page views per listing, unit session percentage (the share of visits that become orders), search ranking for the primary keywords, Buy Box share where several sellers share a listing, sponsored advertising cost as a share of sales, and return rates with the reasons given. When a change is made to a listing, note the date so you can compare performance before and after. Change one major element at a time on important products so you can tell what made the difference.

FAQs

What does an Amazon SEO agency UK do?

An Amazon SEO agency in the UK researches marketplace keywords, rewrites titles and bullets, fills backend search terms and attributes, briefs images and builds A+ content so listings rank and convert better on Amazon.co.uk.

Is Amazon SEO different from Google SEO?

Yes. Amazon focuses on purchase likelihood, so sales performance, price and reviews carry great weight. Google focuses on answering a search well across many types of page.

How long does Amazon SEO take to work?

Listing changes can be indexed quickly, but rankings depend on sales performance, which builds over weeks and months. Results typically build over three to six months.

Do I need brand registry for A+ content?

Yes, A+ content is available to brand owners enrolled in Amazon Brand Registry. Without it, focus on titles, bullets, images and backend terms.

Does eBay listing SEO matter as much as Amazon?

For many UK sellers eBay is a meaningful channel. Complete item specifics, clear titles and good images make a noticeable difference to visibility there.

Can I use the same listing copy on my website?

Use the same research, but write fuller, original product pages for your own site so they can rank in Google rather than duplicating marketplace copy.
