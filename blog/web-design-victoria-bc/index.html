Web Design in Victoria, BC: A Guide for Local Businesses

Looking for web design in Victoria, BC? What a good business website needs, how remote web design works, timelines, costs and questions to ask any designer.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Victoria business website should be fast on mobile, clear about what you offer and built to handle seasonal visitors as well as year-round locals.
  • Remote web design works when the content plan, approvals and ownership of the domain, hosting and site are agreed before the build starts.
  • Our small business websites start at £1,000-£2,000 and typically take three to five weeks, with Canadian dollar quotes on request.

Web design in Victoria, BC should give your business a fast, clear website that turns visitors into bookings, enquiries or sales, and that your own team can keep up to date. It does not need a local office: Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites for Canadian businesses remotely, with no office in Victoria, working through video calls, shared documents and a private staging site you can review at every step.

This guide covers what Victoria and Vancouver Island businesses usually need from a website, how a remote project runs across the time difference, typical timelines and costs with us, and what to ask any designer before you commit.

What Victoria businesses need from a website

Victoria's economy leans on tourism and hospitality, government and public-sector suppliers, education, technology, marine businesses, wellness and a strong independent retail scene. Many businesses serve two audiences at once: residents across Greater Victoria, Saanich, Esquimalt, Oak Bay, Langford and the Saanich Peninsula, and visitors planning a trip from the mainland, the United States or further afield. A good site handles both:

  • Clear answers for locals. Services, prices or price guidance, opening hours and how to get in touch, all easy to find on a phone.
  • Useful planning information for visitors. What to expect, how to book, how to find you and what is seasonal.
  • Online booking where it fits. Tours, clinics, studios and restaurants often benefit from booking built into the site. Our guide to online booking systems compares the options.
  • Mobile first. Visitors browsing on the ferry or walking around downtown are almost always on phones. Our guide to mobile website design covers what matters.
  • Good photography. Real images of your place, products and people do more than stock photos ever can.
  • Search foundations. One page per main service, sensible titles and clean structure so the site can rank from launch.

Web design for Victoria hospitality and tourism businesses

Hotels, guest houses, tour operators, restaurants and attractions face a particular challenge: much of their booking traffic passes through large third-party platforms that take a commission. A strong website will not replace those platforms overnight, but it can win more direct bookings from people who already know your name.

  • A clear direct booking path. Make booking on your own site as easy as booking elsewhere.
  • Reasons to book direct. Flexible terms, local tips or small extras, stated honestly.
  • Seasonal content. Winter storm-watching, summer whale-watching, festivals and shoulder-season offers, kept current.
  • Fast image-heavy pages. Galleries need careful image handling so they do not slow the site.

Our guide to winning more hotel direct bookings goes into more depth, and our hospitality sector page shows how we approach the wider sector.

How remote web design works for Victoria clients

The UK is usually eight hours ahead of Victoria, so UK late afternoons overlap with Victoria mornings. That rhythm suits a website project well: you send feedback during your day and wake up to the changes. A typical project runs like this:

  1. Discovery call. Goals, customers, services, seasons and any existing site.
  2. Content plan. Who writes what, which photos are needed and which pages matter most. Our guide to preparing content before a build explains why this step saves time.
  3. Design. Key page designs shared for comment before the build starts.
  4. Build on staging. A private address you can test on your own phone and computer.
  5. Testing. Forms, booking, speed, mobile layouts and accessibility checked.
  6. Launch and handover. The site goes live on your domain with redirects from any old pages, and you receive logins and a short editing guide.

Small business websites typically take three to five weeks, and ecommerce builds five to twelve weeks. Timing around your busy season matters: launching before summer is far less stressful than launching in the middle of it.

Redesigning an existing website without losing ground

If your current site already brings in visitors from Google, a redesign needs care. Changing page addresses without redirects, cutting useful content or launching a slower site can lose rankings that took years to earn. We map existing pages first, note which bring traffic and bookings, and plan redirects for anything that moves. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings covers the full process.

Common website problems for Island businesses

When we look at existing small business sites, the same issues come up again and again. Most are simple to fix during a rebuild, and fixing them often makes more difference than a new colour scheme.

  • Out-of-date information. Old opening hours, last year's prices or a menu that no longer exists frustrate visitors and generate unnecessary calls. A site your team can edit easily solves most of this.
  • Booking hidden away. If visitors have to hunt for a booking link, many will go back to a third-party platform instead. The booking button should be visible on every page that needs it.
  • Huge image files. Beautiful photos of the harbour or the coast can make pages painfully slow on mobile data if they are not resized and compressed properly.
  • No clear location details. Visitors need to know exactly where you are, where to park and how you relate to downtown, the ferry terminals or the airport.
  • Contact forms that go nowhere. Forms that send to an old inbox, or land in spam, quietly lose enquiries. Every form should be tested at launch and checked regularly afterwards.
  • One page doing every job. A single page listing every service rarely ranks well for any of them. Separate, focused pages give each service a fair chance in search.

Canadian points to plan into the site

  • Privacy. Forms, analytics and cookies should reflect federal privacy law and BC's private-sector privacy legislation where it applies. Check current guidance from the relevant privacy commissioners and take your own advice on policy wording.
  • Accessibility. We build to recognised accessibility good practice. Some organisations have specific obligations, so check current official guidance for what applies to you.
  • Email consent. Newsletter sign-ups should follow Canada's anti-spam legislation on consent. Check current official guidance.
  • French and other languages. We write in English. If you want French pages, or pages for international visitors, they are written with your team or a translator.

Questions to ask any web designer in Victoria

  • Who owns the domain, hosting and site? Your business should. Our guide to website ownership and handover lists what to confirm in writing.
  • Can my team update it? Ask to see the editing screens before signing.
  • What support follows launch? Understand updates, backups and how small changes are charged.
  • Is search part of the build? Titles, structure, redirects and speed should be included, not added later.

FAQs

Can a UK studio handle web design in Victoria, BC?

Yes. Prism Works Studio builds websites for Canadian businesses remotely and has no office in Victoria. Calls, design reviews and testing all happen online, with a staging site you can check at any time.

How much does a website cost for a Victoria business?

Our small business websites are £1,000-£2,000 and ecommerce websites £1,500-£3,500, published in pounds sterling. Canadian dollar quotes are available on request once the scope is agreed.

How long does a new website take?

Small business websites typically take three to five weeks and ecommerce sites five to twelve weeks. Content and feedback speed are usually the biggest factors.

Can my website take bookings directly?

Yes. Many hospitality, tour and wellness businesses add a booking system to their site. We help you choose one that suits how you work and fits the design.

When is the best time for a Victoria tourism business to relaunch?

Usually in the quieter months, well before the main summer season, so any issues can be fixed before traffic peaks.

Will I own my website after launch?

Yes. Your business should own the domain, hosting account and website, and you receive full logins at handover.
