Polish and English search

Many Warsaw firms need to rank in two languages. Polish buyers usually search in Polish, while international buyers, investors and recruits often search in English. Doing both well takes planning.

  • Separate keyword research. Polish phrasing is not a direct translation of English. Search terms, word endings and question forms differ, so each language needs its own research.
  • Separate URLs. Each language version should live on its own URL, such as a /pl/ and /en/ folder, rather than switching language with a button that leaves one URL.
  • Hreflang tags. These tell search engines which version suits which searcher.
  • Native-quality copy. Machine translation without review tends to read badly and convert poorly. Polish copy should come from your team or a professional translator.

We build the keyword map, structure and briefs in English, then optimise the Polish pages your translator supplies.

Compliance notes for Polish websites

SEO work touches tracking, forms and content, so it helps to know the main EU rules. These notes are general; please check official guidance or your own legal adviser.

  • GDPR. Personal data collected through forms and analytics must have a lawful basis and be explained in a clear privacy notice.
  • Cookie consent. ePrivacy rules generally require consent before non-essential cookies are set. We configure analytics to respect consent choices, which can reduce the data you see, so reports are read with that in mind.
  • European Accessibility Act. From June 2025, the EAA applies to many consumer-facing digital services, including much of e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt. The harmonised standard EN 301 549 refers to WCAG AA, which also lines up well with good SEO practice.

Our accessibility guide covers the practical checks. Compliance decisions rest with you and your advisers.

Common SEO problems we see on Warsaw sites

When we audit sites for Warsaw companies, the same issues come up again and again. Most are fixable within the first few months.

  • One URL for two languages. A language switcher that changes text without changing the URL means search engines only ever see one version.
  • Machine-translated pages. Pages translated automatically and never reviewed tend to rank poorly and put readers off.
  • Thin service pages. A single page listing every service, instead of a focused page for each, makes it hard to rank for any of them.
  • Slow, heavy pages. Large images, sliders and too many scripts slow pages down on mobile.
  • Missing tracking. Forms and calls that are not tracked make it impossible to tell which pages bring business.
  • Old redirects. Previous site moves that left broken links and lost authority behind.

We prioritise fixes by how much they are likely to matter, so the first months focus on the changes with the biggest effect.
