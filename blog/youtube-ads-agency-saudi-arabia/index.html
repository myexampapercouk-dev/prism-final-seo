This guide explains how YouTube advertising works for the Saudi market, how we approach creative and tracking, and what it costs.

Why YouTube matters in Saudi Arabia

Video is a central part of daily media use in Saudi Arabia, and YouTube is where many people watch everything from entertainment and cooking to car reviews, technology, education and religious content. Viewing happens on phones, laptops and increasingly on connected televisions, which gives brands several ways to reach people.

For Saudi businesses, YouTube suits products and services that benefit from explanation or demonstration: cars, property, electronics, education, healthcare services, home improvement, travel, food and consumer brands. It also works well for launches, when you need to reach many people quickly and then retarget the ones who watched. Because it runs inside Google Ads, YouTube can be connected to your search campaigns. Our Google Ads guide for Saudi Arabia covers the search side.

Targeting Saudi audiences

YouTube offers several ways to reach the right viewers in Saudi Arabia. We combine them based on your goals and budget:

  • Location and language targeting, for example the whole Kingdom or specific cities such as Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam and Khobar.
  • Audience segments based on interests, life events and in-market behaviour.
  • Custom segments built from search terms and websites related to your offer.
  • Your own data, including website visitors, YouTube channel viewers and customer lists where you have permission.
  • Placements and topics when you want your ads to appear around specific types of content.

We also apply brand safety settings and content exclusions so your ads avoid content you would not want to appear alongside. Many businesses choose to run Arabic and English campaigns separately so that targeting, creative and reporting stay clean.

Creative for YouTube in Saudi Arabia

Video creative decides most of a YouTube campaign's success. Strong YouTube ads usually show the brand or product early, grab attention in the first five seconds before the skip button appears, and end with a clear next step. We plan creative around these principles and write briefs that your team or production company can film.

We write English scripts where needed. Arabic scripts, voiceovers and on-screen text are written with your team or a professional translator, because Saudi audiences respond to natural phrasing and dialect choices that a literal translation misses. We usually recommend a set of videos rather than one: a short bumper, a fifteen to thirty second ad and a longer explainer, plus vertical cuts for Shorts. Our website video guide shows how the same footage can work on your own site too.

Timing matters as well. Viewing patterns shift during Ramadan, when evening viewing rises, and around Eid, Saudi National Day and Founding Day, so we plan budgets and creative around the calendar.

Tracking and measurement

YouTube can be measured in views and reach, but most businesses also want to know whether it brings customers. We set up Google Ads conversion tracking and Google Analytics so that form submissions, calls, WhatsApp clicks and purchases are recorded. Our conversion tracking setup guide explains the main steps.

Video often influences people who convert later through search or by visiting your site directly, so we look at view-through and assisted conversions as well as direct ones. We also recommend a clear privacy notice and consent approach, and you should check your obligations under Saudi Arabia's personal data protection rules with your own adviser.

Budgets, bidding and optimisation

YouTube budgets can be spent quickly, so we set clear limits and goals before launch. For awareness campaigns we usually focus on reach and frequency, so the same viewers see your message enough times to remember it without being annoyed by it. For conversion-focused campaigns we let Google's bidding work towards the actions you have tracked, once enough data has built up.

During the first weeks we watch view rates, watch time, click-through rates and early conversions closely. We then shift budget towards the videos, audiences and placements that perform and pause those that do not. Over time we test new hooks, lengths, calls to action and audience combinations, because even strong videos lose impact once most of your audience has seen them.

We also review where your ads actually appeared. Placement reports show the channels and videos that carried your ads, which helps us exclude content that is irrelevant or unsuitable for your brand. For businesses with a YouTube channel, we look at how paid views affect subscribers and organic viewing, since a well-run channel supports both paid and unpaid video.

Landing pages and websites

When a viewer clicks, the page they land on must match the video and load quickly on a phone. A bilingual, mobile-first landing page with a clear offer will turn more clicks into enquiries than a generic home page. Our guide to web design in Saudi Arabia explains how we build bilingual sites, and our SEO guide for Riyadh covers how to build search visibility alongside paid campaigns.

YouTube alongside TikTok and other channels

YouTube and TikTok both rely on video, but they behave differently. YouTube includes long-form viewing, search-driven intent and television screens, while TikTok is built around short, fast, vertical content. Our comparison of YouTube Ads and TikTok Ads explains the differences, and our TikTok Ads guide for Saudi Arabia covers that platform locally. Policies and regulated sectors

Google has advertising policies that restrict some categories, including healthcare, financial services and gambling, and Saudi Arabia has its own rules on advertising content. We keep campaigns within Google's policies and flag anything that needs checking, but we do not give legal advice. In regulated sectors, compliance approval rests with you and your own adviser, with reference to official guidance from the relevant Saudi authorities.
