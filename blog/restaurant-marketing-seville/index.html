Why restaurant marketing in Seville needs a clear plan

Seville's food scene is busy and competitive. Locals eat late and know their favourite places well, while visitors arrive with lists of tapas bars and neighbourhoods to explore, from Triana and Santa Cruz to Alameda and the streets around the cathedral. Demand also shifts through the year, with spring festivals bringing huge crowds and the hottest summer months changing how and when people eat out.

In that environment, being good is not enough. Diners decide in seconds, usually on a phone, often while standing in the street. If your map listing has old opening hours, your menu is a blurry photo or your booking link is broken, they walk on. A clear marketing plan makes sure every touchpoint helps rather than hinders.

A restaurant website that works on a phone

Your website is where diners check the details before they commit. It should load quickly on mobile data, show your menu as real text rather than a PDF or photo, and make booking, calling or getting directions possible with a single tap. Text menus also help search engines understand what you serve, which can help you appear for searches such as specific dishes or styles of food.

For Seville, we usually recommend Spanish and English versions of the key pages, with clear language switching. We write the English pages, and your team or translator provides the Spanish text. Our guide to restaurant website design covers the essentials in more detail, and our article on multilingual website design explains how to structure more than one language.

Local SEO and map listings

When someone searches for tapas near them or a restaurant in a particular neighbourhood, map results usually appear first. Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing diners see, so it needs to be complete and accurate. We help you:

  • Choose the right primary and secondary categories.
  • Keep opening hours up to date, including special hours for holidays and festivals.
  • Add good photos of food, the room and the terrace, supplied by your team.
  • Link to your menu and booking page.
  • Set up a simple, fair process for asking happy guests for reviews and responding to all reviews politely.

We also make sure your name, address and phone number match across directories and booking platforms. Our local SEO guide is written for UK businesses, but the same principles apply in Spain. For ongoing search work, see our SEO and GEO service.

Reaching visitors before they arrive

Many visitors plan where to eat before they land in Seville. They search for the best tapas bars, places to eat near the cathedral or restaurants with a view, often in English. Helpful content, such as a page about your neighbourhood, your specialities or what makes your kitchen different, can help you appear in those searches and in AI-generated travel answers.

Paid campaigns can help too. Meta and TikTok Ads can reach people who have shown interest in visiting Seville, while Google Ads can catch searches for restaurants in specific areas. We set clear budgets and track bookings, calls and direction requests so you can see what each channel delivers.

Social ads for local diners

Locals are the backbone of most restaurants, especially outside peak tourist periods. Meta Ads, covering Facebook and Instagram, and TikTok Ads let you reach people within a set distance of your restaurant with offers such as a new menu, a weekday lunch or a special event. We plan the targeting, structure and testing, and work with photos and video clips your team supplies. We do not film or edit video ourselves.

Instagram matters a great deal for restaurants, but organic posts alone rarely reach new people. A modest, well-targeted ad budget alongside your regular posts usually works better than posting more often. Ad spend is paid separately to each platform.

Bookings, delivery and third-party platforms

Most Seville restaurants use a mix of direct bookings, walk-ins, booking platforms and delivery apps. Each has its place, but third-party platforms usually take a commission. A good website and strong local search help you win more direct bookings, which keeps more of each sale and gives you a direct relationship with the guest.

We link your booking tool clearly from your website and map listing, and we track how many bookings each source brings so you can make informed decisions.

Common mistakes Seville restaurants make online

Many of the restaurant listings and websites we review lose diners for avoidable reasons. These are the issues we see most often:

  • Menus only as photos or PDFs. They load slowly, are hard to read on a phone and tell search engines very little about what you serve.
  • Out-of-date opening hours. Nothing frustrates a hungry visitor more than arriving at a closed door, especially during festival weeks when hours change.
  • No English information. Visitors who cannot understand the menu or booking page often choose somewhere that explains itself clearly.
  • Ignored reviews. Unanswered reviews, especially negative ones, suggest nobody is listening. A short, polite reply shows care.
  • Cookie banners that block the menu. Consent is required under European rules, but the banner should not hide the page. Our guide to cookie banner design shows how to keep it clear and light.

Fixing these basics often brings more bookings than any new campaign, so we always start here before spending money on ads.

Seasonal planning for Seville restaurants

Seville's calendar shapes demand. Spring festivals bring large crowds and higher prices, summer heat changes when people eat out, and autumn and winter bring cooler evenings and a different mix of visitors. We plan campaigns and content around these patterns, raising budgets when demand peaks and focusing on locals during quieter months.
