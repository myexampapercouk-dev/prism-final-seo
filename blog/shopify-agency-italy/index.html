Shopify Agency for Italian Brands: Remote Shopify Design and Builds

Looking for a Shopify agency in Italy? Store builds, Italian and English stores, selling across Europe, what a remote UK studio covers and our fees in pounds.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Italian Shopify stores usually need Italian and English versions, clear delivery and returns information, local payment options and pages that reflect the quality of the product.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Italy; we build Shopify stores remotely, write in English and work with your team or a translator on Italian copy.
  • Ecommerce websites are £1,500 to £3,500, quoted in pounds with other currencies on request, and most Shopify builds take five to twelve weeks.

A Shopify agency for an Italian brand should build a store that presents your products well, makes buying simple and works for customers in Italy and abroad. That means a well-planned store structure, strong product pages, Italian and English versions where needed, the right payment and delivery settings, and a store your team can run day to day.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds Shopify stores remotely for brands across Europe. We have no office in Italy. Our website and ecommerce development service runs by video call, shared designs and a private preview store, one hour behind Italian time. We write in English and work with your team or a translator on Italian copy.

What Italian brands need from a Shopify store

Italy is known for fashion, leather goods, jewellery, furniture and homeware, food and drink, cosmetics and craft products. Many of these brands sell to customers across Europe and beyond, and their stores have to carry that reputation online:

  • Product presentation. Photography, materials, origin and craftsmanship need space on the page, not just a price and a button.
  • Italian and English. Domestic customers expect Italian, while international buyers often shop in English or their own language.
  • Selling across borders. Delivery times, duties outside the EU, returns and currencies all need to be clear.
  • Familiar payment options. Customers expect the cards, wallets and payment methods they use every day.

Our guide to Shopify Markets for Europe explains how one store can serve several countries with local currencies, languages and domains.

Italian and English stores

Shopify can run several languages from one store, with each language on its own URL. Each version needs its own product titles, descriptions, collection text and page titles, so it reads naturally and can rank in search. Automatic translation apps are a starting point at best; customers notice awkward phrasing, especially on premium products.

Prism writes English copy in-house. For Italian content, we brief your team or a native-speaking translator on each page and product template, then check the finished text in the store. Our multilingual website design guide covers language switchers, URLs and keeping each version consistent.

Product pages that sell quality

For many Italian brands the product page does most of the selling. We design product templates that give room for:

  • Large, fast-loading photography and, where helpful, short video.
  • Materials, care instructions, dimensions and sizing in a clear, scannable layout.
  • The story behind the product, such as where and how it is made, without burying the buy button.
  • Delivery, returns and payment information close to the add-to-cart area.
  • Genuine customer reviews, collected through a reviews app once you have them.

Our guides to writing product descriptions and briefing ecommerce photography explain how to prepare content before the build.

Payments, tax and delivery

We set up Shopify Payments or other providers available for your business, and the payment methods your customers expect. Tax settings, VAT registration across EU countries and invoicing rules should be agreed with your accountant, and we configure the store to match their advice. We keep our guidance general: check current official guidance on EU VAT for online sales, and on any duties when shipping outside the EU.

Delivery settings need to reflect real costs and times for each region. We set up shipping profiles and rates, and write clear delivery and returns pages. Our guide to Shopify shipping profiles shows how rates can differ by product and destination.

Legal pages and consent

Online shops in Italy and the rest of the EU usually need terms of sale, a privacy policy, a cookie policy, returns and withdrawal information and clear company details. Analytics and marketing cookies normally need consent. We design and place these pages and the consent banner, but the wording should come from your legal adviser, who has final approval. Requirements change, so check current official guidance before launch.

Moving to Shopify from another platform

Many Italian brands start on WooCommerce, PrestaShop, Magento or a marketplace and move to Shopify as they grow. A careful migration keeps products, customers and order history where possible, and maps every old URL to a new one so search rankings are protected. Our WooCommerce to Shopify migration guide sets out the steps, and much of it applies to other platforms too. For larger brands, we also handle Shopify Plus migrations.

Getting traffic after launch

A new store needs visitors as well as good design. We build every Shopify store with search in mind, including clean collection and product URLs, unique titles and descriptions for each language, structured product data and fast templates. After launch, the main ways Italian brands grow traffic are:

  • Ecommerce SEO for collection and product pages in each language, based on real search demand in each market.
  • Google Shopping campaigns using a well-maintained product feed, set up by country and currency.
  • Paid social on Meta or TikTok for products that sell well on visuals.
  • Email marketing for welcome offers, abandoned carts and repeat customers.

We plan which of these to use with you, based on your products, margins and budget. Our guide to Shopify SEO explains the store settings and page work that help collections and products rank.
