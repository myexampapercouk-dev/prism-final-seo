Nursery Marketing UK: How Independent Nurseries Fill Childcare Places

Nursery marketing UK guide for independent nurseries and preschools: local SEO, Google Business Profile, websites, paid social, show-rounds and enquiries.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Parents choose childcare close to home or work, so local search and Google Business Profile matter most.
  • A warm, honest website and an easy show-round booking turn searches into visits.
  • Children's privacy and accurate claims must shape every photo, post and advert.

Nursery marketing in the UK is about earning parents' trust and making it easy for them to visit. Families usually look for childcare close to home or work, often months before a place is needed, and they compare settings carefully. An independent nursery or preschool that is easy to find, shows its environment honestly and responds warmly to enquiries is well placed to fill places.

This guide is for owners and managers of independent nurseries, preschools and childcare settings. It covers local search, websites, paid social, enquiries, content and measurement, with notes on privacy and claims.

Why nursery marketing is different

Choosing childcare is one of the most important decisions parents make. They want to know their child will be safe, happy and well cared for. They read inspection reports, ask friends, check reviews and almost always visit before committing. Marketing has to reassure as much as it attracts.

Demand is also highly local and often timed around parental leave, school terms and changes to funded childcare entitlements. Many parents start looking well before they need a place, so your marketing should be visible continuously, not only when places are free.

Childcare settings in England are inspected by Ofsted, and equivalent bodies operate in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. How you describe inspection outcomes, funded hours and your provision should be accurate and current. Requirements and funding rules can change, so check official guidance and take advice before publishing claims. Final sign-off should sit with the setting.

Start with local search and Google Business Profile

When parents search for a nursery near them, the map results are often the first thing they see. Get your Google Business Profile right:

  • Correct category. Choose the primary category that best fits, such as nursery school or child care agency, and keep it accurate.
  • Accurate hours. Show opening hours and note closures, such as bank holidays.
  • Thoughtful photos. Show rooms, outdoor space and facilities. Avoid identifiable children unless you have clear, written parental consent for that specific use.
  • Reviews. Ask parents for honest reviews and reply politely, without sharing details about any child.
  • Posts. Promote open days, new rooms or availability updates.

A website that reassures parents

Your website should answer the questions parents ask before they book a visit. A strong nursery website usually includes:

  • A clear headline saying where you are, which ages you care for and your opening hours.
  • Information about your approach to learning and care, explained in plain language.
  • Room or age group pages, so parents can see what a typical day looks like.
  • Staff introductions, where team members are happy to be featured.
  • Clear information on fees, sessions and funded hours, kept up to date.
  • A link to your latest inspection report, described accurately.
  • Food, safeguarding and settling-in information.
  • An easy way to book a show-round or join a waiting list.

Paid social and search

Meta ads can reach parents within a defined radius, which suits promoting open days or new availability. Use imagery of your rooms and outdoor space rather than identifiable children unless you have specific consent, and keep any claims accurate. Send traffic to a focused page with a simple show-round booking form. Our Facebook ads guide explains setup and tracking.

Google Ads can catch parents actively searching, especially when you have places to fill. Keep campaigns tight with sensible location targeting and negative keywords to filter out jobs and training searches. Turning enquiries into visits and registrations

Most parents will visit before deciding, so the show-round is your most important conversion step.

  1. Respond quickly. Acknowledge enquiries promptly and offer visit times.
  2. Make booking simple. An online booking option reduces back-and-forth.
  3. Prepare for visits. Make sure someone is available to show parents round and answer questions.
  4. Follow up. Send a friendly message after the visit with next steps and information.
  5. Record the source. Ask how parents heard about you so you know which channels work.

Content, social media and privacy

Social media can show the warmth and daily life of your setting: activities, seasonal events, food, outdoor play and staff. It can also create privacy risks. Only post images of children with clear, written parental consent for that specific use, avoid sharing names or identifying details, and respect parents who decline. Keep data protection obligations under UK GDPR in mind.

Helpful content, such as guides to settling in, what to pack or how funded hours work, can attract searches and reassure parents. Keep funding and regulatory information general, link to official sources and review it regularly. Our content strategy guide covers how to plan this.

Waiting lists and retention

Marketing does not stop once a child starts. Happy families are your strongest source of referrals, and a well-managed waiting list smooths out occupancy through the year. Practical steps include:

  • Keep waiting list families informed with occasional, useful updates rather than silence.
  • Send a welcome sequence to new families explaining settling in, daily routines and how to contact staff.
  • Hold events such as stay-and-play sessions or seasonal celebrations that families can share with friends.
  • Ask for feedback regularly and act on it, so small issues do not become reasons to leave.
  • Plan ahead for children moving on to school, so you know which places will become available and when.

Only send marketing messages to families who have agreed to receive them, and make opting out easy. Operational messages about a child's care are different from marketing, so check current guidance on how UK GDPR and PECR apply to each.

Word of mouth matters enormously in childcare. Parents talk at the school gate, in local groups and online, so every positive experience supports your marketing.

Measuring what works

Track a few key numbers each month: enquiries by source, show-rounds booked and attended, registrations, occupancy by room and cost per registration for paid channels. FAQs

What is the best marketing for a nursery?

For most independent nurseries, a strong Google Business Profile, local SEO and a reassuring website with easy show-round booking form the foundation. Paid social can help promote open days and availability.

Can I use photos of children in nursery marketing?

Only with clear, written parental consent for that specific use, and with care to avoid identifying details. Many settings use photos of rooms, resources and outdoor space instead.

Should a nursery publish its fees?

Many parents appreciate clear information on fees, sessions and funded hours. How much you publish is your decision, but transparency can reduce unsuitable enquiries.

How should I mention my inspection result?

Describe it accurately and link to the official report. Check current guidance on how inspection outcomes can be presented, and update your website when a new report is published.

Are Facebook ads worth it for nurseries?

They can work well for reaching local parents, especially for open days. Track cost per show-round and registration, not just clicks.

How long does nursery SEO take?

Improvements to your Google Business Profile can help relatively quickly, while broader SEO usually takes several months to build.
