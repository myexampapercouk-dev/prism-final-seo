Amazon SEO for Saudi Arabia Sellers: Listings, Arabic Keywords and Cost

Need Amazon SEO in Saudi Arabia? How we optimise Amazon.sa listings in Arabic and English: keywords, titles, images and content, with fees in £ and timelines.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Amazon SEO for Saudi Arabia means making your Amazon.sa listings easy to find and convincing to buy from, in both Arabic and English.
  • We optimise listings remotely from the UK, with no office in Saudi Arabia, and Arabic copy is written with your team or a professional translator.
  • Marketplace SEO is part of our retainers from £350 per month, results typically build over 3 to 6 months, and other currencies are quoted on request.

Amazon SEO for Saudi Arabia is the work of making your products appear when shoppers search on Amazon.sa, and then persuading them to buy once they land on your listing. It covers keyword research in Arabic and English, titles, bullet points, descriptions, backend search terms, images and the reviews and sales history that Amazon uses to decide which products to show first.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that provides marketplace listing SEO, including Amazon, as part of our SEO and GEO service. We work remotely with sellers and brands in Saudi Arabia and have no office in the Kingdom. This guide explains how Amazon SEO works on Amazon.sa, what we do, what it costs and what to watch out for.

How Amazon search works

Amazon's search engine is built to sell. It looks at how relevant a listing is to the search, and at how well that listing has performed for similar searches in the past. Amazon does not publish the full details of its ranking system, and it changes over time, but the broad signals sellers can influence are well understood.

SignalWhat it meansWhat you can do
RelevanceWhether the listing matches the words in the searchUse the right keywords in titles, bullets and backend fields
Click-throughWhether shoppers choose your product from the resultsStrong main image, clear title, competitive price
ConversionWhether visitors buy once they land on the listingUseful images, clear bullets, good content and reviews
AvailabilityWhether the product is in stock and can be deliveredPlan stock and fulfilment so listings do not go inactive
Customer experienceReturns, feedback and complaintsAccurate descriptions and honest product information

SEO work focuses mainly on relevance and conversion, because those are the areas a listing directly controls. Price, stock and fulfilment decisions sit with you, but we flag where they are holding a listing back.

Amazon SEO Saudi Arabia: Arabic and English keywords

Shoppers on Amazon.sa search in Arabic and English, sometimes switching between the two for the same product. Arabic searches can use different spellings, regional terms and words that are not a direct translation of your English product name. Some shoppers also type English brand or product names in Latin letters while browsing the Arabic site.

We research search terms in both languages, look at the language competing listings use, and build a keyword map for each product or product family. The most important terms go in the title and bullet points, while variations and synonyms go into the backend search term fields where Amazon allows it. We write in English. Arabic listing copy is written with your team or a professional translator, and we recommend a fluent speaker reviews it before it goes live, because awkward translations reduce trust and conversions.

What our Amazon listing optimisation covers

Each listing is a small sales page, and every part of it has a job to do.

  • Titles. Clear, readable titles that include the brand, product type and the details shoppers search for, within Amazon's current category rules.
  • Bullet points. Benefits and key facts that answer the questions shoppers ask before buying, such as size, materials, compatibility or what is in the box.
  • Descriptions and brand content. Where your account has access to enhanced content, we plan modules that explain the product and brand clearly. Our guide to writing ecommerce product descriptions covers the principles.
  • Backend search terms. Extra keywords, spellings and synonyms that shoppers use but that would not read naturally in visible copy.
  • Images. A clear main image that meets Amazon's rules, plus supporting images that show scale, detail and use. Our product photography guide explains what to brief.
  • Variations. Sensible parent and child listings for sizes, colours or pack sizes, so reviews and sales history are grouped.

How we prioritise a catalogue

Few sellers need every listing rewritten at once. We start with the products that matter most to your business, usually your best sellers, your highest-margin items and listings with plenty of traffic but weak conversion. Improving those first tends to show where the biggest gains are and gives a clear template for the rest of the range.

For each priority listing we record the current title, bullets and keywords, make changes, then compare visibility and sales over the following weeks. That record makes it easier to see what helped and to roll the same approach out across similar products.

Reviews and listing performance

Reviews influence both rankings and buying decisions. Amazon has strict rules on how sellers can request reviews, and breaking them can lead to listings being removed or accounts being suspended. We never use incentivised, fake or manipulated reviews, and we recommend sellers use only Amazon's own approved methods for requesting feedback.

What we can do is improve the things that lead to better reviews: accurate descriptions, clear sizing and specifications, honest images and fewer surprises when the product arrives. Our guide to ecommerce product reviews covers this on your own store as well as on marketplaces.

Amazon and your own online store

Amazon gives you access to a large audience of ready-to-buy shoppers, but you play by its rules, pay its fees and have limited access to customer data. Your own store gives you control of the brand, pricing and customer relationship, but you must bring the traffic yourself. Many brands use both. Our article on selling on Amazon versus your own website weighs up the trade-offs, and our guide to Shopify for Saudi Arabian brands covers building your own store.

If you also sell in the UAE, the approach is similar but keyword demand and competitors differ. Our guide to Amazon SEO in the UAE covers that marketplace. For a wider view of how we work with online retailers, see our ecommerce sector page.

Compliance and account health

Selling in Saudi Arabia involves rules on product categories, labelling, consumer information, tax and import that can change over time. Amazon also has its own category restrictions and listing policies. We work within Amazon's published listing guidelines, but we are not legal or regulatory advisers. You should check current official guidance in Saudi Arabia and Amazon's seller documentation, and confirm specific requirements with your own advisers before listing new products.

We also keep an eye on account health signals when working on listings. Sudden suppressed listings, policy warnings or category approval requests are usually a sign to pause and check the rules rather than push more changes.

How to choose an Amazon SEO agency

Whether you work with us or another agency, ask these questions before you sign:

  • How do you research Arabic search terms, and who writes and checks Arabic copy?
  • Will you work inside my Seller Central account with user permissions, rather than asking for my login?
  • How do you handle reviews, and what methods do you refuse to use?
  • How will you report on changes, rankings and sales?
  • Do you also manage Amazon advertising, or will that need another provider?

We work through user permissions in your own Seller Central account and never ask for your main login. You keep full ownership of your account, listings and data.
