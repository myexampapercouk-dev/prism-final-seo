What diners in Winnipeg look for

Winnipeg has a varied food scene, from long-standing family restaurants and neighbourhood diners to new spots in the Exchange District, Osborne Village, Corydon Avenue, St. Boniface and around The Forks. Diners range from students and young professionals to families, visitors in town for events and people heading out before a game or a show.

Most of these diners decide the same way. They search for a type of food or a neighbourhood, look at photos and reviews on Google, open the menu, check opening hours and then book, order or simply turn up. Every step is a chance to win or lose them. Good restaurant marketing makes each step quick and convincing.

Winnipeg's seasons shape this too. Patio season is short and valuable, so diners actively search for patios when the weather turns. Long winters push people towards delivery, takeout and cosy indoor spots, and festive periods bring group bookings and gift card sales. Marketing should follow that calendar.

Your Google Business Profile comes first

For most restaurants, the Google Business Profile drives more decisions than the website. It is what appears when someone searches for brunch near them, pizza in Osborne Village or a restaurant near Canada Life Centre. We check your categories, opening hours, holiday hours, menu link, booking link, attributes such as patio seating or wheelchair access, and your photos.

Reviews matter a great deal. We help you set up a simple routine for asking happy guests to leave a review and replying to reviews politely and promptly, including the critical ones. We never write fake reviews or offer incentives for positive ones, as that breaks Google's rules. Our guides to Google Business Profile, choosing profile categories and profile posts go into more detail.

A restaurant website that works on a phone

Most diners view restaurant websites on their phones, often while standing on the street or sitting in a car. A good restaurant site loads quickly, shows the menu as readable text, makes opening hours and location obvious, and has one clear button to book or order.

PDF menus are slow on phones and hard for search engines to read. We recommend an HTML menu that is easy to update, with dietary labels and prices kept current. If you take reservations through a booking platform or orders through your own system, we link them clearly rather than hiding them. Our guide to restaurant website design covers layout in more detail, and our Winnipeg web design guide looks at local considerations.

If you need a new site, our website development service builds small websites for £1,000 to £2,000, usually in 3 to 5 weeks.

Local SEO for Winnipeg restaurants

Local SEO helps your restaurant appear when people search for a cuisine, a dish or a neighbourhood. That means clear page titles, a menu search engines can read, consistent name, address and phone details across listings, and content that answers real questions, such as whether you take groups, have a patio or offer gluten-free options.

Our SEO and GEO service also covers how AI search tools describe your restaurant, which increasingly matters as people ask assistants for recommendations. Our Winnipeg SEO guide explains our local approach, and our local SEO guide for Canada covers listings and reviews across the country.

Social ads and Google Ads for restaurants

Instagram and Facebook are natural channels for food. A short clip of a dish being served, a carousel of a new seasonal menu or a post about a patio opening can reach people nearby who were not planning to go out. We set up Meta campaigns with a tight radius around your restaurant and a clear goal, such as bookings for a set menu or orders on a quiet weeknight. You supply the photos and footage, and we write the copy and run the campaigns; we do not film or edit video.

Google Ads works differently. It catches people already searching, such as for a private dining room, a group brunch venue or catering. A small, focused search campaign can work well for these higher-value bookings. Our guides to Meta Ads in Winnipeg and Google Ads in Winnipeg explain how we run both.

Planning around the Winnipeg calendar

We plan restaurant marketing month by month. In spring, we prepare patio content and update Google profile attributes. In summer, we focus on patio searches, events and visitors. In autumn, we promote new menus and group bookings. In winter, we lean towards takeout, delivery, festive parties and gift cards, then a push for January and February when many diners stay home.

Large events in the city, home games and festivals all create short windows of demand. A restaurant close to a venue can run time-limited ads and update its profile to reach people heading out before or after. Planning these ahead is much more effective than reacting on the day.

FAQs

What does restaurant marketing in Winnipeg involve?

It usually covers your Google Business Profile, a fast mobile website and menu, local SEO, social ads, Google Ads and email. Prism Works Studio is UK-based and supports Winnipeg restaurants remotely, with no local office.

How much does restaurant marketing cost?

Our retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month depending on scope. Canadian dollar pricing is quoted on request, and ad spend is paid directly to Google or Meta.

Should my menu be a PDF?

We recommend a menu as a web page. It loads faster on phones, is easier to update and can be read by search engines. You can still offer a PDF for printing.

How do I get more Google reviews?

Ask happy guests directly, add a review link to receipts or follow-up emails, and reply to every review. Never buy reviews or offer rewards for positive ones.

Do you create food photos or videos?

No. We work with photos and footage you or your photographer supply, and we advise on what to capture. We do not film or edit video.

How long does restaurant marketing take to work?

Profile fixes and ads can bring quick improvements, but local search and paid results typically settle over 3 to 6 months.
