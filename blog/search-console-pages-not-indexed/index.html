Pages Not Indexed in Google Search Console: What It Means and How to Fix It

Why pages are not indexed in Google Search Console, what each common reason means, which ones to ignore and how to fix the pages that really matter.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Not every page should be indexed, so start by checking whether the pages listed actually matter to your business.
  • Each reason in the page indexing report points to a different cause, from redirects and duplicates to weak content or blocked crawling.
  • Fix the cause, then use URL Inspection to check the page and request indexing for your most important URLs.

Pages are not indexed in Google Search Console for a reason shown in the page indexing report, such as a redirect, a noindex tag, a duplicate that Google has chosen not to index, a page blocked by robots.txt, or a page Google crawled but decided was not worth indexing yet. Many of these are normal. The job is to find the important pages in the list, work out the cause and fix it.

This guide is for UK small business owners who have opened Search Console and found a worrying number of pages listed as not indexed. It explains what the common reasons mean, which ones you can safely ignore and how to fix the rest. Google changes report names and wording from time to time, so check Google's current Search Console help pages for the latest definitions.

First, do not panic about the number

Almost every website has more URLs that are not indexed than are indexed. That is normal. Search engines discover lots of addresses that should not appear in search results: redirected old pages, filtered product views, tag archives, login pages, URLs with tracking parameters and so on. A high count is only a problem if pages you want people to find are among them.

So start with a simple question: which pages on this site should appear in Google? For most small businesses that means the home page, service or product pages, category pages, location pages, key blog posts and contact or about pages. If those are indexed, the rest of the report is largely housekeeping. If you have not yet set up Search Console, our Google Search Console setup guide walks through it.

Where to find the page indexing report

In Search Console, open the indexing section and select the pages report. You will see a chart of indexed and not indexed pages, and a table of reasons below it. Click a reason to see example URLs. You can also filter the report to show only URLs in a submitted sitemap, which is a quick way to focus on pages you have told Google matter. Our XML sitemap guide explains how to keep that sitemap clean.

Fixing the reasons that matter most

Noindex on pages that should rank

This often happens after a site launch, when a setting used to hide the staging site is left switched on. On WordPress, check the reading settings for the option that discourages search engines. Also check SEO plugin settings for individual pages, posts and content types. Remove the noindex, then inspect the URL and request indexing.

Blocked by robots.txt

Robots.txt controls crawling, not indexing, but blocking important sections stops Google reading them. Review the rules and remove anything that blocks pages you want found. Our robots.txt guide covers safe set-ups.

Crawled or discovered, but not indexed

These two are the most common worry for small sites. They usually mean Google does not yet see the page as valuable enough, or has not prioritised crawling it. Helpful fixes include:

  • Make the page genuinely useful, with original information that answers a real question, rather than a thin page that repeats others on your site.
  • Link to it from relevant, well-visited pages. Our internal linking guide shows how.
  • Merge or remove near-duplicate pages, such as several location pages with only the town name changed.
  • Make sure the page is in your XML sitemap and returns a normal 200 status.

A website content audit is a good way to find weak and overlapping pages across the whole site.

Duplicates and canonical choices

If Google picks a different canonical from the one you prefer, look for mixed signals. Internal links, sitemaps, canonical tags and redirects should all point to the same version, including consistent use of https, www or non-www, and trailing slashes. Ecommerce sites often see this with product URLs that appear under several collections or filters.

404s and redirects after a redesign

A spike in not found pages after a relaunch usually means old URLs were not redirected. Map old addresses to the closest new page with permanent redirects. Our guide to website redirects when relaunching explains the process, and a helpful 404 page catches anything that slips through.

How to check a fix and request indexing

  1. Paste the URL into the URL Inspection tool at the top of Search Console.
  2. Run a live test to see how Google fetches the page now.
  3. If the live test shows the page can be indexed, request indexing.
  4. For groups of URLs with the same issue, use the validate fix option on that reason in the report.
  5. Wait. Recrawling and reprocessing can take days or weeks, and requesting indexing does not guarantee it.

Request indexing for your most important pages only. Submitting the same URL repeatedly does not speed things up.

Mistakes that make indexing worse

  • Publishing many thin pages at once. Dozens of near-empty location or tag pages can make Google slower to index the pages that matter.
  • Blocking a page in robots.txt and adding noindex. If Google cannot crawl the page, it cannot see the noindex tag. Pick one approach.
  • Leaving old URLs in the sitemap. Redirected and deleted pages in the sitemap send mixed signals.
  • Orphan pages. Pages with no internal links pointing to them are hard for search engines to find and judge.
  • Chasing every URL. Time spent forcing low-value pages into the index is better spent improving the pages that bring enquiries.

A simple monthly routine

  • Check the indexed pages trend for sudden drops.
  • Filter to sitemap URLs and review any key pages that are not indexed.
  • Look at server errors and soft 404s first, as they often signal technical faults.
  • Note new reasons appearing after site changes, plugin updates or theme updates.

FAQs

Why are pages not indexed in Google Search Console?

Each URL has a reason listed in the page indexing report, such as a redirect, noindex tag, robots.txt block, duplicate, error or Google choosing not to index it yet. Many reasons are normal. Focus on important pages and fix the specific cause shown.

Is it bad to have lots of pages not indexed?

Not necessarily. Most sites have many URLs that should not be indexed, such as redirects and filtered views. It only matters if pages you want to rank appear in the list.

What does crawled, currently not indexed mean?

Google has visited the page but decided not to index it for now. Improving the content, removing duplication and adding internal links from relevant pages usually helps.

How long does it take for a page to be indexed?

It varies from a few days to several weeks, depending on the site and the page. Requesting indexing can help Google notice a change, but it does not guarantee inclusion.

Should I request indexing for every page?

No. Use it for new or significantly updated key pages. For the rest, a clean sitemap and good internal links help Google find and prioritise them.

Can a website redesign cause pages not to be indexed?

Yes. Leftover noindex settings, missing redirects, changed URLs and blocked sections are common after relaunches. Check the report closely in the weeks after any redesign.
