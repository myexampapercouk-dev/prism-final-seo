Dental Marketing in the UK: Local SEO, Private Treatments and the Rules

Dental marketing in the UK explained: local SEO, private treatment pages, paid search, GDC and CAP rules, and how to choose a dental marketing agency.

By the Prism Works Studio team · Updated 7 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Local search and a well-run Google Business Profile are the foundation of dental marketing, because most patients choose a practice nearby.
  • Each private treatment deserves its own clear, factual page with fees, process and what to expect.
  • Dental advertising is regulated, so final approval of claims should rest with the practice and its compliance advisers.

Dental marketing in the UK works best when it starts locally: a strong Google Business Profile, a fast website and local SEO that puts your practice in front of people searching nearby. From there, clear pages for private treatments such as implants, aligners and whitening help patients compare options, provided every claim stays within GDC guidance and advertising rules.

This guide is written for practice owners and managers. It covers the channels that matter, how to market private treatments responsibly and what to check before publishing. For a wider view across clinics and health businesses, see our healthcare marketing agency guide; here we focus specifically on dental practices.

Dental marketing in the UK: where to start

Most patients choose a dentist close to home or work, and most start with a search on their phone. That makes local visibility the foundation. Before spending on ads, make sure the basics are right:

  • Google Business Profile complete, with correct categories, opening hours, photos, accessibility details and booking link.
  • Consistent name, address and phone number across your website and directories.
  • A mobile-friendly website that loads quickly and makes booking or calling easy.
  • Clear information on NHS and private availability, so patients know what to expect.

Our UK local SEO guide walks through each of these in more detail.

Local SEO for dental practices

Local SEO helps your practice appear in the map results and the organic listings for searches like "dentist near me" or "emergency dentist" plus your town. The main levers are relevance, proximity and prominence.

Relevance

Your website should have a page for each core service and each location you serve. A practice with two sites should have a page for each, with unique content, directions, parking and transport details rather than duplicated text.

Prominence

Reviews, local links and mentions all build prominence. Ask patients for reviews in a consistent, compliant way, and respond to every review politely and without disclosing any clinical or personal information. Local partnerships, community involvement and mentions in local press can also help, as covered in our guide to digital PR and link building.

Technical health

A slow or poorly structured site holds everything back. If your site has grown messy over the years, our technical SEO audit checklist is a useful starting point.

Marketing private dental treatments

Private treatments are where marketing makes the most commercial difference, and where care is most needed. Patients considering implants, clear aligners, veneers or whitening tend to research thoroughly, compare practices and read reviews before booking a consultation.

Give each treatment its own page that answers the questions patients actually ask:

  • What the treatment involves and who it may suit.
  • The consultation process and typical number of appointments.
  • Fees or "from" prices, and any finance options, explained clearly.
  • Risks, alternatives and aftercare, stated plainly.
  • Who carries out the treatment, with accurate titles and registration details.

This kind of honest, detailed content tends to perform well in search and in AI-generated answers, because it directly answers the questions people ask. Our SEO and GEO service is built around that approach.

Dental advertising rules: GDC, ASA and CAP Code

Dental marketing in the UK sits under several sets of guidance. The following is general information only, not legal or regulatory advice; responsibility for approving claims rests with the practice and its compliance advisers.

SourceWhat it broadly coversPractical implication
General Dental Council (GDC)Professional standards, including honest and accurate advertisingUse correct titles; avoid misleading claims about skills or outcomes
CAP Code (enforced by the ASA)Non-broadcast advertising, including websites and social mediaClaims need evidence; avoid exaggeration and misleading pricing
Prescription-only medicines rulesRestrictions on advertising certain medicines to the publicTake particular care with how some cosmetic treatments are described
UK GDPR and PECRPatient data, consent and electronic marketingGet proper consent for marketing emails and texts; protect patient data

In practice, that means being careful with words like "specialist" (which has a specific meaning in dentistry), avoiding guarantees of results, making sure "from" prices are genuinely available, and using before-and-after images only with consent and without implying every patient will see the same result. Check the current guidance from each body before publishing, as it can change. If your practice mentions its CQC registration or rating, or is regulated elsewhere in the UK, check that regulator's current guidance too.

Paid search and social for dentists

Paid search can be effective for high-intent searches such as "emergency dentist" or treatment-specific terms in your area. Keep campaigns tightly targeted by location, send each ad to the matching treatment page, and track bookings and calls rather than clicks. Our guide to PPC management costs explains how fees and ad spend work; spend is always paid separately to the platform.

Paid social can support awareness for cosmetic treatments, but the same advertising rules apply, and platform policies on health-related ads add another layer. Keep the message factual and the call to action simple, such as booking a consultation.

Retaining and recalling existing patients

New patients matter, but a practice's existing list is often its most valuable asset. Recall reminders, hygiene appointment prompts and clear information about treatment options help patients stay with the practice and make informed choices. Make sure reminders are sent with the right consent and through channels patients have agreed to, and keep marketing messages separate from essential clinical communications.

A simple, well-organised website helps here too. Patients returning to check opening hours, emergency arrangements or fees should find them in seconds. Pages answering common questions, such as what to do in a dental emergency out of hours or how payment plans work, reduce calls to reception and build trust. Review these pages at least once a year so fees and arrangements stay accurate.

Choosing a dental marketing agency

A good dental marketing agency will be cautious with wording and will expect your team to sign off clinical content. When comparing agencies, ask:

  • How will you handle claims about treatments, titles, prices and results?
  • Will you report on bookings and calls rather than clicks alone?
  • Will our Google Ads, analytics, website and business profile stay in our name?
  • How will you track enquiries without passing patient information to analytics or ad platforms?
  • How does your plan differ for NHS availability and private treatments?
  • What will you not promise?

Be wary of guaranteed rankings or a promised number of new patients. Our guide on how to choose a digital marketing agency has a fuller list of questions.

Measuring what works

Practices often know how many new patients they register but not where they came from. Set up tracking for online bookings, form submissions and calls from the website and Google Business Profile. Ask new patients how they found you at reception, and record it consistently. Over a few months this shows which channels bring patients worth the investment.

Dental marketing checklist

  • Google Business Profile is complete, accurate and regularly updated.
  • Name, address and phone number match everywhere.
  • Each core service and private treatment has its own factual page.
  • Fees, finance and NHS or private availability are explained clearly.
  • Clinician titles and registration details are accurate.
  • Claims, images and offers have been checked against current GDC guidance and the CAP Code.
  • Marketing consent for email and text is recorded properly.
  • Bookings and calls are tracked by source.
  • Reviews are requested consistently and answered without sharing patient information.

FAQs

How much should a dental practice spend on marketing?

There is no single right figure. It depends on your location, competition, treatment mix and capacity for new patients. Track the cost per booking by channel, then decide where to spend more. Our retainer prices are on our pricing page, and ad spend is separate.

What should I look for in a dental marketing agency?

Look for careful wording, reporting based on bookings and calls, clear account ownership and no guaranteed results. The agency should also expect your practice to approve clinical content.

What is the best dental marketing in the UK for a single practice?

For most single practices, a complete Google Business Profile, local SEO and clear treatment pages come first. Paid search can then add targeted enquiries for high-value treatments.

How do dentists get more private patients?

Detailed, honest treatment pages, strong reviews, local search visibility and targeted paid search all help. Patients researching private care want clear information on process, fees and aftercare.

Can dentists advertise prices online?

Generally yes, but prices must not mislead. "From" prices should be genuinely available, and any finance terms should be clear. Check current CAP Code guidance before publishing.

Can a dental practice use before-and-after photos?

Often, with patient consent and care not to imply every patient will get the same result. Check the latest GDC and ASA guidance, and have your compliance adviser review them.

How long does dental SEO take?

SEO typically shows meaningful results within 3 to 6 months. Google Business Profile improvements and technical fixes can sometimes help sooner.

Who approves dental marketing claims?

The practice does. An agency can flag risks, but final approval of claims and wording rests with the practice and its compliance advisers.
