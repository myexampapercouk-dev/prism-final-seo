Shopify for Children's Products Brands in the UK: Safety, Trust and Selling to Parents

Guide to running a UK children's products brand on Shopify: product safety information, age guidance, gifting, sizing, parent trust, privacy and store setup.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Parents and gift buyers want clear, accurate safety and age information before they buy anything for a child.
  • Product pages, sizing, gifting options and delivery details matter as much as attractive photography for a children's brand.
  • Product safety and labelling rules are complex, so check them with the relevant UK guidance or a specialist rather than relying on your website platform.

Selling children's products online is different from selling most other things. The person buying is usually a parent, grandparent, relative or friend, not the child who will use the product. They are often cautious, they read details carefully, and they want to feel confident that what they are buying is suitable, well made and safe. A children's brand store has to earn that confidence quickly.

This guide is for UK brands selling toys, baby products, children's clothing, nursery items, books, educational products or accessories through Shopify. It covers how to present safety and age information, product pages, sizing, gifting, building trust with parents, privacy and the practical setup of a Shopify store. It is a website and ecommerce guide, not legal advice. Children's products can be subject to specific safety, labelling and testing requirements in the UK, and you should check what applies to your products with official guidance or a qualified adviser.

Why Shopify suits many children's brands

Shopify is a hosted ecommerce platform, which means it handles hosting, security updates and checkout for you. For a small or growing children's brand this removes a lot of technical work, leaving more time for product development, sourcing and marketing. Its theme system makes it possible to build an attractive, mobile-friendly store, and its product and variant structure handles things like sizes, colours and age ranges well.

It is not the only option. Some brands prefer WooCommerce for more control, and our Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison explains the trade-offs. If you are just starting out, our Shopify store setup guide covers the foundations.

Safety information: be accurate and visible

For many children's products, safety information is one of the first things a careful buyer looks for. How you present it matters, and so does what you say.

Only state what is true and documented

If a product has been tested or meets a particular safety standard, you can say so, ideally in specific terms. If you are unsure whether it has, do not imply that it has. Vague phrases such as "completely safe" or "100% child safe" are best avoided, as they are hard to support and can mislead. Claims in your marketing generally need to be accurate and supportable under UK consumer protection and advertising rules.

Show age guidance and warnings clearly

Where a product carries age guidance or warnings, for example about small parts or adult supervision, consider showing that information on the product page as well as on the packaging. A short, clearly labelled section near the add to cart button is easier to find than text buried in a long description.

Check your obligations

Toys, cosmetics for children, nursery equipment, children's nightwear and some other categories can have specific UK requirements around safety, marking and labelling. The Office for Product Safety and Standards publishes guidance for businesses, and Trading Standards services can also help. Requirements can change and can differ for different products, so check rather than assume. Your store should reflect the position you have confirmed, not replace that check.

Product pages that answer parents' questions

A children's product page should answer the questions a thoughtful buyer will have before they need to ask. Depending on the product, these might include:

  • What age or stage the product suits.
  • What it is made of, and any relevant care or cleaning instructions.
  • Dimensions and weight, especially for nursery items and larger toys.
  • Whether batteries are needed, and if they are included.
  • Any assembly required.
  • Safety information and warnings that apply.
  • Delivery and returns details.

Use clear headings or tabs so people can scan for what matters to them. Our product descriptions guide and product page SEO guide go into more detail.

Photography that shows scale and use

Parents want to see how big something is and how it is used. Alongside clean product shots, consider images that show the product in context, close-ups of materials and finishes, and photos of key features. If you photograph children using your products, make sure you have proper, informed consent from parents or guardians and think carefully about how images are stored and used. Our product photography guide covers the practical side.

Sizing for children's clothing and footwear

Children grow quickly and sizing varies between brands, so size uncertainty is a common reason for hesitation and returns. A good size guide helps. Consider:

  • Showing sizes by age and by measurements, as ages alone can be misleading.
  • Explaining how to measure a child accurately.
  • Noting whether a garment runs large or small, if you know this from your own product specifications.
  • Linking to the size guide directly from every relevant product page.

Our ecommerce size guides article explains how to build one that actually gets used. Variants for size and colour should be set up so out-of-stock combinations are clear; see our guide to product variants and bundles.

Designing for gift buyers

Many children's products are bought as gifts, often by people who do not know the child's exact size, interests or what they already own. Helping these buyers can make a real difference.

  • Shop by age. Collections organised by age range are an intuitive way in for gift buyers.
  • Shop by occasion or price. Birthdays, new baby gifts, christenings and seasonal collections can all help, as can gifts under a set amount.
  • Gift wrapping and messages. If you offer them, make them easy to add and show what the result looks like.
  • Gift receipts and returns. Explain clearly how gift recipients can exchange items.
  • Gift cards. Useful for people who are unsure what to buy. Our gift cards guide explains how to set them up.

Collection pages need careful design so that filtering by age, price and category is simple on a phone. Our category page design guide covers the details.

Building trust with parents

Trust is especially important when the product is for a child. Several elements of a store contribute to it.

A genuine brand story

Parents often like to know who is behind a children's brand and why it exists. An honest about page that explains how the business started, what you care about and how you choose or make your products can help. Avoid exaggeration; a straightforward account tends to be more convincing. Our about us page guide has more.

Real reviews

Reviews from other parents can be persuasive, but they must be genuine. Do not write, buy or selectively suppress reviews. UK consumer law has specific rules about fake and misleading reviews, so check the current guidance from the Competition and Markets Authority if you are unsure. Our product reviews guide covers collecting and displaying them properly.

Clear policies

Delivery times, costs, returns and how to get in touch should be easy to find and written in plain English. Our guide to delivery and returns pages and our article on website trust signals explain what to include.

Privacy and marketing to families

Your customers are adults, but the products relate to children, and sometimes you may collect information such as a child's age or birthday for reminders or personalised products. Treat this carefully.

  • Only collect information you genuinely need, and explain why you are collecting it.
  • Make sure your privacy notice covers what you collect and how you use it.
  • Get proper consent for marketing emails, and make it easy to unsubscribe.
  • Think carefully before designing any feature that would collect data directly from children.

The Information Commissioner's Office publishes guidance on UK GDPR, marketing consent and, for online services likely to be accessed by children, its Children's Code. Check what applies to your store. Cookie consent also needs to be handled properly; see our cookie banner design guide.

Email and repeat purchases

Children's products naturally lend themselves to repeat purchases, as children move from one stage to the next. With proper consent, email can help you stay in touch with customers as their needs change, through new arrivals, seasonal collections or helpful content. Our ecommerce email marketing guide covers the essentials, and our article on transactional emails explains the order and delivery messages every store sends.

Getting found in search

Parents often search for specific products, ages and problems, such as gifts for a particular age or a type of nursery item. Collection pages built around these searches, well-written product pages and helpful buying guides can all support your visibility. Our Shopify SEO guide and ecommerce SEO checklist explain how to approach it. For paid visibility, our Google Shopping guide is a good starting point. Note that advertising platforms can have their own policies for children's products, so review them before launching campaigns.

Seasonality and stock

Demand for children's products often rises around Christmas, birthdays and back to school, and gift buyers can be very sensitive to delivery deadlines. Plan stock, delivery cut-off messages and seasonal collections in advance. Our seasonal sales preparation guide and our article on stock availability display can help.

FAQs

Is Shopify a good platform for a children's products brand?

It suits many children's brands, particularly those who want a hosted platform that handles security and checkout, with good support for sizes, variants and gift options.

Can I say my products are safe on my website?

Only make safety claims you can support, ideally in specific terms such as a standard the product has been tested against. Avoid vague absolute claims and check the relevant UK guidance.

Do children's products have special legal requirements in the UK?

Many do, including toys and some nursery and clothing items. The Office for Product Safety and Standards and Trading Standards publish guidance, and a specialist can advise on your products.

How can I reduce returns on children's clothing?

A clear size guide with measurements as well as ages, accurate product details and good photography can all help buyers choose the right size first time.

Can I collect a child's birthday for marketing?

Think carefully, collect only what you need, explain why and get proper consent. Check ICO guidance on UK GDPR and marketing before setting this up.
