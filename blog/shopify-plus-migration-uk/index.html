Shopify Plus Migration UK: How to Move Platforms Without Losing SEO

Shopify Plus migration UK guide: how to move to Shopify or Shopify Plus without losing SEO, with redirect mapping, data transfer and a launch checklist.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A full URL inventory and one-to-one 301 redirect map is the single biggest protection for rankings during a migration.
  • Migrate customers, orders, products, reviews and content in tested passes, with a staging review before launch.
  • Monitor Search Console, crawl errors and revenue daily for the first weeks and fix issues fast.

A Shopify Plus migration moves your store from another platform, or from standard Shopify, onto Shopify Plus, and it can be done without losing SEO if every old URL is mapped to a matching new one with 301 redirects and content, metadata and structured data carry across. Most ranking drops after a migration come from missed redirects, lost content or changed internal links rather than from Shopify itself.

This guide covers when Shopify Plus makes sense, how to protect rankings, what data to move and a launch checklist. For the general principles of keeping rankings during any rebuild, see our guide to a website redesign without losing rankings.

Shopify or Shopify Plus?

Shopify Plus is Shopify's higher tier for larger merchants. It adds features such as checkout customisation options, automation tools, more staff accounts, B2B features and expansion stores. Standard Shopify plans suit many UK stores perfectly well. Plus usually makes sense when order volume, complexity or international selling has outgrown the standard plans.

Shopify Plus migration without losing SEO

The SEO work happens before, during and after launch. Skipping the before stage is the most common and most expensive mistake.

1. Inventory every URL

Crawl the current site and export every URL that matters: products, collections or categories, blog posts, landing pages, images and PDFs. Add URLs from Search Console, GA4 landing page reports and backlink tools, because a crawl misses orphan pages that still earn traffic or links.

2. Understand Shopify's URL structure

Shopify uses fixed patterns such as /products/, /collections/, /pages/ and /blogs/news/. You cannot keep most old URL paths exactly, so redirects are essential. Products also appear under collection paths, with canonical tags pointing to the main product URL; check your theme handles this cleanly.

3. Build a one-to-one redirect map

Map each old URL to its closest new equivalent with a 301 redirect. Avoid sending everything to the home page, which loses relevance. Discontinued products should go to the nearest category or replacement. Shopify's URL redirect tool accepts bulk imports, which makes large maps manageable.

4. Carry content and metadata across

Move page titles, meta descriptions, headings, product descriptions, category copy, image alt text and blog content. Rewriting everything at the same time as moving platforms makes it hard to diagnose ranking changes, so keep copy largely the same at launch and improve it afterwards.

5. Keep structured data and internal links

Check product schema (price, availability, reviews) outputs correctly in your new theme. Update menus, footer links and in-content links to point at new URLs directly rather than through redirects.

Shopify migration checklist

Before launch

  • Full URL inventory from crawl, Search Console, analytics and backlinks.
  • Benchmark rankings, organic traffic and revenue by page.
  • One-to-one 301 redirect map reviewed and imported.
  • Titles, meta descriptions, copy and alt text migrated.
  • Product schema and canonical tags checked on staging.
  • Internal links updated to new URLs.
  • Test orders placed with every payment method, including Klarna or Clearpay if used.
  • Shipping rates for Royal Mail, Evri, DPD or your couriers checked.
  • GA4, Google Ads, Meta and other tags installed with consent management.
  • Site speed checked on mobile. See our site speed guide.

Launch day

  • DNS switched at a quiet trading time.
  • Redirects spot-checked from the top pages by traffic and links.
  • New XML sitemap submitted in Search Console.
  • Robots settings confirmed, with no leftover staging blocks.

After launch

  • Monitor Search Console coverage, crawl errors and 404s daily for the first weeks.
  • Compare organic traffic and revenue with benchmarks.
  • Fix redirect gaps as soon as they appear.
  • Run a full technical SEO audit once the site has settled.

Some movement in rankings during the first few weeks is normal while search engines process redirects. A sustained drop usually points to a specific fault that can be found and fixed.

Common migration mistakes

Most migration problems are predictable, which means they can be prevented:

  • Redirecting everything to the home page. This throws away the relevance of each old page. Map pages one to one wherever an equivalent exists.
  • Forgetting image and PDF URLs. Images that rank in Google Images, and documents that earn links, need redirects too.
  • Leaving staging blocks in place. A password-protected or noindexed site at launch can drop out of search quickly. Check robots settings on launch day.
  • Changing everything at once. New platform, new URLs, new design, new copy and new navigation in one go makes any drop very hard to diagnose.
  • Losing filtered and faceted pages. If the old site had valuable filtered category pages ranking for specific searches, plan equivalent collection pages on Shopify.
  • Launching at peak season. Avoid switching platforms just before Black Friday, Christmas or your busiest trading period.
  • Not testing tracking. Broken purchase tracking after launch makes it impossible to judge the migration or keep ad campaigns optimised.

Apps, themes and integrations

Shopify relies on apps for many features that other platforms build in, such as reviews, subscriptions, advanced filtering and some marketing tools. List every function the current site performs, then decide which ones Shopify handles natively, which need an app and which can be dropped. Too many apps can slow a store down and add scripts that affect site speed, so choose carefully.

Check integrations early: accounting software such as Xero or QuickBooks, stock and warehouse systems, courier labels, email marketing platforms and any marketplace connections. Integrations often take longer to test than the storefront itself. Choose a theme that is fast, well supported and outputs clean structured data, and customise it rather than rebuilding everything from scratch unless you have a clear reason.

Finally, agree who signs off each stage. A migration touches marketing, operations, customer service and finance, so a short sign-off list for products, payments, shipping, tracking and redirects avoids last-minute surprises. Keep the old platform available in read-only form for a few weeks after launch in case you need to check old settings, order details or content that was missed.

FAQs

How do I do a Shopify Plus migration UK businesses can trust without losing SEO?

Inventory every URL, build a one-to-one 301 redirect map, carry content and metadata across unchanged, check structured data on staging, then monitor Search Console closely after launch.

Will I lose rankings when moving to Shopify?

Short-term movement is common, but well-planned migrations usually recover. Lasting drops tend to come from missed redirects, removed content or broken internal links.

Can I keep my existing URLs on Shopify?

Mostly not, because Shopify uses fixed paths such as /products/ and /collections/. That is why a complete redirect map is essential.

Can customer passwords be migrated?

No. Passwords cannot be transferred, so customers need to activate new accounts. A clear activation email at launch keeps friction low.

Do I need Shopify Plus or will standard Shopify do?

Many UK stores run well on standard Shopify. Plus suits higher volume, complex checkout needs, B2B or multiple regional stores. Check Shopify's current plan terms before deciding.

How long does a Shopify migration take?

Ecommerce projects typically take five to twelve weeks, depending on catalogue size, data complexity, integrations and design work.
