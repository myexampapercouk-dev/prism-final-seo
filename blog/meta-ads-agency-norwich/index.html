What a Meta Ads agency in Norwich does

Meta Ads lets you show adverts on Facebook, Instagram and Messenger to people chosen by location, interests, behaviour or their past contact with your business. Unlike search ads, most people seeing them are not actively looking for you, so the work is about grabbing attention and making a clear offer. An agency handles:

  • Strategy: choosing goals, offers and the right campaign objectives.
  • Tracking: setting up the Meta Pixel and Conversions API so results are measured properly.
  • Audiences: local targeting, remarketing and lookalike audiences.
  • Creative: ad copy, image layouts and formats, using photos and video supplied by you.
  • Optimisation: testing, budget changes and regular reporting.

We do not shoot or edit video ourselves. We work with the photos and footage your team provides and advise on what tends to perform. Our paid media service explains the full process, and our guide to Meta Ads agencies in the UK covers the wider picture.

How long Meta Ads take to work

Ads can go live within days, but paid campaigns typically take three to six months to settle into steady performance. The early weeks are spent learning which audiences, offers and creative work best. Results improve as poor performers are paused and budget moves to what works.

  1. Install and test tracking before spending money.
  2. Start with a clear offer and a small number of audiences.
  3. Test several creative angles rather than tiny wording changes.
  4. Review lead quality with you, not just lead volume.
  5. Refresh creative regularly to avoid ad fatigue.

Landing pages and follow-up

Common Meta Ads mistakes we see in Norfolk

Boosting posts from a Facebook page is the most common starting point, and the most common source of wasted money. Boosted posts often optimise for likes and comments rather than enquiries or sales, and they give you little control over placement or tracking. Another frequent problem is targeting that is either far too broad, showing ads across the whole country, or far too narrow, stacking so many interests that the audience is tiny and expensive to reach.

We also see accounts where the pixel is installed but never checked, so purchases or form submissions are not recorded. Without that data, Meta's delivery system cannot find more people likely to convert. Creative is another weak point: the same image runs for months until people stop noticing it, and costs climb. Finally, many businesses send ad traffic to a homepage rather than a page that matches the offer in the advert, which wastes much of the attention the ad has won.
