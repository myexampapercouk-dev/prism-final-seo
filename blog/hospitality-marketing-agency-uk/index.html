Hospitality Marketing Agency UK: Digital Marketing for Hotels, Restaurants and Venues

A hospitality marketing agency in the UK fills rooms, tables and event dates with local SEO, paid ads and a booking-ready website. What it does and costs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Hospitality marketing lives or dies on local search, reviews and a fast mobile booking journey, because most guests decide on their phone.
  • Hotels, restaurants and event venues need different plans: direct bookings for hotels, covers and reservations for restaurants, enquiries for venues.
  • Measure bookings, covers and enquiries by channel, and plan campaigns ahead of seasonal peaks rather than reacting when they arrive.

A hospitality marketing agency in the UK helps hotels, restaurants, pubs, bars and event venues win more direct bookings, reservations and enquiries through local SEO, Google Business Profile, reviews, paid search and social ads and a fast, booking-ready website. The right agency plans around your seasons and measures bookings and covers, not just likes and clicks.

This guide explains what hospitality digital marketing covers, how plans differ for hotels, restaurants and venues, what it typically costs and what to check before choosing an agency.

What does a hospitality marketing agency UK businesses hire actually do?

Hospitality guests make quick decisions, often on a phone and often within a few miles of where they want to eat, stay or celebrate. They compare photos, menus, prices and reviews, then book wherever is easiest. Marketing therefore has two jobs: get you seen at the moment of decision, and make booking effortless.

Typical work includes:

  • Local SEO and Google Business Profile: accurate opening hours, menus, photos, categories and review management, plus location pages that rank. This sits within our SEO and GEO service.
  • Paid search and social: Google Ads for high-intent searches and Meta or other social ads for occasions, offers and events, run through our paid media service.
  • Website and booking journey: fast mobile pages, clear calls to book and properly integrated booking engines or reservation tools.
  • Content and PR: seasonal menus, event pages, local guides and coverage in regional or trade media.
  • Email and guest retention: bringing past guests back for birthdays, seasonal menus and off-peak stays, with appropriate consent.

Hotels and direct bookings

Online travel agents bring visibility but take commission on every booking. Most hotels want a healthier balance, using direct channels for repeat guests and people who already know the property. That means ranking for your own name and local searches, running brand search ads where competitors bid on your name, and making the direct booking engine at least as easy as the OTA. Our guide to increasing hotel direct bookings in the UK covers this in detail.

Restaurants and pubs

For restaurants, Google Business Profile is often the most important digital asset. People search "Italian restaurant near me" or "Sunday roast in Bath", check the map results, read a few reviews and book. Keeping hours, menus and photos accurate, replying to reviews and making reservation links obvious can matter more than anything else. Our local SEO guide explains the basics.

Event and wedding venues

Venue enquiries are high value but slow to convert, with couples and event organisers often comparing many options over months. Detailed pages for each type of event, capacity information, sample pricing where possible and galleries help. Paid search can work well for specific searches, and our guide to what a Google Ads agency does explains how those campaigns are run.

Planning around hospitality seasons

Hospitality demand rises and falls with the calendar: Christmas parties, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, summer holidays, wedding season and bank holidays. Searches for many of these start weeks before the date itself. A good agency maps your peaks and quiet periods, then:

  1. Publishes seasonal pages and menus early enough to rank.
  2. Launches paid campaigns ahead of booking windows, not on the week itself.
  3. Uses email to fill quieter midweek and off-season periods with past guests.
  4. Reviews which channels drove bookings after each peak, ready for next year.

Checklist: choosing a hospitality marketing agency

  • Do they start with your Google Business Profile, reviews and booking journey before proposing ads?
  • Will they track bookings, reservations, calls and enquiries by channel?
  • Do they understand the difference between hotel, restaurant and venue goals?
  • Will they plan around your seasonal peaks and quiet periods?
  • Can they work with your existing booking engine or reservation system?
  • Is ad spend separate from the fee and visible in accounts you own?
  • Do they handle email consent properly under UK GDPR and PECR?
  • Are they realistic that SEO typically takes 3 to 6 months to build?

Reviews, photos and the details that win bookings

In hospitality, small details often decide where someone books. Guests scan photos, check the latest reviews, look at the menu and want to know practical things: is there parking, is it dog-friendly, is there step-free access, can it handle a group of twelve? If the answers are hard to find, many people simply move on to the next option.

A good agency will help you keep those details accurate everywhere guests look, including your website, Google Business Profile and key listings. Practical steps include:

  • Updating photos regularly so they reflect the current menu, rooms and interiors.
  • Replying to reviews, positive and negative, in a calm and helpful tone.
  • Keeping menus as readable web pages rather than only as downloadable files.
  • Adding clear answers on accessibility, parking, dietary options and group bookings.
  • Making sure special opening hours for bank holidays and Christmas are set in advance.

Review platforms have their own rules on how reviews can be requested, so incentivised or selective review requests should be avoided. Honest, consistent requests after a visit are the safer approach.

Measuring what matters

Likes and followers are easy to count but rarely pay the bills. Useful hospitality reporting connects activity to bookings: reservations made through the website, calls from Google Business Profile, direct room bookings and revenue, and enquiries for events. Where a booking engine or reservation tool is involved, tracking needs to be set up carefully so that bookings are attributed to the right channel. Without that, a busy Saturday might be credited to the wrong campaign, and budget ends up in the wrong place. A short monthly summary of bookings by channel, alongside notes on upcoming seasonal pushes, is usually more useful than a long dashboard nobody reads.

FAQs

What does a hospitality marketing agency in the UK do?

It helps hotels, restaurants, pubs and venues win more bookings, reservations and enquiries through local SEO, Google Business Profile, reviews, paid ads, email and a booking-ready website.

How can a hotel get more direct bookings?

Rank for your own name and local searches, run brand search ads where needed, make the direct booking engine fast and simple, and use email to bring past guests back directly.

Is Google Business Profile important for restaurants?

Very. It is often where diners first see your hours, menu, photos and reviews, and where they click to book or call. Keeping it accurate and active is one of the highest-value tasks.

How much does hospitality digital marketing cost?

Our retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month, with ad spend paid separately. A small website, if needed, typically costs £1,000 to £2,000.

When should we start marketing for Christmas bookings?

Many venues start promoting Christmas parties and festive menus in late summer or early autumn, because group organisers often book well ahead. Seasonal pages should be live early enough to rank.

Should hospitality businesses use social media ads?

Often, yes, especially for events, offers and occasions where visuals sell. Social works best alongside search and Google Business Profile rather than as the only channel.
