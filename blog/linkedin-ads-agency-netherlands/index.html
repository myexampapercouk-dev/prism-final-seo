Why LinkedIn suits Dutch B2B marketing

The Netherlands has a dense, internationally minded business community. Amsterdam is home to technology firms, financial services and the European offices of many international companies. Rotterdam is a major port and logistics centre, Eindhoven is known for high-tech manufacturing and engineering, Utrecht has a strong services sector and The Hague hosts government bodies and international organisations. Many of these businesses sell to other businesses, often across borders.

LinkedIn is a natural fit for that market because:

  • Buyers are there in a professional context. People browse LinkedIn while thinking about work, which suits B2B messages.
  • Targeting is role-based. You can reach operations managers at logistics firms or finance directors at mid-sized companies, rather than broad interest groups.
  • English is widely used in Dutch business. Many campaigns can run in English, especially for international or technical audiences.
  • Sales cycles are long. LinkedIn works well for building familiarity before a buyer is ready to talk.

Dutch or English ads?

Many Dutch professionals read English comfortably, and English often works well for international, technical or senior audiences. But Dutch can feel more direct and personal for smaller firms, regional businesses, the public sector and audiences outside international hubs. The right choice depends on who you sell to, not on a general rule.

We often test both. Prism writes the English versions in-house. For Dutch, we supply the brief, structure and English draft, then a native speaker on your team or a translator writes the final copy. Landing pages should match the language of the ad, so a Dutch ad does not lead to an English-only page. Our landing page design guide covers what makes a page convert once someone clicks.

Campaign formats we use

  1. Sponsored Content. Native posts in the feed using single images, video, carousels or documents. The core format for most B2B campaigns.
  2. Lead Gen Forms. Forms that open inside LinkedIn with details pre-filled from the member's profile, which often improves completion on mobile.
  3. Document ads. Useful for guides and reports, letting people preview content before they share their details.
  4. Messaging ads. Direct messages to targeted members, best used sparingly for events or specific offers.
  5. Retargeting. Ads shown to people who visited your site, watched your video or opened a form, to bring warm prospects back.

Our guide to LinkedIn Ads for B2B lead generation explains how we combine these formats into a funnel.

Tracking, consent and privacy

To measure leads and build retargeting audiences, campaigns usually rely on the LinkedIn Insight Tag. Under the EU ePrivacy rules, tracking and marketing cookies normally need consent before they load, so the tag should be connected to your consent banner. Our LinkedIn Insight Tag setup guide walks through the process.

Lead Gen Forms collect personal data, which falls under the GDPR. Your forms need a link to your privacy notice, and your team needs a clear process for storing and following up leads. Legal wording and decisions on compliance should come from your legal adviser, and official guidance should be checked before relying on any summary.

If you send leads to your own website, accessibility matters too. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing products and services. Most B2B services fall outside its main scope, and microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but building to WCAG AA, the level referenced by the harmonised standard EN 301 549, is good practice for any landing page.

LinkedIn or Google Ads?

LinkedIn and Google Ads do different jobs. Google Ads reaches people actively searching for a solution, while LinkedIn reaches people who match your target profile whether or not they are searching today. LinkedIn costs per click are usually higher, so it works best where a single customer is worth a lot. Our management fees are listed on our pricing page: Launch at £350 a month, Growth at £750 a month and Scale at £1,500 a month. The right level depends on the number of campaigns, audiences, languages and how much creative you need. Ad spend is paid separately, directly to LinkedIn. It depends on your audience. English often suits international and senior audiences, while Dutch can work better for domestic and regional ones. We often test both, with Dutch copy written by your team or a translator.
