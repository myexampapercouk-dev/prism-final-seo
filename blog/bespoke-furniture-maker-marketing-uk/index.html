Bespoke Furniture Maker Marketing in the UK: How Makers Win More Commissions

Bespoke furniture maker marketing in the UK works best with a strong portfolio site, a clear commission process, project pages, local SEO and referrals.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Commissions are high value, considered purchases, so a portfolio website with detailed project pages does more selling than any advert.
  • Explaining your commission process, typical lead times and budget ranges up front attracts the right clients and filters out poor fits.
  • Interior designers, architects, builders and kitchen fitters can send steady work, so marketing to the trade deserves as much attention as marketing to homeowners.

Bespoke furniture maker marketing in the UK is about showing the quality of your work clearly enough that the right clients feel confident commissioning you, often for a significant sum and a wait of several weeks or months. The makers who keep a healthy order book usually combine a portfolio website with detailed project pages, a clear explanation of how commissions work, local search visibility, a strong visual social presence and good relationships with designers and builders.

This guide is for independent furniture makers, cabinet makers, fitted furniture and joinery workshops, makers of tables and chairs, and small studios producing one off or limited edition pieces. We cover how clients search, what your website needs, pricing and enquiry handling, social media, the trade and paid campaigns.

How clients find a furniture maker

People looking for bespoke furniture usually start with a specific piece or problem: a dining table that seats ten, alcove cupboards, a fitted wardrobe for a sloping ceiling, a walnut desk or a built in bookcase. They search for bespoke furniture maker plus a town or county, fitted furniture near me, or handmade oak dining table. Many also browse visual platforms for ideas long before they contact anyone.

Others arrive through recommendations. An interior designer, architect, kitchen company or builder may suggest a maker, or a friend may have commissioned a piece. Even then, the client will almost always check your website and portfolio before getting in touch, so that is where much of the decision is made.

Bespoke furniture maker marketing UK: a portfolio that sells

Your portfolio is the most important part of your marketing. A gallery of small images is not enough. Detailed project pages that tell the story of each commission help clients picture working with you and give search engines rich content to rank.

Be careful with sourcing claims. If you describe timber as locally grown, reclaimed or from certified sources, make sure you can back it up. Our guide to carpenter and joiner marketing covers related ideas for workshops that also take on joinery and fitting work.

Explaining price and lead times

Bespoke work is hard to price online, but saying nothing about cost often brings in enquiries that will never go ahead. Many makers publish a starting from figure for common pieces, or a range for typical commissions, and explain what affects the price, such as size, timber, complexity and fitting. Stating typical lead times, for example that your diary is usually booked a set number of weeks ahead, helps clients plan and shows you are in demand.

A well designed enquiry form can ask for the type of piece, the room, rough dimensions, timing and a budget range. Our guide to website contact forms explains how to ask for this without putting people off. Reply quickly and personally, since a prompt, thoughtful response is part of the experience clients are paying for.

Local SEO for furniture makers

Many clients prefer a maker within reach, especially for fitted work that needs a survey and installation. A Google Business Profile with an accurate category, workshop address if you welcome visits or a service area if you do not, and strong photos helps you appear in local searches. Ask happy clients for reviews, which matter for a purchase of this size. Mention the towns and counties you regularly work in on your website, with genuine examples rather than lists of place names.

Helpful written content can also rank: how to choose a timber for a dining table, solid wood versus veneer, how to care for an oiled finish, or what to expect when commissioning a fitted wardrobe. Our content strategy guide explains how to plan topics that bring in the right visitors.

Social media and visual platforms

Furniture making is ideal for visual channels. Process shots, timber selection, close ups of joints, finishing and the moment a piece is installed all make engaging posts. Instagram and Pinterest suit inspiration seekers, while short videos can show the craft behind a piece. Post consistently, link back to project pages and include your location so local clients know you can work for them.

Keep captions specific and useful. Explaining why you chose a particular joint or finish shows expertise and helps people understand the value of handmade work. Grouping posts into themed collections, such as dining tables, alcove storage or desks, also makes it easier for someone browsing your profile to find the type of piece they want and then follow a link to the matching project page on your website.

Working with interior designers and the trade

Interior designers, architects, builders and kitchen companies can provide a steady flow of commissions. They want a maker who is reliable, communicates well, works from drawings and meets deadlines. A dedicated trade page explaining how you collaborate, the type of work you take on and your typical lead times makes it easier for them to recommend you. Our guides to interior designer website design and upholsterer marketing show how related businesses present their work, which can help you understand what trade partners look for.

Paid campaigns and shows

Paid search can help for specific, high value searches such as fitted wardrobes or bespoke kitchens in your area, but keep budgets controlled and track enquiries carefully, since commissions take time to close. Paid social works best for showing finished pieces to homeowners in your target area or retargeting people who have viewed your portfolio. Ad spend is always paid separately from any management fee.

Design shows, open studio events and local makers markets can also bring in clients. Use them to collect email addresses with consent and follow up afterwards with a short note and links to recent projects. Keep all marketing within UK GDPR and PECR rules and check current ICO guidance before you start.

Your website and next steps

A typical small portfolio website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and usually takes three to five weeks to build. If you also sell smaller pieces or homeware online, an e-commerce site usually costs £1,500 to £3,500 and takes five to twelve weeks. SEO typically takes three to six months to show clear results, so start building project pages early. FAQs

What is the best approach to bespoke furniture maker marketing in the UK?

Build a portfolio website with detailed project pages, explain your commission process and price ranges, keep a complete Google Business Profile, post your work on visual platforms and build relationships with designers and builders.

Should a furniture maker show prices on their website?

A starting from figure or typical range usually helps, along with what affects the price. It sets expectations and reduces enquiries that will not go ahead.

How do furniture makers get commissions from interior designers?

Create a trade page, show finished projects with clear details, reach out to local designers with examples of your work and prove you are reliable on drawings, deadlines and communication.

Is Instagram useful for bespoke furniture makers?

It often is, because furniture is visual and the making process is engaging. Post consistently and link people back to project pages on your website.

What should a furniture commission enquiry form ask?

The type of piece, the room, rough dimensions, preferred timing and a budget range, plus an option to upload photos or drawings of the space.

How much does a website for a furniture maker cost?

A small portfolio website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000. A site that also sells products online usually costs £1,500 to £3,500 depending on features.
