Web Design for Qatar Businesses: Planning, Cost and Choosing a Studio

Need web design in Qatar? How a remote UK studio plans and builds bilingual websites for Doha businesses, what it costs in pounds and what to ask first.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A strong website for a Qatar business is quick on mobile, uses Arabic and English where customers need them, and makes calling, messaging or buying easy.
  • A remote studio can deliver a Qatar website smoothly when scope, feedback rounds, ownership and meeting times are agreed in writing at the start.
  • Our small business websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and e-commerce sites £1,500 to £3,500, priced in pounds with other currencies quoted on request.

Web design for a Qatar business means creating a site that loads fast on mobile, speaks to customers in Arabic, English or both, and turns visits into calls, WhatsApp chats, bookings or online orders. You can hire locally or remotely; what matters most is a clear process, a fixed scope and full ownership of the finished site.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely. We have no office in Qatar, so projects run through video calls, shared documents and a private staging site you can check at any time. This guide explains how our website and e-commerce development work fits Doha and wider Qatar businesses, what it costs and how to compare studios fairly.

Web design in Qatar: what customers expect

Qatar has a very high share of people browsing on phones and a population that mixes Qatari nationals with large expatriate communities. That shapes what a website needs to do. Visitors in Doha, Al Wakrah, Al Rayyan or Lusail often compare several providers quickly, so the first screen has to say clearly what you do, who you serve and how to get in touch.

  • Speed on mobile. Compressed images, light code and careful use of video keep pages quick on any connection.
  • Language that fits the audience. Some sites need full Arabic and English, others need English with a few Arabic pages.
  • Simple contact routes. Click-to-call, WhatsApp buttons, short forms and booking tools where people expect them.
  • Clear service and product pages. One page per main service or category helps both visitors and search engines.
  • Accurate business details. Opening hours, service areas and policies that answer common questions before they are asked.

Choosing your language set-up

The language decision affects design, content, cost and SEO, so it is worth settling early. Arabic reads right to left, which mirrors layouts, menus, icons and form fields. Good Arabic copy is usually adapted for local readers rather than translated word for word.

We write in English. Arabic pages are written with your team or a professional translator, and we build the templates so both versions look and work properly. Our guide to bilingual Arabic and English websites explains fonts, layout mirroring and SEO for each language.

How a remote web design project runs

Distance is rarely the problem in a website project. Unclear scope and slow decisions are. We keep things simple with the same stages for every client.

  1. Discovery call. Goals, customers, languages, competitors, features and deadlines.
  2. Written proposal. Scope, timeline and a fixed price in pounds.
  3. Sitemap and content plan. Agreed page list and outlines, with a clear note of who supplies each piece of content.
  4. Design. Key page designs shared for feedback, gathered in one place and revised.
  5. Build on staging. A private address you can review on your own devices throughout.
  6. Testing. Forms, speed, both languages, mobile layouts and analytics checked before launch.
  7. Launch and handover. Logins, a short guide and every account in your name.

Qatar is a few hours ahead of the UK and the working week differs, so we agree fixed call slots early. Most feedback happens in writing, which keeps a record of decisions. Having copy and photos ready before the build starts makes the biggest difference to timing; a short content checklist agreed at discovery keeps everyone on track. Platforms for Qatar websites

We recommend a platform based on how you will run the site day to day.

  • WordPress for service businesses, clinics, consultancies and content-led sites that need flexible pages and a blog.
  • Shopify for most online shops, with built-in checkout, multi-currency and multi-language options. Our guide to choosing a Shopify agency in Qatar goes into store set-up in more detail.
  • WooCommerce for stores that already run WordPress or need specific customisation.

Whatever the platform, the domain, hosting and site accounts are set up in your name. Our guide to website ownership and handover explains why this protects you if you ever switch provider.

Online selling and local rules

If you sell online in Qatar, customers expect familiar payment options, clear delivery times for Doha and other areas, and easy returns information. If you ship to other Gulf countries, delivery and pricing pages need to say so plainly. Rules on e-commerce, consumer protection, data handling and tax apply and change over time, so please check current official guidance and confirm details with your adviser. We build the pages, settings and checkout; compliance decisions stay with the business.

Getting found after launch

A new site needs a sound technical base to rank: clean URLs, fast pages, correct language tags, structured data and a page for each main service. We build these in from the start. For businesses that want ongoing visibility, our notes on choosing an SEO agency in Qatar explain how search work continues after launch, and our SEO and GEO service covers technical health, content and AI search.

Design that reflects your brand

A Qatar website often has to look polished to a demanding audience while staying easy to use. That means a consistent colour palette and type that work in both scripts, photography that shows your real premises, products or team rather than generic stock, and layouts that leave room for Arabic text, which can run longer or shorter than English. If your logo, colours or messaging need work first, our brand strategy service can sit alongside the build so the site launches with a clear identity. We also plan accessible contrast, readable font sizes and clear focus states, which help every visitor and make the site easier to maintain.

Questions to ask any web design studio

  • Who owns the domain, hosting, design files and content when the project ends?
  • How will the Arabic and English versions be built, and who checks the Arabic copy?
  • What exactly is included in the price, and what would cost extra?
  • How many rounds of design changes are included?
  • How will you test speed and mobile layouts before launch?
  • What support is available after launch, and at what cost?
  • How and when will we communicate during the project?

Be cautious of any provider that registers your domain in its own name, cannot explain what happens if you leave or will not put the scope in writing.

Looking after the site

Websites need updates, security patches, content changes and the occasional review of what visitors are doing. We offer support and marketing retainers for clients who want ongoing help, and we are equally happy to hand over a site your own team can run.

FAQs

How much does web design in Qatar cost?

Our small business websites are £1,000 to £2,000 and e-commerce sites £1,500 to £3,500, priced in pounds. Bilingual builds and extra features can add to this, and other currencies are quoted on request.

Do you have an office in Doha?

No. Prism Works Studio is based in the UK and works with Qatar clients remotely through video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can review at any time.

Can you build an Arabic and English website?

Yes. We build right-to-left templates, separate language URLs and hreflang tags. Arabic copy is written with your team or a professional translator so it reads naturally.

How long does a new website take?

A small business website usually takes 3 to 5 weeks and an e-commerce site 5 to 12 weeks. Content readiness and feedback speed are the biggest factors.

Will I own the website?

Yes. The domain, hosting and platform accounts are set up in your name, and all logins are handed over at launch.

Do you help with legal and policy pages for Qatar?

We build and lay out the pages, but the wording depends on your business and current rules, so please check official guidance and confirm the content with your legal adviser.
