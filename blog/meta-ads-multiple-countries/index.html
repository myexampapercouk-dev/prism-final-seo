Meta Ads in Multiple Countries: How to Run Facebook and Instagram Ads Across Europe

How to run Meta ads in multiple countries across Europe: campaign structure, languages, creative, budgets, pixel and consent, and reporting by market.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Meta can target many countries from one campaign, but splitting the main markets lets you control budget, creative and reporting for each one.
  • Creative and copy should be produced or adapted for each language, with landing pages, prices and delivery information that match the market.
  • Pixel, Conversions API and consent set-up must work on every market version of your site, checked against current EU guidance and Meta's policies.

To run Meta ads in multiple countries well, split your main markets into separate campaigns or ad sets, write ads in each audience's language, and send people to landing pages that match their language, currency and delivery options. Meta will happily spend one budget across many countries, but without that structure it tends to favour the cheapest audiences rather than the most profitable ones.

We are a UK-based studio managing paid social remotely for businesses across Europe, from Ireland and the Netherlands to Germany, France, Spain and the Nordics. This guide covers how we structure multi-country Facebook and Instagram campaigns, how to handle language and creative, what tracking needs, and how to judge whether each market is working.

Running Meta ads in multiple countries: the main options

Meta lets you target audiences by country, region or city, and by language. That gives you several ways to structure a European account.

For most businesses we start with separate campaigns or ad sets for the two or three strongest markets and a grouped test for smaller ones. As a market proves itself, it gets its own budget. This mirrors how we approach search; see our guide to location targeting for the equivalent decisions in Google Ads.

Language, copy and creative

People scroll quickly, and an ad in the wrong language or with the wrong currency is easy to ignore. Good multi-country creative usually means:

  • Native copy. Written or reviewed by a fluent speaker, not just machine-translated.
  • Local details. Prices in local currency, local delivery times and clear returns information.
  • Text-light visuals. Images and videos with little baked-in text are easier to reuse across markets.
  • Subtitles. Many people watch with the sound off, so captions in each language help video ads.
  • Cultural sense-checks. Humour, gestures, seasons and holidays differ between countries.

Meta's tools can help serve different text to different languages within one ad, which reduces duplication. It is still worth checking how each version renders on mobile before launch. Ads should send people to a landing page in the same language; sending a German-speaking shopper to an English page often wastes the click.

Audiences and targeting across Europe

Broad targeting combined with strong creative often performs well on Meta, because the algorithm finds buyers within a market. That works best when each market has enough budget and conversion data. Interest targeting and lookalike audiences can still help early on, particularly when your customer list is large enough in a given country. Our guide to lookalike audiences explains how they work, and remember that customer lists must be used lawfully under the GDPR and Meta's terms.

Exclusions matter too. Exclude existing customers from prospecting where appropriate, and keep remarketing audiences separate by market so offers and languages stay consistent.

Budgets and bidding per market

Costs and conversion rates vary between countries, and so do your margins once delivery, duties and returns are included. A sale in one market may be worth far less than the same sale in another. We set budgets and targets per market based on what a customer is worth there, rather than one blended target for Europe.

Smaller markets may need patience and a minimum budget before results settle. If a market cannot be given enough budget to learn, it is often better to group it with similar markets or pause it until you can fund it properly.

Pixel, Conversions API and consent in the EU

Tracking is where multi-country Meta campaigns most often go wrong. Your Meta pixel and, ideally, the Conversions API should be installed on every language and country version of your site, with events firing consistently and deduplicated correctly. Our guide to Meta pixel set-up covers the basics.

Across the EU, the GDPR and ePrivacy rules affect when tracking technologies can be used, and national regulators have issued their own guidance on cookies and consent. Your consent banner should work in every language and should control the pixel properly. Check current official guidance in the countries you target, along with Meta's own policies, and take legal advice where needed. Our cookie banner design guide covers the user experience side.

Landing pages and the shopping journey

Strong ads cannot rescue a weak landing page. For each market, check that the page loads quickly on mobile, shows local currency and delivery information, and offers payment methods people in that country expect. If you sell on Shopify, Markets can help present local currencies and languages from one store; our website and ecommerce development service covers that set-up.

Use UTM parameters on every ad so analytics can separate traffic by market and campaign. Our guide to UTM tracking links explains a naming system that keeps reports tidy.

Reporting by market

Account totals hide problems. We report on spend, purchases or leads, cost per result and return for each market, alongside creative performance by language. That makes it clear which countries deserve more budget, which need better creative or landing pages, and which should be paused.

Review creative separately for each language too. An image or hook that works well in Ireland may fall flat in Germany or Spain, and a strong local-language ad can lift a market that looked weak. We refresh creative regularly, retire ads that have tired, and share what we learn across markets where it makes sense, while keeping each country's results visible on their own.

Meta's reported conversions and your own analytics or store data will rarely match exactly, especially where consent reduces tracking. Compare trends across both, and use your store's order data by country as the final check.

Meta, Google or both?

Meta is strong for creating demand with visual products and offers, while search ads capture people already looking. Many European ecommerce brands use both, with Meta introducing the brand and search catching people who later look for it. Our comparison of Google Ads and Facebook ads covers the differences, and the same principles apply in European markets.

FAQs

Can I run Meta ads in multiple countries from one campaign?

Yes, Meta allows one campaign or ad set to target several countries. It is fine for early tests, but spend tends to drift to the cheapest audiences, so most businesses split their main markets into separate ad sets or campaigns.

Should I translate my Facebook ads for each country?

If you are targeting people who use another language, yes. Copy should be written or reviewed by a fluent speaker, with local currency and delivery details, and the landing page should be in the same language.

How much budget does each country need?

Enough for the campaign to gather conversion data, which depends on your prices and conversion rate. If a market cannot be funded properly, group it with similar markets or pause it until you can.

Does EU consent law affect the Meta pixel?

Yes. GDPR and ePrivacy rules affect when tracking can run, so your consent banner should control the pixel in every language version. Check current official guidance and Meta's policies, and take legal advice if unsure.

Is English enough for ads in the Nordics or the Netherlands?

English can work for some audiences in these markets, and it is a reasonable starting test. Local-language ads and landing pages often feel more relevant, so test both where you have the content.

How do I know which country is profitable?

Report spend, results and return per country, and compare Meta's figures with your store or CRM data by country. Include delivery, returns and other costs that differ by market when judging profitability.
