Wix vs WordPress for UK Businesses: An Honest Comparison

Wix vs WordPress for UK small businesses: ease of use, design control, SEO, ecommerce, ownership, maintenance and when it makes sense to switch.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Wix is a hosted, all-in-one builder that trades flexibility for convenience; WordPress is open-source software that trades convenience for control.
  • The right choice depends on who will run the site, how much it needs to grow and how much maintenance you are prepared to take on.
  • Moving from one to the other is possible but rarely automatic, so it is worth choosing with the next few years in mind.

Wix and WordPress are two of the most common ways UK businesses build a website. Both can produce a professional site, and both are used by sole traders, local service firms and growing companies. They are built on very different ideas, though, and those differences matter more as a business grows. This guide compares them on the points that tend to decide the question in practice: who runs the site, how much control you need, how search visibility is handled, what ecommerce looks like and what happens if you want to leave.

It is written for UK business owners and marketing leads choosing a platform for a new site or wondering whether to move an existing one.

The basic difference

Wix is a hosted website builder. You pay a subscription and Wix provides the software, hosting, security updates and a drag-and-drop editor in one package. You build within the tools Wix offers, plus apps from its marketplace.

WordPress (in the sense most businesses mean it, the self-hosted software from WordPress.org) is free, open-source software that you install on hosting you choose. You pick a theme, add plugins and can change almost anything with code. In return you, or someone you appoint, are responsible for hosting, updates, backups and security.

WordPress.com, the hosted service, sits somewhere in between. When this guide says WordPress, it means the self-hosted version unless stated.

Ease of use

Wix is designed for people who want to build and edit a site without technical knowledge. The editor is visual, templates are ready to customise and there is nothing to install. For a small business owner who wants to update their own opening hours, photos and services, that is a real advantage.

WordPress has a steeper start. The block editor is now fairly approachable for editing pages and posts, but setting up a site well involves decisions about hosting, themes and plugins. Once a site is built properly, day-to-day editing can be straightforward, and many businesses have a developer build the site and then manage content themselves. Our guide to WordPress page builders explains the editing options.

Design and flexibility

Wix gives a lot of visual freedom within its editor, and templates cover most common business types. The limits appear when you need something the platform does not support: unusual layouts across many pages, custom functionality, complex integrations or a specific content structure. Wix does offer developer tools, but you are still working inside its system.

WordPress can be shaped into almost anything, from a simple brochure site to a large content site, membership site or online shop. Custom post types, custom fields and bespoke themes let a site be structured around how the business actually works. That flexibility is why WordPress is common for sites expected to grow. It also means quality varies widely depending on how the site is built.

SEO

Both platforms can rank well in Google. Search performance depends far more on content, site structure, speed and links than on the platform alone. That said, there are practical differences.

  • Wix covers the essentials: editable titles and meta descriptions, custom URLs, redirects, sitemaps and structured data options. It has improved considerably over the years, though some technical settings are less configurable than on a self-hosted site.
  • WordPress gives full control over URLs, templates, markup, structured data and server configuration, usually via SEO plugins and theme code. That control is useful for larger sites but also leaves more room for mistakes.

For most small service businesses, either platform can do the job if the site is set up sensibly. Our SEO and GEO service page explains how we approach search.

Blogging and content

If content marketing is part of your plans, consider how each platform handles it. WordPress began as blogging software, and publishing, categorising and organising large amounts of content remains one of its strengths, with fine control over archives, categories and templates. Wix includes a capable blog and is perfectly adequate for publishing regular articles on a small site. The gap tends to show on sites with hundreds of posts, multiple authors or complex content types, where WordPress's structure is easier to scale.

Speed and performance

Wix manages hosting and performance for you, which removes a set of decisions but also limits how much you can tune. WordPress performance depends heavily on hosting, theme and plugins: a lean, well-built site can be very fast, while a site overloaded with plugins on cheap hosting can be slow. Our guide to WordPress hosting in the UK covers the main choices.

Ecommerce

Both can sell online. Wix has built-in ecommerce on its business plans, which suits small catalogues and simple shops. WordPress sells through WooCommerce, a free plugin that turns the site into a full store and can be extended a long way, at the cost of more setup and maintenance.

If selling online is the main purpose of the site, it is worth comparing a dedicated ecommerce platform too. See our comparisons of Wix vs Shopify and Shopify vs WooCommerce.

Maintenance and security

This is where the two differ most in daily life.

  • Wix handles software updates, hosting and platform security. Your responsibilities are mostly your own content, account security and any apps you add.
  • WordPress needs regular core, theme and plugin updates, backups, monitoring and good hosting. Neglected WordPress sites can become vulnerable. Many businesses pay for a maintenance arrangement for this reason.

Our guide to WordPress security explains what good maintenance involves.

Ownership and portability

With WordPress you own the software installation and can move it to another host whenever you like. Your content, database and files are yours to export and take elsewhere.

With Wix, your site lives on Wix. You can export some content, such as blog posts, but you cannot take the site's design and structure to another platform. If you outgrow Wix, moving usually means rebuilding the site and carefully redirecting old URLs. Our guide to migrating from Wix to WordPress covers that process.

Costs

Cost comparisons are less simple than they look. Wix bundles hosting and software into a subscription, with premium apps on top. WordPress software is free, but hosting, premium themes or plugins, development and maintenance all cost money. A professionally built site on either platform involves design and build time too. Platform pricing changes, so check current plans directly. For website project costs, see our WordPress website cost guide and our pricing page.

Which should you choose?

Wix may suit you if:

  • You want to build and edit the site yourself with minimal technical involvement.
  • The site is a fairly simple brochure site for a small or local business.
  • You would rather not deal with hosting, updates or security.
  • You do not expect complex functionality or integrations.

WordPress may suit you if:

  • You expect the site to grow in content, features or traffic.
  • You need custom functionality, integrations or a specific content structure.
  • Owning and being able to move your site matters to you.
  • You have, or are willing to pay for, someone to build and maintain it properly.

Our guide to WordPress for small business websites goes further into whether WordPress is a good fit.

Thinking about switching?

If you are on Wix and running into limits, write down what you actually cannot do. Sometimes the answer is better use of the current platform rather than a rebuild. If a move is justified, plan it properly: map every URL, set up redirects, move content carefully and check analytics and search performance afterwards. Moving from WordPress to Wix is less common but follows the same principles.

FAQs

Is Wix or WordPress better for SEO?

Both can rank well. WordPress offers more technical control, which matters more for larger sites. For a small site, content quality and structure usually matter more than the platform.

Is Wix cheaper than WordPress?

Not necessarily. Wix bundles hosting into a subscription; WordPress is free software but needs hosting, maintenance and often development. Compare the full cost over several years.

Can I move my Wix site to WordPress?

Yes, but it is usually a rebuild rather than a one-click transfer. Content can be moved, and redirects are needed to protect search visibility.

Is WordPress hard to use?

Editing content on a well-built WordPress site is fairly straightforward. Setting one up and maintaining it takes more technical knowledge than Wix.

Which is better for a small business?

It depends on who runs the site and how much it needs to grow. Wix suits simple sites run by the owner; WordPress suits businesses that need flexibility and control.
