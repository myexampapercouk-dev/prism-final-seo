Restaurant Marketing in Toronto: Websites, Local SEO, Reviews and Ads That Fill Tables

Restaurant marketing in Toronto: websites, local SEO, Google reviews, bookings and Meta ads for restaurants and bars, from a UK studio with no local office.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

  • Restaurant marketing in Toronto works best when a fast website, a complete Google Business Profile, steady reviews and simple online booking all point diners to the same next step.
  • We are a UK-based studio that builds restaurant websites and runs local SEO and social ads remotely for Toronto venues, with no office in Canada.

Restaurant marketing in Toronto is about being the obvious choice when someone nearby searches for somewhere to eat, then making it effortless for them to book, order or walk in. The restaurants that do this consistently well have a fast mobile website with a readable menu, a complete Google Business Profile, a steady stream of genuine reviews, simple online booking and social content that shows the food and the room as they really are.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio. We work remotely with Canadian businesses and have no office in Toronto or elsewhere in Canada. This guide explains how we approach marketing for Toronto restaurants, cafes and bars, which channels matter most and what the work costs.

Who this guide is for

We wrote this guide for independent operators and small groups across the city and the wider Greater Toronto Area, including:

  • Independent restaurants that rely on local regulars and word of mouth.
  • Cafes and bakeries that want more weekday trade and online orders.
  • Bars and cocktail venues that need to stand out for evenings and events.
  • Small restaurant groups running several locations under one brand.
  • New openings that need visibility before and after launch day.

Toronto's neighbourhoods each have their own dining character, and diners often search by area, cuisine or occasion. Good marketing reflects that rather than treating the whole city as one market.

A restaurant website that does its job

Most people visit a restaurant website on their phone, often while standing on the street or deciding with friends. They want four things fast: the menu, the opening hours, the location and a way to book. Everything else is secondary. A good Toronto restaurant website should include:

  • A menu written as text on the page, not only as a PDF or image, so it loads quickly and search engines can read it.
  • Opening hours that match your Google Business Profile exactly.
  • A clear booking button near the top of every page.
  • Your address, nearest transit stop and parking notes.
  • Pages for private dining, events and catering if you offer them.
  • Honest photos of real dishes and the dining room.

We go into the design detail in our guide to restaurant website design, and our guide to web design in Toronto covers how we run projects for clients in the city.

Online booking and ordering

Booking should take a few taps. Many Toronto restaurants use a reservation platform such as OpenTable or a booking tool connected to their point of sale. Whatever you choose, link to it from every page and from your Google Business Profile. Our guide to website online booking systems explains how to choose and embed one without slowing your site down.

Delivery apps such as Uber Eats, DoorDash and SkipTheDishes can bring in extra orders, but they take a commission on each sale. Where it makes sense, give regular customers a direct ordering option on your own website, and make sure menus and prices match across every channel.

Local SEO and your Google Business Profile

For most restaurants, the Google Business Profile is the single most valuable marketing asset. To get the most from it:

  • Choose the most specific primary category that matches your cuisine or format.
  • Keep hours accurate, including holiday hours and special closures.
  • Add a menu link, a booking link and plenty of current photos.
  • Post updates for new dishes, seasonal menus and events.
  • Answer common questions about parking, dietary options and accessibility.

On your website, build pages around how people actually search, such as cuisine, neighbourhood, occasions like brunch or group dinners, and dietary needs. Our guide to choosing an SEO agency in Toronto explains our wider local SEO approach, and our SEO service sets out what we do each month.

Reviews: the lifeblood of a restaurant

Diners read reviews before choosing where to eat, and review volume and recency influence map rankings. Build a simple habit of asking for reviews: a small card with the bill, a QR code at the counter or a follow-up email after an online booking. Our guide on how to get more Google reviews has practical ideas.

Reply to every review, good or bad, briefly and politely. A calm, helpful reply to a negative review often reassures future diners more than any number of five-star ratings. Never buy reviews, offer rewards in exchange for them or post them yourself.

Social media and Meta Ads

Instagram and Facebook remain central for restaurants because food, drinks and atmosphere are visual. Short videos of dishes being plated, behind-the-scenes kitchen clips and event highlights tend to work well. Organic posting builds familiarity, while paid ads let you reach people within a set distance of your venue.

Meta Ads work particularly well for launches, new menus, ticketed events and seasonal offers such as patio season or holiday parties. We explain how we plan and manage campaigns in our guide to choosing a Meta Ads agency in Toronto. If your audience skews younger, TikTok Ads can also be worth testing.

Rules, privacy and consent

Restaurants in Ontario operate under a range of rules, including food safety requirements and, for licensed venues, provincial alcohol regulations that can affect how you advertise drinks and promotions. Allergen and dietary claims on menus also need care. We keep our notes general and are not legal advisers, so please check official guidance or speak to your own adviser. Responsibility for licensing, menu accuracy and promotional claims rests with you.

If you collect emails or phone numbers for marketing, Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation, known as CASL, requires consent and an easy way to unsubscribe. PIPEDA, Canada's federal privacy law, governs how you handle personal information. We set up sign-up forms and email tools to support good practice, and you remain responsible for meeting your obligations.

French and multilingual menus

Toronto is home to diners who speak many languages, and some restaurants serve a strong French-speaking or international clientele. We write in English. If you want menus or pages in French or another language, the content should come from your own team or a professional translator, and we can build the site to support it cleanly.

FAQs

What is the most effective restaurant marketing in Toronto?

For most venues, a complete Google Business Profile, a fast mobile website with a text menu, regular genuine reviews and simple online booking deliver the most value. Social media and ads then amplify launches, events and new menus.

Do you have an office in Toronto?

No. Prism Works Studio is UK-based and works remotely. We schedule calls around Eastern Time and handle all design, SEO and ad management online.

Should my menu be a PDF?

We recommend putting the menu on the page as text. It loads faster on phones, is easier to read and can be understood by search engines. You can still offer a PDF download as an extra.

Are delivery apps worth it for a Toronto restaurant?

They can add orders and reach new customers, but commissions reduce margins. Many restaurants use apps for discovery while encouraging regulars to order directly through their own website.

Can you write our menu in French?

We write in English. French or other language content should come from your team or a professional translator, and we build the website to display it properly.

How much should a restaurant spend on Meta Ads?

Start with a modest local budget tied to a specific goal, such as an event or a new menu, then scale what works. Ad spend is paid directly to Meta and is separate from our retainer.
