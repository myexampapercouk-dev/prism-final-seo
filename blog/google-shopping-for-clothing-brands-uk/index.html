Google Shopping for Clothing Brands: Feeds, Campaigns and Returns for UK Fashion Retailers

Google Shopping for UK clothing brands: apparel feed attributes, size and colour variants, images, campaign structure, returns policies and protecting margins.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Google Shopping for clothing brands depends on a clean apparel feed, with accurate size, colour, gender, age group and variant grouping, plus images that meet Google policies.
  • Structure campaigns around margin, stock depth and seasonality rather than one catch-all campaign, and keep returns and delivery information consistent between site and feed.
  • Prism is a UK-based studio; Shopping management sits within retainers from £350 a month with ad spend paid separately, and results typically build over 3 to 6 months.

Google Shopping for clothing brands means listing your garments in Google's product ads and free listings through a Merchant Center feed, then managing campaigns so that the right styles, sizes and colours appear for the right searches at a profitable cost. Fashion is one of the most competitive Shopping categories in the UK, and it has stricter product data requirements than most, so feed quality decides much of your success.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio. We set up and manage Google Shopping for independent fashion and clothing brands through our paid media service. This guide explains what clothing retailers need to get right, from apparel attributes to campaign structure and returns.

Why clothing is different in Google Shopping

Clothing products come in many variants, change with the seasons and depend heavily on images. Shoppers search in very specific ways, such as a colour, fabric, fit or occasion, and they compare prices and delivery terms across many retailers at once. Returns are common, which affects how much you can afford to pay for each sale.

Google also treats apparel as a special category. For clothing sold in the UK and several other countries, Merchant Center generally expects extra attributes describing each product's size, colour, gender and age group, and products with missing or inaccurate data may be limited or disapproved. Requirements change, so always check the current Merchant Center help pages for the details that apply to your products.

Writing product titles for fashion

Product titles are one of the strongest factors in which searches your ads appear for. A title made up of a single style name might suit your website's style, but it tells Google and shoppers very little. A better feed title usually includes the brand, product type, key attributes and, where helpful, the colour or material: for example, a women's linen wide-leg trousers listing that names the colour.

You can keep creative product names on the website and use more descriptive titles in the feed, as long as the feed accurately describes the product. Many brands use feed rules or a feed tool to build titles from existing product data, which avoids rewriting hundreds of products by hand.

Variants, sizes and stock

Each size and colour combination is normally submitted as a separate item, linked by an item group ID. This lets Google show the right variant and lets shoppers land on the right product page with the correct options selected.

Stock accuracy matters a great deal. Advertising a dress in a size that is sold out wastes money and frustrates shoppers. Make sure availability updates at variant level and that your feed refreshes often enough to reflect stock changes. A clear size guide on the product page also reduces both bounce rates and returns; our guide to e-commerce size guides covers how to present one.

Images that work in Shopping ads

In fashion, the image often decides the click. Google's image policies generally rule out promotional text, watermarks and borders on the main image, and require that the image accurately shows the product being sold. Clean, well-lit images on a plain or simple background usually work best for the main image, with lifestyle shots available as additional images.

Show the actual colour accurately, because colour mismatches are a common reason for returns. Our guide to e-commerce product photography covers how to brief photography for both your site and your ads.

Campaign structure for clothing retailers

A single Shopping or Performance Max campaign covering every product is easy to set up, but it gives you little control. Budget tends to flow to a small number of products, while new arrivals and higher-margin lines may barely show. We usually structure campaigns around business priorities:

  1. Margin. Higher-margin lines can usually support more aggressive bidding.
  2. Bestsellers and stock depth. Products with good availability across sizes deserve more budget than lines with broken size runs.
  3. Season and collection. New season stock, core lines and clearance often need separate targets.
  4. Brand and generic searches. Searches for your brand name behave very differently from generic searches and should be measured separately.

Performance Max is now a common choice for Shopping inventory, and it can work well for fashion with good feeds and conversion data. Our Performance Max guide explains how it works and where standard Shopping campaigns still give useful control.

Returns, delivery and true profitability

Clothing has a higher return rate than many categories, so revenue reported in Google Ads can overstate real profit. We encourage clothing brands to look at returns by product and category, and to set targets based on net revenue where possible. Products with very high return rates may need lower bids, better size information or exclusion from campaigns.

Merchant Center lets you provide shipping and returns information, which can appear in your listings. Make sure delivery charges, delivery times and returns windows in Merchant Center match your website exactly. Clear, fair policies also help conversion; our guide to delivery and returns pages explains how to present them. UK consumer law gives online shoppers certain cancellation and returns rights, so check official guidance or your adviser when writing your policy.

Seasonality and promotions

Fashion demand moves with the seasons, weather and events such as Black Friday, Christmas, summer holidays and back to school. We plan budgets and targets around these peaks, prepare feeds and campaigns before new collections launch, and use Merchant Center promotions where appropriate. Sale prices should be accurate and genuine, and any comparison with a previous price should be one you can support under UK pricing rules.

Your website matters as much as the ad

Shopping ads send shoppers straight to product pages, so those pages need to load quickly, show every image clearly, make size selection easy and display delivery and returns information near the add to basket button. Weak product pages waste ad spend no matter how good the campaign is. FAQs

How does Google Shopping for clothing brands work?

You submit a product feed to Merchant Center with apparel details such as size, colour, gender and age group, then run Shopping or Performance Max campaigns that show your products for relevant searches.

Why are my clothing products disapproved?

Common causes include missing apparel attributes, price or availability mismatches with the website, and images with text overlays or watermarks. Merchant Center diagnostics show the specific issue.

Should each size be a separate product in the feed?

Usually, yes. Each size and colour combination is submitted as its own item and linked by an item group ID, so Google shows the right variant.

Is Performance Max good for fashion brands?

It can be, with a strong feed and reliable conversion tracking. Many brands still use some standard Shopping campaigns for more control over specific products.

How do returns affect Google Shopping performance?

High returns reduce real profit, so judge campaigns on net revenue where possible and lower bids on products that are often returned.

Can I use creative product names in the feed?

Use them on your website if you like, but feed titles work better when they describe the brand, product type, colour and material that shoppers actually search for.
