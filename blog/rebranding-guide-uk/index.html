How to Rebrand a UK Business Without Losing Customers or Rankings

To rebrand a business without losing customers or rankings, set the strategy first, tell customers early and map 301 redirects before any domain change.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with the reason for the rebrand and a clear brand strategy, not a new logo.
  • Tell existing customers early and explain what changes for them and what stays the same.
  • Protect search rankings with a full redirect map, updated listings and checks for weeks after launch.

To rebrand a business without losing customers or rankings, start with a clear reason and brand strategy, tell existing customers early, and plan the technical side, especially 301 redirects, before you change your name, domain or website. Most of the damage from rebrands comes from rushing the launch and skipping the unglamorous steps, not from the new identity itself.

This guide walks through the process, what changes when, how to protect SEO, and a checklist you can use. For help with the strategy itself, see our brand strategy service.

Why rebrand, and when not to

Good reasons to rebrand include a merger, a change in what you sell or who you sell to, a name that causes confusion or legal problems, expansion into new markets, or an identity that no longer reflects the quality of the business. Weaker reasons include boredom with the logo or a new competitor with a fresher look.

Rebrands vary in scale. A refresh updates the visual identity while keeping the name. A full rebrand changes the name, positioning and often the domain. The bigger the change, the more planning it needs.

How to rebrand a business, step by step

1. Define the strategy

Before any design work, agree who you serve, what makes you different, what you want to be known for and how the brand should sound. A short brand strategy document keeps decisions consistent and stops the project turning into a matter of taste. Our guide to brand strategy agencies explains what this stage usually involves.

2. Check the name

If you are changing the name, check availability at Companies House, search the UK trade mark register, and check domains and social handles. Take legal advice on trade marks if the name matters to the business. Remember that a change of company name needs to be registered and reflected on invoices, your website and other official documents.

3. Audit what you have

List every place the current brand appears: website, domain, email signatures, social profiles, Google Business Profile, directories, marketplace listings, packaging, vehicles, signage, contracts and invoices. This audit becomes your launch checklist.

4. Design the identity

Develop the logo, colours, typography, imagery style and tone of voice, guided by the strategy. Test them in real contexts such as the website header, a social post and an invoice, not just on a presentation slide.

5. Plan the website and SEO changes

Decide whether URLs, content or the domain will change. If they will, plan redirects and content updates in detail before launch. More on this below.

6. Tell customers and staff

Explain the change to staff first so they can answer questions. Then tell customers what is changing, why, and what stays the same, such as their account, contact details and service.

7. Launch and monitor

Launch in a planned window, avoiding your busiest season. Monitor search, enquiries and customer feedback closely for at least a few weeks.

Keeping customers through a rebrand

Customers rarely leave because of a new logo. They leave when they are confused, when they think the business has been sold or when they cannot find what they used to buy.

  • Announce the change by email to existing customers before launch, in plain language.
  • Use a "formerly known as" line on your website and profiles for several months.
  • Keep phone numbers and key contact details the same where possible.
  • Update packaging and invoices so customers recognise you when payments go out.
  • Brief staff to answer questions consistently.
  • Watch reviews and social comments for confusion and respond quickly.

Protecting your search rankings

SEO is where rebrands most often go wrong. If the domain or URLs change without proper redirects, years of rankings can drop overnight.

Redirects

Map every important old URL to its closest new equivalent and set permanent 301 redirects. Do not send everything to the new homepage. Keep the redirects in place long term, and keep the old domain registered. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings covers redirect mapping in detail.

Search Console and listings

  • If the domain changes, verify the new domain in Google Search Console and use the change of address tool.
  • Submit the new sitemap.
  • Update your Google Business Profile name only to match the real new name used on signage and documents.
  • Update directories, social profiles and key backlinks where you can.

Brand searches

People will keep searching for the old name for a while. Mention it on your About page and in key places so search engines connect the two names. Consider running brand ads on both names for a short period.

After launch, check for broken links, redirect chains and crawl errors. Our technical SEO audit checklist is useful here, and our SEO service can handle migration checks.

Common rebranding mistakes

  • Starting with the logo. Without an agreed strategy, design rounds turn into debates about taste and the result rarely solves the real problem.
  • Changing everything at once without a plan. Name, domain, website, content and pricing all changing on the same day makes it hard to work out what caused any drop in enquiries.
  • Redirecting every old page to the homepage. This throws away the relevance built up by individual pages and frustrates visitors following old links.
  • Forgetting offline touchpoints. Vans, signage, uniforms, packaging and printed stationery often take longer to change than the website.
  • Launching in peak season. A busy period leaves no room to fix problems and makes any confusion more costly.
  • Not telling customers. A surprise new name on an invoice or bank statement can look like fraud or a takeover.

Most of these are avoided simply by allowing enough time and working through the audit and checklist methodically.

Rebranding checklist

  • Write down why you are rebranding and what success looks like.
  • Agree a brand strategy before design starts.
  • Check name availability, trade marks, domains and social handles.
  • Audit every place the current brand appears.
  • Create the identity and test it in real contexts.
  • Map old URLs to new ones and plan 301 redirects.
  • Brief staff, then tell customers before launch.
  • Update Companies House, invoices, website, profiles and listings.
  • Use Search Console's change of address tool if the domain changes.
  • Monitor rankings, enquiries and feedback for several weeks after launch.

FAQs

How do you rebrand a business without losing customers?

Explain the change early and clearly, keep contact details and service consistent, use a "formerly known as" line for a while, and brief staff so they can answer questions. Confusion, not the new look, is what loses customers.

Will a rebrand affect my Google rankings?

It can, especially if the domain or URLs change. Full 301 redirect mapping, Search Console's change of address tool and updated listings usually limit the impact. A visual refresh on the same URLs carries little SEO risk.

How long does a rebrand take?

A visual refresh can take a few weeks; a full rebrand with a new name and website often takes several months. A small website build typically takes 3 to 5 weeks once the brand is agreed.

Do I need to tell Companies House if I change my business name?

If you change the registered name of a limited company, yes. A trading name used alongside the registered name follows different rules, so check the current guidance or take advice.

Should I keep my old domain after a rebrand?

Yes. Keep it registered and keep redirects in place long term, so old links, bookmarks and emails still reach you.

Is it better to refresh or fully rebrand?

If the name and positioning still work, a refresh is lower risk and cheaper. A full rebrand makes sense when the name or positioning is genuinely holding the business back.
