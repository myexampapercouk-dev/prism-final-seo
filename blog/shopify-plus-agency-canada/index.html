What is Shopify Plus?

Shopify Plus is Shopify's plan for higher-volume and more complex businesses. It runs on the same platform as standard Shopify but adds features aimed at larger teams, such as more control over checkout, more staff accounts, more automation options, extra storefronts and stronger B2B features. Exact features and pricing change over time, so check Shopify's own site for the current plan details.

For many Canadian brands the question is not "Is Plus good?" but "Do we need it yet?" A good agency helps you answer that honestly.

Signs your Canadian brand may need Shopify Plus

  • You run separate stores for Canada, the US or other markets and want more central control.
  • You sell wholesale to retailers as well as direct to consumers and need richer B2B features.
  • Your team has outgrown the staff account limits of standard plans.
  • You want more control over checkout customisation.
  • You rely on automation for tagging orders, managing stock or running promotions.
  • Your order volume makes the Plus fee structure worthwhile, based on Shopify's current pricing.

If only one or two of these apply, a standard Shopify plan with good apps may be enough. We will tell you if we think that is the case.

Shopify Plus migrations for Canadian brands

Most Plus projects start with a migration, either from a standard Shopify plan or from another platform such as Magento, WooCommerce, BigCommerce or a custom system. The migration is where the biggest risks sit, so we plan it carefully.

  1. Audit. We list every product, variant, collection, customer field, integration and URL on the current store.
  2. Mapping. We map old data to Shopify's structure and decide what moves, what is cleaned up and what is retired.
  3. Design and build. We build the theme in a development environment, so your live store keeps trading.
  4. Configuration. We set up markets, currencies, Canadian tax settings you and your accountant confirm, shipping, payments and languages.
  5. Integrations. We connect the systems your team relies on, such as ERP, stock, email and reviews tools, using supported apps and connectors.
  6. Redirects and SEO. We redirect old URLs, carry over titles and descriptions, and check structured data.
  7. Testing. We test checkout, taxes and shipping for addresses across provinces, B2B pricing, tracking and emails.
  8. Launch and monitoring. We switch over at a quiet time and monitor closely after launch.

Our detailed Shopify Plus migration guide covers the same steps from a UK angle, and our WooCommerce to Shopify migration guide covers the most common replatforming route.

Canada-specific setup on Shopify Plus

Larger Canadian brands often sell across provinces, into the US and sometimes further afield. Shopify Plus makes multi-market selling easier, but the setup needs care.

  • Taxes. GST, HST and provincial sales taxes depend on where customers are. We configure the store based on the decisions you and your advisers make; we do not give tax advice.
  • Currencies and markets. Canadian shoppers should see Canadian dollars, US shoppers US dollars, with shipping and duties shown clearly where relevant.
  • Languages. English and French storefronts for shoppers in Quebec and elsewhere. French copy comes from your team or a translator, and Quebec language rules should be checked against official guidance.
  • B2B. Trade price lists, payment terms and company accounts for wholesale buyers, using Shopify's B2B features where your plan includes them.
  • Privacy and email consent. Consent-friendly forms and settings, with compliance decisions resting with you and your advisers.

When headless makes sense

Some Plus brands consider a headless setup, where a custom front end sits on top of Shopify's back end. It can offer more design freedom, but it adds cost and complexity. For most Canadian brands a well-built theme is faster to launch and easier to run. Our explainer on headless commerce sets out the trade-offs.

Working with a remote Shopify Plus agency

Plus projects involve more people than a typical store build. Your team might include an e-commerce manager, a marketing lead, someone from finance, a warehouse or operations contact and sometimes an external ERP or IT provider. Keeping everyone aligned matters more than where the agency sits.

We run Plus projects with a shared project board that lists every task, owner and deadline, so nobody has to chase updates by email. Decisions are recorded in writing, which helps when several departments need to sign off. Video calls are scheduled in the overlap between UK afternoons and Canadian mornings, and we plan launch windows around your quietest trading times rather than our own working hours.

A few practical points help remote Plus projects run smoothly:

  • Name one decision-maker. One person on your side who can approve designs and scope changes keeps the project moving.
  • Share access early. Collaborator access to your current store and any integration dashboards lets us audit properly before planning.
  • Agree a content freeze. During the final migration days, pausing product edits on the old store avoids data getting out of sync.
  • Plan for peak seasons. Avoid launching close to Black Friday, Boxing Day or your own big sales periods.
  • Keep a post-launch list. Small improvements will come up after launch; a shared list makes sure they are handled in order of priority.

Because Shopify runs the hosting, security and checkout infrastructure, there is no server for you or us to maintain. That leaves more of your budget and attention for the work that actually grows sales: better product pages, stronger search visibility and well-run campaigns.
