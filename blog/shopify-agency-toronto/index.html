Shopify Agency for Toronto Businesses: Builds, Migrations and Growth

Looking for a Shopify agency in Toronto? How remote Shopify builds, migrations and SEO work for GTA brands, with prices in pounds, timelines and what to ask.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Shopify agency for a Toronto brand should plan structure, payments, shipping and taxes for Canadian customers before designing the theme.
  • Migrations from WooCommerce or another platform need careful redirects and data mapping so that search visibility and customer records carry over.
  • With Prism, an e-commerce site typically costs £1,500 to £3,500 and takes five to twelve weeks, with Canadian dollar quotes available on request.

A Shopify agency for a Toronto business should plan your store around how Canadian customers browse, pay and receive orders, build a fast and easy-to-manage theme, set up products, shipping and taxes correctly and make sure the store can be found in search from launch. Shopify is a hosted platform and the work is done online, so it can be delivered remotely: Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds Shopify stores for Canadian brands, and we have no office in Toronto or anywhere else in Canada.

This guide explains what a Shopify project involves for Toronto and GTA businesses, how new builds and migrations differ, what to plan for Canadian ecommerce, what our builds cost and the questions to ask any agency.

What a Shopify agency in Toronto should do

Toronto has a large and varied ecommerce scene, from fashion and beauty brands to food and drink producers, homeware, wellness, sporting goods and B2B suppliers selling online. Most good Shopify projects cover the same building blocks:

  • Planning. Collections, navigation, product data and the customer journey mapped before design starts.
  • Theme design and build. A clean, fast theme that suits your brand and is easy for your team to update.
  • Store set-up. Payments, shipping zones, taxes, notifications and policies configured for Canada. Our Shopify store set-up guide covers the settings involved.
  • Apps. A short list of apps chosen for a real need, rather than a long list that slows the store down.
  • SEO foundations. Page titles, structured data, clean URLs and redirects in place at launch.
  • Training and handover. Your team can add products, run sales and edit pages without calling a developer.

Our website and ecommerce development service covers each stage, and our guide to Shopify agencies for Canadian businesses gives the wider national picture.

Planning a Shopify store for Canadian customers

Selling across Canada brings practical details that are easy to overlook. Shipping costs vary sharply between the GTA and remote regions, sales tax rules differ by province, and many customers expect clear delivery and returns information before they buy. Some Toronto brands also sell into the United States, which adds currency, duties and shipping decisions.

New builds, redesigns and migrations

Toronto projects usually fall into one of three types. A new store starts from a blank slate, so the focus is on structure, brand and getting the basics right. A redesign keeps your existing Shopify store and data but replaces or reworks the theme. A migration moves you from another platform, such as WooCommerce, Wix, Squarespace or a custom system.

Migrations need the most care. Products, customers and order history must be mapped correctly, and every old URL that has search value should redirect to its new equivalent. Without that, rankings and links built up over years can be lost. Our WooCommerce to Shopify migration guide explains the process step by step.

Larger brands moving to Shopify Plus have extra options, including more checkout control and automation. Our Shopify Plus migration guide covers when the upgrade makes sense and what is involved.

Shopify SEO from launch

Shopify handles many technical basics well, but stores still need deliberate SEO work. Collection pages often target the most valuable searches, so they need useful descriptions and a clear structure. Product pages need unique descriptions rather than manufacturer copy, and duplicate URLs created by tags and filters should be kept under control.

Our Shopify SEO guide explains the main settings, and our page on ecommerce SEO in Canada covers how to compete for national searches. For Toronto brands with a shop or showroom, local signals such as Google Business Profile matter alongside the online store.

Turning visitors into customers

A store that looks good can still underperform if product information is thin, delivery details are hard to find or checkout feels slow. We pay attention to product photography layout, clear pricing and shipping information, trust signals such as reviews and returns policies and a mobile experience that makes buying quick.

After launch, small tests often bring steady improvements. Our Shopify conversion rate optimisation guide covers the changes that usually matter most.

It also helps to decide early how you will judge success. Agree which numbers matter, such as conversion rate, average order value and repeat purchases, and make sure analytics and Shopify reports are set up to show them from day one. That way, later design or marketing changes can be measured against a clear starting point rather than guesswork.

Accessibility, privacy and French content

Ontario's Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA) sets accessibility requirements for many organisations, which can include websites. We build with accessible patterns such as clear contrast, keyboard navigation and descriptive labels, and our website accessibility guide covers the practical steps. Check the official Ontario guidance to confirm what applies to your business.

Stores collect personal information at checkout, so the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) is relevant, and Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) applies to marketing email. If you sell into Quebec, Law 25 adds provincial privacy requirements. These notes are general information rather than legal advice, so refer to official guidance from the relevant regulators. French store content is written with your team or a professional translator, and we set up the language versions in Shopify.

How a remote Shopify project works

Toronto runs on Eastern Time, so the UK is usually five hours ahead. UK afternoons overlap with Toronto mornings for calls, and you can review designs and staging links whenever it suits you.

  1. Discovery. We agree goals, products, markets, integrations and must-have features.
  2. Structure and design. We plan collections and navigation, then design key templates.
  3. Build. We build the theme, configure settings and load or migrate products.
  4. Testing. We test checkout, shipping, taxes, devices and redirects before launch.
  5. Launch and handover. We go live, monitor the first days and train your team.

Questions to ask any Shopify agency in Toronto

  • How will you plan collections, navigation and product data?
  • How will you handle shipping zones and provincial taxes?
  • Which apps do you recommend, and why each one?
  • How will you protect search visibility during a migration?
  • Will I own the store, theme and all content?
  • What training and support do you provide after launch?

FAQs

How do I choose a Shopify agency in Toronto?

Look for an agency that plans structure and Canadian store settings before design, explains its app choices, protects SEO during migrations and trains your team. Ask to see a clear project plan with stages and responsibilities.

Can a UK agency build a Shopify store for a Toronto business?

Yes. Shopify is hosted online and projects run through shared staging links and calls. Prism has no Toronto office; we work remotely and schedule calls in the overlap between UK afternoons and Toronto mornings.

How much does a Shopify store cost with Prism?

An e-commerce site typically costs £1,500 to £3,500, depending on scope. We can quote in Canadian dollars on request, and Shopify and app subscriptions are paid separately.

How long does a Shopify build take?

Most ecommerce builds take five to twelve weeks, depending on the number of products, custom features and whether data is being migrated from another platform.

Can you migrate my store from WooCommerce to Shopify?

Yes. We map products, customers and orders, set up redirects from old URLs and test thoroughly before launch so that search visibility carries over as fully as possible.

Do I need a French version of my Shopify store?

If you sell to French-speaking customers, especially in Quebec, a French version is worth considering. French content is written with your team or a professional translator, and we configure the languages in Shopify.
