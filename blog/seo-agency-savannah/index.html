How people search in Savannah

Savannah has an unusual mix of searchers. Residents look for everyday services across Chatham County, from plumbers and dentists to accountants and lawyers. Visitors plan trips around the Historic District, River Street, Tybee Island and the city's squares, searching for hotels, tours, restaurants and wedding venues. The port, logistics and manufacturing sectors bring B2B buyers searching for specialist suppliers. Students and staff at the city's colleges add another layer of demand.

That mix means one generic SEO approach rarely fits. A tour company needs to rank for visitor searches made weeks ahead of a trip. A home services firm needs to appear in the map pack when someone in Pooler or Richmond Hill needs help today. A logistics supplier needs detailed pages that answer technical questions from procurement teams.

What a Savannah SEO plan should include

Most effective local SEO plans combine the same core elements, weighted to suit the business:

  • Google Business Profile. Accurate categories, services, opening hours, photos and regular updates, plus a steady flow of genuine customer reviews.
  • Service pages. One detailed page per core service, written around how customers search.
  • Location pages. Pages for areas you genuinely serve, such as Pooler, Richmond Hill, Tybee Island or Bluffton, with real local detail rather than copied text.
  • Technical SEO. Fast pages, clean indexing, sensible internal links, structured data and no broken redirects.
  • Content. Guides and answers that match the questions people ask before they buy.
  • Local citations and links. Consistent listings in relevant directories and links earned from local organisations, events and partners.

SEO for hospitality and tourism businesses

Hotels, inns, tour operators, restaurants and wedding venues make up a large part of Savannah's economy, and they face heavy competition from booking platforms and review sites. SEO helps them win more direct traffic by:

  • Building pages around the specific trips people plan, such as weekend breaks, ghost tours, riverfront dining or historic district weddings.
  • Making sure every room type, package and menu is described clearly on the website, not only on third-party platforms.
  • Using structured data for hotels, restaurants and events so search engines understand the offer.
  • Keeping map listings complete, with accurate photos and seasonal hours.

SEO in the age of AI search

Search is changing. Google now shows AI-generated summaries for many queries, and more people ask tools such as ChatGPT for recommendations. The fundamentals still matter, but content also needs to be easy for AI systems to understand and quote. That means direct answers near the top of the page, clear headings, factual detail and consistent information about your business across the web.

Common SEO mistakes we see on Savannah websites

When we audit local business websites, the same problems come up again and again. Fixing them is often the quickest way to improve visibility:

  • One page trying to cover every service. A single services page rarely ranks for anything specific. Separate pages for each core service give Google a clear match for each search.
  • Thin location pages. Pages that swap only the town name are easy for search engines to spot and rarely help. Each area page needs genuine detail, such as the work you do there and the questions customers in that area ask.
  • Inconsistent business details. Different phone numbers, old addresses or varied business names across directories weaken trust in your listing.
  • Slow mobile pages. Large images, heavy themes and too many plugins slow sites down, especially for visitors on phones walking around the Historic District.
  • No tracking. Without call and form tracking, it is impossible to see which pages and searches bring real enquiries.
  • Seasonal blind spots. Tourism and event businesses often publish seasonal pages too late. Pages for spring festivals, summer trips or holiday events should go live well before people start planning.

None of these fixes is glamorous, but together they often matter more than any single new piece of content. We usually tackle them in the first month or two of a retainer, before building out new pages and content.
