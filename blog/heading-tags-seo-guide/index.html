Heading Tags for SEO: How to Structure the Headings on Your Website

How to use heading tags for SEO: one clear H1, logical H2s and H3s, and wording that helps readers, search engines and AI answers understand your pages.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Use one H1 that states what the page is about, then H2s for the main sections and H3s for points within them.
  • Headings are structure, not styling: never pick a heading level because of how big it looks.
  • Write headings that describe the content beneath them, using the words your customers search with where it reads naturally.

Heading tags are the HTML labels, from H1 to H6, that mark the titles and subtitles on a web page. For SEO, use one H1 that says clearly what the page is about, H2s for each main section and H3s for points inside those sections, written in plain words that match how people search.

This guide is for UK small business owners who edit their own website in WordPress, Shopify, Squarespace or Wix. It covers what headings do, how to structure them, how to word them and how to check and fix the headings on pages you already have.

What heading tags are and why they matter

When you make a line bigger and bolder in your website editor, you may be creating a heading, or you may simply be changing the font. A proper heading tag tells browsers, screen readers and search engines that this line introduces a section. That structure matters for three reasons.

  • Readers scan. Most visitors skim a page before deciding to read. Clear headings let them jump to the part they need, such as prices, areas covered or how to book.
  • Accessibility. People using screen readers often move through a page heading by heading. A logical order makes the page usable for them. Our website accessibility guide covers this in more depth.
  • Search and AI answers. Headings help search engines understand what each part of a page covers. Well-labelled sections are also easier for AI search tools to pick out and quote, which our guide to AI Overviews and SEO explores.

Headings are not a magic ranking switch. Changing an H2 will not lift a weak page to the top. They are part of making a page clear, and clear pages tend to do better over time.

How to use heading tags for SEO

Think of headings as the outline of a document. If you removed everything except the headings, the outline should still make sense.

TagRoleHow manyExample on a roofer's service page
H1The main title of the pageOneRoof Repairs in Bristol
H2Main sectionsSeveralCommon roof problems we fix
H3Points within a sectionAs neededSlipped or broken tiles
H4 to H6Deeper detailRarely neededUsually unnecessary on small business pages

One clear H1 per page

The H1 is the visible title of the page. Most themes create it automatically from the page or post title, so the main thing is to write that title well. It should say plainly what the page offers. For a service page, that usually means the service and, for local businesses, the area. For a blog post, it is the question or topic the post answers.

Search engines can cope with more than one H1, but one is clearer for everyone and easier to manage. A common problem is a theme that wraps the logo or a homepage slider in an H1, so every page ends up with two or three. That is worth fixing in the theme settings or with your developer.

Your H1 and your title tag do different jobs. The title tag appears in search results and browser tabs. The H1 appears on the page. They can be similar, but they do not have to match. Our guide to title tags and meta descriptions explains how to write the search result version.

H2s for the main sections

Each H2 introduces a major part of the page. On a service page, typical H2s might cover what the service includes, the areas you cover, how pricing works, how the process runs and frequently asked questions. On a blog post, they follow the logical steps of the answer.

Keep H2s in a sensible order and avoid skipping levels. An H3 should sit inside an H2 section, not directly after the H1. Skipping levels does not break the page, but it makes the outline confusing for assistive technology and harder to follow.

H3s and below

Use H3s when a section breaks naturally into smaller points, such as individual steps, products or problems. Most small business pages need nothing deeper than H3. If you find yourself reaching for H5, the page may be trying to cover too much and could be split into separate pages.

How to write good headings

  • Describe the content. A heading like Our approach tells nobody anything. How we plan a kitchen fitting says exactly what follows.
  • Use customer language. If people search for boiler service rather than boiler maintenance, use their words. Our keyword research guide shows how to find them.
  • Answer questions directly. Question headings such as How much does a loft conversion cost? work well when the paragraph beneath answers in the first sentence.
  • Keep them short. A heading is a signpost, not a paragraph. Aim for a few words to one line.
  • Avoid stuffing. Repeating the same keyword in every heading reads badly and gives no benefit. Use it where it fits and vary the rest.
  • Make each unique. Two sections with the same heading suggests the page is repeating itself.

Headings on different types of page

Service pages. Lead with an H1 that names the service, then use H2s for what is included, who it suits, process, pricing guidance and FAQs. Our guide to service page design covers the content to include.

Product and category pages. The product or category name is usually the H1. H2s might cover details, sizing, delivery and reviews. Keep tab or accordion labels clear, as they often act as headings too.

Homepages. The H1 should say what your business does and, where relevant, where. A slogan on its own rarely works as an H1. Our guide to homepage design explains how to structure the page around it.

Blog posts. The H1 is the post title. H2s follow the main points, and an FAQ section with question headings helps people and search tools find direct answers.

How to check and fix your headings

  1. View the outline. Most browsers have free extensions that list a page's headings in order. In WordPress, the block editor's document outline shows the same thing.
  2. Check for one H1. If there are none or several, look at the theme first, as it often controls this.
  3. Read the headings alone. Do they tell the story of the page? If not, rewrite the vague ones.
  4. Look for skipped levels and fake headings. Change bold paragraphs that act as section titles into proper headings.
  5. Prioritise key pages. Start with your homepage, main service pages and best-performing posts rather than every page at once.

If you are planning a wider review, headings fit naturally into a technical SEO audit alongside titles, internal links and indexing.

FAQs

How do I use heading tags for SEO?

Use one H1 that clearly states the page topic, H2s for each main section and H3s for points within those sections. Word them so they describe the content beneath, using the terms your customers search for where it reads naturally.

Can a page have more than one H1?

Technically yes, and search engines can handle it. One H1 is still the clearest approach for readers, screen readers and anyone maintaining the site, so it is the sensible default.

Should my H1 match my title tag?

It does not have to. The title tag is written for search results and the H1 for people already on the page. They are often similar, and both should describe the same topic.

Do heading tags directly improve rankings?

Headings help search engines understand a page, but they are one small part of a much bigger picture. Good content, links and a technically sound site matter far more. Clear headings support all of that.

Is it bad to skip from H2 to H4?

It will not break the page, but it creates a confusing outline for screen reader users. Keep levels in order and change the visual size in your theme if a heading looks too large.

How many H2s should a page have?

There is no set number. Use one for each main section of the page. A short service page might have four or five, while a long guide could have ten or more.
