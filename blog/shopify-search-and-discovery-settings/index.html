Shopify Search and Discovery: Setting Up Search That Helps Shoppers

How to improve Shopify's built-in search with synonyms, filters, product boosts and better product data, so shoppers find the right items faster on your store.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify's built-in search relies heavily on the quality of your product titles, types, tags and descriptions.
  • Synonyms, filters and product boosts help shoppers find products using their own words.
  • Reviewing what people search for, and what returns nothing, shows where to improve next.

Shoppers who use a store's search bar usually know roughly what they want. If the results are irrelevant or empty, they are likely to give up. Shopify includes built-in search and filtering, and Shopify's own Search and Discovery app adds controls for synonyms, filters, product boosts and recommendations. Used well, these tools make it much easier for customers to find the right product.

This guide explains how Shopify search works, how to configure the main settings, and how to use your product data and search reports to keep improving results. It focuses on Shopify's own tools rather than third-party options.

How Shopify's built-in search works

When a shopper types into your store's search box, Shopify looks for matches across product information such as titles, product types, vendors, tags, variant details and descriptions. Depending on your theme, it may also return pages, blog posts and collections. Many themes include predictive search, which shows suggestions as the shopper types.

Because search draws on your product data, the single biggest factor in result quality is usually how clearly and consistently your products are described. Search settings can refine results, but they cannot fix missing or vague information.

Start with your product data

Before changing any settings, review how products are described:

  • Titles. Use plain, descriptive names that include what the item is. A title such as a brand-led nickname alone gives search little to work with.
  • Product type and vendor. Fill these in consistently across the catalogue.
  • Tags. Use tags to capture attributes and terms shoppers might search for, but keep them organised rather than adding endless variations.
  • Variant options. Name options such as size, colour and material clearly and consistently.
  • Descriptions. Write natural descriptions that mention materials, uses and key features.

Clear catalogue organisation helps browsing as well as searching. Our guide to Shopify collections structure covers that side.

Using synonyms

Customers do not always use the same words you do. Someone might search for trainers while your products are labelled sneakers, or search for a sofa when you sell couches. Synonym groups tell Shopify search to treat related terms as equivalent, so a search for one word also returns products containing another.

Good synonym groups come from real evidence:

  • Search terms in your reports that return few or no results.
  • Questions customers ask by email or chat.
  • Regional differences, such as British and American spellings or names.
  • Trade terms versus everyday language.

Keep groups tight. Linking words that are only loosely related can make results less relevant. A search for boots should not necessarily return every type of shoe.

Setting up filters

Filters let shoppers narrow search results and collection pages by attributes such as availability, price, product type, vendor, variant options, tags and metafields. In Shopify's Search and Discovery app, you choose which filters appear and in what order, and your theme displays them.

Some practical tips:

  • Offer filters that match how customers make decisions, such as size, colour or material.
  • Avoid too many filters. A long list can overwhelm rather than help.
  • Make sure the underlying data is complete. A colour filter is only useful if every product has its colour recorded consistently.
  • Use metafields for attributes that do not fit standard fields, such as fabric weight or compatibility.
  • Check how filters look and behave on mobile, where space is limited.

Boosting products

Product boosts let you promote particular products higher in search results for specific search terms. This is useful when several products match a query but one is the most relevant, best stocked or most helpful to show first.

Use boosts sparingly. Pushing products that do not truly match what the shopper asked for can damage trust. Review boosts regularly, especially when stock runs low or ranges change.

Product recommendations

Shopify can show related and complementary products on product pages, depending on your theme. Its Search and Discovery app lets you set complementary products manually, such as suggesting a case alongside a phone accessory, and adjust related products where automatic suggestions are not helpful.

Thoughtful recommendations help discovery beyond search, guiding customers to items they might not have thought to look for.

Predictive search and theme settings

Predictive search behaviour depends on your theme. Check your theme settings to see whether predictive search is enabled and what types of results it shows, such as products, collections, pages and articles. Test it on desktop and mobile with realistic queries, including misspellings and partial words.

The design of the search results page matters too. Clear product images, prices and availability, plus visible filters and sorting, help shoppers compare options quickly.

Handling no-results searches

Searches that return nothing are valuable feedback. They might reveal products you do not stock, terms you have not used, or spelling variations. For each common no-results search, decide whether to:

  • Add a synonym that maps the term to existing products.
  • Update product titles, tags or descriptions to include the term naturally.
  • Consider whether there is demand for a product you do not sell yet.

Also make sure your no-results page is helpful. Suggesting popular collections, offering a way to contact you, and checking spelling all help keep the shopper on the site.

Using search reports

Shopify's analytics can show the terms people search for on your store, along with searches that return no results or lead to few clicks. Reviewing these regularly is one of the most useful habits for improving search. Look for patterns rather than reacting to every single query.

Combine search data with other evidence, such as product page views and customer questions, to build a fuller picture of what shoppers are looking for. Our broader guide to ecommerce site search covers general principles that apply on any platform.

Common mistakes

  • Relying on settings while product titles and tags remain vague or inconsistent.
  • Adding too many broad synonyms that clutter results.
  • Offering filters with incomplete data behind them.
  • Forgetting to test search on mobile.
  • Setting boosts once and never reviewing them.

A simple review routine

A light, regular routine works well for most stores. Check the search terms report, note any no-results searches, add or adjust synonyms, fix product data where needed, and test a handful of common searches yourself. When you launch new ranges, check that filters and tags cover the new products too.

Sorting options

Alongside filters, sorting lets shoppers reorder results by criteria such as relevance, price or date added. Relevance is usually the right default for search results, because it reflects how closely products match the query. Collection pages may suit a different default, such as featured or best selling, depending on your range. Check what your theme offers and make sure the default sort order on each collection makes sense for shoppers.

Search across pages and content

Search is not only about products. Shoppers may search for delivery information, returns, size guides or care instructions. If your theme includes pages and articles in search results, make sure those pages have clear titles and content so they appear when relevant. A shopper who quickly finds the returns policy through search is less likely to contact support or abandon a purchase.

Equally, check that pages you would rather not surface, such as old campaign pages or thin placeholder content, are tidied up so they do not clutter results.

Using metafields to power discovery

Metafields let you store extra structured information about products, such as material, fit, compatibility or care details. When this information is held in metafields rather than buried in descriptions, it can be used for filters and displayed consistently across product pages. Planning metafields early makes it much easier to add useful filters later.

Start with the attributes that genuinely influence buying decisions. Adding dozens of fields that nobody maintains will create gaps and inconsistencies, which undermines the filters built on them.

Testing search like a shopper

Settings and reports are useful, but nothing replaces trying the search yourself. Put together a short list of realistic queries, including:

  • Exact product names.
  • General category terms, such as a product type.
  • Descriptive searches combining attributes, such as a colour and a product type.
  • Common misspellings.
  • Alternative names customers use.

Run through them on mobile and desktop and note anything surprising. Ask a colleague or friend unfamiliar with the catalogue to try too, as they will search in ways you might not expect.

FAQs

Does Shopify include search by default?

Yes. Shopify stores include built-in search, and many themes add predictive search. Shopify's own Search and Discovery app adds further controls such as synonyms, filters and boosts.

What are search synonyms in Shopify?

Synonyms are groups of words that search treats as equivalent, so a shopper searching for one term also sees products described with another.

Why are my filters not showing?

Filters depend on both your settings and your theme's support for them. Check that filters are enabled, that your theme supports them, and that products have the relevant data filled in.

Can I control which products appear first in search?

Product boosts let you promote chosen products for particular search terms. Use them carefully so results stay relevant.

How often should I review search settings?

A regular review, plus checks whenever you add new ranges, keeps search working well. The right frequency depends on how often your catalogue changes.
