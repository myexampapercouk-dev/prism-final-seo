WordPress or Shopify?

We build on two platforms, and the right choice depends on what your website needs to do.

WordPress suits service businesses, content-led sites and companies that publish regularly. It is flexible, widely supported and good for SEO when set up properly. Our guide to WordPress for small business websites explains what to expect.

Shopify suits businesses selling products online. It handles payments, inventory, shipping settings and checkout in one place, and it scales well as you grow. If you sell across the US, our guide for Shopify brands in the United States covers the platform in a US context.

If you are unsure, our comparison of Shopify and WooCommerce sets out the trade-offs clearly. The comparison is written for the UK, but the platform differences are the same in Utah.

Designing for local search in Utah

A new website is a chance to fix the structure that local SEO relies on. For businesses serving customers across the valley, we usually plan:

  • A clear page for each main service, rather than one page listing everything.
  • Area pages for the places you genuinely serve, written with real detail rather than swapped town names.
  • Consistent business name, address and phone details in the footer and contact page.
  • Local business structured data so search engines understand who you are.
  • Links between services, areas and your contact page so visitors and search engines can move around easily.

Our local SEO guide covers this in more depth. If you want ongoing SEO after launch, our SEO and GEO service picks up where the build leaves off.

Landing pages and conversion

A homepage cannot do every job. If you run Google Ads or social campaigns, dedicated landing pages usually perform better because they match the ad, focus on one offer and remove distractions. Our landing page design guide explains the structure we use.

On every site we build, we look at the path a visitor takes from arriving to getting in touch. That includes where calls to action sit, how long forms are, whether phone numbers are tappable on mobile and whether booking or checkout steps are as short as they can be.

We also set up analytics so you can see what is working. Our GA4 setup guide shows the events and conversions we track as standard.

Speed and technical quality

Website speed affects both visitors and rankings. We keep sites fast by choosing lightweight themes, limiting plugins and apps to those you really need, compressing images and testing pages on mobile connections before launch.

We check Core Web Vitals, fix layout shifts and make sure the most important content loads first. Our guide to Core Web Vitals explains what these measures mean in plain terms.

What to prepare before your project starts

Website projects move fastest when the right material is ready early. Content is the most common cause of delay, so it helps to start gathering it before the design stage begins. Before kick-off, we usually ask Salt Lake City clients to pull together:

  • A list of services or products, with a short note on which ones matter most to the business.
  • The areas you serve, such as the city itself, the south valley, Davis County or Utah County, so we can plan location pages honestly.
  • Your logo and brand files, including colors and fonts if you have them.
  • Photos of your team, premises, vehicles or work, since real images usually build more trust than stock photos.
  • Logins for your domain, hosting, current website, Google Business Profile and analytics.
  • Examples of websites you like, and a few notes on why.

Do not worry if some of this is missing. We can write new copy from a structured questionnaire and a call, and we can plan the site around the photos you have now and add more later.

Common mistakes we fix on Utah websites

When we review existing websites, the same issues come up again and again. Each one is fixable, and most cost less to fix during a rebuild than to patch on an old site:

  • Vague headlines that do not say what the business does or where it works.
  • Contact details hidden at the bottom of long pages or behind a menu on mobile.
  • One page for every service, which makes it hard to rank for any of them.
  • Heavy page builders and sliders that slow pages down without adding much.
  • Old pages deleted without redirects, losing rankings and links built over years.
  • No tracking, so the business cannot tell which pages or campaigns bring enquiries.

If you are planning a redesign, we will review your current site first and tell you what is worth keeping. Many older sites have pages with useful rankings, and protecting those during a rebuild is one of the most valuable things a web designer can do.

Legal and practical notes

Please check official guidance and speak to your own adviser about what applies to your business. For online stores, sales tax settings should be confirmed with your accountant before launch.
