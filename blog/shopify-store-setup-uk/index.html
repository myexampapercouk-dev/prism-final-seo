Shopify Store Setup UK: How to Set Up a Store That Is Ready to Sell

Shopify store setup UK guide: settings, VAT, shipping, payments, products, theme, legal pages, SEO basics and pre-launch checks for UK retailers.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Get core settings right first: business details, currency, VAT, shipping and payments.
  • Clear product pages, delivery information and legal policies build trust with UK shoppers.
  • Set up analytics, SEO basics and test orders before you announce your launch.

Shopify store setup in the UK involves more than picking a theme and adding products. To trade properly you need the right business, tax, shipping and payment settings, clear product information, legal policies UK shoppers expect and the basics of SEO and tracking in place. Getting these right before launch saves time and avoids problems later.

This guide is for UK businesses launching a new Shopify store, whether starting out or moving from another platform. If you are still choosing a platform, read our Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison. For costs, see our Shopify website cost guide.

Core store settings

  • Business details. Add your legal business name, address and contact email.
  • Currency and markets. Set GBP as your store currency and decide whether to sell internationally.
  • Staff accounts. Give team members their own logins with appropriate permissions.
  • Domain. Connect your own domain rather than relying on the default store address.

VAT and taxes

If you are VAT registered, configure Shopify's tax settings so prices and invoices reflect UK VAT correctly, and decide whether prices display inclusive of VAT, as UK consumers usually expect. Selling abroad brings extra considerations around duties and overseas tax. Tax rules are specific to your business, so check with an accountant. This guide is not tax advice.

Shipping and delivery

Set up shipping zones and rates that reflect how you actually deliver, whether flat rate, weight based or free over a threshold. Be clear about delivery times, couriers and any areas with different rates, such as the Scottish Highlands or Northern Ireland. Unclear or surprising delivery costs are a common reason shoppers abandon a basket.

Payments

Choose your payment provider and enable the methods your customers expect, such as cards and popular digital wallets. Check payout schedules and test the checkout fully. Our pricing page covers how we work, and Shopify's own site explains its plans.

Products and collections

Good product data is the foundation of a good store. For each product, add a clear title, a helpful description written for your customer, quality images, accurate variants, SKUs and stock levels. Group products into collections that match how people shop. Writing unique descriptions rather than copying manufacturer text helps both shoppers and search engines.

Theme and design

Pick a theme that suits your catalogue size and brand, then customise it so key information is easy to find: navigation, search, delivery and returns details and contact options. Our website and ecommerce development service explains your options.

Legal pages and policies

UK shoppers expect clear terms of service, a privacy policy, a returns and refunds policy and shipping information. Consumer rights, including cancellation rights for online purchases, apply to most sales to consumers. Make sure your cookie consent works properly. Take legal advice where you are unsure; this guide is not legal advice.

SEO basics from day one

  • Write unique page titles and meta descriptions for key pages.
  • Use descriptive, readable URLs for products and collections.
  • Add alt text to product images.
  • Verify your store in Google Search Console and submit your sitemap.
  • Create helpful collection descriptions and guide content.

Our Shopify SEO guide and ecommerce SEO checklist go further.

Analytics and tracking

Set up analytics and conversion tracking before launch so you can see where sales come from. Connect any advertising accounts you plan to use and check events fire on purchase. Make sure tracking respects cookie consent.

Pre-launch checklist

  • Place test orders with each payment and shipping option.
  • Check order and shipping notification emails.
  • Browse the store on mobile and desktop.
  • Check speed on key pages, using our website speed optimisation guide.
  • Proofread product pages and policies.
  • Remove the store password when you are ready to go live.

Navigation and site structure

Plan your menus around how customers shop, not how your stock room is organised. A clear main menu with a manageable number of top-level categories, supported by collection pages and filters, makes browsing easier. Include links to delivery, returns, contact and FAQ pages in the footer so they are always easy to find. Make sure search is visible, especially on mobile.

Email notifications and customer accounts

Shopify sends customers order confirmations, shipping updates and other notifications. Customise these with your branding and check the wording reflects your actual delivery and returns process. Decide whether customers can check out as guests, create accounts or both. Guest checkout reduces friction for first-time buyers.

Apps: add only what you need

The Shopify App Store offers tools for reviews, email marketing, subscriptions, product options and much more. It is tempting to install many apps at launch, but each one adds complexity and can slow your store. Start with your theme's built-in features and add apps only to solve clear problems. Review costs and permissions before installing anything.

Marketing channels to plan for

A launched store still needs visitors. Think about which channels suit your products and budget:

  • Organic search, built through good product content, collection pages and guides.
  • Email marketing, starting with a sign-up form and welcome message.
  • Paid search and shopping ads, which can drive sales while organic visibility grows.
  • Social media, useful for products that show well visually.

Our ecommerce marketing guide covers these channels in more detail.

After launch

Use your first weeks to watch how shoppers behave. Check which products get viewed but not bought, where visitors leave and which searches return no results. Fix obvious problems quickly and gather customer feedback. Keep your theme and apps updated and review your settings as your business grows.

Product photography and descriptions

Online shoppers cannot touch or try your products, so images and descriptions do the work a shop assistant would. Use consistent, well-lit photography on a clean background, with additional images showing scale, detail and the product in use. Keep image dimensions consistent so collection pages look tidy.

Write descriptions that answer the questions customers actually ask: sizes, materials, dimensions, care instructions, compatibility and what is included. Short paragraphs and bullet points are easier to scan on a phone. If you sell many similar products, use metafields to show specifications in a consistent format.

Accessibility and trust

Make sure text is readable, buttons are clearly labelled and images have alt text. An accessible store is easier for everyone to use. Add trust signals that are genuine: real contact details, clear policies, secure payment options and, once you have them, honest customer reviews.

Common setup mistakes

  • Launching on the default store address instead of your own domain.
  • Getting VAT display or tax settings wrong.
  • Hiding delivery costs until checkout.
  • Copying manufacturer descriptions word for word.
  • Installing lots of apps before you know you need them.

FAQs

Can I set up a Shopify store myself?

Yes, many businesses do. Support can help with design, product data, SEO and technical setup if you want a more polished launch.

Do I need to be VAT registered to sell on Shopify?

That depends on your business and turnover, not on Shopify. Check current HMRC rules or speak to an accountant.

What pages does a UK Shopify store need?

At minimum, product and collection pages, contact details, terms, privacy, returns and shipping information.

Should I connect my own domain?

Yes. A custom domain looks more trustworthy and supports your brand and SEO.

How do I get my new store found on Google?

Set up Search Console, submit your sitemap, write unique content and build visibility over time through SEO and, if suitable, paid ads.

How many apps should a new Shopify store have?

There is no right number. Start with your theme's built-in features and add apps only when they solve a clear problem, checking speed and cost each time.

Should I offer free delivery?

Many UK shoppers like it, but it must work for your margins. A free delivery threshold is a common middle ground. Whatever you choose, show it clearly.

Can I sell internationally from day one?

You can, but it adds complexity around shipping, duties, tax and returns. Many stores start with the UK and expand once operations are running smoothly.

Do I need a returns policy?

Yes. UK consumers have legal rights when buying online, and a clear returns policy explains how you handle them. Take advice if unsure of your obligations.
