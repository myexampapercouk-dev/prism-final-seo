Why SEO in Geneva needs its own plan

Geneva is a small city with an international footprint. Searches come from local residents, commuters from neighbouring France, international organisations and their staff, private banking and trading firms, and visitors arriving for conferences and short stays. That mix means a single keyword list rarely works. Someone searching in French for a local service behaves very differently from an English-speaking expat comparing providers, or a procurement team abroad looking for a specialist firm.

Three things shape most Geneva SEO plans:

  • Language: French dominates local search, but English is common for professional services, relocation, education and hospitality. Many sites need both, and some need German for the rest of Switzerland.
  • Geography: Google results set to Switzerland differ from results in France, even for searchers a few kilometres apart. Cross-border businesses have to decide which market each page targets.
  • Competition: professional services, finance and luxury sectors attract strong, established competitors, so content quality and technical health matter more than volume.

French and English SEO for Geneva websites

A bilingual site needs more than translated pages. Each language version should target the terms people really search in that language, and Google needs clear signals about which page serves which audience. We set up language folders or subdomains, hreflang tags, translated metadata and separate keyword research for each version. Our guide to multilingual website design covers the structure in more detail.

Because we write in English, the usual workflow is simple. We research French keywords and brief each page, your translator or in-house team writes or adapts the French copy, and we then check the published page for keyword use, headings, internal links and technical tags. English pages we can write and optimise directly.

If you also sell into France, Germany or further afield, the plan becomes an international one. Our page on international SEO explains how we decide between country folders, language folders and separate domains.

Local SEO for Geneva businesses

For clinics, law firms, consultancies, restaurants, schools and trades, the local map results often bring more enquiries than standard organic listings. Good local SEO in Geneva usually means:

  • A complete Google Business Profile with the right categories, opening hours, services and photos.
  • Consistent name, address and phone details across your website and the directories your sector uses.
  • Location and service pages that mention the districts and nearby communes you genuinely serve.
  • A steady flow of genuine reviews, requested in a way that follows Google's rules.

Our local SEO guide is written for UK businesses, but the same principles apply to Geneva and other Swiss cities.

Technical SEO and site performance

Many Geneva websites look polished but have hidden problems: language versions that point at each other incorrectly, slow image-heavy pages, missing structured data or old URLs that were never redirected after a redesign. These issues quietly hold rankings back.

We fix the basics first. That means clean indexation, correct canonical tags, fast loading on mobile and sensible internal linking. Page speed matters for rankings and for conversions, and our guide to Core Web Vitals explains the metrics Google uses. Common SEO mistakes we see on Geneva websites

A few problems come up again and again when we audit sites for Swiss and cross-border businesses:

  • Machine-translated pages left unedited: they read poorly, rarely match real search terms and can weaken trust with French-speaking buyers.
  • One page trying to serve every audience: mixing French and English on the same URL, or targeting Switzerland and France at once, confuses both searchers and Google.
  • Thin service pages: a single short paragraph per service gives Google little to rank and gives buyers little reason to call.
  • No tracking of enquiries: without form, call and booking tracking, it is hard to tell which pages actually bring in work.
  • Redesigns without redirects: a new site launched without mapping old URLs can lose years of rankings in a few weeks.

Most of these can be fixed within the first few months of a retainer, and fixing them often delivers progress before any new content is written.
