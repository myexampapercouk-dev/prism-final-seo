How to Add Negative Keywords in Google Ads: A Practical UK Guide

Negative keywords stop your Google Ads showing for irrelevant searches. Learn how to find, add and organise them so a small UK budget goes further.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Negative keywords block searches you do not want to pay for, such as jobs, free or DIY queries.
  • Find them in the search terms report and add them at the right level: campaign, ad group or shared list.
  • Review search terms regularly, because new irrelevant queries appear as campaigns run.

To add negative keywords in Google Ads, open the search terms report, spot queries that are not relevant to your business, and add them as negatives at the campaign, ad group or shared list level. This stops your ads appearing for those searches, so less of your budget goes on clicks that were never going to become customers.

For a UK small business with a modest daily budget, this is one of the most useful habits you can build. This guide explains what negative keywords are, the match types, where to add them and how to build a starter list. Google updates the Ads interface regularly, so check the current Google Ads help pages for exact menu names and any recent changes to how negatives behave.

What negative keywords do

Your normal keywords tell Google which searches you want to appear for. Negative keywords do the opposite. If you sell new kitchen worktops and someone searches for kitchen worktop jobs, kitchen worktop repair or free kitchen design software, you probably do not want to pay for that click.

Because broad and phrase match keywords can match a wide range of related searches, negatives act as the guard rails. Without them, campaigns tend to drift into loosely related queries. With a sensible list, your budget concentrates on people who are closer to buying. Our Google Ads guide for small businesses covers the wider set-up if you are starting from scratch.

Negative keyword match types

Negative keywords use match types too, but they behave differently from normal keywords. In particular, negatives generally do not expand to cover close variants such as plurals or misspellings, so you may need to add those separately. Check the current help page to confirm.

Negative match typeHow it is writtenWhat it blocksExample
Negative broadjobsSearches that contain all the words, in any orderBlocks plumber jobs leeds
Negative phrase"second hand"Searches containing the words in that exact orderBlocks second hand sofas, not hand made second sofa
Negative exact[cheap sofas]Only that exact search, with no extra wordsBlocks cheap sofas, not cheap sofas uk

For most small businesses, single-word negative broad terms such as jobs, salary, course, free and diy do most of the heavy lifting. Use phrase and exact negatives when you need to be more careful, for example when a word is irrelevant in one context but valuable in another.

How to add negative keywords in Google Ads

The exact clicks change over time, but the process usually looks like this:

  1. Open the search terms report for a campaign. This shows the actual searches that triggered your ads.
  2. Sort by cost or clicks so you deal with the most expensive irrelevant searches first.
  3. Tick the irrelevant terms and choose the option to add them as negative keywords.
  4. Choose the level: the ad group, the campaign, or a shared negative keyword list.
  5. Edit the term before saving if needed. Often you want to block one word, such as jobs, rather than the full search.
  6. Save and note the date, so you can check later whether the change helped.

You can also add negatives directly through the keywords area of a campaign without using the report, which is useful when building a starter list before launch.

Choosing the right level

Where you add a negative matters as much as what you add.

  • Ad group level: use when a term is irrelevant to one group but fine for another in the same campaign.
  • Campaign level: use when a term never fits that campaign.
  • Shared lists: use for terms that never fit your business at all, such as jobs or careers, and apply the list to every relevant campaign.
  • Account level: Google has offered account-level negatives for some campaign types. Check the help pages for what is currently supported.

Shared lists save a lot of time. Create a few themed lists, such as employment terms, free and DIY terms, and wrong locations, and apply them as campaigns are added.

Before launching a new campaign, brainstorm searches that share your words but not your intent. Common themes for UK service and retail businesses include:

Negatives in Performance Max and Shopping

Campaign types that use automation, such as Performance Max, handle negative keywords differently from standard Search campaigns, and the options have changed over time. Some controls may be available at account level or through brand exclusions. Check the current help pages before relying on any one method. Our Performance Max guide and Google Shopping ads guide explain how those campaigns work more generally.

For Shopping, negatives are particularly useful for stopping generic or wrong-product searches, such as blocking repair or parts if you only sell complete items.

A simple review routine

Negative keywords are never finished. New searches appear constantly, especially with broad match. A routine that suits most small accounts:

  1. Weekly in the first month of a new campaign, review the search terms report and add negatives.
  2. Fortnightly or monthly once things settle down.
  3. Quarterly, review your negative lists for anything blocking searches you now want, for example after adding a new service.

Keep a simple log of what you added and why. It helps if someone else takes over the account later, and it is one of the first things we look at in a PPC audit.

Reading the search terms report well

The search terms report can be long, so focus your time. Sort by cost first, then look for patterns rather than single odd searches. If you see several queries containing the word repair and you only sell new items, add repair as one broad negative rather than blocking each search separately.

Also check searches that look relevant but never convert over a reasonable period. They might not need blocking, but they could point to a landing page that does not answer the question, or an ad that promises something different. That is where conversion tracking becomes essential, because without it you are judging relevance by guesswork alone.

Google may hide some lower-volume search terms for privacy reasons, so the report will not show every query. Work with the data you have and do not worry about the gaps.

Common mistakes

  • Blocking your own keywords by adding a negative that overlaps a term you are bidding on. Google may flag conflicts; check for these.
  • Adding full long searches as exact negatives, which only blocks that one rare query.
  • Forgetting plurals and variants, since negatives may not expand to close variants.
  • Using negatives instead of fixing structure, when a campaign really needs tighter ad groups or better landing pages.
  • Never looking at the report, which is the most common problem of all.

FAQs

How do I add negative keywords in Google Ads?

Open the search terms report for a campaign, select irrelevant searches and choose the option to add them as negatives, picking the ad group, campaign or a shared list. You can also add them directly in the keywords area. Check the current help pages for exact menu names.

How many negative keywords should I have?

There is no ideal number. A new account might start with a few dozen common terms and grow the list over time from real search data. Relevance matters more than volume.

Do negative keywords block plurals and misspellings?

Generally, negatives do not automatically cover close variants in the way normal keywords can, so add important plurals and common misspellings separately. Confirm the current behaviour in Google's help pages.

What is a negative keyword list?

It is a shared set of negatives you can apply to several campaigns at once. It saves time and keeps blocking consistent across your account.

Can negative keywords hurt my campaign?

Yes, if you block terms that bring in good customers. Review lists regularly and check for conflicts with the keywords you are bidding on.

How often should I check search terms?

Weekly for new campaigns, then fortnightly or monthly once results settle. Higher-spend accounts usually need more frequent checks.
