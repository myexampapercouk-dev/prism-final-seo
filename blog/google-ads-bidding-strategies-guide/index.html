Google Ads Bidding Strategies Explained: Which One Should You Use?

Google Ads bidding strategies explained: manual CPC, Maximise Clicks, Maximise Conversions, target CPA and target ROAS, and how a small business can choose.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Your bidding strategy tells Google what to optimise for, so it should match your real goal: clicks, leads or revenue.
  • Automated bidding only works well with accurate conversion tracking and enough conversion data to learn from.
  • Change strategies gradually, give each one time to settle, and judge results on business outcomes rather than clicks.

Google Ads bidding strategies decide how much you bid in each auction and what Google tries to get for your money. Most small businesses start with Maximise Clicks or Maximise Conversions, then move to a target cost per acquisition (target CPA) or target return on ad spend (target ROAS) once conversion tracking is reliable and there is enough data for the system to learn from.

This guide explains the main Google Ads bidding strategies in plain English, when each makes sense for a UK small business, and how to switch without wrecking performance. Google renames and changes its options from time to time, so check the current Google Ads help pages for the exact names and settings available in your account.

What a bidding strategy actually does

Every time someone searches, Google runs an auction among advertisers whose keywords match. Your bid, the quality of your ad and landing page, and the context of the search all affect whether your ad shows and what you pay. A bidding strategy is the set of rules that decides your bid in each of those auctions.

With manual bidding, you set the maximum you are willing to pay per click. With automated, often called Smart Bidding, Google sets the bid for each auction using signals such as device, location, time of day and the search itself. Automation can react far faster than a person, but it can only optimise towards what you tell it to value. That is why the goal you pick matters more than the mechanics. Start with your goal, not the strategy

Before picking a strategy, be clear about what success looks like. A plumber may want phone calls and quote requests. A clinic may want booking requests. An online shop wants profitable sales. Each of those maps to a different strategy.

Next, check that your conversion tracking captures those actions accurately and only once. Automated strategies learn from conversion data, so if tracking counts page views as leads or double-counts purchases, Google will optimise towards the wrong thing with great efficiency. Our guide to Google Ads conversion tracking shows how to set this up properly before you rely on Smart Bidding.

A sensible path for a small business

  1. New account, no conversion data. Many advertisers start with Maximise Clicks with a maximum cost-per-click limit, or with Maximise Conversions if tracking is in place from day one. Keep keyword lists tight and add negatives early.
  2. Tracking working, some conversions. Move to Maximise Conversions so Google focuses on leads rather than clicks.
  3. Steady conversion volume. Once you have a consistent flow of conversions and know what a lead is worth, consider adding a target CPA set close to your recent average cost per conversion.
  4. Ecommerce with reliable values. Use Maximise Conversion Value, then add a target ROAS based on your margins and recent performance.

Google publishes guidance on how much conversion data each strategy works best with, and these recommendations change, so check the current help pages rather than relying on old rules of thumb.

Setting realistic targets

A target that is far tighter than your current performance usually leads Google to bid very cautiously, and your ads may barely show. A target that is too loose can spend budget on expensive conversions. Start near what you are achieving now, then adjust in small steps of perhaps ten to twenty per cent, giving each change time to settle before judging it.

Work out what you can afford to pay. For lead generation, think about how many enquiries become customers and what a customer is worth over time. For ecommerce, factor in product margins, delivery costs and VAT, not just revenue. Our article on Google Ads costs in the UK explains the wider budget picture.

The learning period and how to handle it

When you switch to an automated strategy or make a big change to targets or budget, Google typically shows the campaign as learning for a while. Performance can wobble during this time. Avoid making several changes at once, and try not to judge a new strategy on its first few days. Plan strategy changes for quieter periods rather than just before a busy season such as Black Friday or Christmas.

Bidding does not fix everything

Bidding strategy is one lever among many. Poor keyword choices, missing negatives, weak ads and slow landing pages will hold any strategy back. Before blaming bidding, check:

  • Your search terms report for irrelevant searches, which our negative keywords guide explains how to block.
  • Your location settings, covered in our location targeting guide.
  • Your landing pages, which need to load quickly and make the next step obvious. See our advice on landing page design.
  • Your budget. If a campaign is limited by budget, raising targets will not create more volume.

Performance Max and Shopping campaigns use their own set of bidding options. Our Performance Max guide covers how those work.

Portfolio strategies and bid limits

Some advertisers share one strategy across several campaigns, known as a portfolio bid strategy. This pools conversion data, which can help small accounts where individual campaigns have too few conversions to learn from on their own. It also means one target covers campaigns that may have quite different economics, so group only campaigns with similar goals and values.

Certain strategies let you set maximum or minimum bid limits. These can stop runaway costs in very competitive auctions, but limits that are too tight can restrict the strategy so much that it cannot do its job. Use them as a safety net, review them regularly, and remove them if they are clearly holding back volume you can afford.

Common bidding mistakes

  • Using Smart Bidding with broken tracking. Automation magnifies tracking errors.
  • Optimising for micro-conversions. Counting page views or button clicks as primary conversions trains Google to find cheap, low-value actions.
  • Changing strategies too often. Each switch resets learning.
  • Unrealistic targets. Very aggressive targets can stop campaigns from spending at all.
  • Ignoring offline outcomes. If most leads close by phone, import qualified leads or sales where possible so Google learns which clicks lead to real business.

FAQs

Which Google Ads bidding strategy is best for a small business?

There is no single best option. Many small businesses start with Maximise Clicks or Maximise Conversions, then move to target CPA or target ROAS once tracking is accurate and conversion volume is steady.

What are Google Ads bidding strategies explained simply?

They are the rules that decide how much you bid in each auction. Manual strategies let you set bids yourself, while automated ones let Google set bids to get as many clicks, conversions or as much value as possible.

Is manual CPC still worth using?

It can be for very small or niche accounts where you want tight control, but it needs regular attention. Most accounts with reliable conversion tracking find automated strategies easier to manage.

How long should I wait before judging a new bidding strategy?

Allow the learning period to finish and then gather enough data to compare fairly. For many small accounts that means a few weeks rather than a few days. Check Google's current guidance for your campaign type.

Why has my campaign stopped spending after setting a target CPA?

The target is probably too low compared with your recent cost per conversion, so Google is bidding cautiously. Raise it closer to your recent average and lower it gradually.

Do I need conversion tracking to use Smart Bidding?

For conversion-based strategies, yes. They learn from your conversion data, so tracking must be accurate and record only the actions that matter to your business.
