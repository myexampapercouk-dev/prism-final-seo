How to Set Up the LinkedIn Insight Tag: A Practical UK Guide

Set up the LinkedIn Insight Tag to track conversions and build retargeting audiences. Install it via Tag Manager, respect consent and test it properly.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • The Insight Tag is a small piece of code that lets LinkedIn record conversions and build website audiences for your ads.
  • Install it once across the whole site, ideally through Google Tag Manager, and only fire it after the right consent.
  • Create conversion actions and test them before you spend, or your LinkedIn reporting will be guesswork.

To set up the LinkedIn Insight Tag, open Campaign Manager, find the Insight Tag option under your account's data or analytics tools, copy the tag, and install it on every page of your website, most easily through Google Tag Manager. Once it is firing, you can create conversion actions and website retargeting audiences that your LinkedIn campaigns can use.

For UK B2B firms, the tag is what turns LinkedIn Ads from a branding exercise into something you can measure. This guide covers what the tag does, the installation options, consent under UK rules, setting up conversions and how to check everything works. LinkedIn updates Campaign Manager regularly, so check LinkedIn's current help pages for exact menu names before you start.

What the LinkedIn Insight Tag does

The Insight Tag is a short JavaScript snippet that sits on your website. When a LinkedIn member visits a page with the tag, LinkedIn can match that visit to their profile in an aggregated way. That unlocks three things:

  • Conversion tracking. You can see which campaigns led to form fills, downloads, demo requests or purchases.
  • Website retargeting. You can build audiences of people who visited your site or specific pages, then show them ads.
  • Website demographics. You can see aggregated information about visitors, such as job function, seniority and industry, which helps when deciding who to target.

Without the tag, LinkedIn can still report on clicks and lead gen forms filled inside LinkedIn, but it cannot see what happens on your own website. If you are still deciding whether LinkedIn is right for you, our guide to LinkedIn Ads for B2B lead generation covers the strategy side.

Before you start

Check you have the following in place:

  • Access to LinkedIn Campaign Manager for your ad account, with a role that allows you to manage tags and conversions.
  • Access to your website or to Google Tag Manager.
  • A working cookie consent banner, because the Insight Tag sets cookies used for advertising.
  • A list of the actions you want to count as conversions, with the page or event that represents each one.

How to set up the LinkedIn Insight Tag

There are three common ways to install it. Choose one, not all three, or you risk counting visits twice.

MethodBest forProsWatch out for
Google Tag ManagerMost small businesses already using GTMNo code changes, easy to link to consent settings, easy to add eventsGTM must already be installed on every page
Paste code into site headerSimple sites without GTMDirect and quickHarder to control consent and add event tracking later
Platform integration or pluginSome CMS and ecommerce platformsLittle technical workCheck what it fires, when, and whether it respects consent

Here is the Google Tag Manager route, which we recommend for most sites:

  1. In Campaign Manager, open the Insight Tag section and note your Partner ID.
  2. In Google Tag Manager, create a new tag and choose the LinkedIn Insight tag template from the gallery, if available, or a custom HTML tag with the code LinkedIn provides.
  3. Enter your Partner ID.
  4. Set the trigger to fire on all pages, subject to advertising consent.
  5. Use Preview mode to check the tag fires on page load once consent is given and does not fire before.
  6. Publish the container.

If you have not set up Tag Manager yet, our Google Tag Manager setup guide walks through it. The same approach applies to other ad platform tags, such as the one in our Meta Pixel setup guide.

Consent and UK privacy rules

The Insight Tag sets cookies and similar technologies for advertising purposes. Under UK GDPR and PECR, non-essential cookies such as these generally need the visitor's consent before they are set. In practice, that means the tag should only fire after someone has accepted advertising or marketing cookies in your banner.

A few practical points:

  • Make sure your cookie banner offers a genuine choice, with rejecting as easy as accepting. Our guide to cookie banner design covers good practice.
  • List LinkedIn in your cookie and privacy policies, explaining what data is collected and why.
  • Expect lower recorded numbers than you might in a world without consent. That is normal and better than collecting data unlawfully.

This is general information rather than legal advice. Check the ICO's current guidance on cookies and similar technologies, and take advice if you are unsure how it applies to your site.

Setting up LinkedIn conversion tracking

Once the tag is live, create conversion actions in Campaign Manager. There are broadly two approaches:

Conversion typeHow it worksGood for
Page load (URL based)Counts a conversion when someone reaches a specific URL, such as a thank you pageContact forms, brochure downloads, booking confirmations
Event specificCounts a conversion when a defined event fires, such as a button click or form submission without a new pageForms that show a message instead of redirecting, click to call buttons

When creating each conversion, you will usually set a name, a category such as lead or sign up, a value if relevant, a click-through and view-through attribution window, and the campaigns it applies to. Keep names clear, for example Contact form submitted or Demo request, so reports make sense months later.

If your forms do not redirect to a thank you page, ask your developer to add one, or use event-based conversions fired from Tag Manager. Our article on website contact forms explains why a dedicated confirmation page is useful for tracking as well as for the visitor.

How to test the Insight Tag

Never assume the tag is working because you pasted it in. Check it properly:

  1. Visit your site in a normal browser window, accept cookies and browse a few pages.
  2. Use Tag Manager's Preview mode to confirm the LinkedIn tag fires after consent and not before.
  3. Check the Insight Tag status in Campaign Manager. It can take some time before the tag shows as active.
  4. Complete a test conversion, such as a form submission, and confirm the conversion action begins to record activity. Again, allow time for data to appear.
  5. Reject cookies in a private window and confirm the tag does not fire.

Building retargeting audiences

With the tag active, you can create matched audiences based on website visits. Useful starting audiences include all website visitors, visitors to your pricing or service pages, and people who reached a thank you page so you can exclude existing leads. LinkedIn sets minimum audience sizes before an audience can be used, so smaller UK sites may need to keep audiences broad or allow time for them to build. Our remarketing guide covers how to sequence messages to warm audiences across platforms.

Common Insight Tag mistakes

  • Installing it twice. A plugin plus a Tag Manager tag can double count visits and conversions.
  • Firing before consent. This is a compliance risk and is easy to miss without testing.
  • Counting page views as leads. A conversion should be a meaningful action, not simply visiting the contact page.
  • Not tagging UTMs. Add UTM parameters to LinkedIn ad links so traffic is also attributed correctly in GA4.
  • Forgetting old tags. If you change ad accounts or agencies, remove tags you no longer use.

FAQs

How do I set up the LinkedIn Insight Tag?

Find the Insight Tag in Campaign Manager, copy your Partner ID or tag code, and install it across your whole site, ideally with Google Tag Manager. Set it to fire only after advertising consent, publish, then test it and create your conversion actions.

Is the LinkedIn Insight Tag free?

Yes, the tag itself costs nothing to install. You only pay for the ads you run. It is still worth installing before you launch campaigns so audiences and conversion data can start building.

Do I need cookie consent for the LinkedIn Insight Tag?

In most UK cases, yes, because it uses cookies for advertising purposes. Fire it only after a visitor accepts the relevant cookie category, and check the ICO's current guidance for your situation.

How long does it take for the Insight Tag to show as active?

It can take some time after installation for Campaign Manager to confirm the tag is receiving data. If it still shows no activity after a day or two of normal traffic, recheck the installation and consent settings.

Can I track LinkedIn lead gen forms with the Insight Tag?

Lead gen forms are completed inside LinkedIn, so they are tracked by LinkedIn directly rather than through your website tag. The Insight Tag is for actions that happen on your own site.

Can I use the Insight Tag without running ads?

You can install it, and the website demographics view can still be informative. However, its main value is in measuring and targeting LinkedIn ad campaigns, so it is most useful once ads are running.
