SEO Migration Checklist: How to Move, Rebuild or Replatform a Website Without Losing Rankings

An SEO migration checklist covers crawling the old site, mapping redirects, testing staging and monitoring after launch. Use our step-by-step UK guide.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Benchmark and crawl the old site first, so you know every URL, ranking and conversion you need to protect.
  • A one-to-one redirect map from every old URL to its closest new equivalent is the single most important task.
  • Monitor closely for at least eight weeks after launch and fix redirect errors, missing pages and tracking gaps quickly.

An SEO migration checklist is a step-by-step plan for moving a website to a new domain, platform, design or URL structure without losing search traffic. The core steps are: benchmark the old site, crawl every URL, map 301 redirects, test the new site on staging, launch carefully and monitor for several weeks.

Migrations are one of the riskiest moments in a website's life. Done well, rankings usually settle within weeks. Done badly, a site can lose much of its organic traffic. This checklist is written for UK businesses replatforming, redesigning or rebranding, and pairs with our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings.

Types of website migration

Not every migration carries the same risk. Knowing which type you are doing helps you plan.

Migration typeExampleRisk level
Design refresh, same URLsNew theme, same pages and addressesLower
URL structure changeMoving products into new category pathsMedium
Platform changeWooCommerce to Shopify, or Wix to WordPressMedium to high
Protocol or subdomain changeHTTP to HTTPS, www to non-wwwLower if redirects are correct
Domain changeRebrand to a new domain nameHigh
Combined changesNew domain, platform and structure at onceHighest

Where possible, avoid changing everything at once. If you must, plan more time for testing and monitoring.

Phase 1: Planning and benchmarking

  1. Agree the scope. List exactly what is changing: domain, platform, URLs, content, design, navigation.
  2. Pick a sensible launch date. Avoid your peak season. For many retailers that means steering clear of November and December.
  3. Record baseline performance. Export organic sessions, conversions and revenue by landing page from GA4, and clicks and impressions by page and query from Google Search Console.
  4. Record rankings. Note positions for your most important search terms.
  5. Identify priority pages. The pages that bring the most organic traffic, conversions and backlinks need the most care.
  6. Keep access. Make sure you have admin access to the domain, DNS, Search Console, GA4 and the old platform until well after launch.

Phase 2: Crawl and inventory the old site

  1. Crawl the full site with an SEO crawler and export every URL with its status code, title, meta description, H1 and canonical.
  2. Add URLs from your XML sitemaps, Search Console, GA4 landing pages and backlink reports. Crawlers miss orphaned pages that still get traffic or links.
  3. Include images, PDFs and other files that receive traffic or links.
  4. Note existing redirects so you do not create redirect chains.
  5. Save a copy of key page content in case anything is lost during the rebuild.

Phase 3: Build the redirect map

The redirect map is the heart of any SEO migration checklist. It lists every old URL and the new URL it should permanently redirect to with a 301.

  • Map one-to-one wherever possible. Each old page should go to the most relevant new page, not to the homepage.
  • Handle removed pages carefully. If a product or service is gone, redirect to the closest category or alternative. If there is no relevant equivalent, a 410 or 404 can be more honest than an irrelevant redirect.
  • Avoid chains and loops. Update older redirects so they point straight to the final URL.
  • Preserve parameters where needed. Check filtered or tracked URLs that matter.
  • Cover every variation. HTTP and HTTPS, www and non-www, trailing slashes and uppercase versions.

For ecommerce sites, platform defaults matter. Shopify, for example, uses fixed URL patterns for products and collections, so URLs almost always change when moving to it. Our guides to Shopify vs WooCommerce and Shopify Plus migration cover platform-specific points.

Phase 4: Build and test on staging

  1. Block staging from search engines. Use password protection rather than relying only on robots.txt.
  2. Migrate content fully. Titles, meta descriptions, headings, body content, image alt text and structured data should carry over or improve.
  3. Check internal links. Navigation, footers and in-content links should point to new URLs directly, not through redirects.
  4. Set canonicals correctly. Each page should reference its own new URL unless there is a reason not to.
  5. Test speed and mobile layout. A new design should not be slower than the old one. Our site speed guide lists common fixes.
  6. Test the redirect map. Run the old URL list against staging where possible and check every redirect lands on a 200 page.
  7. Set up tracking. GA4, conversion tags, consent mode and any ad platform pixels should be ready and tested before launch. See our GA4 setup guide.
  8. Prepare the new XML sitemap and robots.txt. Make sure the live robots.txt does not carry over a staging block.

Phase 5: Launch day

  1. Launch at a quiet time early in the week, with developers available.
  2. Remove staging password protection and any noindex tags.
  3. Switch on redirects and test a sample of priority URLs immediately.
  4. Check robots.txt, canonicals and the XML sitemap on the live site.
  5. Submit the new sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  6. For domain changes, use the Change of Address tool in Search Console.
  7. Confirm conversions and purchases are recording in GA4 and ad platforms.
  8. Update your Google Business Profile, social profiles and key directory listings if URLs or the domain changed.

Phase 6: Monitoring after launch

Most migration problems show up in the first few weeks. Monitor daily at first, then weekly for at least eight weeks.

  • Recrawl the old URL list and fix any that return errors or redirect to the wrong place.
  • Watch Search Console for spikes in 404s, indexing issues and coverage changes.
  • Compare organic clicks, sessions and conversions by page against your baseline.
  • Check rankings for priority terms.
  • Ask sites that link to you most often to update their links where practical.
  • Keep redirects in place long term, ideally permanently.

Some movement in rankings is normal. A temporary dip that recovers is common. A sharp, sustained drop usually points to missing redirects, blocked pages or lost content, which our technical SEO audit checklist can help you track down.

Who should be involved

A migration touches several teams, and problems usually happen in the gaps between them. Agree owners for each area before work starts.

  • Project owner. Signs off scope, launch date and any content changes.
  • SEO lead. Owns the crawl, benchmarks, redirect map, staging checks and post-launch monitoring.
  • Developers. Build the new site, implement redirects and fix issues found in testing and after launch.
  • Content owners. Make sure important copy, product information and images move across intact.
  • Analytics and paid media. Confirm tracking, update ad final URLs and product feeds, and check conversions after launch.

Paid campaigns are easy to forget. If ad destination URLs or Merchant Center feeds still point to old addresses, ads may be disapproved or send traffic through redirects. Update them as part of launch day, and check email templates, social profiles and printed QR codes too.

Write the checklist into a shared document with a named owner and a status for every task. On launch day, work through it in order and do not skip steps because time is short.

Common migration mistakes

  • Redirecting everything to the homepage.
  • Launching with the staging noindex or robots.txt block still in place.
  • Cutting content during a redesign, removing text that pages ranked for.
  • Forgetting images, PDFs and blog posts in the redirect map.
  • Losing tracking, so you cannot tell whether traffic has dropped.
  • Letting the old domain expire after a rebrand.

FAQs

What is an SEO migration checklist?

It is a step-by-step list of tasks for moving or rebuilding a website while protecting search rankings, covering benchmarking, crawling, redirect mapping, staging tests, launch checks and monitoring.

Will I lose rankings when I migrate my website?

Some short-term movement is common, but a well-planned migration with complete redirects and preserved content usually settles. Large or lasting drops normally come from mistakes that can be found and fixed.

How long does it take to recover after a migration?

It varies with the size of the site and type of change. Smaller changes can settle within weeks; domain changes and large restructures can take longer. Monitor for at least eight weeks.

Should I use 301 or 302 redirects?

Use 301 permanent redirects for pages that have moved for good. A 302 signals a temporary move and is not the right choice for a migration.

How long should I keep redirects in place?

Ideally permanently. Old links, bookmarks and references can keep sending visitors for years, and removing redirects risks losing that value.

When is the best time to migrate a website?

During a quieter trading period, early in the week, with developers available to fix problems. Avoid peak seasons and major sales events.
