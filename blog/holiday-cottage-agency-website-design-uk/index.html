Holiday Cottage Agency Website Design in the UK: Multi-Property Sites That Win Guests and Owners

Holiday cottage agency website design in the UK: multi-property search, booking integration, area SEO and owner pages that help letting agencies grow bookings.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A holiday cottage agency website has two audiences, guests searching for a stay and owners choosing an agency, and it needs strong journeys for both.
  • Fast property search, live availability from your booking system, rich property pages and area guides do most of the work for guest bookings and search visibility.
  • Agency sites are usually quoted after discovery, with small sites from £1,000 to £2,000 and retainers from £350 a month, and data protection decisions rest with the agency.

Holiday cottage agency website design in the UK is about building a site that helps guests find and book the right property from a portfolio of many, while also persuading cottage owners to list with the agency. A good agency site combines fast property search, live availability, detailed property pages, useful area guides and a clear owner section, all connected to the booking system the agency already uses.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio that works remotely with hospitality and tourism businesses. This guide is for regional and specialist holiday cottage agencies managing several properties for different owners. If you own one or a few holiday lets yourself, our guide to holiday let website design is a better fit.

How an agency site differs from a single holiday let site

A single-property website can be simple: a few pages, a gallery and a booking calendar. An agency website is closer to a small marketplace. It has to manage dozens or hundreds of properties, each with its own photos, descriptions, rules, prices and availability, and present them in a way that helps guests choose quickly.

It also has a second audience. Owners compare agencies before signing up, and the website is often the first place they look. A clear, professional site that shows how the agency markets properties can be as important for growth as guest bookings.

Connecting to your booking system

Most agencies already use a specialist holiday rental or property management system to handle availability, pricing, bookings, owner statements and channel connections. The website should work with that system rather than replace it. Depending on the platform, this can mean embedding its search and booking tools, connecting through an integration or building custom pages that pull data from it.

During discovery we review what your system can provide, how other agencies use it and what limits it has. The goal is a site where availability and prices are always accurate, bookings flow straight into your system and your team does not have to update information in two places. Our guide to website online booking systems explains the general trade-offs, and our website development service covers the build itself.

Property pages that convert

The property page is where guests decide. Strong property pages usually share the same structure, which also makes them easier for your team to maintain.

  • A summary at the top with sleeps, bedrooms, bathrooms, pets, parking and key features.
  • High-quality photos of every room and the outside space, in a logical order.
  • Sleeping arrangements showing bed types in each room.
  • An honest description of the property and its setting, written from information the owner and agency supply.
  • House rules covering pets, check-in times, minimum stays and any accessibility notes.
  • Location information with nearby walks, pubs, beaches and attractions.
  • Live availability and pricing with a clear booking button.

We build templates in the content management system so every property follows the same high standard, and new properties can be added quickly when owners sign up.

SEO for areas, themes and properties

Guests rarely search for an agency by name at first. They search for "holiday cottages in" a county or town, "dog friendly cottages" with a place name, "cottages with hot tubs", "large group holiday homes" or "coastal cottages". An agency site has a natural advantage here, because it can create genuinely useful pages for each area and theme backed by real properties.

We plan a structure of area pages, theme pages and combined pages where demand justifies them, each with original introductions, local guidance and a filtered list of properties. Duplicate or thin pages can harm visibility, so we avoid creating pages for every possible combination. Our guide to multi-location SEO explains this approach, and our hotel SEO guide covers technical points that also apply to holiday accommodation, such as structured data and page speed.

Area guides written with local knowledge also help guests plan their trip and help AI search tools understand what the agency offers in each place.

Direct bookings versus online travel agencies

Many agencies list properties on large booking platforms as well as their own website. Those platforms can bring volume, but they charge commission and own the guest relationship. A strong agency website helps shift more bookings to direct channels over time.

Clear reasons to book direct, such as flexible terms, local knowledge, offers for returning guests or no booking fees, should appear across the site. Our guide to increasing direct bookings explains tactics that work just as well for cottage agencies as for hotels.

Pages that win new owners

Most agencies grow by adding properties, so the owner section deserves real attention. Owners want to understand what the agency does, how properties are marketed, how bookings and changeovers are handled, how they are paid and what they can expect at each step. A simple, honest owner section with a short enquiry form often brings in a steady flow of property enquiries.

We avoid claims about earnings or occupancy unless they come from the agency's own verified records, and we recommend explaining fees and contract terms clearly so owners trust the process.

Marketing beyond the website

Once the site is in place, it becomes the base for wider marketing: Google Ads for high-intent searches, Meta Ads for inspiration and seasonal offers, and email to past guests who agreed to hear from you. If you also work with holiday parks or glamping sites, our guides to holiday park marketing and glamping site marketing cover those formats.

Data protection, cookies and accessibility

Agency websites collect guest and owner data through enquiry and booking forms, so UK GDPR applies, and PECR applies to cookies and marketing emails. Tracking tools should only run where the visitor has agreed when consent is required. Accessibility matters too, both for the website itself and for the accuracy of accessibility information on each property page.

Holiday lets are also affected by rules on safety, licensing or registration in some parts of the UK, and these change from time to time. We are not legal advisers. Check official guidance from the ICO and the relevant UK and local authorities, and take your own advice. Compliance decisions rest with the agency and its owners.

FAQs

What is holiday cottage agency website design in the UK?

It is the design and build of a multi-property website with search, live availability, property pages, area guides and an owner section, connected to the agency's booking system.

Can you connect our website to our existing booking system?

Usually yes. We review what your system supports and then embed its tools, use an integration or build pages that pull data from it.

How many area pages should an agency create?

Only as many as there is real demand and enough properties to support. Thin or duplicate pages can harm search visibility, so we plan them carefully.

Will a new website reduce our reliance on booking platforms?

It can help over time by giving guests clear reasons to book direct, alongside SEO, ads and email to past guests.

Do you write property descriptions?

We can structure and edit them, but the details should come from the agency and owners so they are accurate and up to date.

How long does an agency website take?

A smaller site takes 3 to 5 weeks. Larger sites with custom search and integrations are scoped and quoted after discovery.
