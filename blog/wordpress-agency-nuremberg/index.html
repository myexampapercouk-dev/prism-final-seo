Nuremberg businesses and their websites

Nuremberg is the largest city in Franconia, in northern Bavaria, and forms part of a wider metropolitan region with Fürth and Erlangen. Its economy includes engineering, electronics, energy technology, market research, logistics and a large trade fair business. The city also draws many visitors to its historic old town and its well-known Christmas market.

That variety means WordPress projects in the area tend to fall into a few groups:

  • Industrial and business-to-business sites that explain technical products and services, often in German and English.
  • Local service sites for trades, clinics, law and tax firms, restaurants and shops serving the metropolitan area.
  • Hospitality and tourism sites for hotels, guest houses and attractions that need booking links and content for visitors.
  • Online shops built with WooCommerce for regional producers and specialist retailers.

German and English on one WordPress site

Many Nuremberg firms need their site in German and English, particularly those that exhibit at trade fairs or sell abroad. WordPress handles this well with a good multilingual plugin or a multisite set-up, but it needs planning from the start. We decide with you:

  • Which pages need both languages, and which only one.
  • How URLs will be structured for each language.
  • How hreflang tags will tell search engines which version to show.
  • Who will write and translate each language version, and how updates stay in sync.

Our guides to multilingual website design and the WordPress multisite guide set out the options in more detail.

Fixing an existing WordPress site

Not every project starts from scratch. Many businesses come to us with a WordPress site that has become slow, hard to edit or insecure after years of added plugins. In that case we usually start with a review covering:

  1. Plugins that are unused, outdated or duplicate each other. Our WordPress plugin audit guide shows how we check them.
  2. Theme quality and whether it can be kept or needs replacing.
  3. Hosting performance, backups and PHP version.
  4. Security settings, user accounts and update routines.
  5. Speed, mobile usability and technical SEO issues.

Sometimes a careful clean-up is enough. Sometimes a rebuild on a lighter theme is cheaper over time. We explain the trade-offs so you can decide.

Security, hosting and care

WordPress powers a large share of the web, which makes it a common target for automated attacks. Most problems come from outdated plugins, weak passwords and poor hosting rather than from WordPress itself. A sensible care routine includes regular updates, daily backups stored off the server, limited administrator accounts and good hosting with server-level caching. Our WordPress security guide covers the essentials, and our WordPress hosting guide explains what to look for in a host, including data location if that matters to you.

Data protection and cookies

German businesses are often especially careful about privacy, and rightly so. On a WordPress site, that means checking what data forms collect, where it is stored, which plugins load external services and how cookie consent is handled. We set up consent and tracking in line with good practice. Your imprint, privacy policy and legal wording should come from your own adviser or official guidance, and compliance decisions rest with you.

Content that works for trade fair visitors

Nuremberg's exhibition centre hosts major international trade fairs throughout the year, and many local and regional firms rely on them for new business. A WordPress site can support that effort in practical ways. Before a fair, a dedicated page can explain where to find your stand, what you will be showing and how to book a meeting. During and after the fair, that page becomes the place to send contacts for product details, downloads and follow-up.

For technical products, clear product or service pages matter most. Each one should explain what the product does, who it is for, the key specifications and how to request a quote or a call. Downloadable data sheets are useful, but the main information should also sit on the page itself as text, so search engines and AI tools can read it.

We also recommend a simple enquiry form on each key page rather than a single contact page. Visitors from a trade fair often arrive with a specific question, and making it easy to ask that question straight away leads to more conversations. Tracking these forms in GA4 then shows which products and pages create the most interest.
