Pub Marketing in the UK: How to Fill Your Pub Beyond Busy Weekends

Pub marketing in the UK works when your website, Google profile, events calendar and table booking give locals and visitors a clear reason to come in midweek.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most pubs are busy at the same times as every other pub, so marketing should focus on giving people a reason to visit on quieter days.
  • An up-to-date Google Business Profile, menus on the website and simple table booking make it easy for people to choose you on the spot.
  • Events, food nights, sport, function rooms and email to regulars build habits that keep a pub busy through the year.

Pub marketing in the UK works when people can find you quickly, see what is on, and book a table or plan a visit in a few taps, with a clear reason to come in on the nights that are usually quiet. Successful pubs tend to combine an accurate Google Business Profile, a mobile-friendly website with current menus and events, simple table booking and regular contact with their regulars.

This guide is for independent free houses, tenanted and leased pubs, gastropubs, village and community pubs and small pub groups. We are a studio that builds websites and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so we focus on the digital side of bringing people through the door. If you also offer rooms, our guide to winning direct bookings covers the accommodation side in more detail.

How people choose a pub

Choosing a pub is often a quick decision. Someone searches "pub near me", "dog friendly pub with garden", "Sunday roast near me" or "pub showing the football", glances at photos, reviews and opening hours, and heads to whichever looks right. Others plan ahead for a birthday meal, a wake, a Christmas party or a quiz night with friends.

The details that tip the decision are practical. Is food served now? Is there space for a pushchair or a dog? Is there a garden, a fire, live music or a big screen? Can I book? Pubs that answer these questions clearly online win more of these moments.

Typical audiences include:

  • Regulars who already know you and respond to events and offers.
  • Diners looking for a meal out, especially Sunday lunch.
  • Families and dog owners wanting a welcoming, practical space.
  • Sports fans looking for a place to watch matches.
  • Walkers, cyclists and tourists passing through rural areas.
  • Groups and function bookers planning parties, wakes and meetings.

Pub marketing UK: get the basics right first

Before spending on adverts, make sure the fundamentals are working, because every campaign depends on them.

  • Google Business Profile: correct opening and kitchen hours, attributes such as dog friendly or outdoor seating, recent photos, a menu link and a booking link. Our Google Business Profile guide covers the setup.
  • Website: current menus as web pages rather than only PDFs, an events calendar, opening hours, how to find you, parking and accessibility information, and a clear book a table button.
  • Reviews: ask happy customers for reviews and reply to every one, calmly and briefly. Our guide on how to respond to Google reviews has examples.
  • Table booking: an online system that works well on a phone and confirms quickly.

Out-of-date hours or a menu from last season are among the quickest ways to lose trust. Make updating them part of your weekly routine.

Your pub website

A pub website does not need to be large, but it should be fast and easy to use on a phone, since that is where most people look. Key pages usually include food and drink, events and what's on, functions and private hire, rooms if you have them, about the pub, and contact and directions. Our article on restaurant website design covers menus, booking and photography in more depth, and most of it applies to food-led pubs.

Write menus as text on the page so search engines and customers can read them easily, and note dietary and allergen information clearly. For allergens, follow current Food Standards Agency guidance and make it easy for customers to ask staff. Use real photos of your food, rooms, garden and fire rather than stock images, and keep them current.

Events and reasons to visit

Events are one of the most powerful tools a pub has, because they give people a specific reason to come on a specific night. Common ideas include quiz nights, live music, open mic, curry or steak nights, tasting evenings, games nights, community clubs and seasonal celebrations. Local SEO for pubs

Most pub searches are local, so local SEO matters more than broad rankings. As well as your Google Business Profile, build website pages that match the searches your customers make: Sunday roast, dog friendly, beer garden, function room, live music, and your village or area name. Make sure your name, address and phone number are consistent across directories and listing sites. Our local SEO guide explains the main steps, and our SEO and GEO service can handle it for you, including how AI search tools describe your pub.

Social media and email

Social media helps a pub show its personality: the fire on a cold night, a new guest ale, the Sunday roast coming out of the kitchen, the quiz team celebrating. Post consistently about what is coming up rather than only what happened, and include a booking link where relevant.

Email is often overlooked but works very well for pubs because regulars like hearing about events and new menus. Collect sign-ups with clear consent through your website, booking system and in the pub, then send a short, regular update. Our guide to building an email list covers consent; check current ICO guidance on UK GDPR and PECR for your setup.

Paid ads for pubs

Paid campaigns work best for specific goals: Christmas party bookings, Mother's Day and Father's Day lunches, a new menu launch, function room hire or a big sporting event. Meta ads on Facebook and Instagram suit local awareness with a tight radius around the pub, while Google search ads catch people actively looking for things like "Christmas party venue" or "function room hire" nearby. Keep each campaign linked to a matching page with a clear way to book.

Alcohol advertising is covered by rules in the UK advertising codes and by the platforms' own policies, including restrictions on targeting and how drinking is shown. Keep ads responsible, aim them at adults, and check current CAP Code guidance and platform policies before launching. Our paid media service can plan and manage campaigns like these, with ad spend paid separately.

Christmas and seasonal planning

For many pubs, the Christmas party season is the biggest marketing moment of the year, and bookings start much earlier than most people expect. Publish festive menus and party packages early, give them a dedicated page with an enquiry or deposit route, and promote them through email, social media and search. Plan similar pushes for other key dates such as Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, bank holiday weekends and major tournaments.

Measuring results

Track table bookings, function enquiries and event sign-ups by source. Set up analytics events for booking clicks and form submissions, and add tracking links to emails and social posts. Compare the results with your till data by day so you can see whether campaigns are lifting the quiet nights you targeted. FAQs

What is the most effective pub marketing UK pubs can do on a small budget?

Keep your Google Business Profile accurate and active, put current menus and events on a mobile-friendly website, make table booking easy and build an email list of regulars. These cost little beyond time and support everything else you do.

How can a pub get busier midweek?

Create a regular reason to visit on a specific night, such as a quiz, a food theme night or a club meeting, then promote it consistently through your website, Google Business Profile, social media and email so it becomes a habit.

Does a pub need its own website if it has social media?

Yes, in most cases. A website gives you a stable place for menus, events, booking and functions that you control, and it helps you appear in search. Social media works best as a way to drive people to it.

Can pubs advertise alcohol on Facebook and Instagram?

Alcohol advertising is allowed in many cases but is subject to the UK advertising codes and platform policies, including age targeting and responsible messaging. Check current CAP Code guidance and platform rules before running ads.

When should a pub start promoting Christmas parties?

Earlier than you might think. Many groups plan in late summer and early autumn, so publish packages and start promoting well before December, then remind people as dates fill up.

How long does pub SEO take to work?

Local SEO typically builds over three to six months. Changes to your Google Business Profile, such as accurate hours and fresh photos, can help sooner, while website content and reviews build steadily over time.
