Florist Marketing UK: How Independent Florists Grow Orders and Bookings

Florist marketing UK guide for independent florists: local SEO, Google Business Profile, ecommerce websites, seasonal campaigns, social media and weddings.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Florists sell to two audiences at once: urgent local gift buyers and planners booking events.
  • A simple online shop with clear delivery areas and cut-off times can capture last-minute orders.
  • Peak dates need planning weeks in advance, from content to ads to capacity.

Florist marketing in the UK is about being found at the moment someone needs flowers and making it easy for them to order. Many customers want same-day or next-day delivery to someone local, while others are planning a wedding or event months ahead. An independent florist that is easy to find, shows its work beautifully and makes ordering simple is well placed to compete with large national networks.

This guide is for owners of independent florists and floral studios. It covers local search, ecommerce websites, seasonal campaigns, social media, weddings and events, and measurement.

Why florist marketing is different

Florists serve two quite different audiences. Gift buyers often search urgently, want to see prices and delivery options straight away and order on their phone. Event and wedding clients research carefully, compare styles and expect a consultation.

Demand is also highly seasonal. Valentine's Day, Mother's Day and Christmas can bring a large share of the year's orders in a short time, while sympathy and funeral flowers require a sensitive, reliable service all year round.

Independent florists also compete with national relay networks and large online brands. Your advantages are local knowledge, personal service, freshness and a distinctive style. Good marketing makes those advantages visible.

Start with Google Business Profile and local search

When someone searches for a florist near them or flower delivery in a town, the map results are often the first thing they see. A complete, well-maintained Google Business Profile is essential.

Choose the right primary category, add your shop address and delivery area, keep opening hours accurate, including special hours around peak dates, and add plenty of real photos of your arrangements and shop. Our Google Business Profile guide covers the setup.

Reviews from happy customers help both rankings and trust. Ask for honest feedback after delivery and make it easy with a short link. Never write or buy reviews, and reply politely to every one. Be especially thoughtful when replying to reviews about sympathy flowers.

Our local SEO guide explains how map and organic rankings work together.

Build an online shop that takes orders

For many florists, an online shop is the most important marketing investment after their profile. Customers want to choose, pay and arrange delivery without phoning. Key features include:

  • Clear product pages. Good photos, a description of what is included, sizes and prices.
  • Delivery information. Areas covered, delivery charges and same-day cut-off times, shown clearly before checkout.
  • Occasion categories. Birthdays, anniversaries, sympathy, new baby and thank you, so customers can browse quickly.
  • Card messages. An easy way to add a personal message at checkout.
  • Substitution policy. An honest note that flowers may vary with season and availability.
  • Mobile-friendly checkout. Most urgent orders come from phones.

Platforms such as Shopify and WooCommerce can both work well for florists, depending on your needs. Our website and ecommerce development service can help you choose and build the right setup, and our Shopify website cost guide explains what affects budgets.

Create pages for local delivery and occasions

Customers often search for flower delivery plus a town or area. A page for each main area you deliver to can help, provided each includes genuinely useful information, such as delivery times, nearby landmarks, hospitals or venues you deliver to, and any area-specific charges. Avoid copying the same text and changing the town name.

Occasion pages work in a similar way. A sympathy flowers page might explain how to order for a funeral, how timing works with funeral directors and what wording to use on a card. A wedding flowers page might explain your consultation process and the areas you cover. Our SEO and GEO service can help plan and structure these pages.

Plan peak dates well in advance

Peak dates reward planning. Several weeks before Valentine's Day, Mother's Day or Christmas, you can:

  • Publish dedicated collection pages so they have time to be found.
  • Set clear order cut-off times and delivery windows.
  • Email past customers who have agreed to hear from you.
  • Prepare social media content and paid campaigns.
  • Plan stock and staffing so you can deliver what you promise.

It is better to stop taking orders when you reach capacity than to over-promise. A clear message about sold-out slots protects your reputation.

Paid ads for urgent demand

Google Ads can capture people searching for flower delivery right now, especially around peak dates. Keep targeting to your delivery area, send clicks to the most relevant collection or product and make delivery options obvious. Shopping campaigns can show product images directly in search results if your shop is set up for them.

Facebook and Instagram ads can promote seasonal collections, workshops or subscriptions to local audiences. Track orders and revenue rather than clicks. Our Google Ads guide and paid media service cover the basics.

Social media for florists

Flowers are made for social media. Regular posts showing fresh arrivals, arrangements in progress, finished bouquets and behind-the-scenes moments help people get to know your style. Short videos of a bouquet being made can be especially engaging.

Instagram and Pinterest are particularly useful for wedding and event clients looking for inspiration. Always link back to your shop or enquiry page so interest can turn into orders.

When sharing wedding or event work, get permission from the couple or client and credit other suppliers where appropriate.

Weddings and events

Wedding and event work is often higher value but takes longer to win. Couples want to see a portfolio, understand your style and feel confident you can deliver on the day. A dedicated weddings page with real examples, an outline of your process and an easy way to book a consultation helps.

Relationships with venues, planners and other suppliers can bring steady referrals. Our wedding business marketing guide covers more ideas.

Retention, subscriptions and email

Repeat customers are valuable. Simple ideas include reminder emails for birthdays or anniversaries, where customers have opted in, flower subscriptions for homes or offices and workshops for local people. Only send marketing emails to people who have agreed to receive them and make unsubscribing easy.

Our ecommerce email marketing guide covers how to set up useful automated emails.

Sympathy and funeral flowers

Sympathy orders need particular care. Customers are often grieving, short of time and unsure what is appropriate. A calm, clear page that explains the options, how to coordinate timing with a funeral director and how to write a card message can be genuinely helpful. Keep the tone respectful, avoid pushy upsells and make it easy to phone if someone would rather speak to a person.

Reliability matters more here than anywhere else. Be clear about the latest time you can accept an order for a given service and confirm delivery promptly. A florist known for handling these orders with sensitivity often earns lasting loyalty and recommendations.

Corporate and contract work

Local businesses such as hotels, restaurants, offices and reception areas may want regular arrangements. A short page explaining how contract flowers work, how often arrangements are refreshed and how to request a quote can open up steady, predictable income outside peak dates. Keep proposals simple and follow up promptly.

Measure what matters

  • Online orders and revenue by source.
  • Calls and direction requests from your profile.
  • Performance of peak-date campaigns compared with previous years.
  • Wedding and event enquiries and how many convert.
  • Repeat purchase rate.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Hiding delivery areas, charges or cut-off times until checkout.
  • Starting peak-date marketing too late.
  • Using supplier stock photos that do not reflect your own work.
  • Thin area pages copied for every town.
  • Taking more orders than you can deliver well.

FAQs

What is the best marketing for a florist?

For most independent florists, a strong Google Business Profile, a mobile-friendly online shop with clear delivery information and well-planned peak-date campaigns form the foundation.

Should a florist sell online?

Many customers prefer to order online, especially for urgent gifts. A simple, well-built shop can capture orders that might otherwise go to national networks.

When should I start Valentine's Day marketing?

Several weeks ahead is sensible, so collection pages can be found and campaigns have time to run. Plan stock and capacity at the same time.

Are Google Ads worth it for florists?

They can be, particularly around peak dates and for delivery searches. Track orders and revenue rather than clicks.

How can florists compete with national flower delivery brands?

Focus on local delivery, freshness, personal service and a distinctive style, and make those advantages clear on your profile and website.

How long does SEO take for a florist?

Profile improvements can help relatively quickly, while broader SEO usually takes several months, so start well before peak dates.
