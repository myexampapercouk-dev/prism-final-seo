Outsourcing SEO to an Overseas Agency: A Guide for US Businesses

Outsourcing SEO to an overseas agency can work for US businesses if you check market research, reporting, ownership and process. Here is what to ask first.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Outsourced SEO works when the agency researches the US market properly, reports clearly and leaves every account and asset in your name.
  • Judge an overseas SEO agency on its process, its questions and its honesty about timelines, not on promises of rankings.
  • SEO results typically take 3 to 6 months; our published retainers are in pounds and other currencies are quoted on request.

Outsourcing SEO to an overseas agency can work well for a US business, provided the agency researches the US search market rather than reusing work from another country, reports in plain English and keeps every account in your name. The location of the team matters far less than the quality of its process and how clearly it communicates.

We are a UK-based studio and we work remotely with US businesses on SEO, websites and paid media. This guide explains what outsourced SEO should include, how to vet an agency outside the USA, and the warning signs that tell you to walk away.

What outsourcing SEO actually means

Outsourcing SEO means handing some or all of your search work to an external team instead of hiring in-house. It ranges from a one-off audit to a monthly retainer that covers technical fixes, content and links. For most small and mid-sized businesses, it is the practical way to get specialist skills without building a department.

A sound SEO programme usually covers four areas:

  • Technical health: crawling, indexing, site speed, structured data and redirects. Our technical SEO audit checklist shows what gets checked.
  • Research and strategy: which searches your customers use and which pages should target them. See our keyword research guide for the method.
  • Content: service pages, product pages and articles that answer real questions.
  • Authority: earning mentions and links from relevant sites through digital PR and useful content.

A growing part of the work now covers AI search too: making sure your pages are clear enough to be cited in AI answers. Our explainer on AI Overviews and SEO covers what changes and what stays the same.

Outsourcing SEO to an overseas agency: the honest pros and cons

There are real advantages and real risks. Weigh them against your own situation rather than general claims.

Most of the risks are solved by the same thing: a clear written scope with research methods, reporting and communication rules agreed up front.

Make sure the research is done for the US market

This is the single most important check. Search behaviour, competitors and even words differ between countries. An agency outside the USA must run its keyword research and competitor checks with US location settings, look at US search results and write for US readers.

  • Ask which location their tools are set to when they pull keyword volumes and rankings.
  • Ask how they will handle spelling. Your website should use US spelling and phrasing, even if the agency writes in UK English internally, as we do.
  • Ask whether you need local or national SEO. A dental practice in one city needs a very different plan from an online brand shipping coast to coast. Our guide to local SEO vs national SEO explains the difference.
  • Ask how they will learn your sector. Expect questions about your customers, sales cycle and the services that make the most money.

If a proposal arrives without any of these questions being asked, the research behind it is probably generic.

What to ask before you sign

  1. What will you do in the first 90 days? A good answer names an audit, fixes, research and a content plan, in that order.
  2. How do you report? You should see rankings, organic traffic and leads or sales, explained in plain language. Read our guide to SEO reporting to know what good looks like.
  3. Whose accounts are used? Google Search Console, Google Analytics and any tools that hold your data should belong to you, with the agency given access.
  4. How do you build links? Look for digital PR and genuinely useful content. Avoid anyone selling bulk links.
  5. Who will do the work? Ask whether the team that pitches is the team that delivers, and whether any work is subcontracted.
  6. How do we communicate? Agree a regular call slot in the overlap between time zones and a written update rhythm.
  7. How do we end the contract? Check notice periods and what happens to content and data if you leave.

If you are also weighing an agency against an individual specialist, our comparison of SEO agency vs freelancer sets out the trade-offs.

Time zones, reporting and day-to-day contact

SEO is not a minute-by-minute service, which makes it well suited to remote working. Most tasks are planned weeks ahead, and the urgent moments are rare: a site migration, a sudden drop in traffic or a technical fault. Agree in advance how those urgent moments are handled.

A sensible rhythm for a US client working with a UK team looks like this:

  • A monthly call in the UK afternoon, which is the US morning.
  • A written monthly report with a short summary at the top.
  • A shared task list showing what is in progress, done and waiting on you.
  • An agreed route for urgent issues, with an expected response time.

What outsourced SEO costs and how long it takes

SEO is a medium-term investment. Results typically take 3 to 6 months to show, and depend on your starting point, competition and how quickly changes are made on your site. Anyone promising first-page rankings in weeks is selling something other than SEO.

Our monthly retainers are published in pounds: Launch at £350 a month, Growth at £750 and Scale at £1,500. Other currencies, including US dollars, are quoted on request. You can see what each level includes on our pricing page, and our guide to how much SEO costs explains what drives the price up or down.

What to outsource and what to keep in-house

You do not have to hand over everything. Many US businesses get the best results from a split, where the agency does the specialist work and the people closest to customers supply the knowledge.

  • Usually worth outsourcing: technical audits and fixes, keyword research, site structure, schema, migration planning and reporting setup.
  • Best shared: content, where the agency plans and drafts and your team adds expertise, examples and approval.
  • Usually kept in-house: customer reviews, sales insight, product knowledge and sign-off on anything regulated or legal.

Be clear about who publishes changes to the website. If your developers control the site, agree how SEO recommendations reach them and how quickly they will be put live, because fixes that sit in a queue for months cannot improve rankings. Write the split into the scope so both sides know exactly what they own from the first month, and review it at each quarterly check-in as your needs change.

Warning signs to walk away from

  • Guaranteed rankings or a guaranteed number of leads.
  • Reports full of numbers with no explanation of what they mean for your business.
  • Refusal to give you access to accounts, or accounts created in the agency's name.
  • Large numbers of links delivered every month with no explanation of where they come from.
  • Content that reads as though it was written for another country.
  • Long lock-in contracts before any work has been delivered.

FAQs

Is outsourcing SEO to an overseas agency a good idea for a US business?

It can be, if the agency researches the US market, reports clearly and keeps your accounts in your name. Judge the process and communication, not the postcode.

Will an overseas agency understand US search results?

It should, as long as keyword and competitor research is run with US location settings and the content is written for US readers. Ask exactly how they do this.

How long does outsourced SEO take to work?

Results typically take 3 to 6 months, depending on your site, your competition and how quickly recommended changes are put live.

Should I own my Google Search Console and Analytics accounts?

Yes. These accounts hold your data, so they should be created in your name, with the agency given access that you can remove at any time.

Can I pay for SEO in US dollars?

Our published prices are in pounds sterling. Other currencies, including US dollars, are quoted on request.

What should a monthly SEO report include?

A short summary, rankings for priority searches, organic traffic, leads or sales from search, the work completed and the plan for next month.
