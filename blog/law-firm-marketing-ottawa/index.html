Ottawa's legal market and how clients search

Ottawa's economy shapes its legal work. As the national capital, it generates demand for government relations, administrative and public law, procurement, employment and labour law, and regulatory work. The technology sector in Kanata and the wider region brings corporate, commercial, intellectual property and employment matters. A large and growing population means steady demand for family law, wills and estates, real estate, immigration, personal injury and criminal defence across neighbourhoods from Centretown and the Glebe to Orleans, Barrhaven and Kanata.

Private clients usually start with a search: a practice area plus Ottawa, or a description of their problem in everyday words. Business and institutional clients research more slowly. They check LinkedIn, ask colleagues and read a firm's website closely before making contact. In both cases your website is where the decision gets made. Ottawa is a bilingual city, and many firms serve clients in French as well as English. We write in English only, so French content should come from your own lawyers or a professional translator, and we will structure the site so both language versions work well.

A website that earns trust

A law firm website must explain who you help, with which problems, and how to take the first step. Visitors want practice-area pages in plain language, a sense of how you work, an explanation of how consultations and fees are approached, and simple contact routes. Business clients want depth on the issues they face and clear profiles of the lawyers they would deal with.

We build law firm websites with:

  • A structured page for each practice area and its main sub-topics.
  • Lawyer profile pages your firm writes or approves.
  • Contact forms that tell visitors not to send confidential details before a retainer is agreed.
  • Clear calls to action on every page, including phone and consultation requests.
  • Fast, accessible, mobile-first layouts and secure hosting.
  • A content management system your team can use without a developer.

A small firm website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks. Our guides to web design in Ottawa and WordPress in Ottawa explain our build process, and our article on legal website design covers the principles in a UK setting.

SEO for Ottawa law firms

SEO is often the most valuable long-term channel for law firms, because people look for answers before they look for a lawyer. We concentrate on:

  • Practice-area pages that match the words clients actually use, alongside Ottawa and the communities you serve.
  • Helpful guides answering common questions in general terms, reviewed and approved by your lawyers.
  • A complete Google Business Profile with the right categories, hours and contact details.
  • Technical health: speed, structured data, crawlability and internal linking.
  • Content written clearly enough that AI search tools can understand and quote it accurately.

Results typically take three to six months to build in competitive practice areas. Read our guides to SEO in Ottawa and local SEO in Canada, and see our SEO and GEO service for what a monthly programme includes.

Paid advertising for law firms

Google Ads can bring enquiries quickly for urgent matters, but legal keywords are some of the most expensive in search, so structure matters. We build campaigns by practice area, exclude irrelevant searches such as job seekers and free legal information, write factual ads, send each one to a matching page and track calls and form enquiries. Ad spend is paid directly to Google, separately from our fee. Our guide to Google Ads in Ottawa goes into more detail.

LinkedIn Ads suit firms selling to businesses, associations and institutions, because you can reach people by industry, seniority and job function. Meta Ads can support immigration, family and estates work with careful, informative campaigns. Our guide to LinkedIn Ads in Canada explains how we use it.

Professional rules and confidentiality

Lawyers in Ontario are regulated, and marketing by lawyers is subject to professional conduct rules covering matters such as accuracy, claims of specialisation, testimonials, comparisons and referral arrangements. Confidentiality also shapes how forms, tracking and client communications should be set up.

We give general marketing information only and are not legal or regulatory advisers, which may feel obvious when writing for lawyers but is worth stating. Your firm should check all marketing against your professional regulator's guidance, and approval of every page, ad and post rests with the firm. We support that by writing carefully, avoiding outcome promises and keeping a record of approvals.

AI-powered marketing for legal practices

We use AI-powered tools for keyword research, content outlines, competitor gap analysis and data summaries. A person always checks the output, and your lawyers approve anything published. As more people ask AI assistants for legal help, clear and well-structured practice pages make it more likely your firm is described accurately.

Measuring what matters

Legal marketing is easy to judge on the wrong numbers. Traffic and clicks are useful signals, but what a firm needs is the right enquiries in the right practice areas, at a cost that makes sense for the value of the work. We set up measurement around that from the start.

MeasureWhat it tells you
Calls and form enquiries by practice areaWhich areas the marketing is actually feeding
Enquiry quality, as judged by your intake teamWhether the right clients are getting in touch
Cost per enquiry from adsWhether a campaign is worth its spend
Search visibility for priority termsHow SEO is progressing before enquiries grow

We ask your intake team to mark enquiries simply, for example as suitable, unsuitable or outside your practice areas. No confidential details are needed for this, only a category. Over a few months this shows which pages and campaigns bring work your firm wants, so budget can move towards them. Our monthly report summarises these numbers in plain English, with a short list of what we will do next and why.
