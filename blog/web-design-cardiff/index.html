Web Design in Cardiff: Bilingual Welsh and English Websites and Sites for Public Sector Suppliers

Web design for Cardiff businesses: bilingual Welsh and English sites, accessibility, and websites that help suppliers show credibility to public sector buyers.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Bilingual Welsh and English websites need planning at the start: structure, language switching, translation workflow and SEO for both languages.
  • Firms that supply the public sector benefit from clear capability pages, policies and accessible design that buyers can check during evaluation.
  • Prism Works Studio works with Cardiff businesses remotely and has no office in Cardiff or Wales.

Web design in Cardiff often raises two questions that rarely come up elsewhere in the UK: should the site be bilingual in Welsh and English, and how should it present a business that sells to councils, health boards, universities or other public bodies? Getting either right takes planning before the first page is designed. Prism Works Studio works with Cardiff businesses remotely, handling planning, design and build over video calls and shared drafts.

This guide covers bilingual website structure, the practical side of translation, what public sector buyers may look for when they visit a supplier's site, and the accessibility basics that matter for both.

Should a Cardiff business have a bilingual website?

For public bodies in Wales, Welsh language duties apply under the Welsh Language Standards framework. For most private businesses, there is generally no blanket legal requirement to publish a bilingual website, though the position can differ for some organisations, such as those delivering services on behalf of public bodies or in certain regulated sectors. If you are unsure, check guidance from the Welsh Language Commissioner or take advice on your own situation.

Beyond the law, there are good commercial reasons to consider Welsh content:

  • Customer preference. Some customers prefer to use Welsh, and offering it can be a meaningful sign of respect and commitment to Wales.
  • Public sector contracts. If you deliver services to or on behalf of Welsh public bodies, contract terms may include Welsh language expectations.
  • Brand positioning. For businesses rooted in Welsh culture, food, tourism or education, bilingual content can be part of the identity.

Bilingual does not have to mean every page on day one. Some businesses start with key pages in Welsh, such as the homepage, main services and contact page, and expand over time. Whatever you choose, be clear and consistent about what is available in each language.

Structuring a bilingual website

The way languages are organised affects usability, editing and SEO. The main options are:

ApproachExample structureNotes
Subfolders/en/ and /cy/ on one domainCommon and straightforward to manage; both languages share the domain's authority
Separate domainsOne domain for English, another for WelshCan give a strong identity to each language but doubles domain and SEO work
Language toggle on the same URLContent swaps without the URL changingGenerally best avoided, because search engines may struggle to index both versions

For most Cardiff businesses, subfolders are the practical default. Each page should link to its equivalent in the other language, and the language switcher should be in the same place on every page, usually top right, labelled in each language rather than with flags.

Translation workflow

The biggest risk with bilingual sites is not design, it is maintenance. If English pages are updated and Welsh pages are not, the site quickly becomes inconsistent. Questions to settle early include:

  1. Who translates content, and who checks it?
  2. How will new pages and edits be flagged for translation?
  3. Will forms, error messages, cookie notices and emails also be translated?
  4. How will images with text, PDFs and downloads be handled in both languages?

Machine translation can help with drafts, but Welsh content intended for customers should be checked by a competent Welsh speaker. Poor translation can do more harm than having no translation at all.

On WordPress, multilingual plugins can link pages across languages and manage the switcher. Shopify also supports multiple languages for stores. The right setup depends on how much content you have and who will maintain it.

SEO for Welsh and English

Search engines treat each language version as its own page. To help them serve the right one:

  • Use hreflang tags so Google understands which pages are equivalents in English and Welsh.
  • Write unique titles and meta descriptions in each language rather than leaving English metadata on Welsh pages.
  • Translate URLs where it makes sense, so Welsh pages have Welsh slugs.
  • Research how people actually search in Welsh, as phrasing may differ from a direct translation.

Websites for public sector suppliers

Cardiff has a large public sector presence, with the Welsh Government, local authorities, health bodies and universities all buying goods and services. Many local businesses, from construction and facilities firms to IT, consultancy and creative suppliers, compete for that work.

A website will not win a tender on its own, since evaluation is based on the submission. But buyers and evaluators may look at a supplier's site to check who they are dealing with, and a clear, credible site can support your bid. Useful elements include:

  • Capability pages that describe exactly what you deliver and to whom.
  • Sector experience described accurately, with case studies only where you have permission to share them.
  • Policies buyers often ask about, such as environmental, equality, health and safety and modern slavery statements, where they apply to you.
  • Accreditations you genuinely hold, shown clearly.
  • Social value information, such as local employment, training or community work, kept factual.
  • Accessible design, which signals that you take inclusion seriously.

Accessibility

Public sector bodies have specific accessibility regulations for their own websites. Private businesses are not bound by those same regulations, but the Equality Act 2010 places duties on service providers that may be relevant to digital services, and building to recognised accessibility standards is good practice regardless. Clear headings, good colour contrast, descriptive link text, keyboard navigation and alt text for images all help.

Beyond the public sector, Cardiff businesses span hospitality around the city centre and Cardiff Bay, media and creative work, professional services and a large student population. Each of those has its own needs, but the principles of fast, mobile-friendly, clearly structured websites apply to all. Hospitality businesses may find our restaurant website design guide useful.

Keeping bilingual content consistent over time

Launch is the easy part of a bilingual site. The harder part is keeping both languages in step for years afterwards. A simple content log listing each page, its last update in each language and who is responsible can prevent drift. Some teams agree that no English change goes live until the Welsh version is ready, while others publish English first and set a clear deadline for the Welsh update. Either approach can work, as long as it is agreed and followed.

It is also worth thinking about news, blog posts and social content. If you publish regularly in English only, be clear about that on the Welsh side of the site, rather than leaving visitors to discover gaps by accident.

FAQs

Is a bilingual website a legal requirement for businesses in Cardiff?

For most private businesses there is generally no blanket legal requirement, but public bodies have Welsh language duties and some organisations working with them may have obligations. Check guidance from the Welsh Language Commissioner or take advice for your situation.

What is the best structure for a Welsh and English website?

For most businesses, language subfolders on one domain, such as /en/ and /cy/, are the practical choice. Each page should link to its equivalent and use hreflang tags.

Can I use machine translation for Welsh pages?

It can help with drafts, but customer-facing Welsh content should be checked by a competent Welsh speaker. Poor translation can damage trust.

Does my website matter when bidding for public sector contracts?

Tenders are judged on the submission, but buyers may look at your site to understand who you are. Clear capability pages, accurate policies and accessible design can support your credibility.

Do private businesses have to meet accessibility standards?

The public sector accessibility regulations apply to public bodies, but the Equality Act 2010 may be relevant to private service providers. Building to recognised accessibility standards is good practice either way.

Does Prism Works Studio provide Welsh translation?

No. We plan and build bilingual sites, with your translator or Welsh-speaking team supplying and checking the Welsh content.
