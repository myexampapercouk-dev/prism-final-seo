Web Design for Irish Businesses: What a Good Website Project Looks Like

Web design for Irish businesses explained: what a good site needs for Irish customers, how a remote UK studio works with you, timelines and typical costs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A website for an Irish business should be built for Irish customers: euro pricing, Irish contact details, local place names and clear answers to the questions buyers ask.
  • A remote, UK-based web design studio can plan, design and build sites for Irish companies, sharing the same time zone and a close working culture.
  • Small business websites typically take three to five weeks and our published prices are in pounds, with other currencies such as euro quoted on request.

Web design for an Irish business should produce a fast, clear website that wins the trust of Irish customers and turns visits into calls, bookings or sales. That means pages written for the Irish market, euro pricing where relevant, mobile-first design, sensible privacy and cookie handling, and a structure search engines can understand.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites for businesses in the UK, Ireland and across Europe, working remotely. We do not have an office in Ireland and will not pretend to. We provide website and ecommerce development delivered online, in the same time zone as Dublin, Cork, Galway and Limerick. This guide explains what a good project includes, how to compare designers and what it usually costs.

What Irish customers look for on a business website

Whether you are a trades business in Kildare, a clinic in Cork or a software company in Dublin, the people visiting your site want the same few things: to understand what you do, to trust you and to take the next step without friction.

  • Clear local relevance. Irish contact details, the counties or towns you serve and euro pricing tell visitors straight away that you are set up for them, not a UK firm that happens to deliver.
  • Fast pages on a phone. Most visits now happen on mobile, often on the move. Our guide to mobile website design covers what makes the difference.
  • Proof you are real. Company details, genuine reviews, photos of your work and an easy way to get in touch reduce doubt.
  • Simple next steps. A visible phone number, a short enquiry form or online booking, depending on how you sell.
  • Answers to common questions. Prices or price ranges, timescales, service areas and what happens after someone gets in touch.

Web design in Ireland: what a good project includes

A good website project follows a clear sequence, whoever builds it. The table below shows the usual stages and the points that matter most for an Irish business.

DiscoveryGoals, audience, services, competitors, content planWhich counties you serve, whether you also sell into Northern Ireland or Great Britain
StructureSitemap, page list, navigation, calls to actionSeparate pages for key services and genuine service areas
DesignLayouts for mobile and desktop, brand colours and typeAccessible contrast and readable text from the start
ContentCopy, photos, testimonials you already hold, FAQsIrish spelling conventions, place names and euro prices where shown
Build and testingDevelopment, forms, speed, devices, analyticsConsent banner set up for EU cookie rules before tracking runs
Launch and handoverGo live, redirects, training, ownership transferDomain, hosting and accounts held in your business name

Ireland and the UK share most writing conventions, so UK English copy usually reads naturally to Irish customers. Even so, references to UK-only services, sterling prices or British regulators should be swapped for their Irish equivalents.

Domains, email and hosting

Many Irish businesses prefer a .ie domain because it signals a connection to Ireland. The registry sets its own eligibility rules for .ie names, so check the current requirements before you commit. A .com domain also works well, particularly if you sell into several countries.

Whichever you choose, the domain and hosting should be registered to your business, not to the designer. Business email on your own domain looks more professional than a free address. Our guide to setting up a business domain and email walks through the options, and our guide to website ownership and handover explains what you should receive at the end of a project.

Privacy, cookies and accessibility

Irish websites fall under the EU General Data Protection Regulation and the ePrivacy rules on cookies. The Irish Data Protection Commission publishes guidance on cookie consent, and in practice most sites need a consent banner that blocks non-essential tracking until a visitor agrees. Our guide to cookie banner design covers how to make that work without harming the experience.

Accessibility matters too. The European Accessibility Act brings duties for some businesses selling products and services to consumers online, and accessible design helps every visitor anyway. We build with accessible patterns, clear headings and good contrast as standard.

We are not lawyers. Rules and guidance change, so check current official guidance or take legal advice on your privacy policy, cookie setup and accessibility obligations.

Serving Ireland, Northern Ireland and Great Britain

Plenty of Irish businesses also take work or sell products across the border and into Great Britain. One website can handle that, but the setup should be deliberate. Service businesses usually add clear area pages and state which locations they cover. Online shops may need separate euro and sterling pricing, different delivery rates and checks on how customs and VAT apply after Brexit. Planning this during discovery is far cheaper than retrofitting it after launch, and it helps search engines show the right page to the right visitor.

Choosing a web designer for your Irish business

You have more choice than ever: local freelancers, Irish agencies, website builders and remote studios. The right choice depends on your budget, how much support you want and how complex the site is. Whoever you speak to, these questions help you compare like with like:

  • What exactly is included in the price, and what costs extra?
  • Who writes the content, and who checks it?
  • Will the domain, hosting and site be owned by my business?
  • How will the site be built for search engines from day one?
  • What happens after launch if something breaks or I need changes?
  • How long will it take, and what do you need from me to keep to that?

Our guide on how to choose a web designer goes into each of these in more depth. The advice applies equally in Ireland.

How a remote web design studio works with Irish clients

Web design is well suited to remote work, because almost everything happens in shared designs, documents and a staging site you can review at any time. Being in the same time zone as Ireland makes the process feel close to working with a local team.

  1. Discovery call. We discuss your business, customers, goals and what the website needs to do.
  2. Proposal. You receive a written scope, timeline and fixed price.
  3. Structure and design. We share the page plan and designs for your feedback before building.
  4. Build. The site is built on a staging link so you can review progress as it happens.
  5. Testing and launch. We test forms, speed and devices, then go live and hand over logins and training.

If you are planning ahead, our guide to how long a website takes to build sets out what can speed a project up or slow it down.

How much does web design cost for an Irish business?

Our published price for a small business website is £1,000 to £2,000, and an ecommerce site is £1,500 to £3,500. Small websites typically take three to five weeks and ecommerce sites five to twelve weeks. Prices are in pounds by default, and other currencies such as euro can be quoted on request. VAT treatment of cross-border services depends on your business, so check it with your accountant.

Hosting, domain renewals, paid plugins or apps, and stock photography are separate costs. Ongoing SEO or advertising can follow as a monthly retainer. See our pricing page for the details, and our guide to website design costs for small businesses explains what drives the price. When you are ready, get in touch for a straight conversation about your project.

FAQs

How much does web design cost in Ireland?

Prices vary widely between designers. Our published price for a small business website is £1,000 to £2,000 and for an ecommerce site £1,500 to £3,500, with other currencies such as euro quoted on request. Hosting, domains and paid tools are extra.

Do you have an office in Ireland?

No. Prism Works Studio is based in the UK and works remotely with Irish businesses through video calls, shared designs and a staging site you can review at any time. We share a time zone with Ireland for most of the year.

Should my business use a .ie or .com domain?

A .ie domain signals a connection to Ireland and suits businesses that mainly serve Irish customers, subject to the registry's current eligibility rules. A .com can suit businesses selling across several countries. Either works well with good content and setup.

Can my website show prices in euro?

Yes. We can build your site to show euro prices, and online stores can be set up for euro checkout, with sterling or other currencies added if you sell into other markets.

How long does it take to build a website for an Irish business?

Small business websites typically take three to five weeks and ecommerce sites five to twelve weeks. Having your content, photos and decisions ready early is the biggest factor in keeping to that timeline.

Will my website be set up for Irish search results?

Yes. We build with clear page structure, fast loading, local signals such as your service areas and contact details, and correct technical setup, so search engines understand who the site is for from launch.
