Farm Park Marketing in the UK: How to Bring In More Family Visitors

Farm park marketing in the UK works when local search, online tickets, clear visitor information and seasonal events turn family day-out searches into visits.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Families plan days out quickly on their phones, so clear prices, opening times, what is included and online tickets matter more than anything else.
  • Seasonal events such as lambing, Easter, summer holidays, pumpkin picking and Christmas give you natural campaigns throughout the year.
  • Rainy day options, accessibility details and food information answer the questions that otherwise stop a family booking.

Farm park marketing in the UK is about being the obvious choice when a family searches for a day out, then answering their questions quickly enough that they book before they look elsewhere. The open farms and farm parks that grow usually combine strong local search, online tickets, honest visitor information and a calendar of seasonal events.

This guide is for farm parks, open farms, petting farms, farm attractions with play barns, and farm diversification projects adding a visitor attraction. It covers how families choose a day out, your website, tickets, local search, social media, paid ads, seasonal events, school visits, parties and repeat visits. We are a studio that builds websites and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so we focus on the digital side of bringing in visitors.

How families choose a day out

A day out is often decided the night before or that morning. Parents search for "farm park near me", "things to do with kids this weekend" or "open farm Somerset", look at a few options and pick the one that seems fun, good value and easy to get to. Weather, travel time and price usually decide it.

Parents want answers to practical questions before they set off:

  • How much does entry cost, and are under-twos free?
  • What is there to do, and how long will it keep children busy?
  • Is there indoor play if it rains?
  • Is there a café, and can we bring a picnic?
  • Is it pushchair and wheelchair friendly?
  • Do we need to book in advance?
  • Are dogs allowed?

If your website answers all of these clearly on a phone, you are already ahead of many attractions.

Farm park marketing UK: your website and visitor information

Your homepage should show prices, opening times and a book tickets button near the top. Families on a phone should not have to hunt for any of these. Beyond that, a few pages earn their place on most farm park sites:

  • Prices and tickets, with adult, child, under-two, family and annual pass options.
  • What's on, with daily activities such as animal feeding times, tractor rides or shows.
  • Plan your visit, covering parking, directions, accessibility, dogs, picnics and facilities.
  • Events, with a page for each seasonal event.
  • Parties, with party packages and what is included.
  • School and group visits, with educational content and booking details.
  • Café and shop, with menus and opening times.

A detailed FAQ page can answer many of the questions that otherwise come in by phone or message. Our guide to website FAQ pages explains how to structure one so it helps both visitors and search engines.

Online tickets and capacity

Online tickets help you manage busy days, plan staffing and reduce queues. Many families now expect to book ahead, especially during school holidays and events. Some attractions offer timed entry on busy days and walk-up tickets on quieter ones.

When choosing a system, check that it works smoothly on mobile, handles family tickets and annual passes, lets you limit capacity by day or time slot, and sends a clear confirmation email. Our guide to website online booking systems covers what to look for and how to connect booking to your website.

Local search and Google Business Profile

Most farm park searches are local, so your Google Business Profile matters a great deal. Make sure your category, opening hours, seasonal closures, photos and attributes such as accessibility and facilities are accurate. Update holiday hours well before each school break.

Photos are especially important. Show animals, play areas, the café and families enjoying the space, with permission where people are identifiable. Encourage happy visitors to leave reviews, and respond to every review politely. Our guide to Google Business Profile in the UK covers setup and ongoing management, and our local SEO guide explains how to rank for searches in your area.

Seasonal events through the year

Seasonal events give farm parks a reason to market throughout the year and bring families back more than once. Each event should have its own page published well in advance so it can be found in search.

Promote each event through email first, then social media, then paid ads if it needs more reach. Keep event pages live year round and update them for the next season, so they build search visibility over time instead of starting from scratch.

Social media and paid ads

Farm parks have a natural advantage on social media: animals. Short videos of feeding time, new arrivals, tractor rides and seasonal activities are easy to make and widely shared. Post regularly, show what a visit is really like, and always include how to book.

Paid social ads can reach families within a sensible travel time of your farm, especially before school holidays and events. Our guide to Facebook ads for small businesses explains how to get started. Paid search can capture high-intent searches such as "farm park near me" or "pumpkin picking near me" during peak periods. If you would like help, our paid media service covers Google, Meta and TikTok campaigns.

School visits, groups and parties

School and group visits can fill weekdays during term time, when family visitor numbers are lower. Create a dedicated school visits page that explains what children will learn, how sessions link to the curriculum, group sizes, prices, facilities, risk assessment information and how to book. Teachers often need this information to get approval from their school.

Birthday parties can also be a steady source of bookings. Explain what each party package includes, such as a party room, animal handling session, food and play time, and give clear prices. Nurseries, childminders, home education groups and community groups can be marketed to directly by email or through local networks.

Animal welfare, hygiene and visitor safety

Visitors increasingly want to know that animals are well cared for and that the farm takes hygiene seriously. Explain your approach plainly on your website, such as handwashing stations, supervised animal contact and how animals rest away from visitors.

Rules and guidance on animal contact, hygiene and licensing for visitor attractions can change. Keep any wording general, describe what you actually do, and check current guidance to make sure your practices and website information are up to date.

Email and repeat visits

Families who enjoyed a visit are your best prospects for the next one. Collect email addresses through ticket bookings and a newsletter signup, with clear consent under UK GDPR and PECR. Send updates before each school holiday and seasonal event, and promote annual passes where they make sense. Our guide to email list building covers simple ways to grow your list.

FAQs

What is the best approach to farm park marketing in the UK?

Start with a fast, mobile-friendly website showing prices, opening times and online tickets, then make your Google Business Profile complete. Add seasonal event pages, regular social media and targeted paid ads before school holidays.

Should a farm park sell tickets online?

In most cases, yes. Online tickets help families plan, let you manage capacity on busy days and reduce queues. Many attractions offer both pre-booked and walk-up options depending on the day.

When should we start promoting seasonal events?

Usually six to eight weeks ahead for major events like lambing, summer holidays, pumpkin picking and Christmas. Publish event pages early so they have time to appear in search.

How do we attract school visits?

Create a clear school visits page with learning outcomes, curriculum links, group sizes, prices, facilities and risk assessment information. Then contact local schools and nurseries directly, ideally in the term before you want to fill.

What should we say about animal contact and hygiene?

Describe what you actually do, such as handwashing facilities and supervised contact. Keep wording general and accurate, and check current guidance on animal contact and hygiene to make sure your information stays up to date.

Which social media works best for farm parks?

Facebook and Instagram suit most farm parks because they reach local parents, and short animal videos also work well on TikTok. Choose the platforms your visitors use and post regularly with clear booking links.
