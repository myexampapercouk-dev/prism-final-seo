Shopify Store Policies: Setting Up Refund, Privacy and Shipping Pages

How to set up refund, privacy, terms of service and shipping policies in Shopify, where they appear, and how to make them clear and easy for customers to find.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify has a dedicated policies area that links your key policies into checkout and the store footer.
  • Templates are a starting point only; policies should reflect how your business actually operates.
  • UK consumer and data protection rules may apply, so take professional advice on the wording.

Store policies are not the most exciting part of running an online shop, but customers look for them more often than you might think. Before buying, many people want to know how returns work, when an order will arrive and what happens to their personal data. Clear policies answer those questions, reduce support emails and help customers feel confident placing an order.

Shopify has a dedicated area for policies that connects them to checkout and other parts of your store. This guide explains how it works, what each policy usually covers and how to present them well. It is general guidance, not legal advice. Consumer protection and data protection rules can apply to UK online sellers, so take advice from a qualified professional on the exact wording for your business.

Where store policies live in Shopify

In the Shopify admin, go to Settings, then Policies. Here you can add and edit several standard policies, typically including:

  • Refund policy
  • Privacy policy
  • Terms of service
  • Shipping policy
  • Contact information

Shopify may offer templates for some of these. Templates are handy for getting the structure right, but they are generic. Read every line and change it to match how your business really operates. A policy that promises something you do not do causes more problems than it solves.

Shopify also provides settings for return rules in some regions, which can tie your stated return window to how returns are handled in the admin. Check what is available for your store, as features vary over time and by plan.

Where policies appear for customers

Once saved, policies are published at their own URLs on your store. Shopify typically links them in the checkout so customers can read them before paying. Most themes also let you add them to the footer menu, which is where shoppers usually look.

Check each of these places:

  • Footer menu. Add links to each policy in your footer navigation. Our guide to Shopify navigation menus explains how to edit menus.
  • Checkout. Review the checkout to confirm the policy links appear and work.
  • Product pages. Short summaries, such as a line about delivery and returns near the add to cart button, help customers without making them leave the page.
  • Help or FAQ page. If you have one, link to the full policies from it.

Refund and returns policy

This is often the policy customers care about most. It should explain, in plain language:

  • How long customers have to request a return
  • What condition items need to be in
  • Which items cannot be returned, such as personalised or perishable goods, where that is permitted
  • How to start a return and where to send items
  • Who pays for return postage
  • How and when refunds are issued
  • How exchanges work, if you offer them

UK customers buying online may have statutory rights, including cancellation rights for many distance sales and rights when goods are faulty. Your policy cannot remove those rights. Your own returns offer can be more generous than the law requires, but it should not suggest customers have fewer rights than they do. A solicitor or a trusted trading standards resource can help you check this.

Privacy policy

Your privacy policy explains what personal data you collect, why, how long you keep it and who you share it with. For a Shopify store this usually includes order and payment details, account information, marketing preferences and data collected through cookies and analytics.

Points to think about:

  • List the categories of data you collect and the purpose of each
  • Explain the services that process data on your behalf, such as payment, email and shipping providers, in general terms
  • Describe how customers can access, correct or delete their data
  • Explain your use of cookies and link to cookie controls where relevant
  • Give a contact route for privacy questions

UK data protection law may apply to your store, and requirements can depend on what you collect and how you use it. Treat the template as a draft and get it reviewed. Our guide to cookie banner design covers how consent fits alongside your privacy policy.

Every app or integration you add can change what data is collected. Review the privacy policy whenever you add or remove something significant. Our Shopify apps audit guide can help you keep track.

Terms of service

Terms of service set out the general rules for using your store and buying from you. They often cover:

  • Who you are and how to contact you
  • How orders are accepted and when a contract is formed
  • Pricing, errors and availability
  • Payment methods
  • Limits on liability, where permitted
  • Intellectual property in your content
  • Which law governs the terms

Terms are the most legal of the policies, and generic wording can conflict with consumer rights. Professional advice is worthwhile here, particularly if you sell internationally or to businesses as well as consumers.

Shipping policy

A shipping policy explains where you deliver, how much it costs and how long it takes. It should match your actual shipping settings in Shopify. If the checkout shows one price and the policy another, customers lose trust quickly.

Include:

  • The countries or regions you ship to
  • Delivery options and how costs are calculated
  • Typical dispatch and delivery times, worded carefully as estimates
  • Any order cut-off times for same-day dispatch
  • What happens if a parcel is lost or damaged
  • Information on customs and import charges for international orders

If you offer collection, explain how it works. Our guide to Shopify local pickup covers that set-up.

Contact information

Shopify includes a contact information policy where you can add your business name, address and contact details. Clear contact details help customers trust that a real business is behind the store. Depending on your business type, there may also be legal requirements about what information you display, so check what applies to you.

Writing policies customers will actually read

Policies are often written defensively and in dense legal language. A few habits make them far more useful:

  • Lead with a short summary. A few sentences at the top covering the essentials, followed by the full detail.
  • Use headings. Customers scan for the bit they need, such as how to return an item.
  • Use plain English. Write as you would explain it to a customer on the phone.
  • Keep the tone consistent with your brand. Policies can be clear and friendly without being casual about obligations.
  • Date your updates. Add a last updated note so customers know the information is current.

Policies for special product types

Some products need extra explanation beyond the standard pages. Personalised or made-to-order items may be treated differently for returns, and the reasons should be explained clearly. Subscriptions need clear information about billing, renewals and how to cancel. Pre-orders need honest wording about when items are expected to ship and what happens if dates move. Gift cards may need terms about expiry and use, and rules on these can vary, so check what applies before setting them.

Where possible, explain these points on the product page as well as in the full policy, so customers see them before they buy rather than after. Our guide to Shopify pre-orders covers how to set expectations for items that are not yet in stock.

Keeping policies up to date

Policies drift out of date when the business changes but the text does not. Review them whenever you:

  • Change couriers, delivery prices or delivery areas
  • Start selling to new countries
  • Add or remove apps that handle customer data
  • Change your returns window or process
  • Launch new product types, such as personalised items or subscriptions

Setting a regular reminder to review your policies is a simple way to catch changes you might otherwise miss.

FAQs

Can I just use the Shopify policy templates?

They are a useful starting point, but they are generic. Edit them to reflect how your business works and consider getting them reviewed by a qualified professional.

Where should policies appear on my store?

Link them in the footer, check they appear at checkout and add short delivery and returns summaries to product pages.

Do I need a privacy policy for a small shop?

If you collect personal data, which any store taking orders does, data protection rules may require you to explain how you use it. Take advice on what applies to your business.

Can my returns policy override customer rights?

Generally no. Statutory consumer rights may still apply regardless of what your policy says. Your policy can offer more, but should not suggest customers have fewer rights.

How often should I review my policies?

Whenever your shipping, returns, apps or markets change, and on a regular schedule in between.
