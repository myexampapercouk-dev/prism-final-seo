How Ottawa patients find a new dentist

Ottawa's population is spread across a wide area. Patients in Centretown, the Glebe, Westboro, Orleans, Kanata, Barrhaven, Nepean and Stittsville each tend to look for a clinic close to home or to work, and many commuters choose a dentist near their office. Public servants, students at the city's universities, young families and retirees all search a little differently, but almost all of them start on Google.

The typical journey is short. Someone searches for a dentist near them, an emergency appointment, a cleaning, implants, Invisalign-style aligners or a children's dentist. They look at the map results, open two or three profiles, read recent reviews, glance at photos and opening hours, and then visit the website of the clinic that looks most credible. If booking is clear, they book. If it is not, they go back to the list.

Ottawa is also a bilingual city, with many French-speaking patients on both sides of the river. We write in English only. If you want French pages, the copy should come from your own team or a professional translator, and we will build the structure so that both language versions work properly in search.

A dental website built for booking

Most dental website visits happen on a phone. The page needs to load quickly, show the clinic's location and hours straight away, and make calling or booking a single tap. Beyond that, patients want to see the people who will treat them and understand what a treatment involves before they commit.

A typical Prism dental website includes:

  • Home, about, team, contact and a page for each treatment you want to promote.
  • A location page for each clinic, with parking, transit and accessibility details.
  • Booking links connected to your existing booking system, plus click-to-call buttons.
  • Information on new-patient visits, insurance billing questions and what to bring, written and approved by your clinic.
  • Fast hosting, good accessibility and a content management system your team can use.

A small clinic website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks. Prices in Canadian dollars are quoted on request. Read our guides to web design in Ottawa and WordPress in Ottawa for how we approach builds in the city.

Local SEO for Ottawa dental clinics

Local SEO is usually the best long-term source of new patients. It has two parts: appearing in the map pack for searches near your clinic, and ranking in the normal results for treatment searches.

  • Profile set-up: the right primary category, accurate services, holiday hours, real photos and a booking link.
  • Reviews: a consistent, policy-compliant way of asking for reviews and replying without revealing any patient information.
  • Treatment content: helpful pages answering the questions patients ask before treatment, approved by your dentists.
  • Location signals: consistent name, address and phone details across directories, and natural mentions of the suburbs you serve.
  • Technical SEO: page speed, structured data, internal links and clean indexing.

Our guide to SEO in Ottawa covers search in the city more broadly, and our overview of local SEO in Canada explains how map rankings work. SEO results typically take three to six months to build.

Google Ads and Meta Ads

Google Ads suits urgent and high-value searches. A patient with toothache wants a clinic today, and a well-structured campaign can put your number in front of them. We build campaigns by treatment, add negative keywords to cut wasted clicks, write factual ads, send each one to a matching page and track calls and booking requests. Ad spend is paid directly to Google and is separate from our fee. See our guide to Google Ads in Ottawa for more.

Meta Ads on Facebook and Instagram can reach households within a set radius of your clinic, which is helpful when you open a new location or want to fill hygiene appointments. Health advertising has platform rules on targeting and claims, so we keep audiences broad and wording careful. Our guide to Meta Ads in Ottawa explains our approach.

Regulation and patient privacy

Dentists in Ontario are regulated health professionals, and there are rules on how they may advertise, including limits on testimonials, comparative claims, guarantees and some types of images. Privacy law also covers patient information, including what arrives through your website forms.

We are not legal or regulatory advisers and give general marketing information only. Your clinic should check its marketing against guidance from your professional regulator and, where needed, your own adviser. Approval of every page, ad and post rests with the clinic. We make that easier by keeping wording factual, avoiding outcome promises, keeping forms short and recording approvals.

Reviews: the quiet driver of new patients

In Ottawa, as in most cities, reviews often decide which clinic a patient calls. A clinic with a steady flow of recent, detailed reviews looks active and trusted, and Google treats recent reviews as one of several signals for map rankings. Yet many clinics only collect reviews in occasional bursts, or not at all.

A simple, sustainable review process usually works best:

  • Ask every patient in the same way, for example with a short message after their appointment, rather than choosing only happy patients.
  • Never offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, which breaks platform rules.
  • Reply to every review politely and briefly, and never confirm that the reviewer is a patient or mention any treatment details.
  • Take any complaint offline with an invitation to contact the clinic directly.
  • Check your professional regulator's guidance on how reviews may be used, especially before quoting any on your website.

We help clinics set up the request messages, a reply approach your team can follow, and a monthly check of new reviews. Combined with a complete profile and good photos, this often makes a noticeable difference to how many people click through from the map.
