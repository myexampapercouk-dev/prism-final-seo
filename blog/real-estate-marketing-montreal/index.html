Real Estate Marketing in Montreal: Websites, SEO and Lead Generation

Real estate marketing in Montreal: listing websites, SEO, Google and Meta ads for brokers and developers from a UK-based remote studio with no Quebec office.

Real estate marketing in Montreal means putting your listings, projects and expertise in front of buyers, sellers and renters, then turning that interest into enquiries through a clear website, local SEO and targeted ads. Prism Works Studio builds property websites and runs search and social campaigns for Montreal brokers, developers and property managers, working remotely from the UK.

We are a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio. We have no office in Montreal, in Quebec or anywhere in Canada. Who we work with in Montreal

Montreal's property market is varied, from condos in Griffintown and the Plateau to family homes on the West Island, plexes in Rosemont and new towers downtown. We support:

  • Residential brokers and teams who want more seller and buyer leads from their own website rather than relying only on portals.
  • Developers launching condo or rental projects that need pre-launch registration pages and campaigns.
  • Property managers filling rental units and attracting landlord clients.
  • Commercial real estate firms letting office, retail and industrial space.
  • Mortgage and real estate service businesses that support buyers, subject to their own compliance checks.

A property website built for two languages

A Montreal property site usually needs to serve French and English speakers well. That affects structure as much as copy. We plan separate, properly linked language versions rather than a single page with a translation widget, so search engines can index each version and visitors land on the language they searched in.

For each version we set clear page titles, headings and descriptions, and we make sure forms, confirmation messages and emails work in both languages. Your team or your translator writes and approves the French text. We handle layout, speed, technical SEO and the links between the two versions.

A good structure for most brokers includes:

  • A homepage that says who you serve and where, with a clear call to action.
  • Neighbourhood pages for the areas you actually sell in, written with local knowledge.
  • A home valuation page with a short form, because seller leads are often the most valuable.
  • Listing pages or a listings feed, depending on what your brokerage and board rules allow.
  • Pages for buyers, sellers, renters and investors, each answering their common questions.
  • A contact page with phone, email and a simple booking option.

If you are planning a build, our guide to web design in Montreal covers timelines and costs in more detail. A smaller website typically takes three to five weeks once copy and photos are ready.

Local SEO for Montreal neighbourhoods

People rarely search for homes in Montreal as a whole. They search for a borough, a street they know or a type of property, often on their phone. Local SEO helps your pages appear for those searches and helps your Google Business Profile appear in map results.

Our SEO work for property businesses usually covers:

  • Keyword research in English, with French terms gathered from your team, for each area and property type.
  • Neighbourhood pages with real detail on transit, schools, parks and housing types, written or checked by people who know the area.
  • Technical fixes so listing pages load quickly and are indexed correctly.
  • Google Business Profile updates, categories, photos and a steady approach to asking clients for reviews.
  • Consistent business details across directories and listing sites.

SEO results typically take three to six months to build. For a wider view of search in the city, read our guide to SEO in Montreal, and our overview of local SEO across Canada explains the map pack in more depth.

Google Ads for buyers and sellers

Google Ads put you in front of people who are actively searching, such as someone looking for a condo in Saint-Henri or a broker to sell a duplex. We build campaigns around tightly grouped keywords, location targeting for the areas you cover and landing pages that match each search.

Seller campaigns usually point to a valuation page. Buyer campaigns point to area pages or project pages. We set up conversion tracking for form fills and calls so you can see which searches lead to real conversations. Property is a special ad category on some platforms, which limits certain targeting options, so we plan campaigns within those rules. Our guide to Google Ads in Montreal explains structure and budgeting.

Meta and Instagram Ads for listings and launches

Property is visual, which suits Facebook and Instagram. We use carousel ads for listings, lead forms for project registrations and remarketing to people who already visited your site. Housing ads fall under special ad category rules on Meta, which restrict targeting by age, gender and narrow location, so creative and messaging do more of the work.

Campaigns run in the languages your team supplies copy for. We often recommend separate ad sets for French and English so performance can be compared fairly. Our page on Meta Ads in Montreal goes into more detail.

Lead handling and follow-up

Ads and SEO only pay off if leads are answered quickly. We help set up simple systems so every enquiry reaches the right person:

  • Form notifications sent to the right broker or team inbox.
  • Clear tracking of the page and campaign each lead came from.
  • Integration with your CRM where it offers a standard connection.
  • Automated confirmation emails in the visitor's language, using text your team approves.

We also use AI-powered tools to help sort enquiries, draft first replies for your review and spot patterns in which pages convert. A person on your team always checks and sends the final message.

Privacy, consent and advertising rules

Property marketing collects personal information, so privacy matters. In general terms, PIPEDA sets federal rules for how private-sector organisations handle personal information, and Quebec's Law 25 adds its own requirements for businesses in the province, including obligations around consent, transparency and privacy governance. CASL covers commercial electronic messages such as marketing emails and texts, and generally requires consent and a working unsubscribe option.

Real estate advertising in Quebec is also subject to rules from the provincial regulator and professional bodies. These notes are general information only. Please check official guidance and speak to your own legal adviser or broker compliance contact. Final approval of ads, forms and privacy wording rests with you. We build cookie banners, consent checkboxes and privacy page layouts to match the wording you approve.
