Why Berlin brands choose Shopify

Berlin has a lively direct-to-consumer scene, from fashion and streetwear labels to coffee roasters, cosmetics, design objects and sustainable goods. Many of these brands start small and need a platform that is quick to launch, easy for a lean team to run and able to scale into other European markets later. Shopify fits that brief well.

  • Fast launch: a well-planned store can go live in weeks rather than months.
  • Simple day-to-day running: products, orders, discounts and stock are managed in one admin.
  • Payments German shoppers expect: Shopify supports a range of payment methods, and apps can add more, so you can offer options your customers trust.
  • Europe-wide growth: Shopify Markets helps you sell in several countries with local currencies and languages.
  • A large app ecosystem: reviews, subscriptions, loyalty, shipping and accounting tools plug in without custom code.

Selling to German shoppers: what to get right

German online shoppers are often careful and detail-focused. A store that feels unclear or incomplete can lose sales even when the products are strong. These are the areas we pay the most attention to during a Berlin build.

Clear legal information: German and EU rules set out what information online shops must show, from company details to cancellation rights. We build the pages and checkout links where this information lives, but the legal wording itself should come from your lawyer or a specialist service. Keep it general in your planning and check official guidance or your own adviser for specifics.

Transparent pricing: show prices clearly, including VAT, along with delivery costs and timescales before checkout.

Trusted payments: offer payment methods your customers already use and trust. We help you choose and configure them inside Shopify.

Privacy and consent: GDPR applies, so analytics and marketing tags should run through a consent tool. We set up tracking to respect consent choices.

Accessibility: the European Accessibility Act affects many online shops selling to EU consumers. We design with readable contrast, clear labels and keyboard-friendly navigation, and you should confirm your own obligations with an adviser.

What to prepare before your Shopify project

Projects move fastest when a few things are ready before the build starts. You do not need everything on day one, but the earlier these arrive, the sooner the store can launch.

  • Product data: names, prices, variants, weights and stock levels, ideally in a spreadsheet.
  • Images: consistent product photos and a few lifestyle shots for banners.
  • Brand assets: logo files, colours and fonts.
  • German copy: home page, collection and product text from your team or translator.
  • Legal text: Impressum, privacy policy, terms and returns wording from your adviser.
  • Shipping plan: which countries you deliver to, carriers and rates.

We share a simple checklist at the start of every project so nothing is missed.

Working with a remote Shopify agency

A remote agency suits many Berlin brands. The time difference between Berlin and the UK is one hour, so working hours overlap for most of the day. We keep every project organised with a shared timeline, written decisions and regular check-ins.
