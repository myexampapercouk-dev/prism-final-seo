Why Ljubljana businesses need a strong website

Ljubljana is a compact capital with an outsized mix of businesses: technology and software firms, pharmaceutical and life sciences suppliers, design studios, tourism and hospitality, professional services and a growing number of online shops. Many of them sell beyond Slovenia, into Austria, Italy, Croatia, Germany and the wider European market.

For these companies the website does a lot of work. It is often the first thing a foreign buyer, investor or visitor sees, and it needs to explain clearly what you do, build trust quickly and make the next step easy. A slow, dated site or one that only works well on desktop loses those visitors before they ever make contact.

Choosing a platform

The best platform depends on what the site needs to do and who will look after it. For service businesses and B2B firms, WordPress is often a good fit because it is flexible and easy for your team to edit. For online shops, Shopify handles payments, stock and shipping reliably and needs less technical upkeep. For simple brochure sites that rarely change, a lightweight static build can be extremely fast and secure.

We talk through the options before recommending one. If you are deciding between online shop platforms, our comparison of Shopify and WooCommerce sets out the trade-offs. If you plan to sell across Europe, our guide to Shopify Markets for Europe explains currencies, languages and local payment options.

Bilingual websites done properly

Most Ljubljana businesses need at least two languages: Slovenian for local customers and English for international buyers and visitors. Some also add German, Italian or Croatian. A bilingual site has to be planned from the start, not bolted on later.

We build a clear structure, usually separate language folders, with a visible language switcher and hreflang tags that tell search engines which page is which. Each language version gets its own titles, descriptions and URLs, so it can rank in its own right. Slovenian uses characters such as č, š and ž, and we make sure fonts, forms and URLs handle them correctly.

We write English copy and content briefs. Slovenian text comes from your team or a translator, and we place it carefully so layouts still look right, since translated text can be longer or shorter than the original.

Speed, accessibility and search

A beautiful site that loads slowly loses visitors. We keep pages lean, compress images, limit third-party scripts and test on real mobile connections. Our guide to Core Web Vitals explains the speed measures Google uses.

We also follow good accessibility practice: readable text sizes, strong colour contrast, descriptive links, labelled form fields and pages that work with a keyboard. European accessibility requirements now apply to many online services, so it is worth building them in from the start. For the details that apply to your business, check official guidance or speak to your adviser.

What to prepare before a website project

Projects run faster and cost less when a few things are ready before design starts. You do not need everything finished, but having a clear picture of the points below saves weeks of back and forth:

  • Your goals. Decide what the site should achieve, such as enquiries, bookings, online sales or recruitment, and how you will measure it.
  • Your audiences. Note who visits, which languages they read and what they need to know before contacting you.
  • Your content. Gather existing text, photos, logos, product data and any approved material you want to show.
  • Your translator. Agree who will write or check the Slovenian text and how quickly they can turn pages around.
  • Your accounts. Collect logins for your domain, hosting, analytics and any existing site, so nothing holds up launch.

If you are replacing an older site, we also list every existing page that gets traffic or links, so we can redirect each one to its new home. Skipping this step is one of the most common reasons a redesign loses search visibility, and it is easy to avoid with a little planning.
