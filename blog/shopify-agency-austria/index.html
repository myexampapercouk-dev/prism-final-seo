Setting up Shopify for Austrian shoppers

Austrian shoppers expect a store that feels local. That starts with clear German copy and goes on to settings that build trust at checkout. We work through:

  • Language and markets. German as the main language, with optional English, and Shopify Markets set up for Austria, Germany, Switzerland and other EU countries where you sell.
  • Payments. Card payments plus the methods your customers prefer, which often include invoice and bank-based options through supported payment providers.
  • Tax. Prices shown including VAT, with settings that you confirm with your accountant, including EU distance selling rules.
  • Delivery and returns. Clear shipping zones, rates and a returns page that reflects your legal duties and your own policy.
  • Legal pages. Imprint, terms, privacy notice and withdrawal information, supplied or approved by your adviser.

We set up the store, and the legal and tax decisions stay with you and your advisers.

Privacy, cookies and accessibility

Online shops in Austria fall under European rules. These notes are general, so please check official guidance or ask your own adviser.

  • GDPR covers customer accounts, orders, newsletter sign-ups and tracking data. Your privacy notice should explain what you collect and why.
  • ePrivacy and cookie consent rules generally require consent before analytics and advertising cookies are set. We configure Shopify's consent tools and tracking so they respect visitor choices.
  • The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 and covers many e-commerce services. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt. The harmonised standard EN 301 549 points to WCAG level AA, which we use as the target for store design.

Our website accessibility guide covers practical checks such as contrast, keyboard access and clear form labels.

Choosing a Shopify agency for Austria

Ask how an agency handles German content, payments and tax settings, who owns the store and its apps, and how it protects search traffic during a migration. Be wary of promises about sales figures, because results depend on your products, pricing and market. A clear written plan and a private preview before launch are good signs.

Common Shopify mistakes we see with Austrian stores

Austrian brands often launch quickly on Shopify and then find small problems that hold sales back. Most are easy to fix once they are spotted, and fixing them often improves both conversions and search visibility.

  • Mixed-language stores. Product pages are in German, but checkout messages, emails or filters still appear in English. We review every template and notification so the store reads consistently.
  • Too many apps. Each app adds code that can slow the store down. We review installed apps, remove those you no longer need and look for lighter alternatives.
  • Thin collection pages. Collections show a grid of products but no helpful text. A short introduction in German, written by your team or translator, helps shoppers and search engines understand the range.
  • Unclear delivery information. Shoppers in Austria, Germany and Switzerland want to know delivery times and costs before checkout. We add clear delivery notes to product pages and the cart.
  • Weak product images. Inconsistent sizes and backgrounds make a store look less professional. We set image guidelines so new products always match.
  • Missing structured data. Without product markup, search engines get less information about price and availability. We check the theme outputs it correctly.

We also help with seasonal planning. Many Austrian brands see strong demand around Christmas, winter sports and the summer outdoor season. Planning collections, landing pages and ad budgets a few weeks ahead makes those peaks easier to handle and avoids rushed changes during the busiest weeks.

After launch, we train your team on everyday tasks such as adding products, editing collections and running discounts, so you are not reliant on us for small changes. If you choose a retainer, we then focus on the work that grows sales over time: SEO, paid campaigns and conversion improvements based on what shoppers actually do on the store.
