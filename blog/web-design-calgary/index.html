Web Design for Calgary Businesses: How a Remote Website Project Works

Web design for Calgary businesses: how a remote build runs on Mountain Time, what affects cost and timelines, and what to check before you hire a web designer.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Calgary business can commission a website remotely as long as the project has a clear brief, agreed review points and call times that suit Mountain Time.
  • Most small business websites take three to five weeks once content is ready, while ecommerce builds usually take five to twelve weeks depending on scope.
  • Before hiring any web designer, confirm who owns the domain, hosting and code, how the site will be maintained and how search rankings will be protected at launch.

Good web design for a Calgary business means a website that loads quickly on a phone, tells visitors what you do within seconds and makes it easy to call, book or buy. That kind of site does not have to be built in Calgary: Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites for Canadian clients remotely, and we have no office or staff in Calgary or anywhere else in Alberta.

This guide explains what a Calgary business should expect from a website project, how remote work runs across the time difference, what drives cost and timelines, and the questions to ask before you commit to any designer.

What web design in Calgary should deliver

Calgary has a wide mix of businesses: energy services and engineering firms, trades and home services across the suburbs, clinics, restaurants, tourism operators heading towards the Rockies and a growing tech scene. The sites differ, but the fundamentals do not:

  • A clear message. What you do, who it is for and where you work, above the fold.
  • A mobile-first layout. Many visitors will arrive on a phone, often from a search or a map listing. Our guide to mobile website design covers what good looks like.
  • Easy enquiries. Forms that are short and work properly, click-to-call buttons and booking where it makes sense.
  • Search foundations. Clean page structure, sensible titles and headings, fast loading and a sitemap, so the site has a fair chance to rank.
  • Accessibility. Good contrast, readable text, keyboard-friendly navigation and labelled forms.
  • Simple editing. You should be able to update hours, prices or team pages without paying a developer each time.

How a remote website project runs on Mountain Time

The UK is usually seven hours ahead of Calgary. In practice that gives a useful rhythm: we work through the UK day and have drafts ready when your office opens, and calls are booked for a UK afternoon, which is a Calgary morning.

What a website costs and how long it takes

Our published prices are in pounds sterling: a small business website is typically £1,000 to £2,000, and an ecommerce site typically £1,500 to £3,500, depending on pages, features and integrations. We can quote in other currencies on request. The full breakdown is on our pricing page.

Timelines depend mostly on content. A small business website usually takes three to five weeks once copy and images are ready, and an ecommerce build typically takes five to twelve weeks. Our article on how long it takes to build a website explains where projects tend to slip.

Choosing the right platform

Most Calgary service businesses are well served by WordPress, which is flexible and easy to edit. Retailers and product brands usually do better on Shopify, which handles payments, stock and shipping well. Our guide to Shopify for Canadian brands explains when it fits. We recommend a platform based on what you sell and who will run the site day to day, not on what is easiest for us.

Whatever the platform, make sure you own the result. Our article on website ownership and handover lists what should be in your name: the domain, hosting account, analytics and admin access.

Protecting your search rankings during a rebuild

If your current site already brings in enquiries from Google, a redesign can put that at risk. The usual causes are changed URLs without redirects, deleted pages that ranked well and lost page titles. Before launch we map old URLs to new ones, keep content that performs and check indexing afterwards. Our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings goes through the process step by step.

A new site is also a good moment to plan local visibility. If you also run search ads, our guide to Google Ads for Calgary businesses explains how landing pages and paid campaigns should work together.

Privacy and accessibility

Canadian websites that collect personal data through forms, analytics or ads need a clear privacy policy and sensible consent handling. Private-sector businesses in Alberta have obligations under provincial privacy law, and federal law can also apply, while accessibility expectations continue to rise across Canada. We build with good practice in mind, but we are not lawyers: check current official guidance or take legal advice for your own situation.

Common web design mistakes to avoid

Many of the problems we see on small business websites have nothing to do with how they look. They come from decisions made early in the project:

  • Starting design before content. Layouts built around placeholder text rarely survive real copy. Planning content first saves rework.
  • Too many pages that say little. A focused site with strong service pages usually beats a sprawling one full of thin content.
  • Hiding the contact route. If someone has to hunt for a phone number or form, many will leave and try a competitor.
  • Heavy images and effects. Large uncompressed photos and animation slow pages down, especially on mobile data.
  • No tracking at launch. Without analytics and form tracking in place from day one, you cannot tell whether the new site is working better than the old one.

After launch: keeping the site healthy

A website is not finished on launch day. Software needs updating, backups need checking and content needs refreshing as your services change. Some clients handle this themselves after the handover call; others prefer an ongoing support arrangement. Either way, agree in writing who is responsible for updates, security and backups, so nothing falls between the cracks. It is also worth reviewing the site every few months: check which pages bring in enquiries, which ones visitors leave quickly and whether any information has gone out of date.

What to prepare before the first call

  • Two or three websites you like, and a note of why.
  • A list of your main services or products, in order of importance.
  • Your logo and any brand colours or fonts. If the brand needs work first, our brand strategy service can come before the build.
  • Login details for your domain registrar and current hosting, shared securely when the project starts.
  • Photos of your work, premises or team, if you have them.

FAQs

How much does web design in Calgary cost?

With us, a small business website is typically £1,000 to £2,000 and an ecommerce site typically £1,500 to £3,500, with other currencies quoted on request. Other designers price differently, so compare what is included rather than the headline figure.

Does Prism Works Studio have an office in Calgary?

No. We are a UK-based studio working with Canadian clients remotely through video calls, shared documents and staging links. Calls are usually booked for Calgary mornings.

How long does a new website take?

A small business website usually takes three to five weeks once content is ready. Ecommerce builds typically take five to twelve weeks, depending on the catalogue, integrations and how quickly feedback comes back.

Will I be able to update the website myself?

Yes. We build sites so you can edit text, images, opening hours and similar details yourself, and we finish every project with a walkthrough of the editor.

Can you redesign my existing site without losing Google rankings?

We plan redirects, keep content that already ranks and check indexing after launch to reduce that risk. No one can guarantee rankings, but a careful migration avoids the most common causes of lost traffic.

Do you build bilingual English and French websites?

We write in English. If you need French pages, they are written with your team or a professional translator, and we set up the structure so each language version works properly for visitors and search engines.
