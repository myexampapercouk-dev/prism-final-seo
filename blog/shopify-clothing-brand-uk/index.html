Shopify for UK Clothing Brands: How to Build a Store That Sells

How UK clothing brands can get the most from Shopify: theme choice, collections, variants, sizing, imagery, returns, launches and growing beyond a first range.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clothing stores depend on navigation, variants, sizing information and imagery more than most other product types.
  • Plan how products, colours and sizes are structured before you build, because it affects filters, stock, search and reporting.
  • Returns are part of selling clothes online, so a clear policy and good fit information protect both margins and customer trust.

Shopify is a common choice for UK clothing brands, from new labels launching their first drop to established brands selling across several channels. It handles products with many variants well, its checkout is familiar to shoppers and its ecosystem covers most of what a fashion store needs. Still, the platform alone does not make a clothing store work. Fashion ecommerce has its own challenges: getting customers to the right product quickly, helping them choose the right size, showing garments well and handling returns without losing money.

This guide is for founders and ecommerce managers at UK clothing, streetwear, activewear and accessories brands. It covers the decisions that shape a successful Shopify clothing store, from structure and theme to launches and growth.

Why Shopify suits clothing brands

Clothing is one of the product types Shopify handles best. A few features matter in particular:

  • Variants and options for size, colour and fit, with stock tracked per variant.
  • Collections that can be built manually or automatically using rules such as product type or tag.
  • Filtering by size, colour, price and other attributes on collection pages, depending on your theme.
  • Multichannel selling, so stock can be shared across your website, social platforms, marketplaces and in-person sales.
  • Markets for selling in different currencies and countries.

If you are still deciding on a platform, our comparisons of Shopify vs WooCommerce and Shopify vs Squarespace may help.

Plan your product structure first

Before anything is built, decide how products are organised. This affects filters, stock management, reporting and search, and it is painful to change later.

Colours: one product or several?

The main choice is whether each colour is a variant of a single product, or each colour is its own product with size variants. Separate products per colour give each colourway its own page, images and URL, which can help search visibility and make collection pages look fuller. Single products with colour variants keep things tidier in the admin. Many clothing stores use separate products per colour, linked with colour swatches. Our guide to product variants and bundles explains the trade-offs.

Consistent options and data

Use the same option names and values across the catalogue, such as "Size" with values in a consistent order and format. Fill in product types, tags and metafields consistently, because filters and automated collections rely on them. A little discipline here saves hours later.

Navigation and collections

Clothing shoppers browse in different ways: by category, by occasion, by new arrivals, by collection or by fit. Your navigation should make the common routes obvious.

  • Keep top-level menus short, such as Women, Men, New In, Collections and Sale, depending on your range.
  • Use mega menus for larger catalogues, with clear category names.
  • Make sure filters for size, colour and price work well on mobile, because a large share of fashion browsing happens on phones.
  • Consider whether to show sold-out sizes, hide sold-out products or push them to the end of a collection.
  • Create collections that match what people search for, such as linen shirts or wide-leg trousers, with their own introductory copy.

Choosing and customising a theme

Shopify themes vary widely in how well they suit fashion. Look for a theme that handles large imagery, colour swatches, quick view or quick add, size selection on product cards and flexible collection layouts, while staying fast. Check how it looks with your own product photography, not the demo images.

Many brands start with a theme and customise it to match their identity. Others need a more bespoke build to stand out. Our Shopify theme customisation guide explains what can be changed and how, and our Shopify speed guide covers keeping image-heavy stores fast.

Product pages for clothing

A clothing product page needs to answer the questions a shopper would ask in a changing room. Strong pages typically include:

  • Several high-quality images: front, back, side, detail shots and the garment on a model, ideally with the model's height and the size they wear.
  • Video where possible, to show movement and drape.
  • Fit information, such as whether it runs small, true to size or oversized.
  • Fabric and composition, care instructions and where it was made, stated accurately.
  • Clear size selection with sold-out sizes visible and an option to be notified when back in stock.
  • Delivery and returns summaries close to the add-to-basket button.
  • Reviews, ideally with fit feedback. See our guide to product reviews.

Be careful with sustainability or ethical claims. The CMA has published guidance on environmental claims, and vague claims such as "eco-friendly" can be risky if not clearly supported. Check current guidance before making such claims.

Sizing and fit

Size uncertainty is one of the biggest barriers in clothing ecommerce. Give shoppers as much help as you can:

  • Size charts for each product type, with garment measurements as well as body measurements.
  • Explanations of how to measure.
  • Fit notes on product pages.
  • International size conversions if you sell overseas.

Our guide to ecommerce size guides covers this in depth.

Returns and exchanges

Returns are an unavoidable part of selling clothes online. UK consumer rules generally give online shoppers a right to cancel and return most goods within a set period, with some exceptions, so a returns process is not optional. What you can control is how clear and efficient it is.

  • Publish a clear returns policy covering time limits, condition, who pays for return postage and how refunds work.
  • Consider offering exchanges, which can keep revenue in the business.
  • Use a returns process that is easy for customers and simple to manage.
  • Track return reasons by product and size so you can improve fit information or spot product issues.

This is a general overview, not legal advice. Our guide to delivery and returns pages explains what to include on your site.

Launches and drops

Many clothing brands sell through launches or limited drops. Shopify can support these, but they need planning:

  • Schedule products to publish at a set time and test the process beforehand.
  • Build anticipation with email and SMS sign-ups, with proper consent under UK GDPR and PECR. Our guide to pre-launch marketing covers this.
  • Make sure stock levels are accurate and that overselling is prevented.
  • Keep the theme and apps lean so pages stay fast under heavy traffic.
  • Plan how sold-out products will be handled after the drop.

Getting found and growing

Clothing is a highly competitive space in search and advertising. On the site itself, unique product and collection descriptions, sensible URLs, structured data and fast pages all help. Our Shopify SEO guide covers the essentials.

Many fashion brands grow through paid social, Google Shopping and email. A clean product feed with accurate titles, colours, sizes and images is the foundation for Shopping ads and free listings. Our paid media service page explains how we approach advertising, and our guide to Shopify conversion rate optimisation covers improving the store once traffic arrives.

As you grow, you may start selling internationally. Shopify Markets helps with currencies and pricing, but duties, delivery and returns need planning. Our guide to selling internationally covers the main points.

Shopify clothing store checklist

  • A clear product structure for colours, sizes and fits.
  • Consistent product data for filters and collections.
  • Simple, mobile-friendly navigation and filtering.
  • A fast theme that shows your imagery well.
  • Product pages with multiple images, fit notes, fabric and care details.
  • Size guides for each product type.
  • A clear, workable returns and exchanges policy.
  • A tested process for launches and drops.

FAQs

Is Shopify good for clothing brands?

Yes, Shopify handles variants, collections, filtering and multichannel selling well, which suits most clothing brands. The quality of the store still depends on structure, imagery and fit information.

Should each colour be a separate product on Shopify?

Many clothing stores do this, linking colours with swatches, because each colour gets its own page and images. Single products with colour variants are simpler to manage. Choose before you build.

What is the best Shopify theme for a clothing brand?

There is no single best theme. Look for strong image handling, swatches, good mobile filtering and fast performance, and test it with your own photos.

How can I reduce clothing returns?

Detailed size guides, fit notes, accurate imagery, model measurements and reviews with fit feedback all help shoppers choose the right size first time.

Can Shopify handle limited drops?

Yes, with planning. Schedule launches, test stock settings, keep the store lean and prepare your email and SMS lists in advance.

Do I need Shopify Plus for a clothing brand?

Most brands start on a standard plan. Shopify Plus may suit larger brands with high volumes or complex needs. Compare features against your plans.
