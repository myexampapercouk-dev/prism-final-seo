Tradesman Marketing UK: How to Get More Local Jobs Online

Tradesman marketing UK guide for plumbers, electricians, builders and roofers: local SEO, Google Business Profile, Google Ads and reviews that win jobs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most trade jobs start with a local search, so a strong Google Business Profile and service-area pages come first.
  • Google Ads suit urgent jobs; SEO builds steady enquiries for planned work over time.
  • Answer calls quickly and show proof of work: speed and trust win most trade jobs.

Tradesman marketing in the UK is about being found first when someone in your area needs a job doing, and then looking trustworthy enough to call. For plumbers, electricians, builders, roofers, joiners, decorators and other trades, that usually means a complete Google Business Profile, a simple website with a page for each service and area, a steady flow of genuine reviews and, for urgent work, tightly targeted Google Ads.

This guide is for sole traders and small trade businesses who want more of their own enquiries and less reliance on word of mouth alone or paid lead directories.

How customers find a tradesman today

Word of mouth still matters, but even recommended trades get checked online. A homeowner hears your name, searches for it and looks at your reviews, photos and website before calling. Others search directly: "emergency plumber near me", "electrician in [town]" or "loft conversion builder [county]". The businesses that appear in the map results with good reviews tend to get the calls.

Jobs also fall into two groups. Urgent jobs, such as a burst pipe or a power fault, are decided in minutes, so being visible and answering the phone matters most. Planned jobs, such as extensions, kitchens or rewires, involve research and quotes over weeks, so trust and proof of work matter more.

Google Business Profile: the most important free tool

For most trades, your Google Business Profile is the single biggest source of local enquiries. Make sure it has:

  • The most accurate primary category, such as plumber, electrician or roofing contractor.
  • A service area that matches where you actually work. If you work from home, you can hide your address and show a service area instead.
  • A list of the services you offer.
  • Photos of real jobs, vans and finished work.
  • A steady stream of genuine reviews, each with a reply.

A simple website that wins calls

A trade website does not need to be complicated. It needs to load quickly on a phone, show your phone number clearly and prove you do good work. Useful pages include:

  • A homepage stating what you do, where you work and how to contact you.
  • A page for each main service, such as boiler installation, rewiring or flat roofing.
  • Pages for the main towns or areas you cover, each with genuinely useful local detail rather than copied text.
  • A gallery of real projects with short descriptions.
  • Details of relevant trade registrations or memberships you genuinely hold.

Only list registrations you actually have, and keep them up to date. Where a scheme or registration is legally required for certain work, such as gas work, make sure your claims are accurate; that responsibility sits with the business. Google Ads for trades

Google Ads put you at the top of the results for searches you choose, which is useful for urgent work. To keep costs under control:

  • Target only the areas you genuinely cover.
  • Bid on specific, high-intent searches, such as "emergency electrician [town]".
  • Add negative keywords to block jobs, training, DIY and parts searches.
  • Schedule ads for the hours you can answer the phone.
  • Use call tracking so you know which ads lead to real jobs.

Reviews and proof of work

For trades, reviews are often the deciding factor. Build a habit of asking every satisfied customer, ideally by text with a direct link, soon after the job is finished. Reply to every review. If you get a negative one, respond calmly, explain what you did to put it right and move the conversation offline.

Photos are proof too. Take before, during and after photos on every job (with the customer's permission where their property is identifiable) and use them on your profile, website and social media.

Answering enquiries fast

The best marketing is wasted if calls go unanswered. Many trades are on a job when the phone rings. Options include a call answering service, a clear voicemail promising a callback time, an enquiry form that sends a notification to your phone and a simple process for replying to messages the same day. Speed of response often decides who wins the job.

Social media for trades

Social media is not essential for every trade, but it can help with planned projects. Short videos of work in progress, finished projects and helpful tips can build local recognition. Local community groups can bring work too, but follow each group's rules on promotion.

Building a brand people remember

Many trades rely on vans, uniforms and word of mouth for recognition. A consistent name, logo and colour scheme across your van, website, quotes and social profiles helps people remember you and makes you look established. It does not need to be expensive. Our brand identity design guide explains the basics.

The way you quote matters too. A clear, well-presented written quote that explains what is included, what is not and when you can start often wins against a cheaper but vaguer one. Follow up quotes after a few days with a polite message; many customers simply forget to reply.

Common tradesman marketing mistakes

  • Covering too wide an area. Advertising across a whole county when you only want work nearby wastes money and time on travel.
  • Copied area pages. Dozens of near-identical town pages rarely rank well. Fewer, genuinely useful pages work better.
  • No reviews strategy. Waiting for reviews to happen naturally means far fewer than you could have.
  • Missed calls. Paying for ads and then not answering the phone is one of the most common and costly problems.
  • Unclear services. If your website does not say clearly what you do and do not do, you will get unsuitable enquiries.
  • Relying on one source. Depending entirely on a single directory or platform leaves you exposed if it changes its rules or fees.

Planning for quiet and busy seasons

Many trades are seasonal. Heating engineers are busiest when the weather turns cold, roofers after storms, and landscapers in spring and summer. Use quiet periods to update your website, gather photos and reviews, and promote services that suit that time of year, such as boiler servicing before winter or exterior work before spring. Adjust ad budgets with demand rather than running the same spend all year.

Measuring what works

Keep it simple: count calls, forms and messages by source, track how many turn into quotes and jobs, and note the value of each job. Our GA4 setup guide explains tracking forms and calls on your website. SEO results typically build over three to six months, while ads can bring calls sooner.

FAQs

What is the best tradesman marketing UK strategy for a sole trader?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile, ask every happy customer for a review and build a simple website with service and area pages. Add Google Ads for urgent work if you can answer calls quickly.

Do tradesmen need a website?

A website is strongly recommended. It helps you rank for specific services and areas, shows proof of work and gives customers somewhere to check you out after a recommendation.

Are lead directories worth it for trades?

They can fill gaps, but leads may be shared and fees continue. Building your own profile, website and reviews gives you enquiries you do not have to pay for each time.

How much do Google Ads cost for a tradesman?

It depends on your trade, area and competition. Urgent trade searches can be competitive, so tight targeting and call tracking matter. Ad spend is separate from management fees.

How do I rank in the Google map pack as a tradesman?

Choose accurate categories, set a correct service area, keep adding genuine reviews and photos, and make sure your website supports the same services and areas.

How long does tradesman marketing take to work?

Google Ads can bring calls within days of launch. Local SEO and reviews typically build over three to six months.
