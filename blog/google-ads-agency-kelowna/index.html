Why Kelowna businesses use Google Ads

Kelowna is a city with a strong seasonal rhythm. Summer brings visitors for the lake, the wineries and the golf courses, while the rest of the year leans on local residents, people moving to the valley and businesses selling across the wider Okanagan. Home services, clinics, trades, outdoor recreation, hospitality and professional services all compete for the same local searches, and many of them only have a few weeks each year when demand peaks.

Google Ads suits this pattern well. Unlike organic search, which takes months to build, a paid campaign can be switched on for the start of patio season, scaled up for a busy long weekend and scaled back when demand drops. The risk is that a loosely built account spends money on the wrong searches, in the wrong places, at the wrong times. Good management is mostly about discipline: tight keywords, sensible location settings, honest tracking and landing pages that turn clicks into enquiries.

What our Google Ads management includes

Our paid media service covers the setup and ongoing running of your account:

  • Account audit or fresh build: we review an existing account for wasted spend and structural problems, or build a new one around your services and locations.
  • Keyword research: we group search terms by intent, so someone searching for an emergency plumber in West Kelowna sees a different ad from someone comparing kitchen renovation quotes.
  • Location targeting: we target Kelowna, West Kelowna, Lake Country and the wider Central Okanagan as needed, and set the location option so ads reach people physically in or regularly in your area rather than anyone who merely shows interest in it.
  • Ad copy and assets: clear headlines, sitelinks, callouts, call assets and location assets linked to your Google Business Profile.
  • Conversion tracking: calls, forms, bookings and purchases measured through Google tag and Google Analytics 4, so bidding is based on real outcomes.
  • Negative keywords: a growing list of searches you never want to pay for, such as jobs, free, DIY or competitor names you do not want to target.
  • Monthly reporting: a short report in plain English covering spend, leads, cost per lead and what we are changing next.

Planning for seasonal demand

Seasonality is one of the biggest factors in Kelowna ad accounts. A boat rental or winery tour business might take most of its bookings between late spring and early autumn. A snow removal or furnace service sees the reverse. Real estate, renovations and moving services often follow their own cycles tied to the spring market and people relocating to the valley.

We plan budgets month by month rather than spreading them evenly across the year. Before a peak, we check that tracking works, landing pages are up to date and ad copy mentions current offers. During the peak, we watch budgets closely so campaigns do not run out by midday. Afterwards, we review which searches drove the most valuable leads and use that to plan the next season. Seasonality adjustments in Google Ads can help smart bidding react to short promotions, but they need to be used carefully.

Landing pages matter as much as the ads

A well-built campaign can still fail if the click lands on a slow, generic home page. People searching on their phones by the lake or in their truck want a fast page that confirms they are in the right place, shows the service clearly and makes it easy to call or book.

We often recommend a dedicated landing page for each main service. If your site needs work, our website and ecommerce development service can build pages designed for paid traffic. Common mistakes we fix in Kelowna accounts

  • Broad location settings: ads showing to people across Canada who are only interested in Kelowna, not people who can actually use the service.
  • No conversion tracking: the account optimises for clicks rather than calls, bookings or sales.
  • One campaign for everything: all services mixed together, so budget flows to the cheapest clicks rather than the most valuable work.
  • Ads running when nobody answers: call ads shown at night or on days the business is closed.
  • Ignoring the search terms report: irrelevant searches left running for months.
  • Same budget all year: no adjustment for the summer peak or the quiet months.
