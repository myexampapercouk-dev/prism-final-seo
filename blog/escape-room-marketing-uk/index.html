Escape Room Marketing in the UK: How to Fill More Booking Slots

Escape room marketing in the UK works best when local search, a fast booking journey, reviews and well-timed ads all point at your quieter slots.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most escape room bookings start with a local search, so Google Business Profile, room pages and reviews do the heavy lifting.
  • Every room needs its own page with difficulty, group size, duration and accessibility details, linked straight to live availability.
  • Paid search and social work best when aimed at specific audiences and quiet slots, such as weekday corporate sessions or birthday parties.

Escape room marketing in the UK comes down to being found when groups search for something to do nearby, and then making it easy for them to book a slot in a minute or two. The venues that stay busy usually combine strong local search visibility, a clear page for every room, a frictionless booking system, plenty of recent reviews and targeted ads that fill the quieter times of the week.

This guide walks through each of those pieces from the point of view of a single-site or small multi-site operator. It covers what to put on your website, how to approach local SEO, where paid media fits, and how to reach the corporate and party audiences that keep weekdays ticking over. We are a studio that builds websites and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so we will be honest about what tends to matter and what is mostly noise.

How people choose an escape room

Very few people search for a specific venue by name the first time. They search for things like "escape room near me", "escape rooms in Leeds" or "things to do in Bristol with friends", then compare two or three options in the map results. They look at the star rating, a few photos, the themes on offer and whether there is a slot on the day they want. If the booking page is slow or confusing, they go back and pick the next venue.

That journey tells you where to focus. Your Google Business Profile and your website carry most of the decision, and the booking flow decides whether interest becomes revenue. Brand awareness matters over time, but early on the practical job is to win the local comparison.

It also helps to think about the different groups you serve, because each one searches and decides differently:

  • Friends and couples looking for an evening or weekend activity, usually booking a few days ahead.
  • Families wanting something for the school holidays, who care about age limits and how scary a theme is.
  • Birthday and hen or stag groups who need several rooms at once, or a package with food nearby.
  • Corporate teams booking team-building sessions, often on weekdays, who want invoices and simple communication.
  • Tourists and visitors searching on the day for something to do in a city they do not know well.

Escape room marketing UK: the website foundations

Your website is where search engines, ads and social posts all send people, so it is worth getting right before spending money elsewhere. A good escape room site is fast on a phone, shows the themes immediately and puts availability one tap away. Our guide to online booking systems for websites covers the booking side in more depth.

Give every room its own page

One page that lists all your rooms makes it hard to rank for theme-specific searches and hard for visitors to compare. Instead, give each room a dedicated page that answers the questions people actually have:

  • The story and theme, written to build curiosity without spoilers.
  • Difficulty, recommended group size and minimum and maximum players.
  • Duration, including how early to arrive for the briefing.
  • Age guidance and whether under-16s need an adult in the room.
  • Accessibility, such as step-free access, lighting, physical tasks and whether the room includes dark or confined spaces.
  • A clear button to see availability for that specific room.

Room pages also give you natural places to use photos and short video, which help people picture the experience. Keep files compressed so the pages stay quick to load on mobile data.

Make booking feel effortless

Most escape room booking platforms can be embedded into your site. The aim is to keep people on your domain where possible, show real-time availability, take payment or a deposit securely and send a confirmation email that explains how to find you, where to park and what time to arrive. Avoid making visitors create an account before they can see slots.

A short, clear cancellation and rescheduling policy also reduces hesitation, particularly for larger group bookings where one person is paying on behalf of others.

Plan pages for your other audiences

If you want corporate or party bookings, give them their own landing pages rather than burying them in an FAQ. A team-building page can explain how sessions run, how many people you can host at once, whether you can provide a debrief space and how invoicing works. A parties page can cover packages, timings and any partner venues nearby for food. Our advice on landing page design applies directly here.

Local SEO for escape rooms

Local search is where most of your bookings will start. Our local SEO guide covers the fundamentals; here is how they apply to escape rooms specifically.

Google Business Profile

Choose the most accurate primary category, add your opening hours including late sessions, and upload photos of the lobby, the outside of the building and each room's set dressing that does not give puzzles away. Use the products or services sections to list each room. Post updates when you launch a new theme or have seasonal events. Our Google Business Profile guide goes through the settings step by step.

Reviews

Escape rooms have a natural moment to ask for reviews: straight after the game, when groups are taking their photo. A small card or a QR code on the photo board, plus a follow-up email the next day, tends to work well. Reply to reviews, including the critical ones, calmly and specifically. We cover tone and wording in our guide on how to respond to Google reviews. Do not offer incentives in exchange for reviews, as this breaks platform rules.

Location and theme content

Beyond your room pages, useful content includes a page on visiting you (parking, public transport, nearby places to eat), an FAQ on what to expect for first-timers and a guide to choosing the right room for your group. Each one answers real searches and gives you something to link to from social posts.

If you run more than one site, each location needs its own page with its own address, rooms and opening hours, rather than a single page listing every venue.

Paid media: filling the quiet slots

Weekend evenings often sell themselves. The commercial challenge is usually weekday afternoons and early evenings. Paid media can help, provided it is pointed at the right people and the right times.

For many venues, a small Google Search campaign plus some Meta activity is plenty to start with. Our guides on Google Ads for small businesses and Facebook ads for small businesses explain how to set sensible budgets. Make sure conversion tracking records completed bookings, not just clicks, so you can see which campaigns actually fill rooms.

Any offers you run, such as weekday discounts, should be clearly stated with their conditions, in line with the CAP Code that governs UK advertising. Avoid claims you cannot back up, such as calling a room the "hardest in the UK".

Social content that does not spoil the game

Escape rooms are visual and social, which makes them a good fit for Instagram and TikTok. The constraint is obvious: you cannot show the puzzles. Content that works without spoiling anything includes:

  • Group photos at the end, shared with permission.
  • Behind-the-scenes clips of set building and new theme development.
  • Short "which room suits you" quizzes or polls.
  • Seasonal announcements, such as a Halloween theme or Christmas corporate sessions.
  • Staff introducing the story of a room in character, without names if you prefer.

Always get clear consent before posting customers' faces, and keep it simple to withdraw. Under UK GDPR, photos of identifiable people are personal data.

Email and repeat bookings

Groups who enjoyed one room are the most likely to book another. With consent captured at booking, a simple email programme can bring them back: a thank-you with a review link, a note when a new room opens, and a reminder ahead of school holidays or the Christmas party season. Keep marketing consent separate from booking confirmations, as PECR requires opt-in for most marketing emails to individuals.

Measuring what works

Track a small number of numbers you can act on: booking conversion rate on the website, bookings by source, occupancy by day and time, and review volume. If occupancy on Tuesday afternoons is persistently low, that is where offers and ads should focus. Analytics should record each completed booking as a conversion so the figures are reliable.

FAQs

What is the best escape room marketing UK operators can start with?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile, a page for every room and a fast booking journey. These three things influence most bookings, and they cost far less than ongoing advertising. Once they are in place, add reviews and targeted ads.

Should each escape room theme have its own web page?

Yes. Separate pages let you describe difficulty, group size, age guidance and accessibility for each room, and they give search engines more relevant content to rank for theme-related searches.

How do I get more weekday escape room bookings?

Weekday demand usually comes from corporate teams, families in school holidays and visitors. Create dedicated pages for team-building and holiday sessions, and point ads and offers at those specific slots rather than at the whole week.

Can I post customer photos on social media?

Only with clear consent. Photos of identifiable people are personal data under UK GDPR, so ask at the photo stage, record the consent and remove images promptly if someone asks.

Is TikTok worth it for an escape room?

It can be, particularly for venues aimed at younger groups. The key is atmosphere and reactions rather than puzzles, so clips should build curiosity without giving anything away.

How long does escape room SEO take to work?

Local SEO improvements often show early movement in map results, but meaningful gains typically take three to six months, depending on competition in your town or city and how many reviews you collect.
