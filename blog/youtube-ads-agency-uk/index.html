YouTube Ads Agency UK: Formats, Targeting and How to Measure Results

YouTube ads agency UK guide: which ad formats suit which goals, how targeting works, how to measure views into sales, and what to ask before you hire.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Pick the YouTube format by goal: skippable in-stream and Shorts for action, bumpers for reach, in-feed for consideration.
  • The first five seconds decide most YouTube results, so brief the opening before anything else.
  • Measure YouTube with conversions, view-through signals, branded search and blended revenue, not views alone.

A YouTube ads agency plans, builds and manages video campaigns on YouTube through Google Ads, so your videos reach the right UK viewers and lead to measurable enquiries or sales. YouTube works well for businesses that need to explain a product, build trust before a purchase or reach new audiences at scale, provided the opening seconds of each video are strong.

This guide explains the main YouTube ad formats, how targeting works, how to measure results honestly and what to ask an agency. Because YouTube runs inside Google Ads, our Google Ads agency guide is useful background.

Why UK businesses use YouTube advertising

YouTube sits between social feeds and search. People go there to learn, compare and be entertained, often with the sound on and for longer than on other platforms. That gives advertisers time to explain something, which is hard on a fast-scrolling feed.

It tends to suit:

  • Products that need explaining. Software, home improvement, education courses, considered ecommerce purchases and services with a process.
  • Brands building trust. Demonstrations, customer stories you are allowed to share, and behind-the-scenes content help people feel confident before they buy.
  • Retargeting. Showing a video to people who visited your site but did not convert.
  • Reach at a sensible cost. Short formats can put a brand in front of many people in a region or interest group.

It is weaker for very low-budget accounts that cannot afford a test period, and for businesses with no video at all. A simple, clear video shot well is usually enough to start.

Targeting on YouTube

YouTube targeting runs through Google Ads, which gives it some of the strongest audience options of any video platform:

  • Custom segments built from search terms people have used on Google, or from types of websites they visit. This is useful for reaching people already researching your category.
  • Your data. Website visitors, customer lists (uploaded in line with UK GDPR) and people who have watched your videos.
  • In-market and life event audiences such as people moving house or researching a purchase type.
  • Topics and placements where you choose subject areas or specific channels.
  • Location, language and device, which matters for UK-only or regional businesses.

As with most modern ad platforms, broader targeting with strong creative often outperforms very narrow targeting. Start with a few clear audiences, compare them, and let data guide where budget goes.

Creative that works on YouTube

Most results are decided in the first five seconds, because that is when a skippable advert can be skipped. Brief the opening first:

  • Show the product, the problem or the person speaking straight away. No long logo intros.
  • Say who the video is for, so the right people stay and others skip cheaply.
  • Explain one idea clearly, with on-screen text for people watching without sound.
  • Finish with a clear call to action and a matching landing page.
  • Make versions in different lengths and aspect ratios: 15 to 30 seconds horizontal, plus vertical cuts for Shorts.

Measuring YouTube ads

Views are not results. A sensible measurement setup includes:

  • Conversion tracking in Google Ads for enquiries and purchases, with values where possible.
  • GA4 linked to Google Ads, so you can see what video viewers do on your site. Our GA4 setup guide walks through it.
  • View-through and engaged-view conversions, treated with care, because they show influence rather than direct clicks.
  • Branded search trends, since good video often lifts people searching for your name.
  • Blended revenue against total spend, to see whether the business grows as video spend rises.
  • Lift studies on larger budgets, which compare people who saw an advert with people who did not.

Expect YouTube to look weaker on last-click reports than search. That does not mean it is not working; it means you need a fuller view of the journey.

YouTube ads checklist

  • A clear goal for each campaign: reach, consideration or conversions.
  • The YouTube channel and Google Ads account are owned by your business.
  • Google Ads and GA4 are linked and conversion tracking is tested.
  • Consent management respects UK GDPR and PECR.
  • At least two or three video variations with different openings.
  • Horizontal and vertical versions ready for in-stream and Shorts.
  • Captions or on-screen text for silent viewing.
  • Audience lists built from site visitors and video viewers.
  • A landing page that matches the video message and loads quickly on mobile.
  • Agreed success measures beyond views, reviewed on a set date.

Questions to ask a YouTube ads agency

  • Which formats would you start with for our goal, and why?
  • Who writes and produces the video, and how many versions do you test?
  • How will you separate YouTube's effect from search and other channels?
  • Will you run YouTube as its own campaign or inside Performance Max?
  • Who owns the channel, videos and ad account?

Common YouTube advertising mistakes

Several mistakes come up again and again when UK businesses first try YouTube, and most are easy to avoid once you know them.

  • Running a TV-style advert unchanged. A slow build-up with the brand at the end works on television, where viewers cannot skip. On YouTube, the same advert is often skipped before the message lands.
  • Judging the channel on last-click sales after two weeks. Video influences people who buy later through search or by typing your address. Give campaigns time and look at the whole picture.
  • Letting adverts run on unsuitable content. Review where adverts appear, add content exclusions and exclude placements that do not fit your brand.
  • Using one video for every audience. A first-time viewer needs a different message from someone who visited your pricing page yesterday. Even two versions, one for new audiences and one for retargeting, make a difference.
  • Sending viewers to the home page. Link to a page that continues the story the video started, with the same offer and wording.
  • Ignoring frequency. Showing the same advert too often to the same people wastes budget and can annoy them. Set sensible frequency caps on awareness campaigns.

Fixing these usually costs nothing but attention, and it often improves results more than raising the budget.

FAQs

What does a YouTube ads agency UK do?

A YouTube ads agency in the UK chooses formats, builds audiences, briefs or produces video, sets up conversion tracking and manages budgets through Google Ads, then reports on results against agreed goals.

How much does YouTube advertising cost in the UK?

Ad spend is set by you and paid to Google, separate from management and production. Costs per view vary by audience, format and competition, so a test budget is the best way to learn your own numbers.

Do I need a professional video for YouTube ads?

No. A clear, well-lit video with a strong opening, captions and one message often works. Production quality matters less than relevance and the first five seconds.

Are YouTube Shorts ads worth running?

They can be for mobile audiences and products that demonstrate quickly. They need native vertical video rather than a cropped horizontal advert.

How long should a YouTube advert be?

For skippable adverts, 15 to 30 seconds is a common starting point, with the key message early. Bumpers are six seconds. Longer videos can work for retargeting people who already know you.

How do I measure if YouTube ads are working?

Track conversions in Google Ads and GA4, watch branded search and blended revenue, and treat view-through conversions as a signal of influence rather than direct sales.
