Web Design in Abu Dhabi: Bilingual Business Websites, Process and Cost

Need web design in Abu Dhabi? How a remote UK studio builds fast Arabic and English business websites, what goes into each stage and what it costs in pounds.

  • A strong Abu Dhabi business website loads fast on mobile, reads naturally in Arabic and English, and makes calling, messaging or enquiring easy.
  • We design and build remotely from the UK with no office in Abu Dhabi, and Arabic content is written with your team or a professional translator.
  • Small business websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000 and take 3 to 5 weeks; other currencies are quoted on request.

Web design in Abu Dhabi means building a website that works quickly on a phone, reads properly in both Arabic and English, and turns visitors into calls, WhatsApp messages or enquiries. A good-looking homepage is only the start: the page structure, the content and the technical setup underneath decide whether the site brings in work.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio. We design and build websites remotely for businesses in Abu Dhabi and across the UAE, and we have no office in the emirate. Projects run through video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can open at any time. This guide covers what an Abu Dhabi business website needs, how our website and ecommerce development service works, and what it costs.

What an Abu Dhabi business website has to do

Abu Dhabi has a broad mix of businesses: professional services firms, consultancies, clinics, property and facilities companies, hospitality venues, training providers, contractors and suppliers selling to larger organisations. Their customers differ, but most of them find a business the same way. They search on Google, tap a link shared in a message or check a social profile, then land on a website on their phone and decide within seconds whether to stay.

A website that does its job for an Abu Dhabi business should:

  • Load quickly on mobile data, with compressed images and only the scripts the page needs.
  • Offer Arabic and English versions that each read as if they were written in that language from the start.
  • Make the next step obvious, whether that is a phone call, a WhatsApp chat, a booking or an enquiry form.
  • Give each main service its own page, so customers and search engines can see exactly what you offer.
  • Show the trust signals buyers look for, such as clear company details, service areas, policies and contact information.
  • Be simple for your own team to update without a developer for every small change.

Most of these decisions are made on the small screen first. Our guide to mobile website design explains the layout choices that matter when most visitors arrive on a phone.

Designing for Arabic and English

Many Abu Dhabi businesses serve both Arabic-speaking customers and a large international community, so a bilingual site is often the sensible default. That is not the same as adding a translation plugin. Arabic reads right to left, so navigation, icons, forms, tables, image positions and even arrows need to mirror correctly. The Arabic typeface needs to be designed for Arabic script and stay readable at small sizes.

We build each language as its own set of pages with separate URLs, linked by a clear language switcher and hreflang tags so search engines show the right version to the right searcher. We write content in English. Arabic pages are written with your team or a professional translator, and we recommend a fluent speaker checks them before launch. Our article on building a bilingual Arabic and English website goes into the technical side in more depth.

Web design Abu Dhabi: our process from brief to launch

Every project follows the same clear sequence, scaled to the size of the site. You can see progress throughout, and nothing goes live until you approve it.

  1. Discovery call. We talk through your business, customers, services, competitors and goals, then agree the pages the site needs.
  2. Sitemap and content plan. We map each page to what customers search for and decide what each one must say. Our guide to preparing website content before the build explains why this step saves weeks later on.
  3. Design. We design the key pages for mobile and desktop, in both languages where needed, and refine them with your feedback.
  4. Build. We develop the site on a private staging address, set up forms, analytics and on-page SEO, and test on real phones and browsers.
  5. Review and launch. You check every page, we make final changes, then we connect your domain and go live.
  6. Handover. We show your team how to edit pages and give you full access to the site, domain, hosting and analytics.

Because we work remotely, we agree meeting times that suit both time zones. Abu Dhabi is a few hours ahead of the UK, which usually leaves a comfortable overlap in the working day for calls and quick replies.

Speed, search and compliance built in

A new website should be ready to rank from the day it launches. On every build we set up page titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, structured data, an XML sitemap and Google Search Console. Pages are built around the services and locations customers actually search for, rather than a single page trying to cover everything.

Speed is part of the design brief, not a fix applied later. We keep page weight low, serve modern image formats, load fonts carefully and avoid heavy plugins. Our guide to Core Web Vitals explains the measures Google uses to judge loading and stability, and why they matter for visitors as much as rankings.

On compliance, rules covering privacy, cookies, consumer information and online selling differ between countries and change over time, and businesses in free zones may have their own frameworks to consider. We build sensible privacy and cookie features into every site, but you should check current official guidance in the UAE and any market you sell into, and confirm the requirements with your own legal adviser.

Working with a remote studio from Abu Dhabi

Hiring a web design studio outside the UAE is common and works well when the process is clear. The questions that matter are communication, ownership and support after launch. Our guide to hiring an overseas web design agency covers what to check before you sign anything.

When you work with us, you can expect:

  • A single point of contact at the studio and agreed times for calls.
  • A shared project plan that shows what is finished and what comes next.
  • A staging site you can open on your own phone at any stage.
  • Written feedback rounds, so nothing gets lost between calls.
  • Full ownership of your domain, hosting, content and analytics. Our article on website ownership and handover lists what you should always receive.

We are also clear about what we do not do. We have no office in Abu Dhabi and cannot attend meetings in person, arrange on-site photography or handle local licensing. If your project needs those things, a local partner can cover them and we are happy to work alongside them. Businesses with customers in more than one emirate may also find our guide to web design in Dubai useful, since many of the same decisions apply.

How to choose a web designer for an Abu Dhabi business

Whether you hire us or another studio, these questions help you compare proposals fairly:

  • Have they built right-to-left Arabic layouts before, and can they explain how they handle mirroring?
  • Who writes the Arabic content, and who checks it before it goes live?
  • Will you own the domain, hosting account and site files outright?
  • What exactly is included in the price, and what would cost extra?
  • How is on-page SEO handled at launch, and is Search Console set up for you?
  • What support is offered after launch, and how is it charged?

Straight answers to these questions are worth more than a polished pitch deck. A studio that is open about its limits is usually easier to work with over the years your website will be live.
