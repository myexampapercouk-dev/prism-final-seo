How to Set Up Google Merchant Center for a UK Online Shop

How to set up Google Merchant Center for a UK shop: account details, verifying your site, shipping and returns, product feeds and fixing disapprovals.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Merchant Center is where Google gets your product data, both for free listings and for Shopping ads, so the quality of the feed matters before any money is spent.
  • Most disapprovals come from mismatches between the feed and the website, such as prices, stock status or missing policy pages.
  • Set up shipping, returns and business details carefully at the start, because they affect eligibility across every product.

If you sell physical products online in the UK, Google Merchant Center is one of the most useful free tools available to you. It is the place where you give Google structured information about your products, and it powers both free product listings across Google and paid Shopping ads. Without it, your products cannot appear in the Shopping tab or in product-rich results in the way many shoppers now expect.

This guide walks through setting up Merchant Center for a UK shop, from creating the account to getting products approved. It focuses on the setup itself. If you already have a working feed and want to run ads, our Google Shopping ads guide covers campaigns, bidding and structure.

What Merchant Center actually does

Merchant Center is a data hub rather than an advertising tool. You tell Google what you sell, what it costs, whether it is in stock, how much delivery is and what your returns policy looks like. Google then checks that information against your website and decides which products are eligible to show.

That information can feed several places:

  • Free listings in the Shopping tab and other Google surfaces, which do not cost anything per click.
  • Shopping ads and Performance Max campaigns, once you link a Google Ads account.
  • Local inventory, if you also have physical shops and want to show in-store availability.

Even if you have no plans to advertise, getting products approved for free listings is worth doing. It is extra visibility for products you already sell.

Before you start: get the website ready

Google reviews your website as well as your feed, and many accounts run into problems because the site is not ready. Before creating an account, check that your shop has:

  • Clear contact information, such as an email address, phone number or postal address that customers can find easily.
  • A returns and refunds policy that explains how customers send items back and how refunds work.
  • Delivery information showing costs and typical delivery times.
  • Secure checkout on HTTPS throughout.
  • Accurate product pages where the price, availability and product details match what you plan to submit.
  • Terms and privacy pages appropriate for your business.

If your delivery and returns pages need work, our guide to delivery and returns pages covers what to include. For legal pages more generally, see our guide to website terms and privacy policies. Google's policies change over time, so read the current Merchant Center requirements rather than relying on any checklist, including this one.

Step 1: Create the Merchant Center account

Sign in with a Google account that belongs to the business rather than a personal account tied to one employee. If that person leaves, you do not want access to leave with them. Add other people as users later rather than sharing a login.

During setup you will be asked for your business name, country, time zone and website. Choose the United Kingdom as your business country and enter the name customers will recognise. The business name should match what appears on your website and, ideally, your other Google profiles.

You will also be asked where customers can buy your products: on your website, in a physical shop, or both. Choose what is true for your business today. You can add options later.

Step 2: Verify and claim your website

Google needs to confirm that you own the website you are submitting products from. This is usually done in one of a few ways:

  • Adding a small HTML tag to your site.
  • Uploading a verification file to your hosting.
  • Using an existing Google Analytics or Google Tag Manager connection.
  • Connecting through your ecommerce platform, which often handles this automatically.

Once verified, you claim the site, which links it to your Merchant Center account. If a previous agency or developer claimed the website in a different account, you may need to reclaim it. This is a common hiccup when businesses change suppliers, and it is one reason to keep your own accounts under your control, as covered in our guide to website ownership and handover.

Step 3: Set up business information

Fill in the business details section fully. This typically includes your address, customer service contact details and, where relevant, your logo and brand colours. Some of these details can appear alongside your listings, and incomplete information can affect how Google treats your account.

Keep everything consistent. If your website footer, your Google Business Profile and Merchant Center all show slightly different business names or addresses, it creates unnecessary doubt. Our guide to Google Business Profile is useful if you also have a physical location.

Step 4: Configure shipping

Shipping settings tell Google what customers will pay for delivery and how long it takes. Getting this right matters because Google compares it with your checkout, and shoppers see delivery costs before they click.

You can usually set shipping in Merchant Center itself or include it in your product feed. For most small UK shops, account-level shipping settings are simpler to manage. Common approaches include:

  • Flat rate: one delivery price for all orders.
  • Free over a threshold: free delivery above a set basket value, with a charge below it.
  • Weight or price bands: charges that change based on order weight or value.
  • Carrier-calculated rates: where supported, rates based on your carrier's pricing.

Add handling time (how long before you dispatch) and transit time (how long the carrier takes) so Google can estimate delivery dates. Be realistic. Promising next-day delivery that you cannot meet leads to unhappy customers and potential policy problems.

If you sell to other countries, you need shipping settings for each one you target. Selling abroad raises wider questions about currency, duties and returns, which we cover in our guide to selling internationally from a UK online store.

Step 5: Set up returns

Merchant Center lets you add your returns policy details, such as the return window, whether returns are free and how customers send items back. This information can appear in listings and helps shoppers compare options. Make sure it matches your website policy exactly.

UK consumer law gives customers certain rights when buying online, including cancellation rights for many distance sales. The specifics depend on what you sell and how, so check current guidance from the relevant UK bodies or take legal advice rather than assuming your policy is compliant.

Step 6: Add your products

There are several ways to get product data into Merchant Center. The right one depends on your platform and the size of your catalogue.

Platform integrations

Shopify and WooCommerce both offer ways to connect your store to Google so products sync automatically. On Shopify this is done through Google's own sales channel. On WooCommerce, Google provides an official extension. Automatic syncing is usually the best option for small and medium shops because prices and stock levels update without manual work.

Spreadsheet or file feeds

You can upload a file or connect a Google Sheet containing your products. This gives you full control but needs regular updating. It suits small catalogues that rarely change, or businesses on platforms without a good integration.

Automatic product discovery

Google can also find products by crawling your website and reading structured data on product pages. This depends heavily on your site having good product markup. Our schema markup guide explains how structured data works. Crawled data is a useful backstop, but most shops get more control from a proper feed.

Product data that matters most

Every product needs certain attributes, and the quality of each one affects whether and how it shows. The essentials are:

AttributeWhat to get right
IDA unique, stable identifier for each product or variant that never changes.
TitleDescriptive and specific: product type, brand, key features, size or colour where relevant.
DescriptionAccurate detail about the product, without promotional claims or shouting.
LinkThe exact product page, landing on the right variant.
Image linkA clear main image on a plain background, without added text or watermarks.
PriceThe price including VAT, matching the website exactly.
AvailabilityIn stock, out of stock, preorder or backorder, kept in sync with the site.
Brand and GTINThe brand name and barcode number where the product has one.
ConditionNew, refurbished or used.

Product titles deserve special attention. They are one of the strongest signals for which searches your products appear on. A title such as "Blue Jumper" says very little. "Women's Merino Wool Crew Neck Jumper, Navy, Size 12" says what the product is and who it is for. The same principles that improve your product pages apply here, as covered in our guide to product page SEO.

If you sell products in several sizes or colours, each variant usually needs its own entry, linked together with an item group ID. Our guide to product variants and bundles covers how to structure these on your site.

Step 7: Review diagnostics and fix disapprovals

After products are submitted, Google reviews them. This can take a while. Check the diagnostics area regularly, as it lists issues at account level and product level.

Common reasons for disapproval include:

  • Price mismatch: the feed price differs from the website, often because of sales, currency settings or VAT display.
  • Availability mismatch: the feed says in stock but the page says sold out, or the other way round.
  • Missing identifiers: branded products submitted without a GTIN.
  • Image problems: placeholder images, promotional overlays or images too small.
  • Landing page problems: broken links, slow pages or pages that redirect elsewhere.
  • Policy issues: restricted product categories or missing policy pages.

Account-level issues such as misrepresentation warnings are more serious because they can affect every product. These often relate to missing contact information, unclear policies or content that looks untrustworthy. Our guide to website trust signals covers many of the basics that help here. Fix the underlying issue on the site, then request a review through Merchant Center.

Step 8: Link to Google Ads and Analytics (optional)

Once your products are approved, you can link Merchant Center to Google Ads to run Shopping or Performance Max campaigns. You can also link it to Google Analytics to see how product traffic behaves on your site. If your analytics are not set up properly yet, our GA4 setup guide is a good starting point.

Before spending on ads, let free listings run for a while and check that your feed is clean. Paying to promote products with weak titles or missing data rarely makes sense.

Keeping the feed healthy

Merchant Center is not a one-off job. Keep an eye on it as part of running your shop:

  • Check diagnostics regularly, especially after site changes, theme updates or sales.
  • Make sure sale prices are set up properly so the feed and site match during promotions.
  • Review product titles for your best sellers and improve weak ones.
  • Remove or mark as out of stock any products you no longer sell.
  • Read Google's policy update emails rather than ignoring them.

FAQs

Is Google Merchant Center free?

Creating an account and appearing in free product listings does not cost anything. You only pay if you choose to run ads through a linked Google Ads account.

Do I need Merchant Center to appear in Google Shopping?

For most shops, yes. Merchant Center is how Google receives structured product information for the Shopping tab and Shopping ads.

Why are my products disapproved?

The diagnostics area explains each issue. The most common causes are mismatches between the feed and website, missing product identifiers, image problems and missing policy information.

Should prices in Merchant Center include VAT?

For UK consumer shoppers, prices are generally shown including VAT, and the feed should match what customers see on your site. Check Google's current requirements for your situation.

Can Shopify and WooCommerce sync with Merchant Center automatically?

Yes. Both platforms have official ways to connect to Google so products, prices and stock update automatically.
