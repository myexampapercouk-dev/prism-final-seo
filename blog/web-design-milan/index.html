Looking for web design in Milan? How we plan and build fast, accessible, consent-ready websites for Italian businesses remotely, with timelines and UK pricing.

  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Milan or anywhere in Italy; we design and build websites remotely in English, with Italian copy from your team or a translator.

Web design for a Milan business should do two jobs at once: look as polished as the city's design reputation demands, and work hard as a sales tool that loads quickly, ranks in search and turns visitors into enquiries. A beautiful site that is slow, hard to navigate or missing basic compliance will hold a business back however good it looks.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Milan or anywhere else in Italy. Our website and e-commerce development service runs by video call, shared documents and staging links you can review at any time, and Milan is one hour ahead of UK time. We work in English. Italian copy is written or checked by your team or a professional translator, so the finished site reads naturally to Italian customers.

What Milan businesses need from a website

Milan is Italy's commercial and financial centre and a global reference point for fashion, furniture and design. That raises the bar. Visitors are used to strong visuals and expect a site to feel considered, but they also expect it to be fast on a phone and easy to use.

The businesses we typically build for in and around Milan include:

  • Design-led brands. Fashion, furniture, lighting and interiors firms that need image-rich sites without slow loading.
  • Professional services. Consultancies, studios and B2B firms that need clear service pages and a strong enquiry route.
  • E-commerce shops. Brands selling across Italy and into other EU markets.
  • Hospitality and events. Restaurants, venues and event organisers that need bookings and up-to-date information.
  • Start-ups and technology firms. Companies that need a credible site quickly, with room to grow.

Design that looks good and performs

For design-led businesses, the temptation is to fill the homepage with large images, video and animation. Those can work, but only when handled carefully. Large unoptimised images and auto-playing video are among the most common causes of slow sites, and slow sites lose visitors and rankings.

We design with performance in mind from the start: modern image formats, sensible sizes, restrained animation and fonts that load quickly. Most visitors will arrive on a phone, so we design the mobile layout first rather than squeezing a desktop design down. Our guide to mobile website design covers the principles we follow.

WordPress or Shopify?

We build on WordPress and Shopify, and the choice depends on what the site needs to do.

  • WordPress suits content-led sites, service businesses, portfolios and businesses that publish regularly. It is flexible and works well with multilingual plugins if you need Italian and English versions.
  • Shopify suits online shops of most sizes. It handles payments, stock, shipping and tax settings well, and supports multiple languages and currencies through Shopify Markets. Our post on Shopify for Italian businesses covers the platform in more depth.

If you already have a site, we plan any redesign to protect existing rankings, with page mapping and redirects. Our guide to a website redesign without losing rankings explains how.

Languages and international visitors

Many Milan businesses sell beyond Italy or attract international visitors. If you need both Italian and English versions, each should be a proper language version with its own URLs and correct hreflang tags, not an automatic translation widget. Our guide to international SEO explains the setup. We can build the English version with you directly, while Italian content comes from your team or a translator.

Compliance basics for Italian websites

Every new site should launch with the basics in place. These notes are general information only. Please follow official guidance from the relevant authorities and take advice from your own legal adviser.

  • GDPR. Contact forms, bookings, newsletters and analytics collect personal data. You need a clear privacy notice, a lawful basis and sensible retention. Italy's data protection authority publishes guidance in Italian.
  • Cookie consent. Under the ePrivacy rules, as applied in Italian law, non-essential cookies generally need prior consent, and rejecting should be as easy as accepting. Analytics and advertising tags should only fire after consent. Our article on cookie banner design covers the design side.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer services, including e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but accessible design is good practice for every business. The expected standard is EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA. See our overview of the European Accessibility Act for websites.

In practice this means sufficient colour contrast, readable text sizes, keyboard-friendly navigation, descriptive links and image alternative text. For design-led brands, that sometimes means adjusting pale grey text or thin fonts that look elegant but are hard to read.

How a remote web design project works

  1. Discovery call. We discuss your goals, audience, competitors and content, and agree scope and timeline.
  2. Sitemap and wireframes. We plan the structure and the layout of key pages before any visual design.
  3. Design. We design the homepage and key templates for your feedback, then roll the design across the site.
  4. Build on staging. We build the site on a private staging link you can review on your own devices.
  5. Content. Your team or translator supplies Italian copy; we place it, format it and check the layout.
  6. Testing and launch. We test forms, speed, devices and consent, set up redirects and launch.
  7. Handover. We show you how to edit the site and agree any ongoing support.

Choosing a web design partner

Whether you work with us or another studio, a few questions help you judge any web design proposal:

  • Will the site be fast on a phone, and how will that be tested?
  • Who writes and checks the Italian content, and when is it needed?
  • How will cookie consent and accessibility be handled at launch?
  • Will you own the domain, hosting account and all site files?
  • What happens to existing pages and rankings if this is a redesign?
  • What support is available after launch, and how is it charged?

A remote UK studio suits businesses that are comfortable working in English and value a structured, well-documented process. If you want regular face-to-face meetings in your Milan office, a local studio may be a better fit, and that is a perfectly reasonable choice.
