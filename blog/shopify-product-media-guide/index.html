Shopify Product Media: A Guide to Images, Video and 3D Models

How to add and manage product images, video and 3D models in Shopify, with practical advice on ordering, variant images, alt text and keeping pages fast.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Product media does the job a customer's hands would do in a shop, so plan it around the questions buyers ask.
  • Order media deliberately, link images to variants and write useful alt text for every file.
  • Video and 3D models can help with some products but add weight, so use them where they genuinely explain something.

Online shoppers cannot pick a product up, turn it over or feel the fabric. The images, videos and models on your product page have to do that work instead. In Shopify, all of these sit together under product media, and how you choose, order and describe them shapes how confident a customer feels before clicking add to cart.

This guide walks through the types of media Shopify supports, how to upload and organise them, how to connect images to variants, and how to keep product pages quick and accessible. It is written for UK small business owners who manage their own store and want product pages that answer buyers' questions clearly.

What counts as product media in Shopify

Shopify lets you attach several kinds of media to a product:

  • Images, such as photographs, cut-outs on plain backgrounds and lifestyle shots.
  • Video, either uploaded directly or linked from a video hosting platform.
  • 3D models, which let customers rotate a product on screen and, on supported devices, view it in their own space using augmented reality.

How each type appears on the page depends on your theme. Most current Shopify themes display a main media area with thumbnails or a swipeable gallery on mobile, but the layout, zoom behaviour and video controls vary. Check your theme's settings and preview pages on a phone after any changes.

Start with the questions buyers ask

Before uploading anything, list the questions a customer would ask if they were standing in front of the product. For clothing that might be fit, length and fabric texture. For furniture it might be scale, finish and how it looks in a room. For skincare it might be the packaging, the texture of the product and the size of the bottle in a hand.

Then plan media that answers each one. A typical set might include:

  • A clear main image on a consistent background.
  • Alternative angles: back, side, detail and underneath where relevant.
  • A close-up of materials, stitching, finish or texture.
  • A scale shot, such as the product in a hand, on a person or next to a familiar object.
  • A lifestyle image showing the product in use.
  • Packaging, if it matters for gifting.

You do not need every type for every product. The aim is to remove guesswork. Shoppers who are unsure about size or colour may hesitate, buy elsewhere or return the item.

Uploading and ordering images

Images are added from the media section of each product in Shopify admin. You can drag files in, upload from your computer or reuse files already in your store's file library.

The first image matters most

The first media item is usually used as the product's main image on collection pages, in search results on your store and in many sales channels. Make it a clean, recognisable shot. Keep the style consistent across a collection so the grid looks tidy.

Order the rest deliberately

Drag media into the order you want customers to see it. A sensible sequence moves from overview to detail: main shot, other angles, close-ups, scale, then lifestyle. Avoid putting a video first unless it truly is the best introduction, because some themes handle a leading video less gracefully on collection pages.

Keep aspect ratios consistent

Mixed shapes, such as some square and some tall images, can make galleries jump around as customers swipe. Many themes let you choose an image ratio or crop. Picking one ratio for your catalogue and shooting to it saves editing time later.

Linking images to variants

If a product comes in several colours or finishes, you can assign an image to each variant. When a customer selects a variant, many themes switch the main image to match. This is one of the simplest ways to reduce confusion on product pages.

To do this, open the variant in Shopify admin and choose its image from the product's media. Some themes go further and show only the media relevant to the selected variant; others show everything. Our guide to Shopify product variants covers structuring options, and it is worth deciding on a variant plan before photographing so each colour gets the shots it needs.

Writing alt text for product images

Every product image can have alt text, a short written description read aloud by screen readers and shown if an image fails to load. It also helps search engines understand what the image shows.

Good product alt text describes what is visible and what distinguishes this image from the others. For example, a description of the back of a navy wool jumper with ribbed cuffs is more useful than the product name repeated across every image. Keep it concise and natural, and avoid stuffing in keywords.

Purely decorative images do not need detailed descriptions, but on a product page almost every image carries information. Our article on website accessibility explains why this matters beyond search.

Image file quality and size

Shopify serves images through its content delivery network and generates different sizes automatically, which helps. You still control the starting point. A few habits help:

  • Upload images large enough to look sharp when zoomed, but not unnecessarily huge.
  • Crop out empty space before uploading rather than relying on the theme.
  • Use descriptive file names rather than camera defaults, as they are easier to manage and can help search engines.
  • Check colour accuracy, as colour mismatch is a frequent reason customers feel a product was not as described.

For more on preparing files, see our guide to website image optimisation.

Using video on product pages

Video is good at showing things still images struggle with: how fabric drapes and moves, how a gadget works, how a lid opens or how big a bag really is when worn. A short clip without spoken narration can work well, as many shoppers browse with sound off.

Uploaded versus linked video

Uploading video directly to Shopify keeps viewers on your page without third-party branding. Linking to a video hosted elsewhere can be convenient if the clip already exists, but it may load extra scripts and show the host's interface. Weigh up which suits your brand and page speed.

Keep videos purposeful

Short, focused clips usually serve product pages better than long brand films. Add captions or on-screen text if the video includes information customers need. Avoid autoplaying video with sound. Our wider website video guide covers planning and production.

Using 3D models

A 3D model lets a customer rotate a product and, on compatible phones and tablets, place it in their room through augmented reality. This can be particularly useful for furniture, homeware, lighting and other products where size and placement matter.

3D models need to be created specifically, either by a 3D artist or using specialist scanning. They must be in a format Shopify accepts and should be checked for accurate scale and colour. A model that looks wrong may do more harm than good, so treat it as an investment only for products where it genuinely helps.

Always keep standard photographs alongside 3D models. Not every device supports augmented reality, and many customers will still prefer to scroll through images.

Keeping product pages fast

Rich media can slow pages down, especially on mobile connections. Speed affects how comfortable a page feels to use and is one of many factors in search performance. To keep media from becoming a burden:

  • Avoid uploading many near-identical images.
  • Check that your theme loads images below the visible area only when needed.
  • Be selective with video and 3D, using them on products where they add real value.
  • Test product pages on a mid-range phone, not just a fast office computer.

Our guide to Shopify speed optimisation goes into theme and app factors as well.

Managing media across a growing catalogue

As your range grows, consistency becomes harder. A simple photography brief helps: background colour, lighting, angles, ratio and file naming. Share it with anyone who shoots products for you. Reuse brand imagery from the store's file library rather than uploading duplicates, and periodically review older products whose photos no longer match the rest.

When products are discontinued, think about what happens to their pages and media before deleting anything. Our article on Shopify URL redirects explains how to handle removed products without leaving dead ends.

FAQs

How many images should a Shopify product have?

There is no fixed number. Use enough images to answer the questions a buyer would ask, without repeating near-identical shots. Simple products may need few; complex or variable products may need more.

Can I show different images for each colour?

Yes. Assign an image to each variant in Shopify admin. Depending on your theme, selecting a variant will switch the main image or filter the gallery.

Should I use video on every product?

Not necessarily. Video is most useful where movement, function or scale is hard to show in photos. For simple products, good photography may be enough.

Do I need alt text on every product image?

It is good practice to add meaningful alt text to product images, because they usually carry information. Describe what each specific image shows rather than repeating the product title.

Will 3D models work on every device?

No. Viewing in augmented reality depends on the customer's device and browser. Keep standard photographs alongside any 3D model.

Can I reuse the same image on several products?

Yes, you can select existing files from your store's file library. This is useful for shared lifestyle images, but each product should still have its own clear main image.
