When WordPress is the right choice

WordPress suits businesses that publish a lot of content, need flexible page layouts, want full ownership of their site and data, or have specific integration needs. It is a good fit for service businesses, publishers, membership sites and many B2B firms.

It is less ideal when a business wants a fully hosted platform with minimal upkeep, or runs a product-heavy store where Shopify's built-in commerce features save time. We are happy to recommend either, depending on what fits. Our broader WordPress agency in Canada guide compares the options in more detail.

Speed, security and maintenance

Many WordPress problems come from heavy themes, too many plugins and missed updates. We build with a lean setup, choose well-maintained plugins only when they add real value, and set up caching and image handling so pages load quickly on mobile connections.

Security relies on regular updates, strong login practices, reliable hosting and tested backups. After launch, we can look after these tasks on a monthly basis, or hand them over to your team with clear notes. Either way, you own the site, the domain and the hosting account.

WooCommerce for Calgary retailers

WooCommerce turns WordPress into a full online store. It handles products, variations, stock, payments and shipping, and it can be extended for subscriptions, bookings and wholesale pricing. For Canadian stores, sales tax settings need care because GST, HST and provincial sales taxes vary by province. We set up the store structure, but you should confirm tax treatment with your accountant.

For stores that want to rank for product searches, our guide to ecommerce SEO in Canada explains how category and product pages should be built.

French and multilingual sites

Most Calgary sites are in English, but some businesses serve French-speaking customers elsewhere in Canada. Prism writes in English. French content should be written or checked by your own team or a professional translator. We then set up the multilingual structure, language tags and navigation so each version works properly.

Privacy and consent on your website

Contact forms, analytics, cookies and email sign-ups all involve personal information. In general terms, PIPEDA is the federal privacy law covering many private-sector businesses, CASL governs commercial electronic messages such as marketing emails, some provinces including Alberta have their own private-sector privacy laws, and Quebec's Law 25 sets specific requirements for businesses dealing with people in Quebec. We build forms and consent tools that make good practice easier, but these notes are general information only. Please check official guidance and speak to your own adviser. In regulated sectors, compliance approval rests with you and your compliance team.

Choosing a WordPress agency

Whoever you choose, ask how they keep sites fast, which plugins they rely on, who owns the hosting and code, how updates are handled and what happens if you move to another provider. Clear answers to these questions are a good sign that the site will stay healthy for years.

Accessibility and good content structure

A good WordPress site should work for everyone, including people using screen readers, keyboard navigation or larger text settings. We follow recognised accessibility practices in design and build, such as clear heading structure, readable colour contrast, descriptive link text, alternative text for images and forms that label every field. Accessibility rules vary by province and sector, so please check official guidance for any duties that apply to your organisation and speak to your own adviser.

Clear structure also helps search engines and AI-powered search tools understand your site. Each page should have one main topic, a descriptive title, logical headings and internal links to related pages. We plan this before design starts, so that the site is easy to navigate for people and easy to read for search engines.

Redesigning an existing WordPress site

Many Calgary businesses already have a WordPress site that has grown messy over the years, with old plugins, slow pages and content nobody owns. A redesign does not always mean starting from scratch. Sometimes the best route is a clean rebuild on a lighter theme while keeping the content and URLs that already rank. Other times a full rebuild with a new structure makes more sense.

Whichever route we take, we map old URLs to new ones and set up redirects, so that search visibility built over years is not lost at launch. We also check tracking, forms and integrations before and after go-live, and we monitor Search Console in the weeks that follow to catch any problems early.
