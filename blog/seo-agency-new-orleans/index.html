Two audiences: locals and visitors

Few US cities depend on visitors the way New Orleans does. Restaurants, bars, music venues, tours, hotels, guest houses and shops in the French Quarter, the Marigny, the Garden District and along Magazine Street all compete for travellers who search weeks before a trip and again once they arrive.

At the same time, the city has a large base of residents who need plumbers, roofers, dentists, lawyers, accountants, contractors and clinics. Their searches look very different. They want someone nearby, available and trustworthy, often in a hurry after a storm or a breakdown.

A good SEO plan separates these audiences. Visitor-facing businesses need pages that answer planning questions and show up for searches made from other cities. Local service firms need strong map visibility in the neighbourhoods and parishes they serve, from Metairie and Kenner to the Northshore and the West Bank.

Local SEO across the region

For most local service firms, the map pack is the most valuable spot in search. Google decides who appears there based on relevance, distance and prominence. You can improve relevance and prominence by choosing accurate categories, listing every service, adding genuine photos, keeping hours current and asking satisfied customers for reviews in a steady, honest way.

Consistency matters too. Your business name, address and phone number should match across your website and the main directories. Where you serve several parishes or suburbs, location pages can help, but only if each one has real, specific information. Pages that swap one place name for another rarely rank.

Seasonal and event-driven search

Search demand in New Orleans rises and falls with the calendar. Carnival season, the big music festivals, sporting events and conventions all bring spikes in searches for food, stays, tours and transport. Hurricane season brings a different spike for roofers, tree services, generators and repairs.

SEO works best when pages for these moments exist well before demand peaks. Google needs time to find and rank them. We plan seasonal content months ahead and update it each year rather than creating new pages from scratch.

Technical SEO and site speed

Many visitors search on mobile data while walking around the city, so slow pages cost real business. We check loading speed on phones, image sizes, code bloat and how Google crawls and indexes your site. We also add structured data so search engines understand your opening hours, location, menus or services.

If your site needs a full rebuild, our website development service can build a fast site with SEO built in. For hotels and venues, direct booking pages also matter, because they help you reduce reliance on third-party booking platforms.

Content that answers real questions

Visitors ask practical questions: where to eat near a hotel, how to get to a venue, what to expect on a tour, whether a place takes groups. Residents ask different ones: how much a repair costs, how fast someone can come out, whether a firm handles a particular type of case. Pages that answer these clearly tend to rank and convert better than thin pages with a list of services.

Clear answers also help with AI-powered search. Google's AI Overviews and chat assistants often pull from pages that give direct, factual responses. We write in English, keep claims factual and avoid invented figures or testimonials.

Reviews and reputation

Reviews carry extra weight in a city where so many customers are making quick decisions far from home. A visitor choosing between two restaurants on the same block will often pick the one with more recent, detailed reviews. A resident looking for a roofer after a storm will do the same.

We help you build a simple, honest process for asking customers to leave reviews, such as a short follow-up message after a job or a card at the end of a meal. We never suggest buying reviews or offering rewards for positive ones, because that breaks platform rules and can lead to removed reviews or a suspended profile. Replying to reviews, good and bad, also shows future customers that you care, and it gives you a chance to correct misunderstandings politely.
