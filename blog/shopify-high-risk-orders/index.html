Shopify High Risk Orders: How to Check Fraud Warnings and Decide What to Do

What a Shopify high risk order warning means, how to review the fraud indicators, when to contact the customer and how to cancel safely before you ship.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A high risk warning in Shopify is a prompt to review the order, not proof of fraud, so check the indicators before deciding.
  • The safest time to act is before you fulfil: once goods have shipped, a fraudulent order is much harder to recover from.
  • A short written review routine, with clear rules on when to contact, hold or cancel, keeps decisions consistent and quick.

A Shopify high risk order is one that Shopify's fraud analysis has flagged as more likely than usual to be fraudulent, based on signals such as mismatched addresses, failed card checks or unusual order patterns. The warning means you should review the order before fulfilling it, not that you must cancel it, because many flagged orders turn out to be genuine.

This guide explains what the fraud analysis shows, how to review a flagged order, when to contact the customer, and how to cancel and refund cleanly if you decide not to proceed. Fraud tools, payment rules and chargeback processes change, so check Shopify's current help pages and your payment provider's guidance for the exact details that apply to your store.

What Shopify's fraud analysis shows

On an order's page in the Shopify admin, you will usually see a fraud analysis or order risk section. It gives an overall risk level and lists the indicators that contributed to it. Exactly what you see depends on your payment provider and plan. Stores using Shopify Payments generally see the most detail, while some third-party gateways provide less information or their own separate risk checks.

Common indicators include:

  • Whether the card verification code and billing address checks passed or failed.
  • Whether the billing and delivery addresses match, and how far apart they are.
  • Whether the customer's IP address location is far from the billing or delivery address, or uses a proxy.
  • Whether there were several payment attempts with different cards before the order went through.
  • Whether the order looks unusual compared with normal orders, such as many units of a high-value item.

No single indicator settles the question. A customer sending a gift to a relative will have mismatched addresses, and someone on a work network may appear to be in a different city. The overall pattern matters more than any one item.

How to review Shopify high risk orders

A consistent routine makes decisions faster and fairer. Here is the review process we suggest for small and mid-sized UK stores.

  1. Pause fulfilment. Do not pack or ship the order until you have reviewed it. If your fulfilment is automated, make sure high risk orders are held. Our Shopify order fulfilment workflow guide covers how to build review steps into your process.
  2. Read the indicators. Note which checks failed and which passed.
  3. Look at the customer history. A returning customer with previous successful orders is a very different case from a brand new account placing a large first order.
  4. Check the order itself. Unusual quantities, the most expensive items only, or express delivery to an address far from the billing address are worth extra thought.
  5. Search the email address and delivery address. Look for obvious problems, such as a delivery address that is a parcel forwarding service when you do not normally sell abroad.
  6. Decide: fulfil, contact the customer for more information, or cancel and refund.
  7. Record the decision. Add a short note to the order explaining what you checked and why you decided as you did.

Contacting the customer

When an order is borderline, a polite message is often the best next step. Keep it neutral and avoid accusing anyone. Something along the lines of asking the customer to confirm a detail of the order before it is dispatched works well. Genuine customers usually reply quickly. A lack of reply, or a reply asking you to change the delivery address to somewhere else, is useful information in itself.

Use the contact details on the order, and be cautious of requests to switch to a different email or phone number. Never ask a customer to send full card details by email or phone. If you need more assurance, cancelling and asking the customer to reorder is safer than handling payment information yourself.

Cancelling and refunding a risky order

If you decide not to go ahead, cancel the order in Shopify and refund the payment through the same method. Cancelling before fulfilment keeps your stock accurate and returns the money to the card holder, which matters if the card was stolen. Our guide to Shopify order editing and refunds explains the cancel and refund options in more detail.

Some stores choose to authorise payments at checkout and capture them manually later, which gives time for review before the money is taken. This changes how payments and refunds behave and may have time limits set by the payment provider, so read Shopify's current guidance on payment capture and look at your Shopify checkout settings before switching.

Chargebacks: what happens if fraud gets through

A chargeback happens when a card holder disputes a payment with their bank. If the card was used without permission, the payment is usually reversed, and the store may also lose the goods and pay a fee. Shopify shows chargebacks on the order and, depending on your payment provider, lets you submit evidence such as proof of delivery, customer communication and order details.

Good records help. Tracked delivery, clear order notes and saved customer messages make it easier to respond. Clear terms and delivery information also help with genuine disputes. Our guide to Shopify store policies covers the refund, shipping and terms pages that support your case.

Reducing fraud risk across the store

Reviewing orders is the main defence, but a few wider measures help reduce how often you need to.

  • Use a payment provider with strong checks. Card payments in the UK generally involve strong customer authentication for many transactions, which helps reduce card fraud. Our guide to payment gateways for UK online shops compares common options.
  • Use tracked delivery for higher-value orders and consider signature on delivery where appropriate. Our online shop shipping rates guide covers how to price tracked options.
  • Automate the flagging. A simple rule can tag high risk orders and alert the person who reviews them, so nothing slips through on a busy day.
  • Limit staff access. Make sure only trusted staff can change payment settings or issue refunds. See our guide to Shopify staff accounts and permissions.
  • Watch for patterns. If several risky orders arrive for the same product or from the same area, consider extra checks on that product for a while.

Getting the balance right

Being too cautious costs sales and annoys genuine customers. Being too relaxed costs stock and fees. The right balance depends on your margins, the resale value of your products and how often fraud has affected you. A store selling low-value consumables can accept more risk than one selling high-end electronics or designer goods.

FAQs

What does a Shopify high risk order mean?

It means Shopify's fraud analysis found several signals that suggest the order is more likely than usual to be fraudulent. It is a prompt to review the order before fulfilling it, not proof of fraud.

Should I cancel all high risk orders?

Not automatically. Many flagged orders are genuine. Review the indicators, the customer's history and the order itself, contact the customer if it is borderline, and cancel only when the overall picture is concerning.

Does Shopify cover losses from fraudulent orders?

This depends on your payment provider, your location and any protection programmes available to your store, which change over time. Check Shopify's current help pages and your payment terms rather than assuming you are covered.

Can I stop high risk orders being fulfilled automatically?

Yes, in most setups. You can use manual fulfilment, an automation rule that tags and holds high risk orders, or settings in your fulfilment app. Check that any third-party warehouse also respects the hold.

Is it safe to ask a customer to verify their order?

Yes, if you use the contact details on the order and keep the message polite and neutral. Never ask for full card details by email or phone. If you remain unsure, cancelling and refunding is the safer choice.

What should I keep in case of a chargeback?

Keep the order details, tracking and proof of delivery, any customer messages, and your published refund and delivery policies. Having these ready makes it easier to respond within the deadline your payment provider sets.
