Why Dublin brands build on Shopify

Dublin is home to a growing mix of ecommerce businesses: fashion and accessories, homeware, food and drink, beauty and skincare, gifts and specialist retailers moving their shops online. Most of them share the same needs. They want a store that is quick to launch, easy for a small team to run and ready to sell beyond Ireland when the time comes.

Shopify suits that well. It handles products, payments, stock, discounts and shipping in one place, and its app ecosystem covers reviews, subscriptions, loyalty and accounting. With Shopify Markets, you can sell into other EU countries and the UK in local currencies. Our guide to Shopify Markets for Europe explains how that works.

Shipping, VAT and selling across borders from Dublin

One of the biggest practical questions for Dublin stores is where you will sell. Selling within Ireland is simple to set up. Selling into other EU countries, Northern Ireland and Great Britain each brings its own shipping, tax and customs considerations.

We set up shipping zones, rates and tax settings in Shopify to match the plan you agree with your accountant. We keep this general: VAT and customs rules for cross-border ecommerce can be detailed, so check the official Revenue guidance or ask your own adviser before you launch into a new market. Our role is to make the store reflect those decisions clearly, with delivery costs and timescales visible before checkout.

Clear delivery and returns pages matter just as much. Our guide to ecommerce delivery and returns pages covers what shoppers look for.

Designing a Shopify store that converts

Traffic is only half the job. A store also needs to turn visitors into buyers. During the build we focus on the parts of the store that most affect sales.

  1. Home page: a clear statement of what you sell and who it is for, with routes into your main collections.
  2. Collection pages: filters, sorting and product cards that make choosing easy.
  3. Product pages: strong images, clear prices, sizes or variants, delivery information and reviews.
  4. Cart and checkout: few distractions, trusted payment options and no surprise costs.
  5. Speed: compressed images, a lean theme and only essential apps.

What to prepare before your Shopify project

Shopify projects run fastest when the raw materials are ready early. You do not need every item on day one, but each one that arrives late can push back the launch date.

  • Product data: names, prices, variants, weights, barcodes and stock levels, ideally in a spreadsheet.
  • Images: consistent product photos on plain backgrounds, plus a few lifestyle images for banners.
  • Brand assets: logo files, colours, fonts and any brand guidelines you already have.
  • Copy: an about page, product descriptions and collection intros, or a brief so we can write English copy for you.
  • Policies: privacy, terms, delivery and returns wording, reviewed by your own adviser.
  • Shipping and tax decisions: which markets you will sell to, carriers, rates and the tax approach agreed with your accountant.

We share a simple checklist at the start of every project and track each item in the shared timeline, so you always know what is outstanding.

Common Shopify mistakes Dublin stores make

Many of the stores we review share the same avoidable problems. Fixing them early saves money and protects sales.

  • Too many apps: each app can add scripts that slow the store down and add monthly fees.
  • Thin product pages: a single photo and a one-line description rarely convince a careful buyer.
  • Hidden delivery costs: surprise charges at checkout are a common reason for abandoned carts.
  • No redirects after a migration: old URLs that break can lose search rankings built up over years.
  • Tracking that ignores consent: analytics set up without a consent tool can create GDPR problems and unreliable data.
  • Duplicate collections: overlapping collections with near-identical products can confuse both shoppers and search engines.

If your store already exists, we can start with a review of these areas before suggesting a rebuild, since a full rebuild is not always needed. Our guide to Shopify speed optimisation covers one of the most common fixes.
