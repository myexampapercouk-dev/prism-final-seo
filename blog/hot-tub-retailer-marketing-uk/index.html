Hot Tub Retailer Marketing in the UK: How to Sell More Spas and Swim Spas

Hot tub retailer marketing in the UK works when your showroom, local SEO, seasonal ads and honest running-cost content turn browsers into wet tests and sales.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Hot tub buying is seasonal and research-heavy, so plan content and ad budgets around the months when people start comparing, not just when they buy.
  • Model pages, honest running-cost explainers and installation guidance answer the questions that otherwise send buyers to cheaper online sellers.
  • Driving showroom visits and wet tests is usually the strongest goal for paid ads, because the in-person experience closes higher-value sales.

Hot tub retailer marketing in the UK is about getting serious buyers into your showroom, ideally for a wet test, and giving them reasons to choose you over cheap online sellers. The retailers who grow usually combine a well-built website with clear model pages, strong local search visibility, seasonal paid campaigns and honest content about running costs, installation and aftercare.

This guide is for independent hot tub and spa showrooms, swim spa specialists, garden leisure retailers and businesses that sell, install and service hot tubs. We cover how buyers search, how to structure your website, where to spend on ads and how to stand out when every competitor seems to be running a sale.

How people shop for a hot tub

Most hot tub buyers spend weeks or months researching. They start with questions such as "inflatable vs hard shell hot tub", "how much does a hot tub cost to run" and "best hot tub for a small garden". As they get closer, searches turn local and commercial: "hot tub showroom near me", "hot tubs for sale [county]", "swim spa dealer" and brand-plus-town searches for the models you stock.

Many people also want to sit in a tub before spending thousands of pounds. That makes your showroom a powerful asset, and the job of your marketing is often to get the right people through the door rather than to sell entirely online.

Build model pages that do the selling

Model pages are the heart of a hot tub retailer's website. Many retailers simply paste manufacturer descriptions, which means dozens of sites share the same text and none stand out. Write your own pages for each model you stock, covering seating layout, number of jets, insulation, filtration, dimensions, power supply, what the price includes and whether it is on display in your showroom.

Add your own photos and short videos from the showroom, and explain who each model suits: couples, families, people wanting hydrotherapy-style seating, or those with limited garden space. Our guide to product page SEO explains how to make these pages rank, and writing ecommerce product descriptions covers the copy itself.

Be honest about running costs and installation

Running costs are one of the biggest worries for buyers, especially when energy prices are in the news. A clear explainer of what affects running costs (insulation, cover quality, usage, temperature settings and the local climate) builds trust. Avoid quoting precise annual figures unless you can back them up for your own models, and explain the assumptions behind any guidance you give.

Installation is the other big question. Explain base requirements, access for delivery, electrical supply and the need for a qualified electrician, using general terms and advising customers to check current official guidance and use a suitably qualified professional for electrical work. A clear "what happens on delivery day" page reduces anxiety and cuts down on repetitive phone calls.

Local SEO to fill the showroom

Showroom visits come from local search. Keep your Google Business Profile accurate, with seasonal opening hours, plenty of showroom photos, the brands you stock and regular posts about events and new models. Ask buyers for reviews after delivery, mentioning the model and the service they received; our guide on getting more Google reviews has practical ideas.

On your website, create a showroom page with directions, parking, wet test booking and opening hours, and consider area pages for the towns you deliver to most often, filled with genuine delivery photos and notes. Plan paid ads around the season

Hot tub demand rises and falls through the year, and research often starts well before the purchase. Many retailers see interest build in late winter and spring, with another push around autumn and the run-up to Christmas, but your own sales data is the best guide. Plan budgets to start before the peak so you are building audiences and remarketing lists when people are still comparing.

  • Google Ads: focus on showroom, model and brand-plus-location searches, and add negative keywords for inflatable tubs, spare parts and jobs if you do not want those searches.
  • Meta ads: promote showroom events, wet test days and new arrivals to homeowners within your delivery radius.
  • Remarketing: show model videos and finance information to people who viewed model pages but did not book a visit.
  • Conversion goals: track wet test bookings, brochure requests and calls rather than clicks alone.

Compete on more than price

Online sellers and big-box retailers can often undercut an independent on headline price. Your advantage is everything around the tub: advice, a showroom, wet tests, professional delivery, water care guidance and local servicing. Make these visible on every model page and in your ads. If you offer finance, explain it clearly and keep the wording general, noting that finance is subject to status and that approval rests with the finance provider.

Avoid permanent "sale" banners. Constant discounts teach buyers to wait and erode trust. Time-limited event offers backed by a genuine reason, such as a showroom open weekend or end of a model line, are more credible.

Sell to owners after the sale

A hot tub owner needs chemicals, filters, covers and servicing for years. An online shop for consumables and a simple email programme turn one sale into a long relationship. Send water care tips, servicing reminders and seasonal checklists, and make reordering easy. Our guide to ecommerce email marketing covers the essentials. If your current site cannot handle online orders well, our website and ecommerce development service builds shops on Shopify or WooCommerce.

Make wet tests easy to book

A wet test is often the moment a buyer decides, so treat booking one as your main website goal. Put a clear button on every model page and your showroom page, offer specific time slots rather than a vague request form, and send a confirmation that tells people what to bring, how long it takes and where to park. A reminder the day before reduces no-shows.

After the visit, follow up within a day or two with the models they tried, any quote you discussed and an easy way to ask further questions. Many buyers want to talk it over at home, so a polite check-in a week later is often welcome.

Measure sales, not just clicks

Track each enquiry from first visit to sale: the channel, the page they landed on, whether they booked a wet test and whether they bought. Even a simple monthly spreadsheet will show which campaigns, models and towns produce profitable sales, so you can move budget away from activity that only brings in browsers.

FAQs

What is the best hot tub retailer marketing in the UK?

For most independents, the strongest mix is a website with original model pages, local SEO and an up-to-date Google Business Profile, seasonal Google and Meta campaigns aimed at showroom visits, and email marketing to existing owners.

Should hot tub retailers sell tubs online?

Some do, but many find that showroom visits and wet tests close higher-value sales. Selling chemicals, covers and accessories online is usually a simpler win.

When should I increase my hot tub ad budget?

Start before your busiest months, so you reach people while they are still researching. Use your own past sales data to judge timing.

Can I publish running costs for my hot tubs?

You can explain what affects running costs. Only give figures you can support for your own models, and state the assumptions behind them.

How do I compete with cheaper online hot tub sellers?

Highlight what they cannot offer: wet tests, expert advice, professional delivery, local servicing and ongoing water care support.

Do I need separate pages for swim spas?

Yes. Swim spa buyers search differently and have different questions about size, installation and use, so a dedicated section usually performs better.
