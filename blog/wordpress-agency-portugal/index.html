A WordPress agency for a Portuguese business should give you a website that is fast, secure and easy for your own team to update, with content that reads naturally in European Portuguese and the compliance basics in place from launch. For online shops, that also means a WooCommerce checkout with the payment methods Portuguese customers expect.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs, builds and supports WordPress websites remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Lisbon, Porto or anywhere else in Portugal. Our website and e-commerce development service runs by video call, shared documents and private staging links, and mainland Portugal shares the same time zone as the UK, which makes scheduling simple. We work in English. Portuguese copy is written or checked by your team or a professional translator.

Why Portuguese businesses choose WordPress

WordPress powers a large share of the world's websites, and for good reason. It is flexible, widely supported and easy to edit once set up properly. Portuguese businesses use it for everything from small brochure sites to busy content hubs and online shops.

The businesses that tend to suit WordPress best include:

  • Hospitality and tourism. Hotels, guest houses, restaurants and tour operators across Lisbon, Porto, the Algarve and the islands.
  • Professional services. Consultancies, architects, accountants and legal firms that need clear service pages and a steady way to publish.
  • Property and relocation. Agencies and service firms that publish listings and guides for local and international clients.
  • Technology and start-ups. Companies in Portugal's growing technology scene that need a credible site that can grow.
  • Small online shops. Food, wine, cork, ceramics and lifestyle brands selling through WooCommerce.

European Portuguese content

Portuguese in Portugal differs from Brazilian Portuguese in spelling, vocabulary and tone. Many website templates, plugins and translation tools default to Brazilian Portuguese, which Portuguese visitors notice immediately. We set WordPress to the correct language locale and check that plugin and theme text, such as checkout labels and form messages, appears in European Portuguese.

The page copy itself comes from your team or a professional translator. We provide page structures, headings and briefs in English, then place and format the Portuguese text. If you serve international customers, as many businesses in Portugal's tourism and property sectors do, we can add English versions as proper language pages with their own URLs, not as an automatic translation widget.

WooCommerce for Portuguese shops

WooCommerce turns WordPress into a capable online shop. For Portuguese customers, the checkout matters most. Familiar payment methods such as Multibanco references and MB WAY are widely used, alongside cards, and are usually added through a payment provider that supports them. Shipping zones should cover the mainland and, if you deliver there, the Azores and Madeira, which often have different rates.

Our guide to choosing a WooCommerce agency covers the platform in more depth. Tax settings, invoicing and any certified invoicing software you need are matters to confirm with your accountant, and we set WooCommerce up to match their advice.

Speed, hosting and security

A WordPress site is only as good as its hosting and maintenance. Slow, cheap hosting and out-of-date plugins are the most common causes of poor performance and security problems. We recommend hosting with servers in Europe, daily backups and a sensible update routine. Our guides to WordPress hosting and WordPress security explain what to look for.

After launch, sites need regular care: core, theme and plugin updates, backups, uptime checks and occasional fixes. Our post on website maintenance covers what a good care routine includes.

Compliance basics for Portuguese websites

These notes are general information only. Please follow official guidance from the relevant authorities and take advice from your own legal adviser.

  • GDPR. Contact forms, bookings, newsletters and analytics all collect personal data. You need a clear privacy notice, a lawful basis and sensible retention. Portugal's data protection authority publishes guidance in Portuguese.
  • Cookie consent. Under the ePrivacy rules, as applied in Portuguese law, non-essential cookies such as analytics and advertising tags generally need prior consent, and rejecting should be as easy as accepting. Our article on cookie banner design covers the practical side.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer services, including e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but accessible design helps every visitor. The expected standard is EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA. See our overview of the European Accessibility Act for websites.

WordPress can meet these requirements well when themes and plugins are chosen carefully, but it does not happen by default. Some popular themes ship with low-contrast colours, sliders that are hard to use with a keyboard or forms without proper labels, and some consent plugins load tracking scripts before visitors have made a choice. We check these points during the build rather than leaving them for an audit later. Accessible block patterns, a consent tool that genuinely blocks tags before consent, and clear form labels all make a difference.

How a remote WordPress project works

  1. Discovery call. We discuss your goals, audience, content, languages and any existing site.
  2. Sitemap and wireframes. We agree the structure and key page layouts.
  3. Design. We design the homepage and main templates for your feedback.
  4. Build on staging. We build the site on a private staging link you can review on your own devices.
  5. Content. Your team or translator supplies European Portuguese copy; we place and format it.
  6. Testing and launch. We test forms, checkout, speed, devices and consent, set up redirects and go live.
  7. Training and care. We show your team how to edit pages and posts, and agree any ongoing support.

Choosing a WordPress partner

Whoever you work with, these questions help you compare proposals:

  • Will the site use a lean, well-supported set of plugins?
  • Will the language be set to European Portuguese throughout, including checkout and form messages?
  • Will you own the domain, hosting and all site files?
  • How will cookie consent and accessibility be handled?
  • What does ongoing care include, and how is it charged?

A remote UK studio suits Portuguese businesses that are comfortable working in English and want a clear, documented process. If you want regular in-person meetings, a local agency may suit you better.
