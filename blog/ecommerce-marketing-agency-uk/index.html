Ecommerce Marketing Agency UK: How SEO, Shopping Ads, Email and CRO Work Together

An ecommerce marketing agency in the UK grows online sales with SEO, Google Shopping, email and CRO. What each channel does, costs and how to pick one.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • An ecommerce marketing agency should join up SEO, Google Shopping, email and conversion work, because each channel makes the others cheaper and more effective.
  • Judge an agency on revenue, margin and returning customers rather than traffic, and make sure you own your ad accounts, data and email lists.
  • Shopping ads can drive sales within weeks, while SEO typically takes 3 to 6 months, so most UK shops need a plan that balances both.

An ecommerce marketing agency in the UK helps online shops sell more by combining SEO, Google Shopping and other paid ads, email marketing and conversion rate optimisation into one plan focused on revenue and margin. The best agencies treat these channels as a system: Shopping brings new buyers, email brings them back, SEO lowers your reliance on paid clicks and CRO makes every visit worth more.

This guide explains what an ecommerce marketing agency does, how each channel contributes, what UK shops typically pay and what to check before you sign anything.

What does an ecommerce marketing agency UK shops use actually do?

Unlike a lead generation business, an online shop can see the commercial effect of marketing almost immediately in orders and revenue. That makes ecommerce marketing more measurable, but it also means cutting corners shows up quickly in margins. A capable agency usually covers four core areas, plus the tracking that ties them together.

  • Ecommerce SEO: technical health, category and product page optimisation, internal linking and content, delivered through our SEO and GEO service.
  • Google Shopping and paid media: product feeds, Performance Max and Standard Shopping, search campaigns and paid social, run through our paid media service.
  • Email and retention: welcome flows, abandoned basket emails, post-purchase sequences and campaigns to past customers.
  • Conversion rate optimisation: product page layout, delivery and returns clarity, checkout friction, site speed and trust signals.

Our ecommerce sector page explains how we approach this for UK online retailers.

Ecommerce SEO

For most shops, category pages are the biggest organic opportunity, because they match how people search: "women's waterproof walking boots" rather than a single product code. Good ecommerce SEO also deals with duplicate content from filters, out-of-stock products, thin descriptions and slow pages. Our guide to choosing an ecommerce SEO agency covers this in depth, and Shopify owners may find our Shopify SEO guide useful.

Google Shopping

Shopping ads show your product image, price and shop name directly in Google results. Performance depends heavily on feed quality: clear titles, accurate prices, good images, correct product categories and up-to-date stock. Our Google Shopping ads guide explains feeds, campaign types and budgets in detail.

A sensible agency will look at profit, not just return on ad spend. A product with a thin margin can look successful on ROAS while losing money once delivery, returns and payment fees are counted.

Email marketing

Email is often the most cost-effective channel a shop has, because you are talking to people who already know you. Automated flows such as welcome series, abandoned basket reminders and post-purchase follow-ups work in the background every day. Marketing emails must respect UK GDPR and PECR, so consent and clear unsubscribe options matter.

Conversion rate optimisation

Small improvements to conversion rate make every other channel cheaper. Common UK fixes include showing delivery costs and cut-off times early, being clear about returns, offering familiar payment options such as Klarna or Clearpay where they suit your customers, and making mobile checkout quick. Testing needs enough traffic to be reliable, so smaller shops often start with best-practice fixes rather than formal A/B tests.

Checklist: choosing an ecommerce marketing agency

  • Do they ask about your margins, average order value and returns rate before proposing budgets?
  • Will reports show revenue and profit by channel, not just clicks and traffic?
  • Do you keep ownership of your Google Ads, Merchant Center, analytics and email accounts?
  • Is ad spend separate from the management fee and visible to you in full?
  • Can they explain how they will improve your product feed, not only bids?
  • Do they have a plan for category pages, filters and out-of-stock products?
  • Will they set up core email flows before chasing new campaigns?
  • Are they realistic that SEO typically takes 3 to 6 months to show meaningful results?

Common mistakes online shops make

Many UK shops pour budget into paid ads while neglecting email, so they pay to acquire the same customer again and again. Others chase traffic with blog content while their category pages remain thin. Some rely entirely on platform-reported ROAS without checking profit after costs. And plenty of shops run a slow mobile site that undermines every channel at once.

Another frequent issue is fragmented suppliers: one freelancer on ads, another on SEO and nobody owning the overall result. That can work, but someone needs to look at the whole picture and decide where the next pound goes.

Is an integrated agency better than specialists?

Specialists can be excellent at one channel. An integrated agency is usually better when you need the channels to work together, for example using search data to shape Shopping campaigns, or using email to lift the value of customers won through paid ads. If you have a strong in-house team, a specialist for one gap may be enough. If you do not, a joined-up plan usually wastes less money.

What a sensible first 90 days looks like

A good agency will not try to do everything at once. In the first month, expect an audit of tracking, product feed, site speed and category pages, plus a check that conversions and revenue are recorded accurately in analytics. Without reliable data, every later decision is a guess.

In the second month, the focus usually moves to quick wins: fixing feed errors, restructuring Shopping campaigns around margin, setting up or repairing core email flows and resolving the most damaging technical SEO issues. In the third month, longer-term work begins in earnest, such as improving category page content, building internal links and testing changes to product pages and checkout.

By the end of that period you should have a clear picture of which products and channels make money, what is being fixed and what the plan is for the next quarter. If an agency cannot explain its plan in those terms, keep looking.

FAQs

What does an ecommerce marketing agency in the UK do?

It grows online sales by managing channels such as ecommerce SEO, Google Shopping and paid ads, email marketing and conversion rate optimisation, with reporting focused on revenue and profit.

How much does an ecommerce marketing agency cost?

Our retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month, with ad spend paid separately. A new ecommerce website, if needed, typically costs £1,500 to £3,500.

Should I start with SEO or Google Shopping?

Shopping usually produces sales faster, while SEO builds lower-cost traffic over 3 to 6 months. Many shops start Shopping for immediate revenue and begin SEO at the same time so organic traffic grows in the background.

Is email marketing still worth it for small shops?

Yes, for most shops. Automated flows such as abandoned basket and welcome emails are relatively cheap to set up and keep working in the background, provided you collect consent properly.

What is a good ROAS for Google Shopping?

It depends on your margins. A shop with high margins can be profitable at a lower ROAS than a shop with thin margins, so the useful target is the ROAS at which you still make a profit after all costs.

Do I need a new website before hiring an ecommerce agency?

Not necessarily. Many stores improve with better product pages, faster loading and clearer delivery information. A rebuild makes sense when the platform itself limits SEO, speed or checkout.
