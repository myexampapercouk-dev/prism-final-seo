Ecommerce Marketing Agency UK: What One Does and How to Choose

What an ecommerce marketing agency does across SEO, paid search, Shopping, social ads and conversion work, and how it differs from a specialist ad agency.

By the Prism Works Studio team · Updated 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • An ecommerce marketing agency joins up channels such as SEO, Google Shopping, paid social, email and conversion work around profitable sales.
  • A specialist ad or paid search agency may be enough if organic and on-site work are already covered in-house.
  • Judge any agency on revenue, margin and returning customers rather than traffic, and keep ownership of your accounts and data.

An ecommerce marketing agency helps online shops sell more profitably by running channels such as SEO, Google Shopping, paid search, paid social, email and conversion work as one plan. A good one treats them as a system: ads reach new buyers, email can bring them back, SEO can reduce reliance on paid clicks and conversion work can make each visit worth more.

This guide explains what an ecommerce marketing agency does, how an ecommerce ad or paid search agency differs, what to compare on cost and what to check before you sign anything.

What does an ecommerce marketing agency do?

An online shop can see the effect of marketing in its orders and revenue, which makes the work measurable, and also means poor decisions can show up in margins. A capable agency usually covers these areas, plus the tracking that ties them together.

  • Ecommerce SEO: technical health, category and product page optimisation, internal linking and content, delivered through our SEO and GEO service.
  • Paid search and Shopping: product feeds, Performance Max and Standard Shopping, and search campaigns, run through our paid media service.
  • Paid social: Meta and TikTok campaigns, including product ads and retargeting.
  • Email and retention: welcome flows, abandoned basket emails, post-purchase sequences and campaigns to past customers.
  • Conversion rate optimisation: product page layout, delivery and returns clarity, checkout friction, site speed and trust signals.

Ecommerce SEO

Category pages are often a strong organic opportunity, because they can match how people search: "women's waterproof walking boots" rather than a single product code. Ecommerce SEO also deals with duplicate content from filters, out-of-stock products, thin descriptions and slow pages. Our guide to choosing an ecommerce SEO agency covers this in depth.

Google Shopping

Shopping ads show your product image, price and shop name directly in Google results. Results rely on feed quality: clear titles, accurate prices, good images, correct product categories and up-to-date stock. Our Google Shopping ads guide explains feeds, campaign types and budgets in detail.

Look for an agency that talks about profit, not just return on ad spend. A product with a thin margin can look successful on ROAS while losing money once delivery, returns and payment fees are counted.

Email marketing

Email lets you talk to people who already know you. Automated flows such as welcome series, abandoned basket reminders and post-purchase follow-ups run in the background once set up. Marketing emails need to respect UK GDPR and PECR, so consent and clear unsubscribe options matter; take advice if you are unsure. See our guides to ecommerce email marketing and abandoned cart recovery.

Conversion rate optimisation

Improving conversion rate can lower the cost of every sale from every other channel. Useful areas to review include showing delivery costs and cut-off times early, being clear about returns, offering payment options that suit your customers and making mobile checkout quick. Testing needs enough traffic to be reliable, so smaller shops may start with clear fixes rather than formal A/B tests. Our ecommerce CRO guide goes further.

Ecommerce ad agency or full marketing agency?

An ecommerce ad agency or ecommerce paid search agency concentrates on paid channels: Google Shopping, search, Performance Max and sometimes paid social. A full ecommerce marketing agency also takes on organic and on-site work. Neither is automatically better.

Checklist: choosing an ecommerce marketing agency

  • Do they ask about your margins, average order value and returns rate before proposing budgets?
  • Will reports show revenue and profit by channel, not just clicks and traffic?
  • Do you keep ownership of your Google Ads, Merchant Center, analytics and email accounts?
  • Is ad spend separate from the management fee and visible to you in full?
  • Can they explain how they will improve your product feed, not only bids?
  • Do they have a plan for category pages, filters and out-of-stock products?
  • Will they set up core email flows before chasing new campaigns?
  • Are they realistic that SEO builds over time, and clear that nobody can promise rankings?

Mistakes online shops can make

Watch for a few patterns: putting budget into paid ads while neglecting email, so you pay to reach the same customer repeatedly; chasing traffic with blog content while category pages remain thin; relying on platform-reported ROAS without checking profit after costs; and running a slow mobile site that undermines every channel.

Fragmented suppliers can also be a problem: one freelancer on ads, another on SEO and nobody owning the overall result. That can work, but someone needs to look at the whole picture and decide where the next pound goes.

What a sensible order of work looks like

A good agency will not try to do everything at once. Expect it to begin with an audit of tracking, product feed, site speed and category pages, plus a check that conversions and revenue are recorded accurately. Without reliable data, later decisions are guesswork.

Next come the clearer fixes: feed errors, Shopping campaigns structured around margin, core email flows and the most damaging technical SEO issues. Longer-term work follows, such as category page content, internal links and tests on product pages and checkout.

You should come away with a clear picture of which products and channels make money, what is being fixed and what happens next. If an agency cannot explain its plan in those terms, keep looking.

FAQs

What does an ecommerce marketing agency do?

It grows online sales by managing channels such as ecommerce SEO, Google Shopping and paid ads, email marketing and conversion rate optimisation, with reporting focused on revenue and profit.

How much does an ecommerce marketing agency cost?

It depends on scope. Our retainers start from £350, £750 or £1,500 a month, with ads management in the Scale retainer and ad spend paid separately. A new ecommerce website, if needed, starts from £1,500 to £3,500.

What is the difference between an ecommerce ad agency and a marketing agency?

An ad agency focuses on paid channels. A broader marketing agency also covers areas such as SEO, conversion work and retention. The right choice depends on what you can already handle in-house.

Should I start with SEO or Google Shopping?

Shopping ads can start reaching buyers sooner, while SEO tends to build over a longer period. The right order depends on your margins, budget and how healthy your store and feed are.

Is email marketing still worth it for small shops?

It can be. Automated flows such as abandoned basket and welcome emails keep running once set up, provided you collect consent properly and have people to send them to.

What is a good ROAS for Google Shopping?

It depends on your margins. A shop with high margins can be profitable at a lower ROAS than a shop with thin margins, so the useful target is the ROAS at which you still make a profit after all costs.

Do I need a new website before hiring an ecommerce agency?

Not necessarily. Some stores improve with better product pages, faster loading and clearer delivery information. A rebuild makes sense when the platform itself limits SEO, speed or checkout.
