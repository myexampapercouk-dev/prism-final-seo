Shopify vs WooCommerce for UK Businesses: Which Should You Choose?

Shopify vs WooCommerce for UK online stores: costs, VAT, payments, shipping, SEO and maintenance compared, with a simple way to decide which suits you.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify suits sellers who want a hosted, low-maintenance store; WooCommerce suits those who want full control and already use WordPress.
  • Compare total cost over two to three years, including apps, plugins, hosting, payment fees and maintenance, not just the monthly plan.
  • Both can rank well and handle UK VAT, payments and shipping; the right choice depends on your catalogue, team and growth plans.

For most UK businesses, the choice comes down to this: Shopify is the better fit if you want a hosted store that is quick to launch and easy to run, while WooCommerce is the better fit if you want full control, already use WordPress or need unusual functionality. Both are capable platforms used by UK retailers of every size.

This guide compares them on the things that matter to UK sellers: total cost, VAT, payments, delivery, SEO, maintenance and growth, so you can make a confident decision before you invest in a build.

Cost: look at the total, not the monthly plan

Shopify's pricing is visible upfront: a monthly plan, plus apps for features the core platform does not include, and possibly a premium theme. If you use a third-party payment gateway rather than Shopify Payments, Shopify adds its own transaction fee on top of the gateway's fees on most plans.

WooCommerce itself is free, but a professional store still costs money: quality hosting, a theme, premium extensions for things like subscriptions or advanced shipping, and regular maintenance to keep WordPress, the theme and plugins updated and secure.

The fair comparison is total cost of ownership over two to three years, including the build. Our guide to ecommerce website costs in the UK breaks down these items, and our pricing page shows our starting project prices.

VAT, invoices and UK compliance

Both platforms can show prices including VAT, apply different VAT rates by product and generate VAT-ready order information. Shopify handles tax settings in its admin; WooCommerce has built-in tax tables plus extensions for more complex cases. If you sell to the EU or Northern Ireland, check how each handles the rules that apply to you, ideally with your accountant.

Whichever platform you choose, you will also need a clear privacy notice, cookie consent that meets UK GDPR and PECR, and terms covering delivery, returns and the Consumer Contracts Regulations cancellation rights.

Payments UK shoppers expect

UK shoppers expect to pay by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay and often PayPal. Buy-now-pay-later options such as Klarna and Clearpay are popular in fashion, beauty and homeware. Shopify Payments covers cards and wallets in one place, and both buy-now-pay-later providers offer Shopify integrations. WooCommerce supports the same methods through plugins, most commonly Stripe and PayPal, with more control over which provider you use.

Shipping and delivery

Clear delivery costs and options are one of the biggest factors in UK checkout completion. Both platforms support flat rates, free delivery thresholds, weight-based rates and click and collect. For label printing and tracking with Royal Mail, Evri, DPD and others, both have integrations, either through apps (Shopify) or plugins and shipping platforms (WooCommerce).

SEO: can both rank well?

Yes. Rankings depend far more on content, site structure, speed and authority than on the platform. That said, there are differences:

  • Shopify uses fixed URL structures (such as /products/ and /collections/) and gives less control over some technical details, but it is fast, secure and well supported.
  • WooCommerce gives full control over URLs, templates and content, and WordPress is excellent for blogging and buying guides, which helps content-led SEO.

On either platform, the work that matters is the same: well-written category pages, unique product descriptions, structured data, fast templates and internal links. Our SEO and GEO service covers ecommerce SEO on both.

Maintenance and security

This is the clearest difference. Shopify handles hosting, security and platform updates for you. With WooCommerce, someone has to keep WordPress core, the theme and every plugin updated, monitor security and take backups. That is very manageable with a maintenance plan, but it should be budgeted for. A neglected WooCommerce store is slower and more vulnerable.

Growth and flexibility

Shopify scales well for high order volumes and offers Shopify Plus for larger merchants who need more automation and checkout customisation. WooCommerce can scale too, but performance depends on hosting and development quality. For unusual requirements, such as complex product configurators, B2B pricing rules or deep integration with other WordPress features, WooCommerce usually offers more freedom.

Day-to-day running: what your team will notice

Platform choice also affects how your team works every day. In Shopify, adding products, processing orders, issuing refunds and creating discount codes all happen in one tidy admin that is designed for non-technical users, and there is a good mobile app for managing orders on the move. Staff accounts with different permissions are straightforward.

WooCommerce lives inside the WordPress dashboard. Anyone who already edits WordPress pages will find it familiar, and editing content, landing pages and blog posts alongside products is a real advantage. The admin can feel busier, because every plugin adds its own settings, so a well-organised build with only the plugins you need makes a big difference.

Whichever you choose, ask for training at handover so your team is confident managing products, content and promotions from day one.

Speed and performance

Page speed affects both conversion rates and search visibility, especially on mobile. Shopify's hosting is fast and consistent, but heavy themes and too many apps slow stores down, because each app can add its own scripts. WooCommerce speed depends heavily on hosting quality, caching, image optimisation and plugin choices. A well-built WooCommerce store on good hosting can be very fast; a cheap shared host with dozens of plugins will not be.

On either platform, the same rules apply: optimise images, limit third-party scripts, use a lightweight theme and test on a real phone, not just a fast office connection.

Which should you choose?

Choose Shopify if:

  • You want to launch quickly and spend your time on products and marketing, not on the website.
  • You do not have, or want, a developer on call for updates.
  • Your products and checkout are fairly standard.
  • You plan to grow order volumes significantly and value platform stability.

Choose WooCommerce if:

  • You already have a WordPress site with good rankings and content.
  • Content marketing is central to your strategy.
  • You need custom functionality or want to avoid platform transaction fees on third-party gateways.
  • You are comfortable budgeting for hosting and maintenance.

A quick rule of thumb

If you are a new product brand with a few hundred products or fewer, no existing website and no developer, Shopify is usually the simpler starting point. If you already have a WordPress site that ranks well, publish a lot of content, or need features that would require several paid apps on Shopify, WooCommerce is often the better fit. If you sit in between, the deciding factors are usually who will maintain the site and how much control you want.

Switching later

Migration in either direction is possible. Products, customers and orders can usually be moved, but URLs change, so redirects must be planned carefully to protect rankings. If you are considering a move, read our guide on redesigning or migrating a site without losing rankings first.

FAQs

Is Shopify or WooCommerce cheaper in the UK?

It depends on your store. Shopify has predictable monthly costs plus apps; WooCommerce is free software but needs hosting, extensions and maintenance. Compare total costs over two to three years.

Which is better for SEO, Shopify or WooCommerce?

Both can rank well. WooCommerce offers more technical control and stronger blogging; Shopify is fast and reliable but has fixed URL structures. Content and authority matter more than the platform.

Can Shopify handle UK VAT?

Yes. Shopify can display prices including VAT and apply VAT rates by product. Check more complex cross-border cases with your accountant.

Does WooCommerce work with Klarna and Clearpay?

Yes, through plugins or via payment providers that support them. Availability and fees depend on the provider you choose.

Can I move from WooCommerce to Shopify later?

Yes. Products, customers and content can usually be migrated, but URLs change, so a redirect plan is essential to keep your rankings.
