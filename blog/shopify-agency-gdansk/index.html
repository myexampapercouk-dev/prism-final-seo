Why Gdansk brands choose Shopify

Gdansk, together with Gdynia and Sopot, forms the Tricity, one of Poland's busiest business regions. The area has a strong port and logistics base, a growing technology sector, amber and jewellery makers, fashion and homeware brands, food producers and many small online sellers. Its position on the Baltic coast also makes it a natural base for brands selling to Scandinavia and Germany.

Shopify suits many of these businesses because it handles hosting, security and checkout, and it scales from a small catalogue to thousands of products. It also makes selling in several countries and currencies easier than many alternatives. For brands that want a store that works without a large in-house technical team, it is often a sensible choice. If you are comparing agencies in other Polish cities, our guide to Shopify in Wroclaw covers similar ground.

What a Shopify build includes

A good Shopify project covers more than picking a theme. For most Gdansk brands, we work through the following stages.

  1. Discovery. We look at your products, customers, current sales channels and target markets.
  2. Store structure. We plan collections, filters and navigation so shoppers and search engines can find products easily.
  3. Design. We customise a quality theme to your brand, focusing on product pages, collection pages and checkout flow.
  4. Payments and delivery. We set up Polish payment methods, shipping rates and locker options.
  5. Product data. We import products, variants, images and descriptions, and set up metafields where needed.
  6. SEO foundations. Page titles, descriptions, clean URLs, structured data and redirects from any old store.
  7. Testing and launch. We test orders, payments, emails and mobile layouts before going live.

Ecommerce builds typically take five to twelve weeks. Simple catalogues on a single market sit at the shorter end. Large catalogues, custom integrations with warehouse or accounting systems, and multi-country setups take longer.

Selling on Shopify and Allegro together

Allegro is Poland's largest online marketplace, and many Gdansk brands sell there as well as on their own store. The two channels work well together: Allegro brings ready-made demand, while your Shopify store builds direct customer relationships and higher margins.

The key is keeping product data, stock and pricing consistent across both. We can help structure your Shopify catalogue so it feeds other channels cleanly, and we offer marketplace SEO to improve how your listings rank. Our guide to Allegro SEO in Poland explains how listings are ranked and how to improve them.

Language and content for a Polish store

Prism writes in English. Polish product descriptions, category text and policy pages should come from your own team or a professional translator. We design layouts that work with Polish text, which often runs longer than English, and we test headings, buttons and menus with real Polish content before launch.

Product descriptions do a lot of selling. They should answer the questions shoppers ask before buying: size, materials, care, delivery times and returns. Our guide to writing ecommerce product descriptions gives a structure your team or translator can follow.

Selling from Gdansk to Scandinavia, Germany and beyond

Many Tricity brands sell abroad, especially to the Nordics, Germany and the UK. Shopify Markets lets you set different currencies, languages, prices and domains for each country from one store. Our guide to Shopify Markets in Europe explains how to plan this.

Cross-border selling brings extra questions about VAT, customs for non-EU countries and delivery times. We set up the store side, but tax and legal questions should go to your accountant or adviser, and official guidance should be your reference point.

Moving to Shopify from another platform

Many Gdansk brands already sell online through another platform, such as WooCommerce, PrestaShop or a custom system built years ago. Moving to Shopify can make the store easier to run, but a migration needs careful planning so you do not lose sales, customer data or search rankings.

Before we move anything, we export your products, customers and order history where the old platform allows it, and we crawl the current store to record every page that receives traffic. Each old product and category address is then mapped to its new Shopify address, and redirects are set up before launch. We also check that product images, variants and descriptions have come across correctly, and that order confirmation and shipping emails read properly in Polish.

Timing matters too. We usually recommend avoiding a launch just before a busy sales period, so there is time to fix any small issues while order volumes are lower.

Mistakes to avoid on a Polish Shopify store

  • Installing too many apps. Each app can add scripts that slow the store down. Start with what you need and review the list regularly.
  • Hiding delivery costs until checkout. Shoppers abandon carts when costs appear late. Show delivery options and prices clearly on product pages.
  • Using a theme's demo content. Placeholder text and images make a store look unfinished and do nothing for search.
  • Skipping mobile testing. Most shoppers browse on phones, so test the full journey, from product page to locker selection and payment, on real devices.
