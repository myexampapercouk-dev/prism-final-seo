Why Minneapolis brands choose Shopify

The Twin Cities have a strong retail and consumer brand culture, from outdoor and cold-weather gear to food and drink, home goods, beauty and apparel. Many of these brands sell direct to customers across the country, and Shopify is a natural fit: it handles payments, inventory, shipping and taxes, and it scales from a few products to thousands.

Shopify also suits businesses that sell both online and in person. A shop in North Loop or Uptown can use Shopify POS to keep stock and customer records in one place. Our guide to Shopify POS for retail covers how that works, and the principles are the same for Minnesota stores.

Seasonality matters here too. Winter gear, holiday gifts and summer lake products all have sharp peaks. A well-built store makes it easy to change collections, banners and offers quickly without needing a developer every time.

Migrating to Shopify without losing rankings

Many Minneapolis brands come to us after outgrowing WooCommerce or an older platform. A migration is a chance to clean up products and improve speed, but it carries real risk if handled carelessly. The biggest danger is losing search rankings because old URLs break.

We map every important old URL to its new Shopify equivalent and set up redirects before launch. We carry over product descriptions, meta titles and reviews where possible, and we check Search Console after launch for errors. Our guide to WooCommerce to Shopify migration walks through the full process.

Selling across the US from Minnesota

Most Twin Cities brands that choose Shopify want to sell well beyond Minnesota. That brings a few practical questions that are worth settling before launch rather than after the first busy season.

Sales tax is the first. Shopify can calculate and collect sales tax in many states, but which states you need to register in depends on your own circumstances and the rules in each state. We set up the tax settings you specify; the decisions on registration and filing rest with you and your accountant, and we point you to official state guidance.

Shipping is the second. Heavy or bulky products, such as furniture, outdoor equipment or food and drink, need careful shipping rules so you do not lose money on long-distance orders. We set up shipping zones, rates and free shipping thresholds that reflect your real costs, and we can connect common shipping apps your team already uses.

The third is returns. Clear returns information on product pages and in the footer builds trust with shoppers who have never heard of your brand. We help structure that content so it is easy to find, while the policy itself is yours to set.

Choosing the right Shopify partner

Whether you work with us or another agency, a few questions will help you compare options fairly:

  • Will we own the store, theme, domain and all data from day one?
  • How many apps will the build rely on, and what will they cost each month?
  • How will you protect our search rankings during a migration?
  • What training and documentation do we get at handover?
  • Who do we contact after launch if something breaks?

Clear answers to these questions say more about an agency than a portfolio page. We are happy to answer all of them in writing before you commit.

It also helps to be clear about your own priorities before you speak to any agency. Decide which products matter most, which platforms you sell on today, what your team can realistically manage each week and what budget you have for the build and for marketing afterwards. A short written brief makes quotes easier to compare and keeps the project focused. Our guide on how to brief a Shopify developer includes a simple template you can adapt for your own store.
