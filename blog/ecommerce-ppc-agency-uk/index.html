Ecommerce PPC Agency UK: What to Expect, What It Costs and How to Choose One

An ecommerce PPC agency runs Google Shopping, Performance Max, search and social ads for online stores. See what they do, costs and how to choose one in the UK.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Ecommerce PPC is built around your product feed, margins and profit, not just clicks and return on ad spend.
  • A good agency covers Google Shopping, Performance Max, search, and social where it fits, with tracking you can trust.
  • Ask how they measure profit, who owns the accounts and what you get each month before signing.

An ecommerce PPC agency manages paid advertising for online stores: Google Shopping, Performance Max, search ads, and often Meta, TikTok and YouTube campaigns, with the aim of selling products profitably. The best ones start from your product feed, margins and stock, then build campaigns and tracking around them.

This guide explains what an ecommerce PPC agency does for UK retailers, how it differs from general pay-per-click management, what it costs and the questions to ask before you hire one.

What an ecommerce PPC agency UK retailers hire actually does

Paid search for a store is different from paid search for a service business. Instead of a few landing pages and a lead form, you have hundreds or thousands of products, changing prices, stock levels and seasonal peaks. Most of the work falls into five areas.

  • Product feed management. Titles, descriptions, categories, identifiers, images and attributes in Google Merchant Center. The feed largely decides which searches your Shopping ads appear for.
  • Campaign structure. Splitting products by margin, price, best sellers or category so budget goes where it earns the most.
  • Bidding and budgets. Setting targets that reflect your margins and adjusting for seasonality, promotions and stock.
  • Tracking and measurement. Accurate conversion tracking, consent mode, GA4 and ideally profit or margin data.
  • Creative and landing pages. Ad copy, images and video for Performance Max and social, plus feedback on product and category pages.

Profit, not just ROAS

Return on ad spend (ROAS) is the most common ecommerce PPC target, but it can mislead. A 500% ROAS on a product with a 20% margin may lose money once delivery, payment fees and returns are included. A lower ROAS on a high-margin product can be very profitable.

A good ecommerce PPC agency will ask about your margins, average order value, returns rate and the value of repeat customers. With that information they can set targets that reflect profit, split products into sensible groups and stop spending on items that sell but never make money. If you can share cost of goods data, even approximately, the campaigns can be steered far more accurately.

It is also worth separating new customers from returning ones. Brand searches and retargeting often report strong ROAS because those shoppers would have bought anyway. An honest agency will show you how much of the revenue is genuinely new.

Feed quality: the hidden lever

For Shopping and Performance Max, the product feed does much of the job that keywords do in search ads. Small improvements can make a large difference to which searches you appear for.

  • Put the most important details at the start of product titles: brand, product type, key attribute such as size, colour or material.
  • Use accurate Google product categories and your own product types.
  • Include GTINs where products have them.
  • Use clean, high-quality images that meet Merchant Center rules.
  • Keep price, availability and delivery information in sync with the site.
  • Fix disapprovals and warnings quickly, especially before peak periods.

If an agency does not mention the feed in its first conversation with you, treat that as a warning sign. Our Google Shopping agency guide explains feed work in more detail.

Tracking and measurement for UK stores

Paid media decisions are only as good as the data behind them. Under UK GDPR and PECR, consent affects what you can track, so consent mode and a properly configured cookie banner matter. The agency should check:

  • Purchase conversions fire once, with the correct value and currency.
  • GA4 ecommerce events are set up and match platform data reasonably closely.
  • Enhanced conversions and server-side options are considered where appropriate.
  • Returns and cancellations are accounted for in reporting where possible.

Our GA4 setup guide covers the basics that every store should have in place before scaling spend.

How much does an ecommerce PPC agency cost?

There are two costs: the ad spend you pay to the platforms, and the management fee you pay the agency. Ad spend is paid separately and is set by you. Management fees vary by agency model: fixed monthly retainers, a percentage of spend, or a mix.

At Prism Works Studio our retainers are Launch at £350 per month, Growth at £750 per month and Scale at £1,500 per month, with ad spend paid separately. Full details are on our pricing page, and our guide to PPC management costs in the UK compares common fee models.

Paid results typically take 3-6 months to settle, as campaigns gather data and bidding stabilises. Expect early weeks to focus on tracking, feed fixes and structure rather than dramatic changes in sales.

Planning for UK seasonal peaks

Ecommerce PPC is shaped by the retail calendar. Black Friday and Cyber Monday, Christmas, January sales, Mother's Day, Father's Day, Easter, summer holidays and back to school all change demand, costs per click and competition. A good agency plans for these weeks ahead rather than reacting on the day.

  • Stock and promotions. Share promotion dates and stock plans early so campaigns, feeds and ad copy are ready.
  • Merchant promotions and sale prices. Set these up in Merchant Center in good time, as they can take a while to be approved.
  • Bidding adjustments. Automated bidding can be slow to react to short sales, so seasonality settings and budget changes need planning.
  • Delivery cut-offs. Update ads and feed delivery details as last order dates for Royal Mail, Evri, DPD and other carriers approach.
  • After the peak. Scale budgets back sensibly and review what worked for next year.

Product launches and new ranges need similar planning. New products have no sales history, so they often need their own campaign or budget for a period while they gather data.

How to choose an ecommerce PPC agency

Use these questions to compare agencies.

  1. Who owns the ad accounts and Merchant Center? You should. Agencies should be given access, not hold your accounts.
  2. How do you measure success? Look for answers that mention profit, margin and new customers, not only ROAS.
  3. What happens in the first 90 days? A clear plan for tracking, feed and structure is a good sign.
  4. Which platforms do you recommend for us, and why? Be wary of anyone who recommends every channel regardless of budget.
  5. What do reports include? Ask for an example and check it covers revenue, cost, profit and actions taken.
  6. What is the notice period? Read our guide to marketing agency contracts before signing.

How PPC fits with the rest of your store's marketing

Paid ads bring traffic, but your site still has to convert it. Slow product pages, unclear delivery costs or a clumsy checkout make every click more expensive. FAQs

What does an ecommerce PPC agency UK stores use actually do?

It manages paid advertising for online stores, including product feeds, Google Shopping, Performance Max, search and social campaigns, tracking and reporting, with the aim of selling products profitably.

Is Performance Max better than standard Shopping?

It depends. Performance Max can reach more people with less manual work, but gives less control and visibility. Many stores use both, with standard Shopping for products that need tighter control.

What budget do I need for ecommerce PPC?

There is no fixed minimum, but the budget needs to be enough for campaigns to gather data. Start focused on best sellers and brand search, then expand as results allow.

Is ad spend included in agency fees?

Usually not. At Prism Works Studio ad spend is paid separately to the platforms, and our management retainers are listed on our pricing page.

How long does ecommerce PPC take to work?

Some sales usually come quickly, but campaigns typically take 3-6 months to settle as bidding learns and feed and structure improvements take effect.

Should I run Meta ads as well as Google?

Often yes, once Google Shopping and brand search are working. Meta is good for discovery and retargeting, especially for visual products, but needs regular fresh creative.
