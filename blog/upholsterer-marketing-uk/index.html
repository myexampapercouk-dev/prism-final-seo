Upholsterer Marketing in the UK: How to Win More Reupholstery and Commercial Work

Upholsterer marketing in the UK works best with before and after galleries, local SEO, clear quote steps and pages for domestic, commercial and fabric work.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Before and after photos are the most persuasive marketing an upholsterer has, so photograph every job consistently and build a gallery by furniture type.
  • Buyers struggle to judge price and process, so explain how quotes work, what affects cost and how fabric selection and collection happen.
  • Commercial work for pubs, restaurants, hotels and offices can bring larger repeat jobs, so give it its own pages and outreach.

Upholsterer marketing in the UK is about showing people what their tired furniture could become and making the quote process feel simple. The upholstery businesses that grow steadily usually combine a strong before and after gallery, good local search visibility, clear explanations of how pricing and fabric choice work, and a separate effort to win commercial contracts.

This guide is for traditional and modern upholsterers, furniture restorers, soft furnishing workshops and firms that reupholster seating for hospitality and offices. We cover how customers search, what your website needs, how to present your work and where paid campaigns and social media fit.

How customers find and choose an upholsterer

Most domestic customers come to an upholsterer with a specific piece in mind. It might be a favourite armchair that has worn through, an inherited sofa with sentimental value, dining chairs that need new seat pads or a vintage find they want restored. They usually search for upholsterers near them, or for the specific job, such as reupholster an armchair or recover dining chairs.

Many have never used an upholsterer before. They do not know whether reupholstering is worth it compared with buying new, how long it takes, how fabric is chosen or how their furniture will be collected. A business that answers these questions clearly removes much of the hesitation.

Commercial customers are different. Pub and restaurant owners, hotel managers, office fit-out firms and theatres need seating refreshed or recovered, often in batches and to a deadline. They value reliability, durable fabrics suited to heavy use and a clear schedule that limits disruption.

Before and after galleries that sell

Nothing persuades an upholstery customer like seeing a transformation. A well-organised gallery of before and after photos, sorted by furniture type and style, can be the single most effective part of your website.

Photograph every piece the same way: in good natural light, against a plain background, from the same angle before and after. Add a short caption describing the piece, the work done and the fabric used, without naming the customer. Compress images so your gallery loads quickly on phones; our guide to website image optimisation explains how.

These same photos work well on social media, particularly visual platforms where interior lovers browse for ideas. Short videos showing a piece stripped back to the frame and rebuilt can also attract interest and demonstrate your craftsmanship.

Explain pricing and process clearly

One of the biggest barriers for customers is not knowing what reupholstery involves or roughly what drives the cost. You do not need to publish fixed prices, but you should explain how quotes work and what affects them. Common factors include:

  • The size and type of the piece
  • The condition of the frame, springs and filling
  • Whether the fabric is supplied by you or the customer
  • Pattern matching and fabric quantities
  • Details such as buttoning, piping, skirts or loose cushions
  • Collection and delivery arrangements

Set out your process step by step: send photos for an initial estimate, choose fabric, arrange collection, carry out the work and return the finished piece. A simple quote form that lets customers upload photos and dimensions saves time on both sides. Our guide to website contact forms covers how to keep forms short while collecting what you need.

A clear FAQ section on your site can answer common worries, such as how long the work takes and whether it is worth reupholstering a particular piece. Our guide to writing a website FAQ page explains how to structure one.

Local SEO for upholsterers

Most domestic work comes from within a reasonable collection distance, so local search visibility is essential. A complete business profile with your workshop location, service area, real photos of finished pieces and regular updates helps you appear in map results. Our guide to Google Business Profile in the UK covers the setup.

Reviews matter, especially for pieces with sentimental value, where customers want reassurance that their furniture will be treated carefully. Ask every customer for a review and encourage them to mention the type of piece and how the finished result looks. Our UK local SEO guide explains how reviews, citations and location pages work together.

Winning commercial upholstery work

Commercial contracts can bring larger jobs and repeat work. Pubs, restaurants, cafes and hotels refresh seating regularly, and fit-out firms and interior designers often need a reliable upholsterer to subcontract to.

  • Create dedicated pages for hospitality, office and trade work, showing commercial projects where you have permission.
  • Explain how you schedule work to minimise disruption, such as out-of-hours collection or phased recovering.
  • Mention suitable durable fabrics and that commercial furniture may need to meet fire safety requirements. Keep this general and tell buyers to check current official guidance.
  • Reach out directly to interior designers, fit-out firms and local hospitality groups with a short portfolio.

Paid campaigns and social media

Google Ads can bring in enquiries for high-intent searches such as sofa reupholstery or recovering dining chairs in your area. Keep targeting tight to your collection area and exclude searches for DIY upholstery supplies, courses you do not offer and jobs. Social ads using before and after images can also work well for reaching homeowners interested in interiors and sustainability.

Start small, track enquiries carefully and scale what brings genuine quote requests. Any ad spend is paid separately to management fees.

Content that answers the worth it question

Many people searching for an upholsterer are really asking whether their piece is worth saving. Helpful articles can capture this early research and bring visitors to your website before they have decided. Useful topics include how to tell whether a sofa frame is good quality, the difference between traditional and modern upholstery methods, how to choose a hard-wearing fabric for a family home or a home with pets, and what happens to a piece when it is stripped back.

Write these in plain English, illustrate them with your own photos and link each article to the relevant service page. Avoid sweeping claims about cost compared with buying new, as this depends entirely on the piece. Honest guidance builds trust, and a reader who learns something useful from you is far more likely to send photos for a quote.

Where to start

  1. Photograph every job consistently and build a gallery organised by furniture type.
  2. Create pages for your main services and a clear explanation of how quotes and collection work.
  3. Optimise your business profile and collect reviews after every job.
  4. Add a quote form that accepts photos and dimensions.
  5. Build pages and outreach for commercial and trade clients.

FAQs

What is the best approach to upholsterer marketing UK?

Build a strong before and after gallery, create pages for each service and furniture type, explain your quote process clearly and strengthen local search with a complete profile and genuine reviews.

Should upholsterers publish prices on their website?

Fixed prices are hard because every piece differs, but explaining what affects cost and how quotes work helps customers feel confident enough to get in touch.

How can an upholsterer get more commercial work?

Create dedicated hospitality and trade pages, show commercial projects where you have permission and contact interior designers, fit-out firms and hospitality groups directly with a short portfolio.

Is social media useful for upholsterers?

Yes, before and after photos and short process videos suit visual platforms well. They work best alongside a website and local search, which capture people actively looking for an upholsterer.

Should we let customers upload photos for a quote?

Yes. A form that accepts photos and rough dimensions lets you give an initial estimate quickly and reduces unnecessary visits.

Do we need to mention fire safety rules on our website?

A short general note is useful, particularly for commercial work. Avoid giving legal advice and tell customers to check current official guidance for their situation.
