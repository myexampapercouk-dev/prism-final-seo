Shopify Agency for Belgian Brands: Remote Builds, Migrations and Support

Looking for a Shopify agency in Belgium? Multilingual stores, Shopify Markets, payments and migrations, plus what a remote UK studio covers and our fees.

A Shopify agency for a Belgian brand should build a store that works for customers in Flanders, Wallonia and Brussels, and often for buyers in neighbouring countries too. That usually means more than one language, local payment options, clear delivery and returns information and a setup your team can run day to day without calling a developer for every change.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs, builds and supports Shopify stores remotely for brands across Europe. We have no office in Belgium. Our website and ecommerce development service runs by video call and shared project boards, one hour behind Brussels time. We write in English and work with your team or a translator on Dutch, French and German content.

What makes a Shopify store for Belgium different

Belgium is a small country with a complicated online market. Several factors shape how a Shopify store should be planned:

  • Several languages. Dutch is spoken in Flanders, French in Wallonia, both in Brussels, and German in a small eastern region. Many stores also need English.
  • Cross-border shoppers. Belgian customers often buy from Dutch, French and German shops, so your store competes with retailers across the region.
  • Local payment habits. Shoppers expect familiar payment methods alongside cards, so payment setup matters at checkout.
  • Selling abroad. Many Belgian brands want to reach the Netherlands, France and Germany, which brings currency, tax and shipping decisions.

Our guide to Shopify Markets for Europe explains how to set up languages, currencies and domains when selling across several European countries.

Dutch, French and other language versions

Shopify supports multiple languages within one store, with translated product content, collections, pages and theme text. Each language gets its own URLs, which search engines can index separately. The hard part is not the technology but keeping every version accurate as products, prices and policies change.

Prism writes English content in-house. For Dutch, French or German, we prepare the structure and content templates and work with your team or a translator, then check the finished pages for layout and technical issues. French text in particular often runs longer than English, so buttons, menus and banners need to be designed with room to spare. Our multilingual website design guide covers language switchers and how to keep translations manageable.

Payments, delivery and checkout

Checkout is where many sales are won or lost. Belgian shoppers want to see familiar payment options, clear delivery costs and realistic delivery times before they commit. We set up payment providers that suit your business, configure shipping zones for Belgium and any other countries you sell to, and make sure delivery and returns information is easy to find.

Shopify's checkout is standardised, which keeps it secure and reliable, but the settings around it still need care. Our guide to Shopify checkout settings explains the options, and our article on Shopify shipping profiles covers setting different rates by product and destination.

Tax for cross-border selling inside the EU follows specific VAT rules, and the right setup depends on your circumstances. We configure the settings you specify, but you should check current official guidance and confirm your tax position with your accountant, who has final approval.

Moving an existing Belgian store to Shopify

Many Belgian brands start on WooCommerce, PrestaShop, Magento or a local platform and later move to Shopify for simpler management. A migration needs careful planning so products, customers, orders and search rankings carry across:

  1. Audit the current store, including products, variants, languages and key pages.
  2. Map every old URL to a new one in each language.
  3. Import products and customers, then check the data carefully.
  4. Set up redirects and test before switching the domain.
  5. Monitor search performance and fix issues after launch.

Our WooCommerce to Shopify migration guide explains the process in more detail. For larger brands with higher order volumes, we also handle moves to Shopify Plus, as set out in our Shopify Plus migration guide.

Accessibility and legal pages

The European Accessibility Act brings accessibility requirements for many online shops selling to consumers in the EU. We build stores with accessibility in mind, including readable text, keyboard navigation, alt text and clear forms. Our European Accessibility Act guide explains the basics in general terms. Requirements and exemptions vary, so check current official guidance and take legal advice on your obligations.

Privacy policies, terms of sale and cookie consent also need attention. We can design and place these pages, but the wording should come from your legal adviser.

Support after launch

A Shopify store is never really finished. After launch, most Belgian brands need help with some or all of the following:

  • New products and collections added in every language, with consistent naming and images.
  • Seasonal campaigns such as sales periods, gift guides and homepage changes.
  • Theme and app updates, tested so they do not break layouts or translations.
  • Speed and SEO checks, including metadata, redirects and image sizes.
  • Reporting on sales, traffic and conversion by country and language.

We offer ongoing support through monthly retainers, so your team has a clear point of contact for changes and fixes. We also train your staff to handle routine tasks such as adding products, editing pages and managing orders, so you are not dependent on us for everyday work. You keep full ownership of the store, the domain and all content throughout.
