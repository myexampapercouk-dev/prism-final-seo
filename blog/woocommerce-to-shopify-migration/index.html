WooCommerce to Shopify Migration: How to Move Your Store Safely

WooCommerce to Shopify migration guide for UK retailers: planning, moving products and customers, redirects, SEO protection, testing and launch.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • A WooCommerce to Shopify migration is mainly a data, design and SEO project, not just a platform switch.
  • Mapping every old URL to a new one with permanent redirects is the most important step for protecting rankings.
  • Test orders, payments, tax, shipping and emails thoroughly before you point your domain at Shopify.

A WooCommerce to Shopify migration moves your products, customers, orders, content and design from a WordPress-based store onto Shopify's hosted platform. Done carefully, it can give you a simpler store to run without losing the search visibility and customer data you have built up. Done in a rush, it can break links, lose rankings and frustrate returning customers.

This guide is for UK store owners and ecommerce managers weighing up or planning a move. It covers why retailers switch, what moves across, how to protect SEO, and what to test before launch. If you are still deciding between the platforms, start with our Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison.

Why UK retailers move from WooCommerce to Shopify

WooCommerce is flexible, but that flexibility comes with responsibility. You manage hosting, security, WordPress updates, plugin compatibility and performance yourself, or pay someone to. For some teams that is fine. For others it becomes a steady drain on time.

Common reasons for switching include:

  • Less maintenance. Shopify handles hosting, security patches and core updates.
  • Fewer plugin conflicts. Stores with many WordPress plugins can become fragile after updates.
  • A simpler admin. Many teams find day-to-day order and product management easier.
  • Growth plans. Retailers planning to sell across more channels often like Shopify's built-in sales channel options.

Shopify is not automatically better for every business. Highly bespoke product logic, unusual checkout requirements or heavy content publishing can sometimes suit WooCommerce. Weigh the trade-offs honestly before committing.

Plan before you move anything

Good migrations start with an inventory of what you have. List your products and variants, categories, customer accounts, order history, blog posts, static pages, plugins and any custom functionality. For each plugin, note what it does and whether you still need that feature on Shopify, whether through a built-in setting, a theme feature or an app.

Agree what success looks like. That usually means no loss of essential data, all important URLs redirected, payments and shipping working correctly and a site that is at least as fast and easy to use as the old one. Set a realistic launch window that avoids your busiest trading periods.

What moves across, and what needs rebuilding

Most structured data can be migrated using import tools, CSV files or migration apps:

  • Products, variants, prices, stock levels, SKUs and images.
  • Product categories, which usually become Shopify collections.
  • Customer records, subject to your privacy obligations.
  • Historical orders, if you need them for reporting or customer service.
  • Blog posts and pages.

Some things cannot simply be copied. Your theme design needs rebuilding or replacing, as WordPress themes do not run on Shopify. Customer passwords cannot be transferred, so customers will need to reactivate their accounts. Custom plugin features need a Shopify equivalent or a custom build. Check variant structures early, because Shopify handles product options differently from WooCommerce and complex products may need restructuring.

Protect your SEO during the migration

URL structures change when you move to Shopify. Products typically sit under /products/, collections under /collections/ and blog posts under /blogs/. Without redirects, every old link from Google, other websites and customer bookmarks would lead to an error page.

  1. Crawl your current site and export every indexable URL.
  2. Map each old URL to the most relevant new URL, one to one wherever possible.
  3. Set up 301 redirects in Shopify's URL redirect settings or through a bulk import.
  4. Carry over metadata, including page titles, meta descriptions, image alt text and headings.
  5. Keep your content. Do not drop category descriptions or blog posts that bring in traffic.
  6. Submit your new sitemap in Google Search Console after launch and monitor coverage.

Some ranking movement after any migration is normal, but careful redirects reduce the risk of lasting losses. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings and our Shopify SEO guide go deeper.

Payments, tax, shipping and legal settings

Recreate your UK VAT settings, shipping zones and rates, and payment methods on Shopify, and check they match how you currently trade. If you sell internationally, review duties and tax settings carefully and take professional advice if unsure. Update your privacy policy, terms, returns policy and cookie consent to reflect the new platform and any new apps you install.

Test thoroughly before launch

Use a checklist and test as a real customer would:

  • Place test orders with each payment method and shipping option.
  • Check order confirmation and shipping emails.
  • Test discount codes, gift cards and any subscriptions.
  • Browse on mobile and desktop and check search and filters.
  • Spot-check redirects from your URL map.
  • Confirm analytics and conversion tracking fire correctly.

Launch day and the weeks after

Launch at a quiet time, connect your domain and keep the old store accessible privately for reference. Afterwards, monitor Search Console for errors, watch organic traffic and conversion rate, and fix broken redirects quickly. Let customers know about account reactivation with a clear email.

Handling content and blog posts

Content is often an afterthought in ecommerce migrations, yet blog posts, buying guides and category descriptions can bring in a meaningful share of organic traffic. Shopify has a built-in blog, but its structure is simpler than WordPress. Categories and tags work differently, and some layouts or shortcodes used on WordPress will not carry across. Review each post, decide whether to keep, merge or retire it, and redirect retired posts to the most relevant remaining page rather than the homepage.

Check internal links inside your content too. Links pointing at old WooCommerce URLs will still work through redirects, but updating them to the new URLs is cleaner and avoids unnecessary redirect hops.

Reviews, subscriptions and integrations

Product reviews stored in WooCommerce, or in a WordPress plugin, usually need exporting and importing into a Shopify reviews app. Check that review content, ratings and dates come across and that reviews stay attached to the right products. Subscriptions are more complex, because active payment agreements may not transfer automatically between providers. Plan subscriber communication carefully and check what your payment and subscription providers allow.

List every external system connected to your store, such as accounting software, stock management, email marketing, couriers and marketplaces. Confirm each has a Shopify integration and plan when to switch it over, so orders and stock do not fall out of sync during launch week.

Communicating with customers

Most customers will barely notice a well-run migration, but regular buyers will notice that they need to reactivate their accounts. A short, friendly email explaining the new store, what has changed and how to set a new password reduces confusion and support queries. Brief your customer service team before launch so they can answer questions consistently.

Run old and new side by side

Until launch, keep trading on WooCommerce as normal. Plan a content freeze for products and prices shortly before go-live, then run a final data sync so recent orders, customers and stock levels are up to date on Shopify. Agree in advance who signs off each part of the checklist, so launch day decisions are quick and clear.

FAQs

Will I lose my Google rankings moving from WooCommerce to Shopify?

Some short-term movement is common, but mapping every important URL with 301 redirects and keeping your content and metadata greatly reduces the risk of lasting losses.

Can I move customer passwords to Shopify?

No. Passwords are stored securely and cannot be transferred, so customers need to reactivate their accounts. A clear email after launch helps.

Can I keep my domain name?

Yes. You can connect your existing domain to Shopify at launch.

Do I need to move my order history?

Not always. Many retailers migrate it for customer service and reporting, but it is a decision based on how you use that data.

How long does a migration take?

It depends on the size of your catalogue, the amount of custom functionality and the design work involved. Simple stores move faster than complex ones.
