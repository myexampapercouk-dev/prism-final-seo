How to Improve Quality Score in Google Ads: A Practical UK Guide

Improve Google Ads Quality Score by tightening keyword themes, writing relevant ads and fixing landing pages. A plain-English guide for UK small businesses.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Quality Score is a keyword-level diagnostic built from expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience.
  • Tight keyword themes, ads that echo the search and fast, relevant landing pages are the main levers you control.
  • Treat it as a guide to where campaigns need work, not a number to chase for its own sake.

To improve Quality Score in Google Ads, group keywords into tight themes, write ads that closely match those searches, and send clicks to fast landing pages that deliver what the ad promised. Those three changes map directly to the three parts Google uses to calculate the score: expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience.

For a UK small business on a modest budget, this matters because better relevance tends to mean your ads can compete more efficiently in the auction. This guide explains what Quality Score is, where to find it, how to read each component and what to fix first. Google changes the Ads interface and its explanations from time to time, so check the current Google Ads help pages for exact column names and definitions.

What Quality Score actually is

Quality Score is a diagnostic rating, shown on a scale of one to ten, that Google gives to individual keywords in Search campaigns. It is an estimate of how relevant your ads, keywords and landing pages are compared with other advertisers showing for the same searches. A higher score suggests your set-up is more relevant than average; a lower score suggests something is out of step.

It is worth being clear about what it is not. Google describes Quality Score as a diagnostic tool rather than a direct input to the auction. The auction itself uses real-time quality signals at the moment of each search. In practice, though, the same things that lift Quality Score also tend to help ad rank and cost, which is why it is a useful health check. If you are new to paid search, our Google Ads guide for small businesses covers the wider basics first.

The three components of Quality Score

Each keyword with enough data shows a rating for three components. Each is labelled as above average, average or below average.

ComponentWhat it measuresMain things that affect it
Expected click-through rateHow likely your ad is to be clicked when shown for that keywordHeadline relevance, offer, use of assets, match types drawing in loosely related searches
Ad relevanceHow closely your ad matches the intent behind the searchWhether the ad group is tightly themed and whether ad copy reflects the keyword
Landing page experienceHow relevant and useful your landing page is to people who clickPage content matching the ad, speed, mobile usability, clear next step

Looking at the components is more useful than staring at the overall number. A keyword with a score of four might have a perfectly good ad but a weak landing page, and the fix for that is completely different from fixing a weak ad.

How to find Quality Score in your account

Quality Score is not shown by default in most keyword views. To see it, go to the keywords section of a Search campaign, open the column options and add the Quality Score columns. These usually include the overall score, the three component ratings and historical versions of each.

A few practical points when you first look:

  • Some keywords show no score at all. That normally means there is not yet enough search volume or data for Google to rate them.
  • Scores relate to exact searches of the keyword, so a broad match keyword that matches lots of varied searches can behave differently from its score.
  • Sort by cost, not by score. A low score on a keyword that spends a few pounds a month matters far less than an average score on your biggest spender.

How to improve Quality Score in Google Ads

Work through the levers below in order. Most small accounts find the biggest gains in the first two.

1. Tighten your ad groups. If one ad group contains keywords for boiler repair, boiler installation and central heating servicing, a single set of ads cannot be highly relevant to all of them. Split them into separate ad groups so each has a narrow theme. You do not need one keyword per ad group, but every keyword in a group should be answered well by the same ad and the same landing page.

2. Write ads that echo the search. Ad relevance improves when headlines reflect the language of the keywords in that group. If the group is about emergency plumbers in Leeds, the ad should say that clearly rather than talk generally about plumbing services. Our guide to responsive search ads explains how to write a varied set of headlines that still stay on theme.

3. Add relevant assets. Sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets and call assets make an ad larger and give searchers more reasons to click, which can support expected click-through rate. Keep them specific and accurate. The Google Ads assets guide walks through each type.

4. Remove irrelevant traffic. When ads show for searches that do not match your offer, click-through rate falls. Check the search terms report and add negatives for jobs, free, DIY, training and any services you do not offer. Our negative keywords guide shows how to build a starter list.

5. Fix the landing page. Send each ad group to the page that best matches it, not the homepage. The page should repeat the promise in the ad, load quickly on mobile, and make the next step obvious, whether that is a call, a form or a basket. If you do not have a suitable page, our advice on landing page design and website speed is a good place to start.

6. Give changes time. Quality Score updates as new data comes in. After restructuring, give it a few weeks of normal traffic before judging the effect, and avoid making several big changes at once if you want to know which one helped.

Common causes of a low Quality Score

When we review small business accounts, the same issues come up repeatedly. Use this as a quick diagnostic.

SymptomLikely causeWhat to try
Ad relevance below average across a groupAd group covers too many different themesSplit the group and write ads for each theme
Expected CTR below averageGeneric headlines or ads showing for loosely related searchesSharpen headlines, add assets, add negatives
Landing page experience below averageTraffic sent to homepage or a slow, cluttered pageUse a dedicated page that matches the ad and loads quickly
Brand keywords scoring well, others poorBrand searches are naturally relevant; generic ones need more workFocus effort on the non-brand groups that spend the most
Score drops after a site changeLanding page content, speed or URL changedCheck the page still loads, still matches and has not redirected

Does Quality Score affect what you pay?

Indirectly, yes. Google says ad rank is influenced by your bid, the quality of your ads and landing pages, and the expected impact of assets, among other factors. Because relevance feeds into ad rank, a more relevant set-up can often achieve a similar position for less than a less relevant competitor would need to bid. The exact relationship is not published and varies by auction, so treat any precise claims you see online with caution.

What you can say with confidence is that the work that improves Quality Score also tends to improve conversion rate. A visitor who lands on a page that matches what they searched for is more likely to enquire or buy. That is often the bigger win for a small budget. What not to do

  • Do not pause good keywords just because of the score. If a keyword converts profitably with a score of five, it is doing its job. Improve it, but do not switch it off.
  • Do not stuff keywords into ads. Ads still need to read naturally and persuade a human. Repeating the keyword awkwardly can lower click-through rate.
  • Do not chase a ten everywhere. Some competitive or broad keywords rarely score highly. Focus on the keywords that carry most of your spend.
  • Do not forget conversion tracking. Quality Score tells you about relevance, not profit. You still need accurate conversion tracking to know what is working.

A simple monthly Quality Score routine

You do not need to check Quality Score daily. A short monthly review is enough for most small accounts:

  1. Add the Quality Score columns to your keyword view and sort by cost.
  2. Look at your top twenty spending keywords and note any with below average components.
  3. For each, decide which component is weakest and pick one fix: split the group, rewrite the ad or change the landing page.
  4. Check the search terms report and add new negatives.
  5. Make a note of what you changed and the date, so you can compare next month.

FAQs

How do I improve Quality Score in Google Ads?

Group keywords into tight themes, write ads that reflect those keywords, add relevant assets, block irrelevant searches with negatives, and send each ad group to a fast landing page that matches the ad. Then give the changes a few weeks of data before judging the effect.

What is a good Quality Score?

There is no universal target. Scores of seven and above generally suggest a relevant set-up, while scores of four or below usually point to something worth fixing. Focus on the components and on the keywords that spend the most rather than an account-wide average.

Why do some keywords show no Quality Score?

Keywords without enough recent search activity often show no score, because Google does not have enough data to rate them. This is common for low-volume local keywords and is not a problem in itself.

Does Quality Score apply to Performance Max or Shopping?

Quality Score is shown for keywords in Search campaigns. Other campaign types use their own quality and relevance signals, but they are not reported as a one-to-ten keyword score. Check current Google guidance for how each campaign type is assessed.

How long does it take for Quality Score to change?

It updates as new impressions and clicks come in, so busy keywords can change within days while quieter ones take longer. Allow a few weeks of normal traffic after a change before drawing conclusions.

Should I send ads to my homepage?

Usually not. A homepage tries to serve everyone, so it rarely matches a specific search as closely as a dedicated service or product page. Matching the landing page to the ad group is one of the most reliable ways to improve landing page experience.
