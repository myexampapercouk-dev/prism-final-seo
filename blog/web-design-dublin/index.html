What Dublin businesses need from a website

Dublin is a busy, competitive market. Professional services firms in the city centre, tech and software companies around the Docklands, hospitality businesses, trades across the suburbs and online shops selling to the whole island all compete for attention in the same search results. A website that looked fine five years ago can now feel slow, awkward on a phone or hard to find.

Most Dublin businesses that come to us want some mix of the following:

  • More enquiries: clear calls to action, short forms, click-to-call and booking links that work on mobile.
  • Better visibility in Google: a structure, page speed and content plan that gives search engines something to rank.
  • A site the team can update: a content management system that does not need a developer for every small change.
  • Trust signals: clear service pages, real contact details, policies and an accessible design.
  • Room to grow: the ability to add pages, locations, products or a second language later.

Our job is to turn those goals into a site plan before any design work begins, so every page has a purpose.

Our web design process for Dublin clients

Because we work remotely, we keep the process structured and written down. That way you always know what is happening, what we need from you and when the next milestone is due.

  1. Discovery call: we talk through your business, customers, competitors, goals and any existing site.
  2. Sitemap and content plan: we agree which pages the site needs and what each one must do.
  3. Wireframes: simple page layouts so we agree on structure before visual design.
  4. Visual design: designs for key pages on desktop and mobile, using your brand colours, fonts and imagery.
  5. Build: development on WordPress, Shopify or a lightweight static build, depending on what suits you.
  6. On-page SEO setup: titles, descriptions, headings, internal links, schema and a clean URL structure.
  7. Testing and launch: checks on speed, forms, mobile layouts and redirects from any old pages.
  8. Handover: training on how to edit your site, plus optional ongoing support.

A typical brochure website takes 3 to 5 weeks from sign-off to launch. Ecommerce builds usually take 5 to 12 weeks, depending on the number of products, integrations and how quickly content arrives.

Choosing a platform: WordPress, Shopify or a static site

The right platform depends on what your website needs to do. We do not push one option for everyone.

WordPress suits service businesses, professional firms and content-heavy sites that want to publish news, guides and case pages. It is flexible and widely supported. If you are leaning that way, our guide to working with a WordPress agency in Ireland covers the options in more detail.

Shopify suits businesses selling products online, whether you ship only within Ireland or across Europe. It handles payments, stock, shipping and taxes well, and it scales as you grow. Our page on choosing a Shopify agency in Ireland explains how a Shopify build works.

A lightweight static build can suit a small, fast brochure site that rarely changes. It is quick to load and simple to host, though content edits are less convenient than in a full content management system.

Designing for search from day one

A beautiful website that nobody finds will not grow your business. We build every site with search in mind, so you are not paying to fix structure later.

  • One clear page for each main service, rather than everything on one long page.
  • Headings and copy that use the words your Dublin customers actually type into Google.
  • Location wording where it is accurate, for example if you serve Dublin city, the wider county or all of Ireland.
  • Fast load times, compressed images and clean code.
  • Schema markup for your organisation, services and FAQs.
  • An XML sitemap and correct setup in Google Search Console.

If you want ongoing visibility after launch, our SEO and GEO service builds on that foundation with content, technical fixes and link building. Our article on finding an SEO agency in Dublin explains what that work involves for local businesses. SEO results typically take 3 to 6 months to build.

Accessibility, privacy and legal basics

Irish websites operate under EU rules, so a few areas deserve attention during the build. We keep this general, and you should check the official guidance or speak to your own adviser for anything specific to your business.

Accessibility: the European Accessibility Act applies to many businesses that sell certain products and services to consumers in the EU. Even where it does not apply directly, an accessible site is easier for everyone to use. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act for websites covers the basics. We design with readable contrast, keyboard navigation, clear labels and descriptive link text.

Privacy and cookies: GDPR applies across Ireland and the EU. We build sites so analytics and marketing tags can sit behind a consent tool, and we add space for your privacy and cookie policies. The wording of those policies is your responsibility, and many businesses ask a solicitor or adviser to review them.

Regulated sectors: if you work in finance, health or another regulated field, we can design and build the site, but approval of claims and wording rests with your own compliance team.

Language and content

Prism writes in English, which suits most Dublin businesses. If you want an Irish-language version of some pages, or translations for customers elsewhere in Europe, that copy comes from your own team or a translator you choose. We then build the structure so each language version is set up correctly for search engines.

If you do not have copy ready, we can write clear, search-focused English copy for your main pages as part of the project scope, based on a brief and a call with you. You stay in control of final approval.

Is a remote web design studio right for you?

Working with a remote studio suits many Dublin businesses, but it is worth being honest about the trade-offs.

It works well if you are comfortable with video calls, can share content and feedback in writing and want published, predictable prices.
