Why SEO in Brighton is so competitive

Brighton and Hove pack a lot of businesses into a small area. There are independent shops in the Lanes and North Laine, a busy food and drink scene, hotels and guest houses along the seafront, wellness and fitness studios, a large creative and digital sector, and many online brands run from small units and home offices. Add a big student population and steady visitor traffic, and you get dense local results where small details decide who appears.

Searches also spread along the coast and inland. People look for services in Hove, Kemptown, Preston Park, Portslade, Shoreham-by-Sea, Lewes and Worthing. Visitors search before they arrive, often from London. A sound plan has to account for both residents and visitors.

How long SEO takes in Brighton

SEO results typically build over 3 to 6 months. Fixing a broken profile or indexing problem can help within weeks. Highly competitive terms, such as restaurants or hotels in central Brighton, can take longer, while specialist services or suburban searches often move sooner. We set realistic expectations after the audit.

AI search and how we use AI

Visitors and locals increasingly ask AI assistants where to eat, stay or book a service. Clear pages with direct answers, accurate details and consistent information across the web are easier for these tools to understand. We use AI-powered tools to speed up research and early drafts, and a person edits and checks everything before it goes live.

Privacy and sector rules

Every website needs a clear privacy notice, consent-based cookies and secure booking and contact forms. In general terms, UK GDPR and PECR apply to most UK websites, and the Information Commissioner's Office publishes guidance. Alcohol, health and wellness claims may face extra advertising rules. We keep our notes general, and compliance approval rests with you and your own adviser.

Common SEO mistakes on Brighton websites

Brighton businesses often have strong brands and great photography, but the same SEO problems appear on many sites we review.

  • Style over text. Pages built around large images with very little text give search engines little to work with. A few clear paragraphs and specific headings help.
  • Menus and timetables as images or PDFs. These are hard for search engines to read and awkward on mobile. Plain web pages work better.
  • Booking widgets that slow everything down. Third-party booking and review tools can add heavy scripts. We look for lighter ways to load them.
  • Old event and offer pages. Expired pages pile up and clutter the site. We redirect or update them so value is not lost.
  • Inactive Google Business Profiles. Old photos, unanswered reviews and wrong seasonal hours cost calls and bookings.

In the first month we complete the audit, fix urgent technical issues and bring your Google Business Profile up to date. In the second month we improve your most valuable pages, such as core services, menus, rooms or key product categories, and tidy your citations. In the third month we begin link work, add new content where there are gaps and review early ranking and enquiry data. Each month you receive a short report showing what was done, what changed and what comes next.

We also review how your website handles enquiries once visitors arrive. Clear phone numbers, short forms, obvious booking buttons and fast confirmation pages all help turn search traffic into real customers. SEO that brings more visitors without improving these steps leaves value on the table, so we flag conversion issues alongside ranking work and suggest simple, practical changes your team can approve.
