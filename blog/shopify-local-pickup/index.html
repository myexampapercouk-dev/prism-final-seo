Shopify Local Pickup: How to Offer Click and Collect From Your Shop

A practical guide to setting up local pickup on Shopify, covering locations, pickup instructions, notifications, stock, checkout wording and in-store handover.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify's local pickup option lets customers order online and collect from a shop, studio or warehouse you choose.
  • Clear pickup instructions, accurate stock at each location and prompt ready-for-pickup emails are what make click and collect feel reliable.
  • A simple handover routine in store, including checking the order and marking it collected, avoids confusion on both sides.

Many independent businesses sell online and also have a physical base, whether that is a high street shop, a studio, a workshop or a unit on an industrial estate. For customers nearby, being able to order online and collect in person can be convenient: they avoid delivery charges, they do not have to wait in for a parcel and they can often pick up sooner.

Shopify includes a built-in local pickup option that lets you offer this at checkout. This guide explains how it works, how to set it up sensibly, and the day-to-day routine that makes click and collect run smoothly for your team and your customers.

What Shopify local pickup does

Local pickup adds a delivery method at checkout that lets customers choose to collect their order from one of your locations instead of having it shipped. When the order comes in, it is flagged as a pickup order in your admin. Once you have prepared it, you mark it as ready, and Shopify can send the customer a notification telling them it is waiting. When they arrive and collect, you mark it as picked up.

It is designed for orders that customers collect themselves from a place you control. It is different from local delivery, where you drop the order off at the customer's address, and from shipping through a carrier.

Is click and collect right for your store?

Before switching it on, think about whether it fits how you operate.

  • Do you have a suitable location? You need somewhere customers can reasonably visit, with someone available to hand orders over during stated hours.
  • Is your stock at that location? Pickup works best when the products are already where the customer will collect them, or when you have a dependable way to move them there.
  • Can you store prepared orders? Orders awaiting collection need a safe, organised place where they will not get mixed up with stock or other orders.
  • Is the location safe and accessible? Think about parking, access and whether you are comfortable with customers visiting a home-based business, if that applies.

If the answers are mostly yes, local pickup can be a straightforward addition alongside your normal shipping rates.

Setting up local pickup on Shopify

Add or check your locations

Pickup is tied to locations in Shopify. In your settings, make sure each place where customers might collect is set up as a location with the correct address. If you have several sites, you can choose which ones offer pickup.

Turn on local pickup for a location

Within the shipping and delivery settings, you can enable local pickup for each eligible location. Here you can usually set how long it typically takes for an order to be ready and add pickup instructions that appear in customer notifications.

Write useful pickup instructions

Instructions are worth getting right. Good ones answer the questions a customer will have when they arrive:

  • Where exactly to go, such as which entrance, counter or unit number.
  • When they can collect, including any days you are closed.
  • What to bring, such as the order confirmation or a form of identification if you require it.
  • How long you will hold the order before contacting them.
  • Who to contact if they have trouble finding you.

Keep them short and practical. A customer may be reading them on their phone outside your door.

Decide whether pickup is free

Many stores do not charge for collection, but you can decide what suits your business. Whatever you choose, make sure it is shown clearly at checkout so that customers can compare it with delivery options.

Keeping stock accurate across locations

Pickup only works if the item is actually available where the customer expects to collect it. Shopify tracks inventory per location, so it is important that those numbers are kept up to date.

  • Assign stock to the correct locations, rather than holding everything at one default location.
  • When moving stock between sites, record the transfer so that availability reflects reality.
  • If you sell in person too, using Shopify POS means in-store sales reduce the same stock counts as online orders. Our guide to Shopify POS for UK retail explains how online and in-store selling fit together.

For a fuller picture of stock settings, see our article on Shopify inventory management.

Presenting pickup on your store

Customers should not have to reach the final step of checkout to discover that collection is possible. Consider mentioning it in a few places:

  • Product pages. A short line such as "Free collection available from our shop" can reassure local buyers. Some themes can show pickup availability for the selected variant directly on the product page.
  • Delivery information page. Explain pickup alongside your delivery options, with the address and opening hours.
  • Announcement bar or footer. A brief mention can be useful if a good share of your customers are nearby.
  • Contact page. Include the collection address and directions where people are likely to look for them.

Keep wording consistent everywhere so customers are not confused by different hours or addresses in different places. Our guide to Shopify theme customisation covers adjusting product and cart templates if your theme needs changes.

Notifications that keep customers informed

Shopify provides notification templates for pickup orders, including a message when the order is ready for collection. It is worth reviewing these and editing them so they match your brand voice and include the information customers need.

  • The order confirmation should make clear that the customer chose pickup and that they should wait for a ready message before travelling.
  • The ready-for-pickup email should repeat the address, collection hours and anything they need to bring.
  • If you send text messages as well as emails, check those templates too.

The most important habit is marking orders as ready only when they truly are. A customer who travels in after a ready notification and finds the order is not prepared is unlikely to be impressed.

An in-store handover routine

A simple, consistent routine prevents mix-ups. You might adapt something like this:

  1. Prepare the order. Pick the items, check them against the order and pack them securely.
  2. Label clearly. Mark the package with the order number and customer name so it is easy to find.
  3. Store it safely. Keep prepared orders together in a dedicated area, ideally out of public reach.
  4. Mark as ready. Update the order in Shopify so the customer is notified.
  5. Check at collection. When the customer arrives, confirm the order number and name, and any identification you ask for.
  6. Mark as picked up. Update the order once it has been handed over, so your records are accurate.

Make sure anyone working at the counter knows this process, and that staff have the right access in Shopify. Our guide to Shopify staff accounts and permissions explains how to give team members what they need without full admin access.

Handling uncollected orders and returns

Sometimes orders are not collected. Decide in advance what you will do and say so in your pickup instructions and policies. For example, you might send a reminder after a while, then contact the customer to arrange a refund or alternative if the order remains uncollected.

Returns and cancellations for pickup orders are generally handled in the same way as other online orders. Orders placed online are typically distance contracts under UK consumer rules even when collected in person, so cancellation rights may still apply. This is a general point rather than legal advice, so check current guidance or take advice on how the rules apply to your business, and make sure your refund policy covers collection orders clearly.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Offering pickup at a location without stock. If inventory is not assigned correctly, customers may order items you cannot hand over.
  • Vague instructions. "Collect from our shop" is not enough if the entrance is round the back or the shop closes early on certain days.
  • Forgetting to mark orders ready or collected. Out-of-date order statuses confuse both customers and staff.
  • Inconsistent opening hours. Make sure the hours in pickup instructions match your website, your Google Business Profile and any signs on the door.
  • No plan for busy periods. At peak times, prepared orders can pile up. Plan storage and staffing so collections stay quick.

FAQs

Is local pickup built into Shopify?

Yes. Shopify includes local pickup as a delivery option that you can enable for your locations in the shipping and delivery settings, without needing a separate app for the basic setup.

Can I offer pickup from more than one location?

Yes, if you have several locations set up in Shopify you can choose which ones offer pickup. Make sure stock is assigned to each one accurately so that customers only see pickup where items are actually available.

Should I charge for click and collect?

That is a business decision. Many stores offer collection at no charge, as it saves on packaging and courier costs, but you can set a fee if that suits you. Whatever you decide, show it clearly at checkout.

Can customers collect from home if I run a home-based business?

Technically you can set your home as a location, but think carefully about privacy, safety and practicalities before doing so. Some home-based businesses prefer to offer local delivery or arrange collections only by appointment.

How is local pickup different from local delivery?

With local pickup, the customer comes to you to collect the order. With local delivery, you take the order to the customer's address within an area you define. Shopify supports both, and some stores offer each alongside standard shipping.
