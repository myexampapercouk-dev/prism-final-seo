Web Design in Jeddah: Websites for Retail, Hospitality and Services

Web design for Jeddah businesses: fast, mobile-first websites with Arabic and English layouts, built by a UK-based studio that works remotely, no Saudi office.

  • A strong Jeddah website is mobile-first, fast on mobile data, planned for Arabic and English from the start and built around calls, WhatsApp and short forms.
  • Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for Jeddah businesses and has no office in Jeddah or anywhere in Saudi Arabia.
  • Small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites £1,500 to £3,500; Saudi riyal pricing is quoted on request and Arabic copy comes from your team or a translator.

Web design for a Jeddah business should give customers a quick, clear website that works on their phone, in Arabic or English, and makes it easy to get in touch or buy. Prism Works Studio designs and builds websites for Jeddah companies, working remotely from the UK.

We are a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio. We have no office in Jeddah or anywhere in Saudi Arabia. We run projects through video calls, private preview links and written updates. We write and build in English, and Arabic content is written by your team or a professional translator, with our team building and testing the right-to-left layout.

Who we design websites for in Jeddah

Jeddah is a port city, a trading hub and the main gateway for pilgrims travelling to Makkah and Madinah. That shapes the kinds of businesses that need strong websites:

  • Hotels, serviced apartments and travel services that serve visitors and pilgrims, often with several languages to consider.
  • Restaurants, cafes and food brands along the Corniche and across the city.
  • Retail and fashion brands selling in store and online.
  • Trading, logistics and import businesses linked to the port.
  • Clinics and wellness providers, with medical wording approved by the client.
  • Professional services such as consultancies, engineering and real estate firms.

Web design Jeddah: what makes a site work

Most visitors arrive on a phone, from a search, a social post or a link shared in a chat. A good Jeddah website needs:

  • Mobile-first layouts with large tap targets and short sections.
  • Fast pages that load well over mobile data, not just on office broadband.
  • A bilingual structure so Arabic and English pages each have their own URLs and are easy to switch between.
  • Proper right-to-left design, with mirrored menus, icons and forms on Arabic pages.
  • Clear contact routes: tap-to-call, WhatsApp, a map and a short enquiry form.
  • Search-ready foundations: clean headings, titles, descriptions, sitemaps and language tags.

Websites for hotels and hospitality in Jeddah

Hospitality is a big part of Jeddah's economy. Hotel and serviced apartment websites need clear room information, photos, location details, transport notes and a simple route to book or enquire. If you use a booking engine or channel manager, we connect it to the site and make sure it works on mobile in both languages.

Restaurants need menus that load quickly and can be updated easily, opening hours, maps and links to the delivery apps they use. We avoid uploading menus as large PDFs where possible, because they load slowly and are hard to read on a phone. Our hospitality sector page sets out how we approach hotel and restaurant marketing more broadly, and our restaurant marketing guide for Dubai covers many points that apply in Jeddah too.

Online shops for Jeddah brands

Retail and fashion brands in Jeddah often sell across the Kingdom and the Gulf. We usually build online shops on Shopify, which handles payments, shipping rules and apps well. We set up product pages, collections, filters, shipping zones and bilingual storefronts, and we connect analytics and ad tracking before launch. Our Shopify guide for Saudi Arabia explains the platform choices in more depth.

Arabic, English and other languages

Jeddah's audience is varied. Local customers often prefer Arabic, while visitors, expatriates and international buyers may use English or other languages. We plan the site structure for the languages you need, set language and direction attributes correctly and add hreflang tags so search engines show the right version.

We write in English. Arabic and any other language content comes from your team or a translator you choose, so the wording sounds natural and is checked by someone fluent before launch. We then build the pages, test spacing and line breaks and check that dates, numbers and prices display properly.

Search visibility built in

Every site we build is ready for search from launch day. That means one page per main service or product category, descriptive titles and meta descriptions, clear headings, image alt text, structured data, an XML sitemap and Google Search Console setup.

Our remote process

  1. Call and brief. We discuss goals, audiences, languages and pages, then send a written scope.
  2. Page plan. We agree the sitemap and who supplies each piece of content, including Arabic.
  3. Design. We share designs for key pages, in both directions for bilingual sites.
  4. Build. We build on a private preview so you can check it on your own devices.
  5. Test. We test speed, forms, tracking, mobile layouts and right-to-left behaviour.
  6. Launch. We go live, submit the sitemap and hand over logins with a short guide.

Saudi Arabia is two or three hours ahead of the UK depending on the time of year, so meetings fit easily into both working days. We plan calls around the Saudi working week.

Speed, hosting and security

We recommend reliable hosting with good performance for visitors in the region, often with a content delivery network in front. We compress images, use modern formats and keep scripts to a minimum. Every site gets SSL, backups and spam protection on forms. Your forms collect personal data, so check your own obligations with official guidance or a legal adviser; we keep the technical setup tidy and simple.

Mistakes to avoid on a Jeddah website

  • Machine-translated Arabic that reads awkwardly and damages trust.
  • Menus and price lists as heavy PDFs that are slow on mobile.
  • Hidden contact details when most customers want to call or message.
  • Stock photos that do not reflect your real premises, team or products.
  • No analytics, so you cannot tell where enquiries come from.

Planning content before the build

Content is the most common cause of delays. Before design starts, we agree a page list and a simple content plan showing who writes each page, which pages need Arabic versions and which photos are needed. We supply English page structures and headings to guide your writers, and we suggest booking your translator early so Arabic copy arrives on time.

Good photography makes a big difference. Real images of your premises, products, rooms or dishes build more trust than stock pictures. If you do not have suitable photos yet, plan a local shoot before launch so the site shows your business as it really is.

After launch: keeping the site healthy

A website needs regular care. We recommend monthly checks on updates, backups, forms and speed, plus a review of Search Console for errors. If your team handles updates, our handover guide explains how to add pages, change menus and swap photos without breaking the layout in either language.
