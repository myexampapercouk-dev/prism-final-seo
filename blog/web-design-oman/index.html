Web Design for Oman Businesses: How to Plan, Budget and Choose a Studio

Looking for web design in Oman? How a remote UK studio builds fast, bilingual websites for Muscat and wider Oman businesses, with pricing and timelines.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good website for an Oman business is fast on mobile, clear in Arabic and English where customers need both, and built around the actions that bring in work.
  • Working with a remote studio suits Oman businesses when scope, content responsibilities, call times and ownership are agreed in writing before work starts.
  • Our small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and e-commerce sites £1,500 to £3,500, priced in pounds with other currencies quoted on request.

Web design for an Oman business means building a website that loads quickly on phones, explains your services in Arabic, English or both, and makes it easy for people to call, send a WhatsApp message, book or buy. Whether you choose a studio in Muscat or one working remotely, the essentials are the same: a written scope, a sensible platform and a site you fully own.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely. We have no office in Oman, so our projects run through video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can review on your own devices at any stage. This guide explains how our website and e-commerce development service works for businesses in Muscat, Sohar, Salalah, Nizwa and across the sultanate, what it costs and what to ask before you commit.

Web design in Oman: where to start

Many Oman businesses come to a website project with one of three starting points. Some have no site at all and rely on social media and word of mouth. Some have an older site that looks dated, loads slowly or is hard to update. Others have outgrown a template builder and need something more flexible. Each starting point leads to slightly different priorities.

Starting pointMain priorityTypical first step
No website yetA clear, credible presence that turns social followers into enquiriesA focused small business site with strong contact options
Dated or slow siteSpeed, mobile layouts and easier editingA redesign that keeps any pages already ranking in search
Outgrown a site builderFlexibility, better SEO control and integrationsA move to WordPress or Shopify with redirects planned

If you are unsure whether you need a custom build at all, our comparison of a website builder and a custom website sets out the trade-offs honestly.

What Oman customers look for on a website

People in Oman, like most Gulf audiences, browse mainly on phones and often decide within seconds whether a site is worth their time. The sites that work best tend to share a few traits.

  • A clear first screen. What you do, where you work and how to contact you, without scrolling.
  • Fast pages. Compressed images, minimal plugins and careful use of video. Our guide to website speed optimisation explains the main fixes.
  • Familiar contact routes. Click-to-call, WhatsApp buttons and short forms, placed where they are easy to tap.
  • Real details. Accurate opening hours, the areas you cover and genuine photos of your premises, products or work.
  • Booking or ordering online where it suits the business, such as clinics, salons, tours and restaurants.

For appointment-based businesses, our guide to website online booking systems compares the main options.

Arabic and English versions

Arabic is the official language of Oman and English is widely used in business, tourism and among expatriate communities. Many Omani businesses therefore need both. A bilingual site involves more than translation: Arabic reads right to left, so layouts, menus, icons and forms are mirrored, and copy usually needs adapting so it reads naturally to local customers.

We write in English. Arabic pages are written with your team or a professional translator, and we build templates that handle both scripts properly, with separate URLs for each language and hreflang tags so search engines show the right version. Our guide to bilingual Arabic and English websites covers fonts, layout and SEO in more depth. Some businesses start in English with a handful of key Arabic pages and expand later, which spreads the cost.

How a remote project with us works

  1. Discovery call. Your goals, customers, languages, competitors and must-have features.
  2. Proposal. A written scope, timeline and fixed price in pounds.
  3. Structure and content. An agreed sitemap, page outlines and a list of who supplies each piece of content.
  4. Design. Key pages designed, shared and revised from feedback gathered in one place.
  5. Build on staging. A private link you can check on your own phone and computer.
  6. Testing. Forms, speed, mobile layouts, both languages and analytics checked before launch.
  7. Launch and handover. All logins and accounts in your name, plus a short editing guide.

Oman is a few hours ahead of the UK and the working week differs, so we agree fixed call slots early and keep most feedback in writing. That gives you a clear record of every decision and avoids misunderstandings across time zones.

Choosing a platform

  • WordPress suits service firms, clinics, tour operators, schools and any business that wants flexible pages and a blog.
  • Shopify suits most online shops, including those shipping across the Gulf, with built-in checkout and multi-currency options.
  • WooCommerce suits shops already running on WordPress or needing particular customisation.

Whichever platform we use, the domain, hosting and site accounts are registered in your name so you are never locked in.

Websites for tourism and hospitality in Oman

Tourism plays a growing part in Oman, and hotels, tour operators, dive centres and restaurants often need to reach visitors who are still planning their trip from abroad. These sites usually need extra attention: clear itineraries or room types, prices shown consistently, booking or enquiry forms that work across time zones, and content in the languages your visitors speak, which may go beyond Arabic and English. Good photography matters more here than almost anywhere else, so we plan image needs early and help you brief a photographer if your current pictures are not strong enough. We also make sure booking engines and third-party widgets load quickly on mobile, because a slow booking step loses direct reservations.

Selling online and local rules

If you sell online in Oman, your checkout should offer payment options your customers trust, explain delivery times for Muscat and other governorates, and set out returns clearly. Rules on e-commerce, consumer protection, data handling and VAT apply in Oman and change over time, so please check current official guidance and confirm details with your accountant or adviser. We build the pages and checkout settings; compliance decisions remain with the business.

Planning for search from day one

A website that nobody finds does not bring in work. We build in the technical basics from the start: clean URLs, fast pages, correct language tags, structured data, a page for each main service and a Google Business Profile that matches your site details. For ongoing visibility, our notes on choosing an SEO agency in Oman explain how search work continues after launch.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Who will own the domain, hosting, design and content when the project ends?
  • How will the Arabic version be built, and who writes or checks the Arabic copy?
  • What is included in the fixed price, and what would be charged extra?
  • How many rounds of design changes are included?
  • How will speed and mobile layouts be tested before launch?
  • What support do you offer after launch, and what does it cost?
