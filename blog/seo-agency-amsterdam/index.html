SEO Agency for Amsterdam Businesses: Dutch and English Search

Looking for an SEO agency in Amsterdam? How SEO works across Dutch and English search, what a remote UK studio can do, what to ask and how pricing works.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Amsterdam companies often serve Dutch customers, English-speaking residents and international buyers, so SEO usually needs a clear plan for both Dutch and English pages.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in the Netherlands; we lead technical SEO, English content and reporting remotely, working with your team or a translator on Dutch pages.
  • SEO results typically build over three to six months, and retainers are priced in pounds, with euro and other currencies quoted on request.

An SEO agency for an Amsterdam business should help you rank for the searches your customers really make, in Dutch, in English or in both, and turn that visibility into leads, bookings or sales. Strong SEO combines technical health, a sensible language structure, keyword research, useful pages, earned mentions and reporting tied to commercial outcomes.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio working remotely with companies in the Netherlands and across Europe. We have no office in Amsterdam. Our SEO and generative engine optimisation service is delivered online, one hour behind Amsterdam time, with video calls, shared documents and clear monthly reporting. We are strongest on English-language SEO and on the technical foundations every language version depends on.

Why Amsterdam SEO is often bilingual

Amsterdam has a large international workforce, a strong start-up and technology scene, and many companies with headquarters or European hubs in the city. English is used widely in business, yet Dutch remains the language most Dutch customers search in, particularly for local services, healthcare, housing, retail and anything involving trust or money.

  • Dutch-first businesses. Shops, trades, clinics and local services selling mainly to Dutch customers need well-written Dutch pages reviewed by native speakers.
  • English-first businesses. Software, B2B, tourism and international services often lead with English to reach expats, visitors and buyers abroad.
  • Both. Many companies need both, with a structure that helps search engines show the right language version to the right searcher.

The common mistakes are English pages outranking the Dutch ones for Dutch searches, thin machine translations and two language versions competing for the same terms. Our international SEO guide explains how to structure language versions so each one has a clear job.

Keyword research in Dutch and English

A translated keyword list is a weak starting point. Dutch searchers often combine words into long compounds, use informal phrasing, or search with English terms that have become standard in sectors such as software, marketing and finance. The reliable approach is to research each language separately, with data set to the Netherlands for Dutch terms and to your target countries for English ones.

The process in our keyword research guide applies directly; we simply run it per language and market. Prism writes in English. For Dutch research checks and copy, we work alongside your in-house team or a native-speaking writer or translator, so Dutch pages sound natural and reflect how people actually search.

SEO for Amsterdam software and international companies

Amsterdam is a base for many software companies, marketplaces, payment businesses and international service firms. Their SEO is less about the local map and more about winning commercial searches across several countries.

For these companies, the pages that tend to matter most are feature and product pages, use-case and industry pages, comparison pages, integration pages and well-structured help content. We prioritise searches that show buying intent, such as comparisons and problem-led queries, before broad educational topics. Our guide to SEO for SaaS companies explains the approach, and our SaaS sector page shows how we support software businesses more broadly.

Local SEO for Amsterdam businesses

For clinics, restaurants, trades, studios and shops, local search often brings the most valuable enquiries. That means a complete business profile, consistent name, address and phone details across directories, genuine reviews and clear pages for each service and area you cover.

Amsterdam neighbourhoods and nearby towns can each deserve their own page if you genuinely serve them, but thin pages that swap one place name for another tend to do more harm than good. Our local SEO guide covers the principles, which apply in the Netherlands with Dutch directories and Dutch-language reviews in place of UK ones.

SEO and AI search in the Netherlands

More buyers now ask AI assistants for recommendations or read AI-generated summaries above search results. The pages most likely to be cited tend to answer questions directly, state facts clearly and make it obvious who the business is and what it offers.

Our work includes generative engine optimisation alongside traditional SEO: clear answers near the top of key pages, structured data, consistent business information and content that earns mentions from credible sources. Our guide to SEO for AI Overviews explains what this means in practice.

What an SEO agency costs for an Amsterdam business

Our SEO retainers are priced in pounds sterling. Launch is £350 per month, Growth is £750 per month and Scale is £1,500 per month. Euro and other currency quotes are available on request, and any third-party translation is agreed separately. The full breakdown is on our pricing page.

The right level depends on your number of languages and markets, the size of your site and the competition in your sector. A focused English-language site may suit Launch, while a bilingual site targeting several countries usually needs Growth or Scale. SEO results typically build over three to six months; technical fixes can help sooner, while content and authority take longer. Treat any guarantee of rankings with caution.

Working remotely with a UK SEO agency

SEO suits remote delivery well, because most of the work happens in analytics, search tools, documents and the website itself. We work one hour behind Amsterdam, so there is ample shared time for calls. A typical engagement runs like this:

  1. Discovery. We learn your markets, languages, goals and current performance.
  2. Audit and plan. We review technical health, content and competitors, then agree priorities for the first few months.
  3. Delivery. We handle technical fixes, English content and on-page changes, and coordinate Dutch content with your team or translator.
  4. Reporting. Monthly reports show what we did, what changed and what comes next.

We work within your existing analytics and consent setup. For GDPR and cookie rules in the Netherlands, check current official guidance with your own advisers. Before signing with any agency, our guide on how to choose a digital marketing agency lists further questions worth asking.

Questions to ask any SEO agency in Amsterdam

A short conversation with each shortlisted agency usually reveals more than a proposal document. These questions help you compare local and remote options fairly:

  • How will you structure the Dutch and English versions of our site, and who writes each one?
  • Which technical issues will you look at first, and why?
  • How will you report results for each language and each market?
  • Who owns the content, analytics accounts and data if we part ways?
  • How much time will you need from our team each month?
  • How do you approach AI search and AI-generated summaries alongside rankings?

Clear, specific answers are a good sign. Vague promises, guaranteed positions or reluctance to explain the work are reasons to keep looking.
