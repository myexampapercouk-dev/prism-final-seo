WooCommerce Wholesale Store: How to Sell to UK Trade Customers Alongside Retail

How to run a WooCommerce wholesale store in the UK: trade accounts, tiered pricing, VAT display, minimum orders, invoice payments and keeping retail separate.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A WooCommerce wholesale store lets approved trade customers log in to see their own prices, minimum quantities and payment terms, while retail shoppers see the normal shop.
  • WooCommerce has no full wholesale system built in, so most trade stores combine user roles, a well-supported extension, careful VAT settings and a clear approval process.
  • Prism is a UK-based studio that builds and improves WooCommerce stores; e-commerce sites cost £1,500 to £3,500 and typically take 5 to 12 weeks, with extensions paid separately.

A WooCommerce wholesale store lets you sell to approved trade customers at their own prices and terms, either on a separate site or alongside your retail shop, with each customer seeing the right prices once they log in. For UK businesses that supply shops, salons, trades or hospitality venues, it can replace emailed price lists and manual orders with a self-service ordering system.

This guide explains how wholesale works in WooCommerce, the decisions you need to make and the common problems to avoid.

Who needs a WooCommerce wholesale store

Wholesale ordering suits businesses that sell repeatedly to other businesses in predictable quantities. Typical examples include food and drink producers supplying delis and cafés, cosmetics brands supplying salons, gift and homeware brands supplying independent shops, building and trade suppliers, and distributors with large catalogues.

If most of your trade customers order the same products every few weeks, an online trade account usually saves time for both sides. If every order is a bespoke quotation, a request for quote process may suit you better than a full wholesale shop.

Retail and wholesale on one site or two

The first decision is whether trade customers use your existing retail store or a separate site. Both approaches work in WooCommerce.

ApproachAdvantagesDrawbacks
One site, hidden trade pricingOne catalogue, one stock record, one site to maintainMore complex rules; retail and trade experiences must both work well
Separate trade siteA simpler, trade-focused design and catalogueStock and product data must stay in sync between two sites
Trade-only siteIdeal if you do not sell to consumers at allNo retail sales channel from the same platform

For most small and medium businesses, a single site with trade accounts is the simplest option, because stock and product information stay in one place. A separate site makes more sense when the trade catalogue, pricing structure or ordering process is very different from retail.

WooCommerce wholesale store features you will need

WooCommerce does not include a complete wholesale system as standard. Most trade stores combine WordPress user roles with a reputable, actively maintained extension for wholesale pricing and ordering. Before choosing one, list the features you actually need.

  • Trade accounts and roles. A customer role for trade buyers, possibly with several tiers. Our guide to WordPress user roles explains how roles work.
  • Trade pricing. Fixed trade prices, percentage discounts or tiered prices by role or quantity.
  • Minimum orders. Minimum order values, quantities or case sizes.
  • Visibility rules. Hiding trade-only products or prices from retail visitors.
  • Payment terms. Invoice or account payment for approved customers, alongside card payments.
  • Quick ordering. An order form or list view so buyers can add many products quickly.
  • Registration and approval. An application form and a manual approval step.

Choose the fewest extensions that do the job, and check that each one is compatible with your theme, payment gateway and any existing plugins. Our WordPress plugin audit guide explains how to keep the plugin list manageable.

Trade account registration and approval

Most wholesale stores should not grant trade prices automatically. A typical process asks applicants for their business name, address, type of business, website or social profile and, where relevant, a VAT number. Your team then reviews the application and approves or declines it.

Keep the registration form short enough that genuine buyers complete it, and explain how long approval usually takes. Under UK GDPR, the form should explain how you use the data collected and link to your privacy notice. Once approved, send a clear welcome email explaining how to log in, how pricing works and any minimum order rules.

Pricing, VAT and display settings

Trade customers in the UK usually expect to see prices excluding VAT, while consumers expect prices including VAT. WooCommerce can display prices differently in different contexts, but getting this right with a wholesale extension needs careful testing so that trade buyers see ex-VAT prices and retail shoppers see VAT-inclusive prices throughout the shop, basket, checkout and emails.

Your VAT treatment depends on your products and circumstances, so confirm the rules with your accountant or HMRC guidance. Our guide to WooCommerce VAT and tax settings covers the main configuration options. Wholesale invoices also need the correct information for VAT-registered customers, so check what your invoicing extension produces before going live.

Minimum orders, case sizes and quantities

Wholesale orders often come with rules: a minimum first order value, products sold only in cases of six or twelve, or minimum quantities per line. These rules protect your margins and simplify picking. Make them visible on product pages and in the basket so buyers understand them before checkout, rather than discovering them through an error message.

Clear stock levels matter even more in wholesale, because trade buyers often plan their own stock around your availability. Our guide to WooCommerce stock management explains how to keep stock accurate across retail and trade orders.

Checkout and payment terms

Many trade customers expect to pay on invoice after delivery, while new accounts may need to pay by card until they have an ordering history. A wholesale checkout can offer different payment methods by customer role, so only approved accounts see invoice payment.

Trade checkouts should also capture purchase order numbers, delivery instructions and preferred delivery days where relevant. Removing retail elements such as gift messages and newsletter prompts keeps the process quick. Our WooCommerce checkout setup guide covers the main options.

Product data and bulk updates

Wholesale catalogues are often large, with trade prices that change when costs move. Updating hundreds of prices one at a time is slow and error-prone. CSV imports and exports let you update product data, including trade price fields from many extensions, in bulk. Our guide to WooCommerce product CSV import explains how to do this safely, including testing on a staging site first.

Good product data also helps trade buyers find items quickly. Product codes, barcodes, pack sizes and clear variant names matter more for trade customers than lifestyle photography.

Keeping trade pages out of search

If trade prices or trade-only products should stay private, make sure they are not visible to search engines or logged-out visitors. At the same time, you probably want a public wholesale page that explains your trade offer and invites applications, because businesses do search for suppliers. That page can rank for searches about wholesale supply in your category. Our WooCommerce SEO guide explains how to structure public pages for search, and our guide to wholesale distributor marketing covers how to attract new trade accounts.

Common wholesale mistakes

  1. Too many plugins doing overlapping jobs, which slows the site and causes conflicts.
  2. Inconsistent VAT display, with ex-VAT prices on product pages but VAT-inclusive totals in the basket.
  3. Trade prices leaking through caching, search results or product feeds.
  4. No testing as each role, so problems only appear when real customers order.
  5. Manual processes left in place, so staff still re-key orders into other systems.

Caching deserves particular attention. Pages cached for logged-out visitors must never show trade prices, and logged-in trade pages generally need to bypass page caching.

FAQs

How do I set up a WooCommerce wholesale store?

Decide between one site or two, create a trade customer role, add a reputable wholesale extension for pricing and ordering, configure VAT display and payment terms, then test thoroughly as each type of customer.

Does WooCommerce support wholesale pricing by default?

Not fully. Core WooCommerce handles coupons and sale prices, but role-based trade pricing, minimum orders and quick order forms usually need an extension.

Can trade customers see prices without VAT?

Yes, with the right configuration. Test the shop, basket, checkout and emails as both a trade and a retail customer, and confirm VAT treatment with your accountant.

Can trade customers pay on invoice?

Yes. You can offer invoice or account payment only to approved trade roles, while new or retail customers pay by card at checkout.

Should retail and wholesale share one WooCommerce site?

Often, because stock and products stay in one place. A separate site suits businesses whose trade catalogue or ordering process differs greatly from retail.

How long does it take to add wholesale to WooCommerce?

It depends on your catalogue and rules. Adding trade accounts to a well-built store can be fairly quick, while a new e-commerce site typically takes 5 to 12 weeks.
