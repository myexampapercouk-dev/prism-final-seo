How to Build a Looker Studio Dashboard for a UK Small Business

A Looker Studio dashboard pulls GA4, Search Console and Google Ads data into one shareable report. Learn how to plan, build and keep one useful.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Decide the handful of questions the dashboard must answer before connecting any data.
  • Connect GA4, Search Console and Google Ads through Google connectors, then build one page per audience or channel.
  • Keep it small: a few clear charts, sensible date comparisons and notes beat dozens of tables nobody reads.

To build a Looker Studio dashboard, connect your data sources such as GA4, Search Console and Google Ads, choose a few charts that answer the questions you care about, and share the report with anyone who needs it. Looker Studio is Google's free reporting tool, and for most small businesses it is the simplest way to see website and marketing performance in one place.

The hard part is not the tool. It is deciding what to show. This guide explains how to plan a dashboard, how to build one step by step, which charts are most useful for a small business and how to keep it accurate over time. Google updates Looker Studio and its connectors regularly, so check the current Looker Studio help pages for exact menu names and features.

What Looker Studio is and why it helps

Looker Studio, previously called Google Data Studio, is a browser-based tool for building reports from different data sources. Instead of logging into GA4, Search Console and Google Ads separately, you see the figures that matter side by side. Reports update from the connected sources, so you are not copying numbers into spreadsheets each month.

It is particularly useful when several people need the same view: an owner, a manager and an agency, for example. Everyone looks at the same numbers with the same definitions, which avoids arguments about whose figures are right. If you are still getting to grips with the basics of measurement, start with our guide to website analytics for small businesses.

Plan before you build

Most dashboards fail because they try to show everything. Before opening Looker Studio, write down the questions the dashboard should answer. For a typical small business these might be:

  • How many enquiries, calls or sales did the website produce this month?
  • Which channels brought those visitors: search, ads, social, email or direct?
  • Which pages and search queries bring the most organic traffic?
  • What did paid campaigns cost and what did they return?
  • How does this period compare with the previous one or the same time last year?

Each question should map to one or two charts. If a chart does not answer one of your questions, leave it out.

How to build a Looker Studio dashboard step by step

The process below gives a simple one-page report. Menu names may differ slightly.

  1. Sign in to Looker Studio with the Google account that has access to your GA4 property, Search Console and Google Ads.
  2. Create a blank report and add your first data source, usually GA4.
  3. Add a date range control at the top so viewers can change the period.
  4. Add scorecards for your headline figures, such as users, key events and conversion rate, with a comparison to the previous period.
  5. Add a time series chart showing sessions or key events over time.
  6. Add a table of sessions and key events by session default channel group.
  7. Add a second data source for Search Console and build a table of top queries and landing pages.
  8. If you run ads, add Google Ads and build a campaign table with cost, conversions and cost per conversion.
  9. Add short text boxes explaining what each section shows and any known issues.
  10. Share the report with view-only access for most people.

Your GA4 set-up must be right for any of this to mean much. Our GA4 set-up guide covers properties, data streams and key events, and our Search Console set-up guide explains how to verify your site.

Make the numbers trustworthy

A dashboard is only as good as the tracking behind it. Before sharing one widely, check a few things:

  • Key events in GA4 match real business outcomes, such as form submissions, calls or purchases.
  • Google Ads conversions are set up properly and not double-counted. Our guide to Google Ads conversion tracking explains the options.
  • Campaign links from email and social carry consistent UTM tags, otherwise traffic lands in the wrong channel. See our UTM tracking links guide.
  • Your own visits and test submissions are filtered where possible.
  • Figures in the dashboard roughly match the figures in each source tool for the same period.

Small differences between tools are normal, because GA4, Search Console and Google Ads count things differently. Add a note explaining this so nobody is surprised.

Useful extras once the basics work

  • Filters: add a control for device or country so viewers can slice the data without editing the report.
  • Calculated fields: create metrics such as cost per enquiry by combining fields.
  • Blended data: join sources, for example ad spend from Google Ads with enquiries from a Google Sheet, though blends can be fiddly and need checking.
  • Scheduled emails: send a PDF of the report to stakeholders on a regular schedule.
  • Theme: apply your brand colours and keep charts consistent so the report is easy to read.

Keeping the dashboard useful over time

A dashboard is not a one-off job. Data sources change, tracking breaks and business priorities shift. A light monthly routine keeps the report trustworthy:

  • Open the report at the start of each month and check every chart loads without errors.
  • Compare the headline figures with the source tools for the same dates.
  • Add a short note about anything that changed: a new campaign, a website update, a sale or a tracking fix.
  • Remove charts nobody has looked at or asked about for several months.
  • Review who has access, especially when staff or suppliers change.

It also helps to agree what action each figure should trigger. If enquiries drop for two months running, who looks into it? If cost per enquiry rises, who reviews the campaigns? A dashboard earns its keep when it leads to decisions, not just when it looks tidy.

FAQs

How do I build a Looker Studio dashboard?

Create a blank report, connect a data source such as GA4, then add a date control, headline scorecards, a trend chart and tables for channels and pages. Add Search Console and Google Ads as further sources and share the report as view only.

Is Looker Studio free?

The standard version is free to use, and Google's own connectors for GA4, Search Console, Google Ads and Sheets are generally free. Some third-party connectors charge a fee, and Google offers a paid Pro version with extra features. Check current terms on Google's pages.

Can I connect Facebook or Meta ads to Looker Studio?

Not with a built-in Google connector, but partner connectors exist, often for a fee. An alternative is exporting data to Google Sheets and connecting that.

Why do Looker Studio figures differ from GA4?

Differences can come from date ranges, time zones, sampling, thresholds or how metrics are defined. Check settings match, and expect small gaps between different tools.

How many charts should a dashboard have?

As few as needed to answer your key questions. For many small businesses, one page of headline figures and one or two supporting pages is enough.

Who should have access to the dashboard?

Anyone who makes decisions from the numbers. Give most people view-only access and limit editing to one or two people who maintain the report.
