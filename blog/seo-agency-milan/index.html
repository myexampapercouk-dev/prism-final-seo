What makes SEO in Milan different

Milan is Italy's main business and financial centre and a global name in fashion and design. Its economy also includes professional services, technology, publishing, food, furniture, education, health and property. That mix creates a few distinct SEO challenges.

  • Italian first. Most searches are in Italian, and buyers expect natural, well-written Italian rather than translated English.
  • International audiences. Fashion, design, furniture and luxury brands often sell worldwide, so English and other language versions may matter as much as Italian.
  • Competitive sectors. Fashion, design and professional services face strong competition from established brands and large marketplaces.
  • Local and national balance. Some businesses serve Milan districts such as Brera, Porta Nuova, Navigli or Città Studi, while others sell across Italy and need national pages.

Italian and English pages done properly

Each language version should have its own URLs, translated titles, descriptions and navigation, and hreflang tags so search engines show the right page to each audience. Automatic translation without review often produces awkward Italian that undermines trust, especially for premium brands where tone matters.

Prism writes in English. Italian content is written by your team or a professional translator, and we provide the keyword plan, page structure and briefs. A fluent speaker on your side approves every Italian page. Our international SEO guide explains how to plan languages and countries, which is especially relevant for Milan brands selling abroad.

Technical SEO for Milan websites

Fashion and design websites often rely on large images, video and visual effects, which can slow pages down and hide content from search engines. We look for issues such as slow mobile loading, products or collections that cannot be crawled, duplicate pages created by filters, missing language tags and broken redirects after redesigns. Structured data helps search engines understand products, articles, organisations and FAQs; our schema markup guide explains the main types.

SEO for fashion, design and e-commerce

For online shops, SEO usually focuses on category pages, because those match the broader searches people make before choosing a product. Each category needs a clear title, a short helpful introduction, sensible filters and internal links to related collections. Product pages need unique descriptions, accurate details, good images with alt text and structured data.

If you sell on Shopify, our guide to Shopify in Italy covers store setup. Many Italian shoppers also browse marketplaces such as Amazon, so we can improve your marketplace listings alongside your own website where that makes sense.

Local SEO for Milan

Showrooms, boutiques, clinics, restaurants, studios and professional firms benefit from strong map visibility. Local SEO for Milan includes a complete and regularly updated Google Business Profile, consistent business details across directories, genuine reviews requested fairly, and location pages only where you truly serve an area. Our local SEO guide explains the fundamentals, which apply in Italy as well as the UK.

We include this AI-powered side of search, often called GEO, in our SEO work. Our guide to AI Overviews and SEO explains what changes and what stays the same.

Data protection, cookies and accessibility in Italy

Italian websites must follow the GDPR and Italian data protection law. Under the ePrivacy rules on cookies, non-essential cookies such as most analytics and advertising tags generally need consent before they are set, and the Italian data protection authority has published its own guidance on cookies. A clear banner that makes rejecting as easy as accepting, an accurate privacy policy and full company details are normal requirements for Italian business websites.

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many businesses selling products or services to consumers online, including e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but accessibility still helps every visitor. The usual reference is the harmonised standard EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act for websites covers the basics. These notes are general; check official Italian and European guidance and ask your own adviser what applies.

Content that Italian buyers trust

Italian buyers want clear, specific answers before they get in touch or place an order. Service pages that explain what is included, who it suits, how the process works and typical timelines usually perform better than short pages full of general claims. For product brands, materials, sizing, care, delivery and returns information answer questions that would otherwise stop a sale. Guides and FAQs based on the questions your sales and customer service teams hear every week tend to outperform generic articles written only around keywords.

We plan content around searches that lead to enquiries and sales, and we prefer a smaller number of genuinely useful pages over many thin ones that compete with each other.

SEO and paid search together

SEO takes time to build, so many Milan businesses run Google Ads alongside it, particularly for launches, seasonal collections or competitive terms. Search data from paid campaigns also shows which topics are worth targeting organically. Our guide to Google Ads in Milan covers the paid side.

A remote UK studio suits businesses that are comfortable working through calls and shared documents, want English-language strategy and reporting, and have Italian writers or a translator available. It can also suit Milan brands that sell into the UK and want one partner for both markets. In every case, your website, Search Console and analytics accounts should stay in your ownership.
