Health and Safety Consultancy Marketing in the UK: How to Win Steady Business Clients

Health and safety consultancy marketing in the UK works best with clear service pages, local and sector search, useful guidance content and steady follow-up.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clients usually look for a consultant when something specific happens, such as a tender, an inspection, a new site or an incident, so match your pages to those triggers.
  • Separate pages for each service and each sector you know well help you appear for the searches buyers actually type.
  • Keep every regulatory statement general and accurate, and point readers to current official guidance rather than promising outcomes.

Health and safety consultancy marketing in the UK works best when you explain your services in plain English, show that you understand the sectors you work in and make it easy for a busy manager to get in touch. Most clients find a consultant when they need help with something specific, so the consultancies that win steady work match their website, search and content to those moments.

This guide is for independent health and safety consultants, small consultancies and training firms that also offer advisory work. It covers how clients choose a consultant, positioning, your website, search, paid campaigns, content, referrals and measurement. It is marketing guidance only. Your clients' legal duties and the detail of any regulation should always be checked against current official guidance, such as that published by the Health and Safety Executive.

How businesses choose a health and safety consultant

Very few businesses wake up wanting a health and safety consultant. Something usually prompts the search. A main contractor asks for documents before a subcontractor can start on site. A tender form asks about accreditations and policies. A business opens a new warehouse, kitchen or workshop. An insurer asks questions at renewal. A visit from an inspector or an incident at work makes the owner realise they need support.

The person searching is often an owner, an office manager, an operations director or an HR lead who has been handed the problem. They want to know whether you can help with their situation, how quickly, what the work involves and roughly what it will cost. They are rarely experts, so jargon and long lists of acronyms can put them off.

Some clients need a one-off job, such as a risk assessment, a fire risk assessment or a policy review. Others want an ongoing retained arrangement, where you act as their competent advice and visit regularly. Your marketing should make both routes easy to understand.

Positioning: be clear about who you help

Many consultancies describe themselves in near identical terms, which makes it hard for buyers to choose. Specific positioning gives prospects a reason to pick you.

  • By sector. Construction, manufacturing, hospitality, education, logistics, retail and offices all have different risks and different buyers. Lead with the sectors you genuinely know.
  • By service. Retained advice, audits, risk assessments, policies, accident investigation support, site inspections and training are separate offers with separate searches.
  • By size. A small business with a handful of staff needs a very different tone from a multi-site organisation with its own safety team.
  • By area. If you mainly visit clients within a region, say so. Local relevance matters in search and in trust.

Positioning does not mean refusing other work. It means leading with your strengths, so the right buyers recognise you quickly. If your proposition feels vague, our brand strategy service can help you sharpen it and agree the language your whole team uses.

Your website: answer the buyer's questions

A consultancy website does not need to be elaborate. It needs to answer the questions a nervous, busy buyer has, and make the next step simple.

  • One page per service. Explain what the service covers, who it is for, how the visit or review runs, what the client receives and how long it usually takes. Our service page design guide covers a structure that works well.
  • Sector pages. A page for each sector you know well, describing the typical risks and how you support businesses like theirs.
  • Honest credentials. List the professional memberships, qualifications and accreditations your consultancy genuinely holds, and nothing more. Buyers and main contractors often check.
  • Clear pricing guidance. Even if every job is quoted, explain what affects the price and whether you offer fixed fees for common jobs.
  • Easy contact. A short enquiry form, a phone number and an indication of how quickly you respond.

Search: match the searches clients actually make

Search is often the most dependable source of enquiries for a consultancy, because buyers search when the need is real. The key is to cover the specific phrases they use, not just a general term.

Buyers tend to search for the job they need doing, for example a type of assessment, policy help or retained advice, often with a town or region added. They also search by sector, such as construction health and safety support. Build pages that match these themes, with useful content on each, rather than one long page listing everything.

Paid search and LinkedIn campaigns

Google Ads can bring enquiries quickly, especially for urgent needs. The common mistake is bidding on broad terms that attract people looking for training courses, jobs or free templates. Use focused keywords, add negative keywords for those audiences and send each campaign to a matching page rather than your home page. Track enquiries properly, so you know which searches lead to work.

LinkedIn can work well for reaching facilities managers, operations directors and HR leads in particular sectors. It suits awareness and nurturing more than instant enquiries. Content that builds trust without fear

Health and safety content can easily become alarming or overly technical. The most useful content is calm, practical and clear about what a business should consider. Ideas that tend to work well include:

  • Plain-English explainers on what a type of assessment involves and how to prepare for one.
  • Checklists for a new site, a new starter induction or an office move.
  • Sector guides explaining common risks in a type of workplace and where to find official guidance.
  • Answers to the questions clients ask you most often during visits.
  • Short updates when official guidance changes, written carefully and linking readers to the source.

Keep regulatory statements general, avoid promising that your service guarantees compliance and always tell readers to check current official guidance. Never invent statistics about accidents or fines to create urgency. Our content strategy guide explains how to plan topics around real buyer questions rather than chasing volume.

Referrals, partners and repeat work

Many consultancies win a large share of work through introductions. Accountants, HR consultancies, insurance brokers, facilities firms and main contractors all meet businesses that need safety support. Build these relationships deliberately, explain clearly what you do and make it easy for partners to refer someone.

Repeat work matters too. A client who needed a single assessment may later need a review, training or retained support. Keep in touch through a useful newsletter and a simple reminder when an annual review is likely to be due, with consent where it is required.

Measuring what works

Track enquiries by source, not just website visits. Set up conversion tracking for form submissions and phone calls, and ask every new client how they found you. Over time you will see which services, sectors and channels bring the work you actually want. Review this every quarter and shift your effort accordingly.

FAQs

What is the best health and safety consultancy marketing in the UK?

For most consultancies, health and safety consultancy marketing in the UK works best as a mix of clear service and sector pages, local search, focused Google Ads for urgent needs, LinkedIn for awareness and regular contact with past clients and referral partners.

How do health and safety consultants get clients?

Most get clients through search, referrals from accountants, HR firms, insurers and contractors, and repeat work from existing clients. A clear website with one page per service makes every one of those routes work better.

Should a health and safety consultancy use Google Ads?

Google Ads can work well for urgent needs, such as a contractor that needs documents quickly. Use focused keywords, exclude searches for jobs, courses and free templates, and send traffic to a matching service page.

What should a health and safety consultant's website include?

It should include a page for each service, pages for the sectors you know well, honest credentials, guidance on what affects pricing, the areas you cover and a simple way to get in touch.

Can we write about regulations on our blog?

Yes, but keep statements general and accurate, avoid promising compliance outcomes and link readers to current official guidance, such as that from the Health and Safety Executive. Review older posts whenever guidance changes.

How long does SEO take for a consultancy?

SEO results typically take three to six months to build, depending on your starting point, competition in your area and how much useful content you publish. Paid search can bring enquiries sooner while search visibility grows.
