How to Get More Google Reviews for Your UK Small Business

How to get more Google reviews the right way: when to ask, how to share your review link, what to say, and the rules to follow so your profile stays safe.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • The simplest way to get more Google reviews is to ask every happy customer, at the right moment, with a direct link that takes them straight to the review box.
  • Make asking a routine step in your process, through receipts, follow-up emails, texts, QR codes and conversations, rather than an occasional push.
  • Never buy reviews, offer rewards for them or only ask customers you expect to be positive. Check Google's current review policies before you run any campaign.

To get more Google reviews, ask every satisfied customer soon after they have had a good experience, and give them a direct link that opens the review box on your Google Business Profile. Make the request a routine part of how you finish each job or order, keep it short and personal, and follow Google's review policies so your profile stays in good standing.

This guide is for UK small businesses that rely on local search, from trades and salons to cafes, beauty studios and professional services. It explains why reviews matter, how to set up your review link, when and how to ask, and what to avoid.

Why Google reviews matter for local businesses

Reviews do two jobs. First, they help people decide. Someone comparing three electricians on Google Maps will read the most recent reviews before they call. Second, reviews are widely understood to be one of the factors Google considers in local rankings, alongside how relevant your profile is to the search and how close you are to the searcher. Our local SEO guide explains how these pieces fit together.

It is not only the number that counts. Recent reviews, reviews that mention the service and the area, and your replies all help show that the business is active and trusted. A steady flow of genuine reviews is more useful than a sudden burst followed by silence.

Set up your review link first

The single biggest barrier to reviews is effort. If a customer has to search for your business, find the profile and hunt for the review button, many will give up. A direct review link removes that friction.

  1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your Business Profile.
  2. Search for your business name on Google or open your profile in Google Maps.
  3. Look for the option to ask for reviews or share a review form. Google shows a short link you can copy.
  4. Test the link on your phone and on a computer while signed out, to check it opens the review box.
  5. Save the link somewhere your whole team can reach it.

Google changes its interface from time to time, so if the steps look different, check Google's current Business Profile help pages. If your profile is not yet claimed or set up properly, start with our Google Business Profile guide.

How to get more Google reviews: ways to ask

Different customers respond to different channels. Most businesses do best with two or three methods running at once.

When to ask

Timing matters more than wording. Ask at the moment the customer is happiest and the experience is fresh.

  • Trades: when the job is finished and the customer has seen the result.
  • Salons and personal services: at the end of the appointment, or by message later the same day.
  • Online shops: a few days after delivery, once the product has been used.
  • Professional services: after a milestone, such as a completed project or a resolved issue.
  • Hospitality: as guests leave, or by email the next day.

Avoid waiting weeks. The longer the gap, the less likely the customer is to bother.

What to say when you ask

Keep it short, polite and specific. People are more likely to help a named business they feel connected to than respond to a generic request. A simple email might read:

Thanks for choosing us for your bathroom refit. If you have a minute, a quick Google review would really help other local people find us. Here is the link. Thank you again.

A few points help:

  • Say why it matters: reviews help other local customers choose.
  • Make it easy: one link, no extra steps.
  • Invite honesty: you want their genuine view, not a five-star script.
  • Do not tell customers what to write. You can suggest they mention the service they had, but leave the words to them.
  • Send one reminder at most if they do not respond.

Rules to follow

Google has clear policies on reviews, and breaking them can lead to reviews being removed or, in serious cases, restrictions on your profile. UK consumer protection law also treats fake or misleading reviews seriously, so check the current guidance from Google and the relevant UK authorities before running any review campaign. In general:

  • Do not buy reviews or use services that promise them.
  • Do not offer incentives such as discounts, prize draws or freebies in exchange for reviews.
  • Do not review gate. That means asking only customers you think will be happy, or sending unhappy customers somewhere else first. Ask everyone in the same way.
  • Do not write reviews for your own business, or ask staff, friends and family to do so.
  • Do not set up review stations in your premises where many reviews come from one device.

Honest asking is enough. A business with good service and a consistent request process will build reviews naturally.

Reply to every review

Replying shows customers and potential customers that you are listening. Thank people for positive reviews, ideally mentioning something specific. Respond calmly and professionally to negative ones, offering to resolve the issue offline. Our guide on how to respond to Google reviews includes approaches for difficult cases.

Build reviews into your routine

The businesses with the most reviews are rarely the ones running campaigns. They simply ask every time. To make that happen:

  1. Add the review request to your job completion checklist or order follow-up.
  2. Automate the follow-up email or text where your booking, invoicing or ecommerce system allows it.
  3. Show staff the link and agree a short, natural way to ask.
  4. Track how many new reviews you get each month so you can see whether the routine is working.
  5. Share good reviews on your website, with permission where needed. Our guide to website testimonials pages explains how to present them.

Pair reviews with other profile activity, such as regular Google Business Profile posts and consistent local citations, so your local presence looks active and accurate everywhere.

FAQs

How do I get more Google reviews?

Ask every satisfied customer soon after a good experience and send them a direct review link. Use several channels, such as in person, email, text and QR codes, and make the request a routine step rather than a one-off campaign.

How do I find my Google review link?

Sign in to the account that manages your Business Profile, open your profile on Google Search or Maps, and look for the option to ask for reviews. Copy the short link Google provides and test it before sharing.

Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Google's policies do not allow incentives for reviews, and offering them can lead to reviews being removed. Check Google's current review policies before planning any review activity.

Is it okay to ask only happy customers for reviews?

Selectively asking only customers you expect to be positive is known as review gating and goes against Google's policies. Ask all customers in the same way and respond professionally to any criticism.

Do Google reviews help local SEO?

Reviews are widely seen as one of the factors in local rankings, and they strongly influence whether people choose to contact you. Recent, genuine reviews with replies help most.

How many Google reviews does a small business need?

There is no set number. What matters is having enough recent, genuine reviews to compare well with nearby competitors and a steady flow of new ones over time.
