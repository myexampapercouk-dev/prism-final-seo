B&Q Marketplace SEO: How to Improve Your Product Listings

How UK home and garden sellers can improve B&Q Marketplace listings, from titles and attributes to images and stock, plus when to bring in outside help.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • B&Q Marketplace listings are more likely to be found when titles, categories and product attributes are complete, accurate and match how customers search.
  • Home and garden buyers often need practical detail such as size, material, compatibility and what is included, so good listings answer those questions before they are asked.
  • Platform rules and requirements can change, so always check current B&Q seller guidance and work from accurate product data.

B&Q Marketplace SEO means improving your product listings so that they are easier to find and easier to choose on the marketplace. The essentials are the same as for most retail search: accurate titles, the correct category, complete product attributes, clear images and reliable stock information. Home improvement buyers often have a specific job to do, so they need practical details such as dimensions, materials and compatibility. Listings that give those details clearly are better placed to earn the sale.

This guide is for UK sellers already on the marketplace or considering it. B&Q decides its own selling rules and how its search works, and these can change, so check your current seller guidance alongside the advice below.

How marketplace search generally works

B&Q does not need to publish the details of how its search results are ordered, and this guide will not pretend to know them. Like other retail platforms, though, a marketplace can only show your product for a search if it understands what you are selling. It also has a commercial reason to show products that customers want and that can be delivered reliably.

That gives you three practical aims:

  • Be understood: describe the product clearly, in the right category, with complete data.
  • Be chosen: make the listing persuasive when it appears alongside similar items.
  • Be reliable: keep your price, stock and delivery information accurate, so that customers get what they were promised.

Start with the words customers use

Good listings begin with how buyers describe the product. A trade supplier's catalogue name may mean little to someone looking for a replacement part or a garden project. Before you edit anything, gather the terms real customers use.

  • Look at the marketplace's own search suggestions as you type.
  • Review the language in customer questions, returns reasons and reviews.
  • Compare how similar products are described in the same category.
  • Check your own website's search and Search Console data, if you have them.
  • Note different names for the same thing, as well as the measurements buyers commonly search for.

Write clear product titles

Your title should say what the product is, and include the details a buyer would use to tell it apart. Follow any format your seller guidance sets. Where you have freedom, a sensible order is usually brand, product type, then the key details such as size, material, colour or pack quantity.

Do not include claims you cannot back up, or the names of other brands you do not sell. Accuracy is more valuable than cramming in extra search terms.

Complete every relevant attribute

Attributes are the structured details attached to a product, such as colour, material, dimensions, power type or finish. They can matter a great deal because customers often narrow results using filters. If a detail is missing or sits in the wrong field, your product may not appear once a filter is applied, even if it would be a perfect match.

  • Select the most accurate category, rather than the one that merely seems busiest.
  • Fill in every attribute that genuinely applies.
  • Use consistent units, and check them carefully. A mistaken measurement can create returns as well as lost sales.
  • Include barcodes or other product identifiers wherever they are required and available.
  • Audit your product data regularly, especially after any bulk upload.

Write descriptions that answer practical questions

Home and garden purchases often come with worries. Will it fit? Will it work with what I already have? Can I install it myself? A useful description deals with those points directly, so the customer does not have to guess or go elsewhere.

  • Size and fit: external dimensions, internal dimensions and weight where these are relevant.
  • Compatibility: what the product works with and, just as importantly, what it does not.
  • What is included: fixings, batteries, tools or anything that must be bought separately.
  • Installation: whether assembly is required, and whether professional installation is recommended.
  • Use and care: indoor or outdoor use, maintenance and suitable conditions.
  • Safety and standards: only the information you can properly support with documentation.

Be especially careful with safety and compliance wording, particularly on electrical, gas, chemical and children's products. Requirements vary by product, so take appropriate advice and do not make statements your documents cannot support.

Use images that do more than look nice

Images help buyers judge a product and reduce doubts that words alone cannot. Check the current image requirements first, then plan a set that does a job at each stage of the decision.

  • A clear main image that shows exactly what the customer receives.
  • Images showing the product in use, in a realistic setting.
  • A scale reference or a dimension diagram.
  • Close-ups of the materials, finishes and important features.
  • A picture of everything included in the box.

Make sure the images match the variant being sold. A photo of the wrong colour or size can lead to disappointed customers and avoidable returns.

Keep price, stock and delivery accurate

Search visibility is not much use if the product cannot be delivered as promised. Stock errors, cancelled orders and missed delivery dates can harm the customer experience and your standing as a seller. Check your account's current performance requirements and pay close attention to these areas.

  • Sync stock regularly, particularly if you sell the same items on several channels.
  • Show realistic delivery estimates, especially for bulky or heavy goods.
  • Review your pricing against the wider market, taking fees and delivery costs into account.
  • Track returns reasons, as they often reveal listing errors you can fix.
  • Respond promptly to customer questions and service issues.

Common listing mistakes

  • Copying manufacturer data without checking or completing it.
  • Choosing the wrong category or leaving attributes blank.
  • Using identical descriptions for products that differ in important ways.
  • Leaving out dimensions, or giving them in inconsistent units.
  • Failing to say what is not included.
  • Using stock photos that do not show the real product or variant.
  • Uploading in bulk without checking a sample first.

How to prioritise a large catalogue

If you have thousands of products, improving everything at once is unrealistic. Begin where better listings are most likely to pay back.

  1. Export your catalogue and your sales data.
  2. Identify your best sellers and your highest-margin items.
  3. Look for products with weak data, high returns or low sales for their category.
  4. Fix your most valuable group first, then build a template for each product type.
  5. Apply what you learn to the rest of the range, checking samples as you go.

Good product data is reusable. Work carried out here can also strengthen your own website and other sales channels, including those covered in our guides to Amazon SEO and eBay SEO.

When to get outside help

You could consider support when your range is too large to manage by hand, your data sits in several systems or you are selling across a number of channels. Ask any provider about these points.

  • Have they worked with marketplace listings and bulk product data?
  • How would they check accuracy, especially for technical details?
  • How will they measure improvement, and over what period of time?
  • Will the improved data remain yours if you stop working together?
  • Will they stay within the marketplace's current rules?

Be wary of anyone promising top positions. No outside provider controls how a marketplace ranks products.

FAQs

How do I start selling on B&Q Marketplace?

Check B&Q's current seller information for eligibility and the application process. Requirements may change and can differ by product type, so rely on the official guidance.

Are marketplace listings the same as Google SEO?

They share some principles, such as matching customer language and being accurate. However, a marketplace works with its own product data, filters and seller information, so complete attributes are particularly important.

How long should a product title be?

Follow any limit in your current seller guidance. Within that, include what a buyer needs to recognise the product and tell it apart, and avoid repeating keywords.

Can I reuse descriptions from my own website?

You can use them as a starting point if your rules allow it. Do check that the details suit the marketplace format, and that the fields and attributes are completed separately.

Do customer reviews matter?

Reviews can influence buying decisions. Focus on accurate listings and reliable delivery, respond to feedback sensibly and do not try to obtain fake or incentivised reviews against the rules.

How should I measure improvements?

Compare views, conversion, sales and returns for the products you changed, using whatever reports your seller account provides. Change batches of products at a time, so you can see what made a difference.

Next steps

Choose your ten most valuable products and read each listing as a customer would. Note every question it leaves unanswered, then fix those first and use the improved listings as templates.
