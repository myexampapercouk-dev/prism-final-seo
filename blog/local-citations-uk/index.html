Local Citations for UK Businesses: How to Build and Clean Up Your Listings

Local citations are online mentions of your business name, address and phone. Learn which UK listings matter, how to keep details consistent and fix errors.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Local citations are mentions of your business name, address and phone number on other websites, and consistent details help search engines and customers trust your listing.
  • Start with your Google Business Profile and a short list of major and relevant UK directories before chasing volume.
  • Cleaning up old or wrong listings, such as a previous address or phone number, is often more valuable than creating new ones.

Local citations are online mentions of your business's name, address and phone number, usually on directories, maps apps, review sites and industry listings. For UK businesses that serve a local area, accurate and consistent citations help search engines confirm where you are and what you do, and help customers find correct details wherever they look.

Citations are not the most exciting part of local SEO, and they are rarely enough on their own to move rankings. But inconsistent or outdated listings are a common, fixable weakness, particularly for businesses that have moved premises, changed phone numbers or rebranded. This guide explains what counts as a citation, which ones are worth your time, and how to run a clean-up.

What are local citations?

A citation is any online reference to your business that includes some or all of your core details, often shortened to NAP: name, address and phone number. Many also include your website, opening hours, categories and a short description.

There are two broad types:

  • Structured citations appear in business directories and data platforms with fixed fields, such as Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yell, Thomson Local or a trade body directory.
  • Unstructured citations are mentions in ordinary content, such as a local news article, a supplier's case study page or an event listing.

Search engines compare these mentions with your Google Business Profile and website. When details agree, it reinforces that your business is genuine and located where you say. When they conflict, it creates doubt for both algorithms and customers. Our wider local SEO guide explains how citations fit alongside reviews, your profile and on-site content.

Why NAP consistency matters

Imagine a customer finds your old phone number on a directory, rings it and gets no answer. That lost enquiry is the practical cost of inconsistency, before you even think about rankings.

From a search point of view, consistency helps search engines connect scattered mentions to one business entity. It matters most when:

  • You have moved premises and old listings still show the previous address.
  • You changed trading name, or listings mix a trading name with a limited company name.
  • You use several phone numbers, such as tracking numbers, mobiles and a landline.
  • You have more than one location. Our guide to multi-location SEO covers that situation in detail.

Decide your master business details first

Before creating or editing any listing, write down the exact version of your details that you will use everywhere. This is your master record.

FieldWhat to decideTip
Business nameYour real-world trading nameDo not add keywords or locations that are not part of the name
AddressOne format, matching Royal Mail's versionService-area businesses may hide the address where platforms allow
PhoneOne main local or national numberUse the same number as your website and Google Business Profile
WebsiteYour preferred domain versionConsider tagging links so you can see directory traffic in GA4
CategoriesMain and secondary servicesMatch your Google Business Profile categories where possible
DescriptionA short, factual summaryWrite a standard version and adapt length for each site

Keep this record in a shared document with logins for each listing. When something changes, you have one place to update from and a list of every site to visit.

Which UK citations to prioritise

Not all listings are equal. A handful of trusted, relevant listings is worth far more than hundreds of low-quality directories. Work in this order:

  1. Google Business Profile. The most important listing for local search. Our Google Business Profile guide covers setup and optimisation.
  2. Other maps and platforms. Bing Places and Apple Business Connect feed maps used on many devices and in voice assistants.
  3. Major UK directories. Long-established general directories that customers still use.
  4. Industry and trade listings. Trade associations, professional body registers and sector directories relevant to your work. These carry relevance as well as consistency.
  5. Local listings. Chamber of commerce, local business groups, town or high street websites and community directories.
  6. Social profiles. Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn pages with matching details.

Be wary of paid directory packages promising hundreds of listings. Mass submissions to obscure sites rarely help and can be hard to undo later. Check each directory's current terms before paying for anything.

How to audit your existing citations

Most established businesses already have citations they never created, because directories scrape public records and other listings. An audit finds them.

  1. Search your business name with and without your town, and look beyond the first page of results.
  2. Search your old details. Previous addresses and phone numbers often reveal outdated listings.
  3. Search your phone number in quotes, in several formats, with and without spaces.
  4. Check the major platforms directly by searching their own sites.
  5. Record each listing in a spreadsheet with the address, whether details are correct, whether you can log in, and what needs changing.

Paid citation tools can speed this up for larger or multi-site businesses, but a careful manual search covers most small businesses well. As you go, note which listings send real visitors or calls. A directory that customers in your area actually use deserves more attention, such as photos, a full description and regular updates, than one that simply exists. Also look out for listings that show your details next to a competitor, or that carry an old business name after a rebrand, and add those to the top of your clean-up list.

Fixing incorrect and duplicate listings

Clean-up often takes longer than creating new listings, because you may need to claim profiles you have never seen before.

  • Claim the listing where possible, usually by phone, email or postcard verification, then edit the details.
  • Request removal of duplicates using the directory's report or support options.
  • Mark old locations as moved or closed rather than leaving them live.
  • Be patient. Some directories take weeks to update, and data aggregators may push old information back if their source is not corrected.

If a listing has reviews attached, merging or moving it carefully can keep that history. Read the platform's current help pages before deleting anything, because removal can be permanent. Once listings are tidy, keep responding to feedback; our guide on how to respond to Google reviews covers that part.

Keeping citations accurate over time

Citations are not a one-off task. Build a light routine:

  • Review your top listings every quarter and after any change to hours, phone or address.
  • Update seasonal and bank holiday hours on major platforms in advance.
  • Keep your website's contact page, footer and schema markup in line with the master record. Our schema markup guide explains local business structured data.
  • When you join a new trade body or local network, ask for a listing using your master details.

Citations versus links

Citations and backlinks overlap but are not the same. Some citations include a followed link to your site and some do not, and many directory links carry little SEO weight. Treat citations as a trust and accuracy exercise. If you want to earn links that build authority, that is a separate job involving content and outreach, which our digital PR and link building guide covers.

Doing it yourself or handing it over

A single-location business with stable details can usually manage its own citations with an afternoon of auditing and a quarterly check. It becomes a bigger job after a move, a rebrand or when several locations are involved. FAQs

What are local citations?

They are mentions of your business name, address and phone number on other websites, such as directories, maps apps, review sites and trade listings. They help customers and search engines confirm your details.

How many citations does a small business need?

There is no magic number. Focus on accuracy across the main maps platforms, a few trusted UK directories and listings relevant to your trade or area, rather than chasing volume.

Do citations still help local SEO?

They help most as a consistency and trust signal, especially when details have changed. They usually matter less than your Google Business Profile, reviews and website content, but wrong listings can still cost enquiries.

Should I pay for a citation building service?

It can save time for multi-location businesses or major clean-ups. Avoid packages promising hundreds of low-quality directories, and check exactly which sites are included before paying.

What if I run a service-area business from home?

Many platforms let you hide your home address and show a service area instead. Keep the hidden address consistent anyway, and check each platform's current rules for service-area businesses.

How long do citation updates take?

Some platforms update within days, others take weeks. Old data can also reappear from aggregators, so recheck key listings a month or two after making changes.
