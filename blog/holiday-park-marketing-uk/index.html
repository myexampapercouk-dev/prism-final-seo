Holiday Park Marketing in the UK: How to Win More Direct Bookings

Holiday park marketing in the UK: win more direct bookings with a fast site, accommodation pages, local and destination SEO, and well-timed seasonal ads.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Direct bookings depend on a fast website, clear accommodation pages and a booking journey that feels as easy as the big listing platforms.
  • Destination content about the surrounding area helps a park appear when people are still deciding where to holiday.
  • Seasonal timing matters: most of the marketing effort should land in the planning months before the peak season.

Holiday park marketing in the UK is mostly about turning holiday planners into direct bookers, rather than relying entirely on listing platforms that take a commission. The parks that do this well combine a fast website with clear accommodation pages, destination content that captures people while they are still choosing where to go, a simple booking journey and email and paid campaigns timed around the planning season.

This guide is written for independent caravan parks, lodge parks and touring and camping sites. It covers your website, SEO, paid media, email and the practical details guests look for before they commit. We are a studio that builds websites and runs search and paid campaigns for hospitality businesses, so the focus is on what we see work in practice. Our hospitality sector page gives an overview of how we work with venues more generally.

How guests plan a holiday park stay

Most guests begin with a destination or an idea rather than a specific park. They search for things like "lodges with hot tubs in Cornwall", "dog-friendly caravan holidays in Norfolk" or "touring sites near the Lake District". They compare options on listing sites, in map results and on park websites, look at photos and prices, then often check reviews before booking.

Many will find you through a listing platform first and then look you up directly. If your own website is slow, unclear or harder to book through, they will simply book through the platform. The job of your marketing is to make the direct route at least as appealing.

Holiday park marketing UK: the website foundations

Your website should do three things very well: show what each type of accommodation is really like, show availability and prices clearly, and take a booking without friction. Our guide to hotel direct bookings covers the commercial thinking, much of which applies to parks too.

Accommodation pages

Each accommodation type deserves its own page: static caravans by grade, lodges, glamping units and touring or camping pitches. Include sleeping arrangements, floor plans where available, kitchen and bathroom details, outdoor space, whether pets are allowed and accessibility information. Use real photography, taken in good light, and keep image files compressed so pages load quickly on mobile.

Facilities and the park itself

Guests want to know what is on site: a shop, play areas, pool, entertainment, laundry, electric hook-ups and Wi-Fi. Be specific about seasonal opening, such as whether the pool or bar is open outside the summer holidays. Clear information prevents disappointed reviews later.

The booking journey

Most parks use a specialist booking system that can be embedded on their website. Make sure it works smoothly on a phone, shows total prices including any extras before the final step, and explains the deposit, balance and cancellation policy plainly. Our guide to online booking systems covers what to look for. If guests have to hunt for the price, many will go back to a listing site where it is shown upfront.

Speed and mobile

A large share of holiday planning happens on phones, often in the evening. Heavy image galleries and slow booking widgets can quietly lose bookings. Our guide to website speed optimisation explains the main fixes.

SEO for holiday parks

Search visibility works on two levels: your park itself and the destination around it.

Park and accommodation searches

Optimise accommodation pages for the phrases people actually use, such as "lodges with hot tubs", "dog-friendly static caravans" or "seasonal touring pitches", combined with your area. Add structured data where your platform supports it, and keep your Google Business Profile accurate, with up-to-date photos and seasonal opening dates. Our local SEO guide covers the basics.

Destination content

Many people searching for a holiday area have not chosen a park yet. Helpful guides to the surrounding area, such as nearby beaches, walks, rainy-day activities and dog-friendly pubs, can bring those planners to your site. Write from genuine local knowledge, keep the information accurate and link naturally to your accommodation pages. Our guide to content strategy explains how to plan this sort of content so it supports bookings rather than just traffic.

AI search and summaries

More people now ask AI assistants for holiday suggestions. Clear, factual pages with consistent details about your park, facilities and location make it easier for these tools to describe you accurately. Our introduction to generative engine optimisation explains the idea.

Paid media and seasonal timing

Holiday bookings follow a clear rhythm. Paid campaigns are most effective when they land as people are planning, rather than when the park is already full.

Boxing Day to FebruaryPlanning and booking summer holidaysSearch and social campaigns for peak weeks; email to past guests
March to MayBooking Easter, half term and late summerPromote remaining summer dates and spring short breaks
June to AugustLast-minute bookingsPush any gaps; reduce spend on fully booked dates
September to NovemberAutumn breaks and festive staysShort breaks, dog-friendly and couples-focused offers

Google Search campaigns can capture high-intent searches for your park type and area. Meta ads work well for showing accommodation visually and reaching people interested in your region. Our paid media service covers Google, Meta, TikTok and YouTube campaigns. Track completed bookings as conversions, not just clicks, so you can see which campaigns pay for themselves.

Any price or offer in an advert must be accurate and include mandatory fees, in line with the CAP Code and UK consumer protection rules on price transparency.

Email and returning guests

Returning guests are often a park's most valuable audience. With consent captured at booking, an email after each stay asking for a review, followed by early-booking news for next year, can fill a good share of the calendar. Owners and seasonal pitch holders may need their own communications too. Under PECR, marketing emails to individuals usually need opt-in consent, so keep that separate from booking confirmations.

Reviews and reputation

Reviews on Google and travel sites heavily influence park bookings. Ask guests for a review after their stay, reply to every review, and use recurring feedback to improve. Respond to complaints calmly and specifically, offering to continue the conversation privately. Never offer incentives in exchange for reviews.

Audiences worth planning for

Different guests value different things, and the strongest park websites speak to each of them clearly rather than trying to appeal to everyone at once.

  • Families want to know about play areas, pools, entertainment, cot and highchair availability, and how far the beach or attractions are.
  • Couples often look for quiet lodges, hot tubs, adult-only areas and short breaks outside school holidays.
  • Dog owners want specific details: how many dogs are allowed, any charges, enclosed areas, walks from the park and dog-friendly pubs nearby.
  • Tourers and campers need pitch sizes, hook-up details, facilities blocks, arrival times and rules on awnings and gazebos.
  • Prospective owners, if you sell holiday homes, need separate information on site fees, the season length and the buying process, kept apart from holiday bookings.

Filtering on the accommodation pages, such as pet-friendly or hot tub, helps each group find what suits them quickly. It also gives you clear themes for campaigns and email segments.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Showing a starting price that rises sharply once compulsory extras are added.
  • Outdated photos that no longer match the accommodation.
  • Facilities listed without seasonal opening dates.
  • Booking widgets that are hard to use on a phone.

FAQs

What is the most effective holiday park marketing UK parks can do?

Improve the direct booking journey first: clear accommodation pages, honest prices and a smooth mobile booking flow. Then build SEO and email so that more planners book with you directly rather than through listing platforms.

Should a holiday park stay on listing platforms?

For many parks, listing platforms remain a useful source of new guests. The aim is usually to balance them with direct bookings, encouraging returning guests and those who find you by search to book on your own site.

When should a holiday park advertise?

The busiest planning period is usually from Boxing Day to February, with further waves before Easter and for autumn breaks. Focus spend on those periods and on any gaps, rather than on dates that are already full.

Do holiday parks need a page for each accommodation type?

Yes. Separate pages make it easier for guests to compare options and for search engines to match your accommodation to specific searches, such as lodges with hot tubs or dog-friendly caravans.

Is destination content worth writing?

It can be, when it is genuinely helpful and accurate. Guides to the local area help you reach people who are choosing where to go, and they give you natural places to link to your accommodation.

How long does a holiday park website take to build?

A typical small business website takes three to five weeks, while larger sites with complex booking integrations can take longer. Timings depend on content, photography and the booking system involved.
