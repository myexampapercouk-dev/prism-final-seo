Website Favicons and App Icons: How to Get Them Right

What favicons and app icons are, which sizes and formats to prepare, and how to add them in WordPress and Shopify so your site looks sharp in tabs and search.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A favicon is the small icon shown in browser tabs, bookmarks and search results, and it helps people recognise your site.
  • Prepare a simple, square version of your logo mark that still reads clearly at very small sizes.
  • WordPress and Shopify both let you upload a site icon without code, but check how it looks across devices afterwards.

The favicon is one of the smallest parts of a website, but it appears in more places than most people realise. It sits in browser tabs, bookmarks and history lists, on phone home screens when someone saves your site, and alongside your listings in some search results. A missing or blurry icon can make a site look unfinished, while a clear one helps visitors find you again.

This guide explains what favicons and app icons are, how to design one that works, which files to prepare and how to add them in WordPress and Shopify.

What is a favicon?

A favicon, short for "favourite icon", is a small image associated with a website. Browsers display it in tabs, bookmarks and address bars. When someone has several tabs open, the favicon is often the quickest way for them to spot your site.

Over time, the idea has expanded. Phones and tablets use larger versions of the icon when someone adds a website to their home screen. Some browsers use icons in their new tab pages or shortcuts. Google can also show a favicon next to a site's name in search results. Together, these are often called site icons or app icons.

Why favicons matter

  • Recognition. A distinctive icon helps returning visitors find your tab or bookmark quickly.
  • Professionalism. A default or blank icon can make a site look unfinished.
  • Search appearance. Where search engines show favicons, a clear icon helps your listing look more polished.
  • Brand consistency. The icon is another touchpoint that should match the rest of your branding.

A favicon will not transform your website on its own, but it is part of the overall impression of care and quality.

Designing a favicon that works

The biggest challenge is size. A favicon is often displayed very small, so detail disappears quickly. A full logo with a name and tagline will almost never work.

Use a simple mark

Most good favicons use a simplified element of the brand: an initial, a symbol or a distinctive shape. If your logo already includes a separate mark, that is often the best starting point. If it does not, consider creating a simplified version as part of your brand assets. Our guide to brand identity design explains how logo variations fit into a wider identity.

Keep it square

Favicons and app icons are square. Design within a square frame and leave a little space around the edges, so the icon does not feel cramped or get clipped when platforms round the corners.

Think about contrast

Browser tabs can be light or dark depending on the visitor's settings. An icon that disappears against a dark tab is a common problem. Test against both, and consider adding a solid background colour to the icon so it stays visible.

Avoid fine detail and small text

Thin lines, gradients and small lettering tend to become blurry at small sizes. Bold shapes and strong colours work best.

Which sizes and formats do you need?

Different browsers and devices request icons in different sizes. You do not need to create dozens of versions by hand, but it helps to understand the main types.

Icon typeWhere it is usedNotes
Small browser faviconTabs, bookmarks, address barTraditionally an ICO or PNG file. Must read clearly at tiny sizes.
SVG faviconModern browsersA scalable format that stays sharp at any size. Not supported everywhere, so use alongside a PNG or ICO.
Apple touch iconiPhone and iPad home screensA larger PNG. The device may round the corners automatically.
Android and web app iconsAndroid home screens and installed web appsLarger PNG files, usually listed in a web app manifest.

The simplest starting point is a high-resolution square image of your icon, which can then be resized for each use. Both WordPress and Shopify can generate smaller versions from a single upload, which covers most needs.

Adding a favicon in WordPress

WordPress calls the favicon a site icon. Where you add it depends on your theme.

Block themes

In a block theme, the site icon is usually managed in the Site Editor. Adding a Site Logo block may give you the option to use the logo as the site icon, or you may find a site icon setting in the general settings area. The exact location has changed between WordPress versions, so if you cannot find it, check the Settings screens as well as the editor.

Classic themes

In a classic theme, go to Appearance, then Customise, then Site Identity. There you can upload a site icon. WordPress asks for a square image and will crop it if needed, then generates the sizes used for browsers and devices.

After uploading, publish your changes and check the site in a new browser tab. Our guide to building a WordPress small business website covers other basics worth setting up at the same time.

Adding a favicon in Shopify

In Shopify, the favicon is managed through the theme editor. Go to Online Store, then Themes, and customise your current theme. Look in Theme settings for a Logo or Favicon section, where you can upload a square image.

The setting is part of the theme, so if you change or update your theme, check the favicon is still in place. Our guide to Shopify theme updates explains how to carry settings across safely.

Why your new favicon might not show

A common frustration is uploading a new icon and still seeing the old one. There are a few likely reasons:

  • Browser caching. Browsers keep favicons for a long time. Try a private window, a different browser or clearing your cache.
  • Site caching. If your site uses caching, clear it so the updated page code is served.
  • Theme overrides. Some themes or custom code add their own favicon tags, which may override the platform setting. A developer can check the page source to see which icon files are referenced.
  • Search results. Search engines update favicons on their own schedule after recrawling your site, so changes there are not immediate.

Favicons in search results

Google publishes guidelines for favicons shown in search. In general, the icon should be square, represent your brand, be accessible to Google's crawlers and follow its content policies. Google may choose not to show a favicon or may replace one it considers unsuitable. Refer to Google's current documentation for the details, as guidance can change.

Make sure your favicon files are not blocked by your robots.txt file, and that the icon is served from your own website rather than a temporary location.

Web app manifests

A web app manifest is a small file that tells browsers about your site's name, colours and icons when it is saved to a home screen or installed as a web app. Not every business website needs one, but if you want your site to look polished when saved to a phone, a manifest with suitable icons helps.

Platforms and themes handle manifests differently. If you need one, a developer can add it and make sure the icons and colours match your branding.

A quick favicon checklist

  • Start with a square, simplified version of your logo mark.
  • Check it is readable at tiny sizes and against light and dark backgrounds.
  • Upload a high-resolution square image through your platform's site icon setting.
  • Test in several browsers, on mobile and when saved to a home screen.
  • Check the icon after theme changes or redesigns.
  • Keep the original file with your brand assets for future use.

FAQs

What size should a favicon be?

Upload the largest square version your platform accepts and let it generate smaller sizes. The design should be simple enough to read clearly when shown very small.

Can I use my full logo as a favicon?

Usually not. Full logos with text become unreadable at small sizes. A simplified mark or initial almost always works better.

Why does Google show a different icon for my site?

Google may show a generic icon if it cannot find a suitable favicon, if the file is blocked or if it has not recrawled your site since you changed it. Check that your icon follows Google's guidelines and is accessible.

Do favicons affect SEO?

A favicon is not a ranking factor in itself, but it can make your search listing look more recognisable and trustworthy, which may help people choose your result.

Should my favicon change with seasonal branding?

It is possible, but frequent changes can confuse returning visitors and may take time to update in browsers and search results. Most sites keep a stable icon.
