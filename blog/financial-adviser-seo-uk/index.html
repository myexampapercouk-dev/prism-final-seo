Financial Adviser SEO in the UK: How IFAs and Planners Can Win More Enquiries from Search

Financial adviser SEO in the UK helps IFAs and planners rank for local and specialist searches and win enquiries. What it involves, costs and how to start.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • SEO for financial advisers works best when it targets local and specialist searches, such as pension or retirement advice in a named town, rather than broad national terms.
  • Trust signals matter more in financial services: clear adviser information, regulatory status, fees explained in plain English and genuinely helpful content.
  • Every page and article should go through the firm's own compliance sign-off, and results typically take 3 to 6 months to build.

Financial adviser SEO in the UK is the work of helping IFAs, restricted advisers and financial planning firms rank in Google and AI search for the local and specialist searches their ideal clients use, then turning those visits into enquiries. For most firms, the biggest wins come from strong local visibility, clear service pages for each type of advice and helpful, compliant content that builds trust.

This guide explains how SEO works for UK financial advisers, which searches are worth targeting, how to handle compliance, what it typically costs and a practical checklist to get started. It is general information, not regulatory advice.

Why financial adviser SEO matters in the UK

People looking for financial advice tend to research carefully. They may search "financial adviser near me", "pension advice Guildford" or "inheritance tax planning adviser", then read several firms' websites before getting in touch. Unlike referrals, search reaches people who do not yet know you, at the moment they are actively looking for help.

Search is also increasingly about AI answers. Tools such as Google's AI Overviews and chat assistants summarise and cite websites, and clear, well-structured, trustworthy content gives your firm a better chance of being included. Our guide to generative engine optimisation explains how this works.

Local SEO for advice firms

Many clients still prefer an adviser they can meet. A complete Google Business Profile with accurate categories, address, hours and photos, plus consistent business details across directories, helps you show up in map results. Reviews can help too, but firms should check how their compliance team wants review requests and responses handled. Our local SEO guide covers the fundamentals.

Service and niche pages

One general "services" page rarely ranks for anything specific. Separate pages for retirement planning, pensions, investments, protection, inheritance tax planning or advice for business owners give Google and readers a clear match for each search. Firms that serve a niche, such as medical professionals, farmers or business owners selling up, can rank well by building content around that audience.

Compliance and financial adviser SEO

Website content and blog posts can count as financial promotions, so they typically need to be fair, clear and not misleading, and in many cases approved before publication. Rules and their interpretation change, and requirements differ between firms and permissions. Prism Works Studio provides marketing and SEO support only; responsibility for compliance, and final sign-off on every page, article and ad, rests with the firm and its compliance team or provider.

In practice, this means building a review step into the SEO workflow: drafts go to compliance, changes are tracked and nothing goes live until approved. Our guide to FCA-compliant marketing for financial advisers covers the general principles in more depth.

Helpful SEO content and compliance pull in the same direction. Clear explanations, balanced statements about risk, plain-English descriptions of fees and accurate information about who you are and how you are regulated all build trust with readers and with search engines.

Technical and trust foundations

Search engines look harder at content that can affect people's finances. Advice firm websites should make it easy to see who is behind the advice and why they are credible. Useful foundations include:

  • Adviser profiles with qualifications and areas of focus, where the firm is happy to publish them.
  • Clear regulatory status and firm details, as required.
  • A plain-English explanation of how fees work.
  • Fast, secure, mobile-friendly pages with simple contact forms.
  • Privacy information that meets UK GDPR for enquiry data.

If your site has deeper problems, our technical SEO audit checklist explains what to check, and our guide on redesigning without losing rankings helps if you are planning a rebuild.

Checklist: getting started with financial adviser SEO

  • List the services and locations that bring your best clients.
  • Claim and complete your Google Business Profile with accurate details.
  • Create a separate page for each core service and any niche audience.
  • Explain fees and the advice process in plain English.
  • Make adviser information and regulatory details easy to find.
  • Agree a compliance review process before any content is written.
  • Track enquiries by source, not just traffic or rankings.
  • Allow typically 3 to 6 months for meaningful movement.

Content ideas that tend to work for advice firms

Helpful content gives potential clients a reason to trust you before they get in touch, and it gives search engines more relevant pages to rank. The best topics come from the questions clients already ask in first meetings. Common examples include:

  • What to expect from a first meeting with a financial adviser.
  • How ongoing advice works and what an annual review covers.
  • Planning steps to consider before retirement.
  • Questions to think about when receiving an inheritance.
  • Financial planning considerations for business owners planning an exit.
  • How to prepare paperwork before speaking to an adviser.

Each piece should explain the topic in general terms, avoid presenting general information as personal advice, include appropriate risk wording as agreed with compliance, and link to the relevant service page so interested readers have a clear next step.

Content does not need to be published in huge volumes. A smaller number of genuinely useful, accurate pages, kept up to date when rules or allowances change, is usually more valuable than frequent thin posts. Dates and figures such as tax allowances should be reviewed regularly, because out-of-date information can mislead readers and create compliance risk.

Measuring results

Rankings are useful signals, but the real measure is enquiries from suitable prospects. Track form submissions and calls by source, note which services enquiries relate to and, where possible, follow them through to new clients so the firm can see which pages and searches are worth more investment. Reviewing this every quarter helps the firm decide where to add new pages, which content to refresh and when to expand into new locations or specialist topics.

FAQs

What is financial adviser SEO in the UK?

It is the process of improving an advice firm's website and online presence so it ranks for the local and specialist searches its ideal clients use, and turns those visits into enquiries.

How long does SEO take for financial advisers?

Meaningful results typically take 3 to 6 months, depending on competition, the current state of the site and how quickly content can pass compliance review.

Does website content need compliance approval?

Often, yes, because pages and articles can count as financial promotions. Requirements vary, so firms should follow their own compliance team's guidance; final approval rests with the firm.

Should IFAs target national or local searches?

Most firms do better starting with local and specialist searches, which are less competitive and more likely to bring suitable clients, then expanding into broader topics over time.

Can financial advisers use reviews for SEO?

Reviews can support local visibility and trust, but firms should check with their compliance team how reviews are requested, displayed and responded to.

How much does SEO for an IFA cost?

Our retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month depending on scope. Wider market prices vary, and time for compliance review should be planned in.
