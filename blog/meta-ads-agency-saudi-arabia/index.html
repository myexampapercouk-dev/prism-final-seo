Meta Ads Agency Saudi Arabia: Running Facebook and Instagram Ads in KSA

Hiring a Meta ads agency in Saudi Arabia: tracking, Arabic creative, targeting across KSA cities, what to ask before you hire and our published monthly fees.

Why Meta ads in Saudi Arabia need their own plan

Saudi Arabia is a large, young and highly mobile market. Social platforms are a major part of how people discover brands, compare products and decide what to buy. A campaign that works in Europe may not work in KSA without changes:

  • Arabic first for many audiences. Plenty of Saudi customers prefer Arabic creative, and tone, humour and references need to feel natural rather than translated.
  • Several major cities. Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam and the Eastern Province, Makkah and Madinah can behave very differently. Separate ad sets or reporting by city often shows where budget works best.
  • Seasonal peaks. Ramadan, Eid, national holidays and big sale periods change what people buy and when. Budgets and creative should be planned around them well in advance.
  • Short-form video. Reels and Stories are central. Static images still have a place, but vertical video built for the first few seconds usually matters most.

Tracking comes first

Meta's delivery system learns from the conversion data you send it. If that data is missing or wrong, the platform optimises towards the wrong people and your results look worse than they are. Before launching or taking over an account, we check the pixel, the events it fires and whether server-side tracking is in place. Our Meta pixel setup guide walks through the basics.

Many Saudi customers prefer to message a business rather than fill in a form. If WhatsApp, Instagram Direct or Messenger is your main enquiry route, those conversations should be measured too, and someone on your team needs to reply quickly.

Creative that works in KSA

Creative is usually the biggest lever in a Meta account. We plan creative around a few clear angles, such as the product benefit, a customer problem, social proof you genuinely have, or an offer, and test them against each other. Good Saudi creative tends to share some traits:

  • A strong opening in the first second or two, with on-screen text for viewers watching without sound.
  • Arabic copy written by a native speaker, with English versions for audiences who prefer them.
  • Products and people shown in ways that respect local cultural norms.
  • Clear prices or offers where relevant, shown in the local currency on your own store and ads.

We brief and structure the creative, write English copy and work with your team or a translator on Arabic. If you have a design team or creators, we provide clear briefs so each round of testing has a purpose.

Ecommerce and lead generation

For online stores, Meta works best with a clean product catalogue, a fast mobile checkout and tracking that reports purchases accurately. If you sell on Shopify, our guide to a Shopify agency in Saudi Arabia covers the store side. For service businesses, lead forms or messaging campaigns can work well, but lead quality needs checking as closely as lead volume.

Wherever traffic lands, the page must match the ad. Our notes on landing page design explain what keeps people moving towards an enquiry or purchase.

Common mistakes in Saudi Meta accounts

When we review existing accounts, the same problems appear often. Most are fixable without raising the budget:

  • Boosting posts instead of running campaigns. Boosts are simple, but they rarely optimise towards sales or qualified leads.
  • Too many small ad sets. Splitting a modest budget across many audiences starves each one of the data it needs to learn.
  • Creative left running too long. When the same people see the same ad repeatedly, costs rise and results fall.
  • Slow replies to messages. Messaging campaigns can generate plenty of conversations, but enquiries go cold quickly if nobody answers.
  • No exclusions. Existing customers and recent buyers often keep seeing acquisition ads, which wastes spend.

Questions to ask before hiring

  1. How will you check our tracking? Expect a specific answer, not reassurance.
  2. Who makes the creative, and how often is it refreshed? Tired ads are a common cause of rising costs.
  3. How do you plan for Ramadan and other peaks? Look for a timeline, not a last-minute push.
  4. How do you report? Reports should connect spend to leads or sales and give next steps.
  5. Who owns the accounts? You should.
  6. What do you not promise? No honest agency guarantees a fixed return on ad spend.
