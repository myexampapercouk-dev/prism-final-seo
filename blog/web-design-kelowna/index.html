Web Design for Kelowna Businesses: How Remote Website Projects Work

Looking for web design in Kelowna? How remote website projects work for Okanagan businesses, from planning and platforms to seasonal demand, timings and costs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Web design for a Kelowna business should produce a fast, clear website that explains what you do, works well on phones and makes it easy for locals and visitors to the Okanagan to book, buy or enquire.
  • Many Kelowna businesses balance year-round local customers with seasonal visitors, so the website needs to handle both audiences and change easily through the year.
  • Our small websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000 and take three to five weeks, with Canadian dollar quotes available on request.

Web design for a Kelowna business should give you a fast, clear website that explains what you do, works well on phones, is easy for search engines to understand and makes it simple for people in Kelowna, across the Okanagan and visiting from elsewhere to book, buy or get in touch. Because planning, design, building and launch all happen online, a website project can be delivered remotely. Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs websites for Canadian businesses online; we have no office in Kelowna or anywhere else in Canada.

This guide explains what a good website needs for a Kelowna business, how to choose a platform, how to plan for seasonal and local customers, what our projects cost and what to ask before choosing a web designer.

What good web design in Kelowna should include

Kelowna's economy includes tourism and hospitality, wineries and agriculture, construction and real estate, healthcare, education, technology and a wide range of trades, clinics, shops, restaurants and professional firms. Some businesses serve residents all year, while others depend heavily on summer and winter visitors. Whatever your sector, a good website should include:

  • A clear message on every key page: what you do, who you serve and how to get in touch.
  • Mobile-friendly design, because many visitors, especially tourists, arrive on phones while they are out and about.
  • Fast loading, with optimised images and lean code, even on photo-heavy pages.
  • Service or product pages that explain each offer in enough detail for customers and search engines.
  • Simple booking and contact options, including click-to-call, short forms, maps and clear opening hours.
  • Accessibility, so people with disabilities can use the site. Our website accessibility guide covers the principles.
  • Search foundations such as page titles, headings, structured data and a sensible site structure.

Our website and ecommerce development service covers all of these, from first plan to launch.

Planning for locals and seasonal visitors

Kelowna is the largest city in the Okanagan, and many businesses there also serve West Kelowna, Lake Country, Peachland, Vernon, Penticton and the wider valley. On top of that, visitors arrive for lake summers, wine touring, golf and winter sports. A winery, tour operator or restaurant may need to speak to someone planning a trip from Vancouver or Calgary as well as to a resident who drops in every week.

That has practical effects on the website. Seasonal information, such as opening hours, events, menus or availability, should be easy for you to update without a developer. Pages for visitors should answer practical questions such as how to get there, whether booking is needed and what to expect. Pages for residents should make repeat visits and regular services easy to arrange. A good structure lets both audiences find what they need quickly.

Your service area also needs honest planning. A strong page for each core service, supported by a small number of area pages where you genuinely work and have something specific to say, usually performs better than many near-identical pages that only change the town name. Choosing a platform: WordPress, Shopify or custom

The right platform depends on what your website needs to do and who will update it. WordPress suits most service, hospitality and tourism businesses because it is flexible, widely supported and easy to edit once set up well, and it connects with most booking tools. Our guide to WordPress for Canadian businesses explains the options.

If you sell products online, Shopify is usually the better choice, handling payments, inventory, shipping and taxes in one place. Our guide to Shopify in Canada covers that route. Businesses selling alcohol, such as wineries, should check the provincial rules on online and direct-to-consumer sales with the relevant authorities before planning an online shop, as these rules are specific and can change.

A lean custom build can make sense for very simple sites that rarely change, or where speed is a priority. Whatever the platform, you should own the domain, hosting account and all content, and be able to move to another provider if you wish.

Pages that turn visitors into bookings and enquiries

A good-looking website is only useful if it brings in work. Every important page should make the next step obvious: a booking button for a tour or tasting, a quote form for a contractor, an appointment link for a clinic. Photography matters a great deal in the Okanagan, but it should support the message rather than slow the page down, so we size and compress images carefully.

If you plan to advertise during peak season, dedicated landing pages built around a single offer usually convert better than sending people to the homepage. Our guide to landing page design explains what to include. We also set up analytics and conversion tracking at launch, so you can see which pages and channels bring bookings.

Trust signals help too. Genuine reviews, photos of real places and work, clear cancellation or booking terms and details of the areas you serve all help visitors feel confident. We plan space for these during design, and we never invent reviews or examples on your behalf.

Accessibility, privacy and French content

British Columbia has its own accessibility law, the Accessible British Columbia Act, which sets out a framework for identifying and removing barriers. What it requires of your organisation can depend on your sector and type, and the requirements continue to develop through regulations, so check the official Government of British Columbia guidance. Building an accessible website is good practice for every visitor regardless.

If your website collects personal information through contact or booking forms, privacy law applies. In British Columbia, many private-sector organisations are covered by the province's own Personal Information Protection Act, while the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) applies in other situations, such as some cross-border activity. Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) applies to marketing email such as newsletters. If you serve customers in Quebec, that province's privacy law, often called Law 25, may add requirements. These notes are general information rather than legal advice, so refer to official guidance from the relevant regulators.

If you want to reach French-speaking visitors, separate French pages work better than automatic translation. We write in English; French content is written with your team or a professional translator, and we set up the site so each language version is indexed correctly.

How a remote web design project works

The UK is usually eight hours ahead of Kelowna, with a short period each spring and autumn when the gap changes because the clocks move on different dates. We agree a regular call slot in the late UK afternoon, which falls in the Kelowna morning, and share designs and progress online so you can review them whenever suits you.

  1. Discovery. We agree goals, pages, features, platform and the customers you want to reach, including seasonal visitors.
  2. Structure and content plan. We map the pages and agree who provides text, images and seasonal details.
  3. Design. We design key pages for your feedback before building the rest.
  4. Build. We develop the site on a staging version you can review at any time.
  5. Testing and launch. We test on phones and desktops, check forms, booking links and tracking, and launch.

We recommend planning launches away from your busiest weeks, so you have time to review the site and learn how to update it before peak demand. Businesses comparing approaches elsewhere in the province may find our guides to web design in Vancouver and web design in Victoria useful.

Questions to ask any web designer

  • Which platform do you recommend for us, and why?
  • Will we own the domain, hosting and all content?
  • How easily can we update seasonal hours, events and offers ourselves?
  • How will the site serve both residents and visitors?
  • How do you approach accessibility and page speed?
  • What support is available after launch?

FAQs

How do I choose a web design company in Kelowna?

Choose a designer who asks about your customers and seasons, recommends a platform for clear reasons, builds with speed, accessibility and search in mind, and leaves you owning everything.

Can a UK studio design a website for a Kelowna business?

Yes. Website projects are planned, designed and built online, so location is not a barrier. Prism has no Canadian office; we work remotely and schedule calls in the Kelowna morning.

How much does web design cost with Prism?

Small websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000, and ecommerce websites £1,500 to £3,500. We can quote in Canadian dollars on request.

How long does a new website take?

A small website typically takes three to five weeks, and an ecommerce website five to twelve weeks, depending on the number of pages, products and features.

When should a seasonal Kelowna business launch a new website?

Ideally a few weeks before your busy season, so you can review the site, learn to update it and check bookings and tracking before demand peaks.

Yes. We can connect most established booking tools to a WordPress site, and we test the booking journey on phones and desktops before launch.
