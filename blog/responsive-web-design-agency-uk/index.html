Choosing a Responsive Web Design Agency in the UK

A responsive web design agency builds sites that work on every screen. See what UK businesses should expect, how we test layouts, costs and questions to ask.

  • A responsive web design agency designs one flexible site that adapts its layout, images and navigation to phones, tablets, laptops and large screens.
  • Prism Works Studio builds responsive business websites from £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites from £1,500 to £3,500, usually in 3 to 5 weeks for a small site.
  • Ask any agency how it designs for phones first, which real devices it tests on and how it keeps pages fast on mobile data.

A responsive web design agency designs and builds a single website that adapts cleanly to every screen size, from a small phone to a wide desktop monitor, without a separate mobile version. For UK businesses, hiring a responsive web design agency means getting one site that reads well, loads quickly and converts on whatever device your customers happen to use.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely with businesses across the country. Every site we design is responsive by default, because that is now simply how good websites are built. This guide explains what responsive design really involves, what a good agency does differently, what it costs and how to judge the work before you sign off.

What responsive web design actually means

Responsive design uses flexible grids, flexible images and rules that change the layout at certain widths. A three-column services section on a laptop might become a single column on a phone. A wide navigation bar becomes a menu button. Tables, forms and image galleries are rearranged so nothing needs pinching or sideways scrolling.

The alternative approaches that were common years ago, such as a separate mobile site on its own subdomain, are now rarely a good idea. They split your content, complicate search, and double the maintenance. Google indexes the mobile version of your pages first, so a responsive site that treats phones as a first-class experience is also the safest option for search visibility. Our wider guide to mobile website design goes deeper on the phone experience itself.

What a responsive web design agency should do differently

Almost every theme on the market claims to be responsive. The difference between a theme that technically resizes and a site that genuinely works on a phone comes down to design decisions. A good responsive web design agency will:

  • Design mobile first. Start with the smallest screen, decide what matters most, then add space and detail for larger screens. This keeps the important content and calls to action near the top on phones.
  • Plan content, not just layout. Long blocks of text, wide tables and large hero images all need a plan for small screens, not just a narrower box.
  • Size tap targets properly. Buttons, links and form fields need enough space for a thumb, with clear spacing between them.
  • Serve sensible images. Phones should not download desktop-sized photos. Modern image formats and responsive image sizes keep pages light.
  • Test on real devices. Browser resizing is a start, but real phones and tablets reveal problems with keyboards, sticky headers and touch gestures.
  • Keep accessibility in view. Text that can be zoomed, good contrast and logical reading order matter on every screen. Our guide to website accessibility in the UK covers the basics.

Responsive design and speed

A layout that rearranges perfectly is still a poor experience if it takes too long to load on a train or in a car park. Speed is part of responsive design, not a separate task. We keep themes lean, avoid heavy sliders and unnecessary scripts, compress and resize images, and limit third-party tools that slow pages down.

Google measures real-world page experience through Core Web Vitals, which look at loading, responsiveness to taps and visual stability. Our guide to Core Web Vitals for UK sites explains each measure, and our website speed optimisation guide covers the fixes we use most often on existing sites.

Platforms we use for responsive builds

We build mostly on WordPress for service businesses and content-heavy sites, and on Shopify for online shops. Both can produce excellent responsive sites, but both can also produce slow, cluttered ones if a bloated theme or too many plugins and apps are added. The platform matters less than the decisions made on top of it.

  • WordPress suits service businesses, professional firms and sites with lots of articles or case-study style pages. See our guide to choosing a WordPress agency.

Redesigning an older site that is not responsive

If your current site was built some years ago and looks cramped or broken on phones, a redesign is usually better value than patching. Patching often means adding mobile rules on top of an old layout, which leaves you with code that is harder to maintain and still not quite right.

The main risk in a redesign is losing search rankings you have already earned. We map old URLs to new ones, keep important content, set up redirects and check indexing after launch. Our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings walks through that process step by step.

Questions to ask a responsive web design agency

Before you choose an agency, ask questions that reveal how they actually work rather than whether they say the word responsive:

  1. Do you design the phone layout first, or adapt the desktop design afterwards?
  2. Which real devices and browsers do you test on before launch?
  3. How do you handle images, video embeds and tables on small screens?
  4. What speed checks do you run, and what do you do if the results are poor?
  5. Is responsive design included in the price, or charged separately?
  6. Who owns the site, domain and content once it is finished?
  7. What support do you offer after launch if something displays incorrectly on a new device?

Clear, specific answers are a good sign. Vague promises that everything is mobile friendly are not enough.

FAQs

What does a responsive web design agency do?

A responsive web design agency plans, designs and builds a website that adjusts its layout, images and navigation to every screen size. The aim is a site that is easy to read and use on phones, tablets and desktops without separate versions.

Is responsive design the same as mobile-friendly?

They overlap but are not identical. A site can pass a basic mobile-friendly check while still being awkward on phones. Responsive design done well considers content order, tap targets, speed and forms as well as layout.

How much does a responsive website cost in the UK?

At Prism Works Studio, a small business website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and an ecommerce site £1,500 to £3,500. Responsive design is included in those prices rather than charged as an extra.

Does responsive design help SEO?

Yes. Google indexes the mobile version of pages first, so a site that works well on phones gives search engines and visitors the same good experience. Speed and stability on mobile also feed into page experience signals.

Can you make my existing website responsive?

Sometimes small fixes are enough, but older sites usually need a redesign to work properly on phones. We review the current site first and explain which option gives better value.

Do you work with businesses outside London?

Yes. We are a UK-based studio that works remotely, so we work with businesses anywhere in the UK using video calls and a shared staging site.
