Website Redesign Agency UK: What They Do and How to Choose One

What a website redesign agency does, when a redesign is the right call, how to compare UK providers and the questions to ask to protect your search rankings.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A website redesign agency rebuilds how your site looks and works, and a good one also protects the search visibility and content you have already earned.
  • Start by naming the business problem, because some problems can be solved with targeted fixes instead of a full redesign.
  • Compare providers on how they handle migration, content, ownership and post-launch support, as well as on the look of their design work.

A website redesign agency improves or rebuilds an existing website so that it works better for your customers and your business. That normally means changes to structure, design, content, mobile experience and speed. A good provider also protects what your current site already does well, particularly its search rankings. When comparing UK agencies, look beyond attractive designs and ask how each one will find the real problem, handle the move and support you after launch.

This guide helps you decide whether you need a redesign and how to choose a provider. If you are trying to set a budget, read our guide to website redesign costs in the UK.

What a website redesign agency does

Scope varies between providers, so ask each one to confirm what is included. A complete redesign will usually involve the following.

  • Review of the current site: looking at analytics, search data, customer journeys and known problems before anything is designed.
  • Structure planning: deciding which pages to keep, merge, improve or remove, and how visitors will find them.
  • Design: layouts for key pages, with mobile use considered from the start.
  • Content work: updating, rewriting or moving your words, images and documents.
  • Development: building the site on a suitable platform, including forms, integrations and any ecommerce functions.
  • Search preservation: mapping old addresses to new ones, setting redirects and carrying over important page details.
  • Testing and launch: checking devices, forms, tracking, payments and speed before and after going live.

When you need a redesign and when you do not

A redesign is a substantial project, and it is not the answer to every website problem. Match the work to the problem you actually have.

ProblemLikely starting point
Site looks dated and undermines trustRedesign, or a design refresh if the structure is sound
Hard to use on a phoneRedesign if the underlying build cannot adapt
Team cannot update content easilyA new platform or an improved editing setup
Pages load slowlyA speed review first, as a rebuild may not be needed
Traffic is good but enquiries are lowConversion review of key pages and messages
Few people find the siteSEO work first, since a new design alone will not solve this
Your business or positioning has changedBrand and content work, usually followed by a redesign

A provider who asks about the problem before suggesting a solution is more useful than one who recommends a full rebuild immediately. If only one or two areas are weak, targeted work may give you what you need for less disruption.

The biggest risk: losing search visibility

Redesigns can damage rankings when pages disappear, addresses change without redirects or well-performing content gets cut for the sake of a cleaner design. This is why migration planning belongs at the start of the project rather than in the final week.

Before work begins, ask the agency to explain how it will handle:

  • A record of your existing pages and which ones earn traffic or links.
  • A redirect map from every old address that matters to the most relevant new page.
  • Page titles, descriptions, headings and structured data.
  • Internal links and navigation.
  • Keeping the test site out of search results, and removing that block at launch.
  • Checking Search Console and analytics after launch.

For the detail, read our guides to redesigning a website without losing rankings and our SEO migration checklist. Even a careful move can bring some short-term movement in rankings, so be wary of anyone who promises that nothing will change.

How to compare redesign agencies

Three proposals can look similar and describe quite different projects. These points help reveal the differences.

  • Diagnosis: have they looked at your data, or only at how your site looks?
  • Relevant work: can they show projects of similar complexity, and explain the reasoning behind them, not just the visuals?
  • Platform advice: do they recommend a platform that suits your needs, or the only one they work with?
  • Content responsibility: who writes, edits and moves content? This is a common source of delay and unexpected cost.
  • SEO involvement: is search planned into the project, or added at the end?
  • Accessibility: how do they approach keyboard use, colour contrast, headings and form labels?
  • Ownership: will you own the design, content, code and accounts?
  • Aftercare: what support exists after launch, and how much does it cost?

Also consider who will do the work day to day. Ask who your main contact is and whether any part of the project will be passed to another company.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. What do you think is holding our current site back, and what evidence did you use?
  2. What is included and excluded, particularly content, images, redirects and integrations?
  3. How many rounds of design changes are included, and how are further changes charged?
  4. What do you need from us, and what happens if our input is late?
  5. How will you protect our existing search visibility?
  6. How will tracking be checked so that we can compare before and after?
  7. Who will own the domain, hosting, code and analytics accounts?
  8. What are the ongoing costs after launch, including hosting, licences and updates?

What to prepare before you speak to agencies

A short brief will help you compare like with like. It does not need to be long, but it should cover:

  • The business reason for the redesign and what you want to improve.
  • Your most important customers and the actions you want them to take.
  • Access to analytics and Search Console, or recent performance figures.
  • Pages you know must stay, such as those that bring in enquiries.
  • The systems the site must connect to, including booking, CRM or stock tools.
  • Your budget range and any fixed business dates.
  • Examples of sites you like, with a sentence on why you like them.
  • Who will make decisions on your side.

Sharing a budget range can feel uncomfortable, but it helps an agency tell you what is realistic rather than proposing something you cannot use. Keep the brief short enough that every agency will actually read it.

Warning signs

  • A price given with no questions about your site, content or goals.
  • No mention of redirects or search visibility.
  • Vague answers on who owns the finished website.
  • Promises of specific rankings or sales after launch.
  • A heavy focus on visual effects with little attention to speed or mobile use.
  • An editing setup your team will not be able to use without paying for every small change.

Redesign agency, freelancer or in-house?

A freelancer can suit a smaller, well-defined project, and an in-house team can work well when you already have the skills and the time. An agency is often worth considering when the project needs several disciplines together, such as strategy, design, development, content and SEO. Whichever route you choose, make sure someone is clearly responsible for the migration and for what happens after launch, because problems often appear in the gaps between roles.

FAQs

Will a website redesign hurt my rankings?

It can if it is handled poorly. Careful redirect mapping, preserving useful content and checking the site after launch all reduce the risk, although some short-term movement is still possible.

Can I keep my existing content?

Usually, and pages that perform well should generally keep their useful content. It is still worth reviewing everything, as a redesign is a good moment to improve weak pages and remove outdated ones.

Should I change platform during a redesign?

Only if there is a clear reason, such as limits on functionality, cost or editing. Changing platform adds work and risk, so ask the agency to explain the benefit for your business specifically.

Who should write the new content?

Whoever is best placed to do it well and on time. Your team knows the business, while a copywriter can make it clearer. Decide this at the start, as late content is a common cause of delay.

Do I need a redesign to improve conversions?

Not always. Clearer messaging, stronger calls to action, simpler forms or faster pages may help without a rebuild. A review of your key pages should show whether targeted changes are enough.

What happens after launch?

Someone should check redirects, forms, tracking and Search Console, and fix any issues found. Longer term, plan for updates, security, backups and continuing improvements based on how people use the site.

Next steps

Write one sentence describing what your current website is stopping your business from doing. Then gather your analytics and a list of the pages that matter most.
