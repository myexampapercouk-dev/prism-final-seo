Small Business Website Checklist: What to Sort Before, During and After Launch

A practical small business website checklist for UK owners: domain and ownership, pages, legal basics, tracking, speed, security and what to do after launch.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Make sure the business owns its domain, hosting account and content from day one.
  • A short list of essential pages, done well, beats a large site full of thin content.
  • Check forms, tracking and mobile layout before launch, then keep reviewing after.

Launching a small business website involves more moving parts than most owners expect. Domains, emails, legal pages, forms, analytics and search listings all need attention, and the small things are often the ones that cause trouble later. This checklist is designed to be worked through, whether you are building the site yourself or with an agency.

1. Ownership and accounts

  • Register the domain in the business's name, using an email address the business controls
  • Keep logins for the domain, hosting and website platform somewhere secure and shared with at least one other trusted person
  • Confirm in writing that you own the design, content and images once the project is paid for
  • Set up business email on your own domain rather than a free webmail address
  • Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it is offered

Ownership problems are a common source of headaches for small businesses, usually when a previous supplier or former employee holds the only login.

2. Essential pages

PageWhat it needs to cover
HomeWhat you do, who for, where, and the main next step
Services or productsA clear page for each main offer rather than one long list
AboutWho runs the business and why customers can trust it
ContactPhone, email, form and, if relevant, address and opening hours
Reviews or examplesGenuine feedback or work you have permission to show

Write each page around what the customer needs to know, with a clear headline and one obvious next step. Generic phrases such as "quality service at competitive prices" tell visitors nothing; specific details about what you do, where and for whom tell them a lot.

3. Legal and policy basics

Requirements vary by business, so treat this as a prompt to check rather than a definitive list, and take professional advice where you are unsure. This is not legal advice.

  • A privacy policy explaining what personal data you collect and why
  • A cookie notice and consent approach suitable for the cookies and tracking you use
  • Company details where required, such as registered name, number and office for limited companies
  • Terms of sale, delivery and returns information if you sell online
  • Any wording your regulator or professional body requires

4. Design and usability

  • Test every page on a phone as well as a desktop
  • Make phone numbers tappable and contact routes easy to find
  • Use readable text sizes and strong colour contrast
  • Give meaningful images descriptive alt text so screen reader users are not left out
  • Check you can move through the site and complete forms using only a keyboard
  • Remove placeholder text, demo content and lorem ipsum

5. Forms and enquiries

  • Submit every form yourself and confirm the message arrives in the right inbox
  • Check the confirmation message tells people what happens next
  • Add spam protection that does not make the form hard to use
  • Ask only for the information you genuinely need

6. Search basics

  • Write a unique title and meta description for each important page
  • Use one clear main heading per page
  • Make sure the site is not blocking search engines, a surprisingly common leftover from development
  • Submit a sitemap in Google Search Console
  • Set up or claim your Google Business Profile if you serve customers locally

Our guide to SEO for small businesses goes further.

7. Tracking

  • Install analytics and check it is recording visits
  • Track enquiries, calls or sales as conversions, not just page views
  • Make sure tracking respects the consent choices visitors make

Our GA4 setup guide walks through this.

8. Speed and security

  • Compress images before uploading them
  • Avoid installing plugins or apps you do not need
  • Check the site loads over HTTPS with no security warnings
  • Confirm backups are running and you know how to restore one

See our speed optimisation guide for more detail.

9. Replacing an old site

If the new site replaces an existing one, list the old URLs and redirect each to its closest new equivalent. Skipping this can lose search visibility the old site had built up. Our SEO migration checklist covers it properly.

10. After launch

  • Check Search Console for crawl errors in the first few weeks
  • Keep software, themes and plugins updated
  • Review your key pages every few months and keep them current
  • Ask happy customers for reviews
  • Look at which pages bring enquiries and improve the ones that do not

Before you start: the planning checklist

Much of what goes wrong at launch is decided weeks earlier. Before any design work begins, it helps to have answers to a few planning questions written down:

  • What is the single most important thing you want visitors to do: call, book, buy or fill in a form?
  • Who are your main types of customer, and what do they need to know before choosing you?
  • Which areas do you serve, and do you want work from further afield?
  • Who will write the content, and when will it be ready?
  • Who will update the site after launch, and how comfortable are they with technology?
  • What photos do you have, and do you have the rights to use them?
  • What is your realistic budget for the build and for ongoing running costs?

Content is the item most likely to hold up a launch. Designers can build templates quickly, but pages cannot go live without words and images. Starting on copy early, even as rough notes, keeps a project moving.

Common small business website mistakes

  • Trying to say everything on the home page. Route people to dedicated pages instead.
  • Hiding prices or next steps. If you cannot list prices, explain how pricing works and how to get a quote.
  • Using stock photos that look like stock photos. Real pictures of you, your premises or your work usually build more trust.
  • Letting the site go stale. An events page from two years ago or a departed staff member signals neglect.
  • Relying on a single person's login. If they leave or become unavailable, you can be locked out of your own site.

A quick content check for each page

Before any page goes live, ask: does the headline say what this page is about? Is there a clear next step? Are names, prices, opening hours and contact details correct? Would a customer who has never heard of you understand it? If a page fails any of these, fix it now; it rarely gets fixed after launch. It also helps to have someone outside the business read the key pages, because owners often miss gaps that are obvious to a newcomer.

Launch day itself

Pick a quiet time for launch rather than your busiest trading day, and have someone available to fix problems. Once the site is live, click through every page, submit each form again, check analytics is recording and search the site on a phone. Let existing customers know about the new site if it offers something useful to them, and update links on your social profiles, email signatures and directory listings.

FAQs

How many pages does a small business website need?

There is no fixed number. Many start with a home page, a page per main service, an about page and a contact page, then add more as they have genuinely useful things to say.

Who should own my domain name?

Your business. Register it in the business's name with an email address you control, even if a supplier manages it for you.

Do I need a privacy policy?

If your site collects personal data, such as through a contact form or analytics, you are likely to need one. Requirements depend on your circumstances, so take advice if unsure. This is not legal advice.

What is the most commonly missed launch task?

Testing forms and checking the site is not still blocking search engines are two frequent ones, along with forgetting redirects when replacing an old site.

Can I launch with a small site and grow it later?

Yes. A focused site done well is a sound starting point, and adding pages over time is normal.
