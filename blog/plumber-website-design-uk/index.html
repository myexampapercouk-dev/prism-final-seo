Plumber Website Design: A Site for Leaks, Boilers and Bathroom Jobs

Plumber website design for UK plumbing and heating firms: emergency leak pages, boiler and heating services, Gas Safe details, quote forms and areas covered.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • A plumbing website usually serves three very different jobs at once: water emergencies, heating and boiler work, and planned installs like bathrooms.
  • Gas work in the UK must be carried out by someone on the Gas Safe Register, so only promote boiler and gas services you are legally able to do and describe registration accurately.
  • Seasonal demand for heating work means your site should make it easy to update messaging and availability without a rebuild.

Plumbing enquiries rarely arrive in a calm state. A burst pipe under the sink, a boiler that has stopped on the first cold morning, a toilet that will not stop running: these are the moments when people search on their phone and call the first firm that looks capable. At the same time, plumbers also want the slower, better-planned jobs, such as a new bathroom, a cylinder replacement or a full heating system. The website has to handle a panicked homeowner and a careful planner without confusing either of them.

This guide covers the website itself: structure, pages, forms and the details that matter for plumbing and heating businesses in the UK. For getting found beyond your own site, such as reviews, directories and local advertising, see our guide to tradesman marketing in the UK.

Three types of plumbing visitor

Thinking about who lands on the site is a more useful starting point than thinking about how it looks.

  • The water emergency. Water is coming through a ceiling or a pipe has burst. This person wants a number, an idea of whether you cover their postcode and, ideally, a quick reminder of how to turn off their stop tap while they wait.
  • The heating problem. No hot water, no heating, a boiler showing a fault code. Often urgent, but less frantic than a flood, and the visitor will usually check whether you work on boilers and gas appliances at all.
  • The planned project. Bathroom refits, radiator moves, a new heating system, outside taps. These visitors compare firms, look at photos and want to request a quote at a convenient time.

Most of the decisions below follow from serving these three groups clearly.

Handling emergency call-outs properly

If you take emergency work, the site should make it obvious within a second on a phone screen. A sticky tap-to-call button, clear hours (for example whether you work evenings or weekends) and a plain statement of the areas you cover for urgent jobs all help. If emergency rates differ from normal rates, it is fairer to say that call-out charges apply and invite people to ask, rather than leaving it until you arrive.

If you do not take emergency work, say so. A polite line such as "we focus on planned plumbing and heating work and do not offer an out-of-hours service" saves you calls you cannot take and saves the customer time.

A short, accurate "while you wait" box on your emergency page can be genuinely helpful: how to find and turn off the stop tap, switching off electrics if water is near sockets, and, if someone smells gas, leaving the property and calling the national gas emergency number rather than a plumber. Keep this brief and factual, and avoid giving advice that could put someone at risk.

Boiler and heating pages, and Gas Safe registration

Heating work is where plumbing sites most often need care. In the UK, anyone working on gas appliances such as boilers, gas fires and cookers must be on the Gas Safe Register. Many plumbers are, and some are not; some firms have one registered engineer while others do not do gas work at all.

Your website should reflect your actual position:

  • If you are registered, you can say so and explain what you are registered to work on. Customers can check registration with the Gas Safe Register themselves, and some will. Make sure what you claim matches your registration and the categories of work it covers.
  • If you do not do gas work, be clear about what you do cover, for example unvented systems, electric heating or wet systems excluding the boiler, and avoid wording that implies otherwise.
  • Only use scheme logos you are entitled to use, and follow the scheme's rules on displaying them. Check current guidance rather than copying wording from other sites.

Separate pages for boiler servicing, boiler repairs, boiler installation (if you offer it), radiators and power flushing let each page explain the job properly. A boiler servicing page might cover what a service involves in general terms, why manufacturers often recommend annual servicing for warranty purposes, and how to book. A boiler installation page might explain how you approach surveys and quotes. If you mention manufacturer warranties or accreditations, only do so where you hold them and describe them accurately.

If you work for landlords, a dedicated page on landlord gas safety checks can be useful, since landlords have legal duties around gas safety. Describe the service rather than summarising the law in detail, and point landlords to official guidance for their obligations.

Pages that suit a plumbing business

  • Home. What you do, where, whether you take emergency work, a phone number and a quote button.
  • Emergency plumbing. Only if you offer it, with hours, areas and the "while you wait" box.
  • Heating and boilers. A hub page linking to individual boiler and heating services.
  • Bathrooms. If you fit them, a page with real photos, how you handle tiling and electrics (yourself or with others) and how quotes work.
  • General plumbing. Leaks, taps, toilets, outside taps, water pressure, appliance plumbing.
  • Areas covered. Towns and postcodes, ideally with any differences between emergency and planned coverage.
  • About and registrations. Who you are, insurance and registrations you hold.
  • Contact or quote. Form, phone, email and typical response times.

Bathroom pages work differently

A bathroom refit is a considered purchase, and the page should feel more like a portfolio than an emergency service. Photos of finished bathrooms you fitted, short notes on each project and an explanation of how you work from first visit to handover help people imagine the result. If you subcontract tiling or electrical work, it is honest and reassuring to say who handles what.

Before and after photos are powerful here, but only use pictures of your own work, and ask customers before showing photos of their home.

Quote forms that save you wasted visits

A good plumbing quote form gathers the information you need without feeling like paperwork. Useful fields might include:

  • Type of job (leak, boiler, bathroom, radiators, other).
  • Postcode, so you can check coverage.
  • For boiler issues, the make and model if known and any fault code shown.
  • Whether the property is owned, rented or managed.
  • A photo upload, which can be very useful for leaks, tap types and existing pipework.
  • Preferred contact method and times.

Keep required fields to a minimum. Our guide to website contact forms covers this in more detail. If you collect photos and personal details, make sure your privacy notice explains how you use and store them.

Seasonal messaging

Heating work tends to rise when the weather turns cold, and plumbers often find their diaries fill up. A site that lets you update a banner or availability note yourself, for example "boiler servicing slots available for next month" or "we are currently prioritising no-heat calls", lets you manage demand honestly. Platforms like WordPress, Squarespace and Wix all make this kind of edit straightforward if the site is set up with it in mind.

The same goes for summer, when you might want to push bathroom installations or boiler replacements while heating demand is lower. Being able to change the homepage emphasis without calling a developer is worth planning for at the build stage.

Local search and areas covered

Plumbing is local, and people often search with a town name or "near me". A clear areas covered page, consistent business details across your site and your Google Business Profile, and service pages that naturally mention where you work all help. Our guides to local SEO and Google Business Profile go into this further.

Be cautious with dozens of near-identical town pages. If you create location pages, each should have something genuinely specific to say, such as jobs you have done there or how you cover that area.

Speed and mobile layout

Emergency searches happen on phones, often with poor signal in a basement or loft. A lightweight site that loads quickly and keeps the phone number visible without scrolling matters more than animations or large videos. See our guide to website speed optimisation for practical steps.

Trust details that matter to plumbing customers

  • Your real name or business name, and a landline or mobile that is answered.
  • Insurance you hold and registrations, accurately described.
  • Genuine reviews, linked to where customers can see them.
  • Photos of your own van, team and work rather than stock images.
  • How you price: fixed quotes for planned work, how call-outs are charged, and whether you provide written quotes.

For pricing, it is reasonable to explain your approach without listing figures you might need to change, and to state whether prices include VAT if you are VAT registered.

FAQs

Should a plumber's website mention Gas Safe registration?

Only if you or your engineers are registered, and only for the types of gas work your registration covers. Customers can check registration themselves, so accuracy matters.

Do I need a separate emergency plumbing page?

If you offer emergency call-outs, a dedicated page helps urgent visitors find what they need quickly. If you do not offer them, say so clearly instead.

Should boiler services have their own pages?

Usually, yes. Servicing, repairs and installation attract different visitors with different questions, and separate pages let you answer each properly.

Should I list prices on my plumbing website?

Many plumbers explain how they price rather than listing exact figures, since jobs vary. If you show prices, keep them up to date and clear about VAT and call-out charges.

Is a photo upload useful on a plumbing quote form?

Often, yes. A photo of a leak, a tap or a boiler fault code can help you prepare. Make sure uploads are handled securely and covered by your privacy notice.
