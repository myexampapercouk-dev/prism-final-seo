eBay SEO for UK Sellers: How to Get Your Listings Found and Chosen

How eBay search works for UK sellers, with practical tips on titles, item specifics, photos, pricing, delivery and seller standards to help listings get found.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • eBay SEO means making listings easy for buyers and for eBay search to match, using accurate titles, complete item specifics and clear product information.
  • Findability is only part of the job, because photos, price, delivery, returns and your seller record can all affect whether a buyer chooses you.
  • Work through your best sellers first, change one thing at a time where possible, and judge results on sales and profit rather than views alone.

eBay SEO is the work of helping your listings appear in eBay search and persuading buyers to choose them. The most useful starting points are accurate titles with the words buyers really use, complete item specifics and the correct category. Clear photos, fair total pricing, reliable delivery and a strong seller record also matter. eBay does not publish a full formula for how it ranks listings, so treat the advice here as good practice rather than a guaranteed route to the top.

This guide is for UK sellers and ecommerce managers who want more from existing listings. If you also sell on your own site or other marketplaces, see our guide to ecommerce SEO agencies for the wider picture.

How eBay search differs from Google

Google tries to answer a very wide range of questions. eBay search is about finding an item to buy, so product details, availability and the buying experience play a large part. A shopper can also filter by condition, price, brand, size or delivery option, which means incomplete listings may drop out of those filtered results.

eBay's own Best Match sort aims to show results relevant to the search, and the seller's details are part of that wider picture. Factors discussed by eBay include how well the listing matches the search, its price and delivery terms, and the seller's record. The exact weighting is not public and may vary, so be sceptical of anyone claiming a secret formula.

Write titles for how buyers search

The title is the most visible text in your listing. It should say clearly what the item is, using the words a buyer would type. eBay sets a character limit, so check the current allowance and use it sensibly.

What to include

  • Brand and product name, where relevant.
  • Model or part number, particularly for spares and technology.
  • Key attributes such as size, colour, material or capacity.
  • Condition, if it would matter to the buyer and is not already obvious.
  • Compatibility details, such as what a part fits, where they are accurate.

What to avoid

  • Filler such as "wow", "look" or "must see", which uses up space and tells buyers little.
  • Unrelated brand names added to catch extra searches, which is misleading and may break eBay's rules.
  • Repeating the same words several times.
  • Stuffing the title until it is difficult to read.

Before you write, type your product into eBay's search box and note the suggestions. Look at sold listings as well, which show what has actually sold, and consider how UK buyers might describe the item. "Trainers" and "sneakers", or "hoover" and "vacuum cleaner", can be very different searches.

Complete your item specifics

Item specifics are the structured fields such as brand, size, colour and type. They feed the filters buyers use, so they are one of the most practical places to improve a listing. Fill in required and recommended fields wherever the information is accurate. Use eBay's suggested values where they fit, because they are more likely to line up with its filters.

Check your listings for these common gaps:

  • Brand left blank or spelled in several ways across listings.
  • Missing MPN, EAN or other product identifiers where they exist.
  • Sizes or dimensions written only in the description.
  • Generic values such as "other" where a precise option is available.

Accuracy matters here. A wrong size or compatibility detail may bring you a sale, followed by a return and an unhappy buyer.

Pick the right category

The category affects which filters and fields appear, and where your item can be browsed. Check where similar successful listings sit, and choose the most specific option that accurately fits. If an item seems to fit in two places, look at the available filters and what buyers would expect, or consider whether eBay's current options allow a second category and whether the cost is justified.

Help buyers decide

Photos

Buyers cannot pick up the item, so photos do much of the selling. Use sharp, well-lit images that show the item from several angles, along with labels, ports, sizes or any wear. For used items, show the actual item and photograph any flaws. Follow eBay's current image rules. Our guide to ecommerce product photography has more detail.

Description

Keep the description easy to read on a phone. Lead with what the buyer most needs to know, then cover dimensions, compatibility, condition, what is included and any limits. Avoid hiding key facts in a graphic, and make sure the description agrees with the title and the item specifics.

Price and delivery

Buyers tend to compare the total cost, not just the headline price. Check sold listings and active competitors for similar items in similar condition. Then look at your margin after fees, postage, packaging and returns. A cheaper listing that loses money is not a win. Show realistic dispatch times and offer a clear delivery choice you can reliably meet.

Returns

A clear and fair returns policy can give buyers more confidence, especially for higher-value items. Make sure your policy meets your legal obligations, as business sellers selling online have duties to consumers. Check current guidance if you are unsure.

Protect your seller record

eBay measures seller performance, including issues such as late dispatch and problems with transactions. Check your seller dashboard regularly and treat it as part of your visibility work, rather than only as customer service. These habits help:

  • Keep stock figures correct so you do not have to cancel orders.
  • Dispatch within the time you promised and upload tracking.
  • Answer buyer questions quickly and clearly.
  • Describe condition honestly to reduce avoidable returns.
  • Look for patterns in returns and problems, then fix the listing or the process behind them.

Promoted listings: when to use them

eBay offers paid promotion that can give listings extra visibility. It can be useful for items with good margins and listings that already convert, but it will not fix poor photos or an uncompetitive price. Tidy up the listing first, then include advertising fees in your margin calculations. Track whether promoted sales are profitable, instead of looking only at sales volume.

A simple way to work through your listings

  1. Export or list your current products, along with their sales and profit.
  2. Start with best sellers and high-margin items that have room to improve.
  3. Check search terms, then rewrite titles where needed.
  4. Fill in missing item specifics and confirm the categories.
  5. Improve photos and descriptions, starting with the items that matter most.
  6. Review price, delivery and returns against similar sold items.
  7. Record what you changed and when, then review traffic, conversion and profit.

Be cautious about making major changes to a listing that is selling well. Test on a small group first, where you can, and remember that seasonality and stock changes can affect results.

eBay compared with Amazon and your own site

Each channel has its own rules. Amazon is built around shared product pages and its own systems, as our guide to Amazon SEO explains, while eBay listings belong more directly to each seller. Your own store gives you more control over brand and customer relationships, but you need to bring in the traffic. Copying the same text everywhere is rarely the best approach. Keep the product facts consistent, then adapt the presentation to each channel.

FAQs

Does eBay use keywords in the description for search?

Titles and item specifics are the main places to focus. Write descriptions for buyers first and include the important details naturally, rather than loading them with extra keywords.

How can I see which search terms work?

Use eBay's search suggestions, sold listings and the traffic data available in your seller account. Compare changes over time, while allowing for season, price and stock differences.

Should I end and relist items to rank higher?

There is no reliable rule that this helps, and it can lose a listing's sales history. Improve the existing listing first, unless you have a specific reason to relist.

Do free delivery listings rank better?

Delivery terms can affect buyer choice and overall appeal, but no single delivery option guarantees a higher position. Compare the total cost and protect your margin.

Can an agency manage eBay SEO for me?

Yes, an agency can review and improve your listings, although you stay responsible for accurate product information and for meeting eBay's rules. Agree who approves changes and who handles customer service.

Next steps

Pick your five best-selling listings and check their titles, item specifics, photos and total price against similar sold items. You will soon see where the gaps are.
