Why LinkedIn Ads suits Vancouver businesses

Vancouver has a strong mix of technology, film and digital media services, clean technology, professional services, engineering, natural resources head offices, port and logistics firms, and a growing life sciences scene. A large share of these companies sell to other organisations, and their buyers are busy people who rarely search Google for a new supplier until they already have a shortlist.

LinkedIn helps you reach those buyers earlier. You can show a guide to operations directors at logistics firms around the Lower Mainland, invite finance leads at mid-sized BC companies to a webinar, or promote a consultation to founders of software start-ups. Because targeting is built on professional profile data, you spend less on people who could never buy from you.

It is not cheap per click, and it is not right for every business. If you sell to consumers, our Meta Ads agency in Vancouver guide or the Google Ads agency in Vancouver guide may be a better place to start.

Campaign types that work for BC firms

LinkedIn offers several ad formats. Most Vancouver campaigns use a small number of them well rather than everything at once.

  • Single image ads in the feed, for clear offers and simple messages.
  • Document ads, which let people preview a guide or checklist and unlock the rest with a form.
  • Carousel ads, useful for showing several services, steps or case-style examples without inventing claims.
  • Conversation and message ads, for direct invitations to events or consultations, used with care so they do not feel like spam.
  • Retargeting of website visitors and people who engaged with earlier ads, often where the strongest leads come from.

Video ads are possible on LinkedIn, but Prism does not film or edit video. If you already have suitable footage, we can use it in campaigns. For a deeper look at formats and funnels, read our guide to LinkedIn Ads for B2B lead generation.

Targeting buyers across Vancouver and the Lower Mainland

Good targeting starts with who signs off the work, not who uses it. A software firm selling to finance teams might target controllers and finance directors at companies with a set number of employees across BC. A commercial construction supplier might target project managers and procurement staff in Vancouver, Burnaby, Richmond, Surrey and the Fraser Valley.

We usually build audiences in layers:

  1. A core audience by job function, seniority and industry.
  2. A location setting matched to the areas you serve, from Metro Vancouver to all of British Columbia or Canada.
  3. Company size and, where useful, a target account list of named organisations.
  4. Exclusions for existing clients, competitors and your own staff.
  5. Retargeting audiences built from website visits and ad engagement.

We keep audiences large enough for LinkedIn to deliver, but narrow enough that every impression is a plausible buyer. The setup steps for tracking are covered in our LinkedIn Insight Tag setup guide.

LinkedIn Ads or Google Ads for Vancouver B2B?

Google Ads catches people who are already searching for a supplier. LinkedIn reaches people who match your ideal buyer but are not searching yet. Many firms use both: Google for active demand and LinkedIn to build awareness and pipeline with the right accounts.

Privacy, consent and lead handling in Canada

LinkedIn campaigns collect personal information, so your forms, privacy notice and follow-up emails need to respect Canadian rules. Federal privacy law and Canada's anti-spam legislation set expectations about consent and commercial electronic messages, and some provinces have their own privacy laws. We keep consent wording clear and avoid adding people to email lists without the right permission, but this is general information only. Please check official guidance or speak to your own adviser, and in regulated sectors such as finance or health, final approval of ads and wording rests with your compliance team.

Common mistakes we fix

  • Audiences so broad that ads reach students and job seekers instead of buyers.
  • No Insight Tag or conversion tracking, so nobody knows which ads produced leads.
  • Asking cold audiences for a sales call before offering anything useful.
  • Leads sitting in Campaign Manager for days without follow-up.
  • Running the same creative for months until performance fades.
  • Judging success on clicks or likes rather than qualified conversations.
