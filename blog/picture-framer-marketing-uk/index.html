Picture Framer Marketing in the UK: How Independent Framers Win More Bespoke Orders

Picture framer marketing in the UK works best with local SEO, clear service pages, before and after photos, trade partnerships and a simple quote process.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most framing customers search locally for a picture framer near them, so a complete Google Business Profile, reviews and clear service pages matter most.
  • Photos of finished work and simple explanations of mount, moulding and glazing choices help visitors trust you with items that matter to them.
  • Partnerships with artists, photographers, galleries and interior designers can bring steady repeat work alongside walk-in customers.

Picture framer marketing in the UK is about being easy to find when someone has a print, painting, photo or keepsake they want to protect and display. The independent framers that do well usually combine strong local search visibility, clear service pages, plenty of photos of finished work, a simple way to ask for a quote and steady relationships with artists and other local businesses.

This guide is for bespoke framing shops, framers attached to galleries, home based framers and framing workshops that also sell art or ready made frames. We look at how customers search, what your website needs, how to show your work and where partnerships, paid campaigns and email fit in.

How customers search for a picture framer

Framing is usually a considered purchase. People search for a picture framer near me or bespoke framing plus a town when they have something specific in hand: a wedding photo, a child's drawing, a limited edition print, a football shirt, a medal set or a piece of needlework. Some search for a particular service, such as box framing, conservation framing or canvas stretching. Others want advice on how to hang a gallery wall or which frame suits their home.

Many customers have not used a framer before and do not know what to expect. They wonder how much it will cost, how long it will take and whether their item will be safe. Your marketing should answer those questions early so a first time visitor feels comfortable walking in or sending a photo for a quote.

Picture framer marketing UK: service pages that answer questions

A homepage that simply says bespoke framing rarely ranks for the many different things people search for. Separate pages for your main services give you a better chance of appearing for those searches and let you explain each one properly.

Each page should include photos, a short explanation in plain language and a clear next step. Avoid jargon where you can, or explain terms such as mount, moulding, slip and glazing in a sentence. Customers trust a framer who makes the choices feel simple.

Show your work clearly

Framing is visual, so photos of finished pieces are your most persuasive content. Build a gallery organised by type of work, such as photos, sports memorabilia, original art and textiles. Before and after shots work well, showing an item as it arrived and the finished piece on the wall.

Good photography takes some care because glass reflects light. Shoot at an angle, use soft natural light and take a second photo without glazing where possible to show colours accurately. Our guide to ecommerce product photography shares simple ways to get cleaner images on a small budget. Always ask permission before showing a customer's item, especially family photos or anything personal.

Short guides can also help. Ideas include how to choose a mount colour, the difference between standard and museum glass, how to frame a jigsaw and how to hang a group of frames. These articles can rank for useful searches and show that you know your craft.

Make quotes easy

Many visitors hesitate because they do not know the cost. Framing prices vary with size, moulding, mount and glazing, so you may not want to publish fixed prices. You can still help by giving a starting point for common sizes or explaining which choices affect cost. A simple quote form that lets people upload a photo and add rough measurements removes a big barrier.

Local SEO and reviews

Most framing work comes from within a short drive, so local search matters. Keep your Google Business Profile complete with accurate categories, opening hours, services and plenty of photos of finished work. Post updates about seasonal offers, new mouldings and turnaround times before busy periods. Our local SEO guide explains how listings, reviews and website content work together.

Reviews carry real weight for a service where customers hand over something precious. Ask happy customers for a review when they collect, and reply to every review. Our guide on how to get more Google reviews covers simple ways to make asking a habit.

Partnerships and trade work

Relationships with other local businesses can bring a steady flow of work. Artists, photographers, galleries, schools, sports clubs, interior designers and hotels all need framing. Create a trade page that explains what you offer, such as volume pricing, consistent styles and installation, and reach out to people whose customers overlap with yours.

Our guides to photographer marketing and selling art prints on Shopify show how these businesses think about their own customers, which can help you shape a useful offer for them. Joint exhibitions, framing offers for local artists and displays in cafes are all simple ways to get your work seen.

Social media and paid campaigns

Instagram and Facebook suit framing because finished pieces look striking. Share short videos of a frame being joined, a mount being cut or an item being fitted, along with finished pieces on the wall. Ask permission before posting customer items and never share personal photos without consent.

Paid search can work for searches with clear intent, such as shirt framing or conservation framing plus a town. Start with a small budget, schedule ads around your opening hours and track enquiries and quote requests as conversions. Paid social can work well before Christmas, Mother's Day, Father's Day and graduation season, when framed gifts are popular. Ad spend is paid separately from any management fee.

Seasonal planning

Many framers see a rush before Christmas, when people want gifts framed in time. Publish your last order date for Christmas well in advance and share it on your website, listing, social channels and emails. Other busy times can include graduation season, wedding season and major sporting events. Use quieter months to add new gallery photos, write guides and ask for reviews.

Email and repeat customers

Framing customers often come back, especially once they trust you with one piece. With consent, send occasional emails about seasonal deadlines, new moulding ranges, local exhibitions and framing ideas. Keep marketing within UK GDPR and PECR rules and check current ICO guidance on consent. Use analytics and enquiry records to see which pages and channels bring in work, and remember that SEO typically takes three to six months to show clear results. FAQs

What is the best approach to picture framer marketing in the UK?

A complete Google Business Profile with reviews, clear service pages, a gallery of finished work, an easy quote process and relationships with local artists and businesses.

Should a picture framer publish prices online?

Fixed prices are hard because every job differs, but giving starting points for common sizes or explaining what affects cost helps visitors feel confident enough to ask for a quote.

How can a framer get more trade work?

Create a clear trade page and approach artists, photographers, galleries, interior designers, schools and hotels whose customers overlap with yours.

Is social media worth it for a framing shop?

Usually yes, because finished pieces and making videos are visual. Post regularly and always ask permission before sharing customer items.

Can a picture framer sell online?

Ready made frames, mirrors and art prints can sell well online. Bespoke work usually still needs a quote or consultation, so combine an online shop with a simple quote form.

How long does it take for SEO to work for a framer?

SEO typically takes three to six months to show clear results. A complete listing, reviews and helpful service pages are usually the quickest improvements to make.
