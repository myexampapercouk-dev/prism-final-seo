Product Reviews on Ecommerce Pages: How to Collect and Display Them Well

How to collect and display product reviews on your online shop: asking customers, page design, negative reviews, moderation, schema and UK consumer rules.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Genuine product reviews help shoppers decide and give you useful feedback, but only if they are honest and easy to find.
  • Asking at the right time, after the product has arrived and been used, tends to produce more helpful reviews.
  • Fake or selectively hidden reviews can mislead customers, and UK consumer law treats this seriously.

Online shoppers cannot pick up a product, try it on or ask a shop assistant what other people think. Product reviews fill that gap. They answer questions your description might not, reassure hesitant buyers and highlight details only real customers notice, such as whether a jumper runs small or a lamp is brighter than expected. For the shop, reviews provide feedback on products, customer service and delivery.

This guide is for UK ecommerce owners and teams who want to collect and display product reviews properly. It covers why reviews matter, how to ask for them, how to design the review section on product pages, handling negative reviews, moderation, structured data and the legal points to keep in mind. It focuses on reviews of individual products on your own site, rather than testimonials about your business as a whole.

Product reviews versus testimonials

It is worth separating two related things:

  • Product reviews are written by customers about a specific item they bought, usually with a star rating, and appear on that product's page.
  • Testimonials are comments about your business more broadly, often selected and displayed on a homepage or dedicated page.

Both can build trust, but product reviews carry particular weight because shoppers expect to see a full, unfiltered picture. Our testimonials page guide covers the other side, and our trust signals guide looks at the bigger picture.

Why product reviews matter

Reviews help shoppers in practical ways:

  • They answer questions about fit, quality, size and real-world use.
  • They reassure first-time buyers who have not used your shop before.
  • They show that real people buy and use your products.
  • They can surface issues you were not aware of, from a confusing instruction leaflet to packaging that damages easily.

A product with a handful of honest, detailed reviews, including some mixed ones, often feels more credible than one with a perfect score.

Choosing a review system

Shopify and WooCommerce both support product reviews. WooCommerce includes basic reviews in the core plugin, while Shopify stores typically use an app. Many dedicated review tools add features such as photo uploads, review request emails and syndication to other channels.

When choosing, consider:

  • How reviews display and whether you can control the design.
  • Whether reviews load quickly and do not slow your product pages.
  • Whether review content is visible to search engines.
  • Support for photos, questions and answers, and verified buyer labels.
  • Moderation tools and how they handle negative reviews.
  • Whether you can export your reviews if you change provider later.

Fewer, well-chosen tools are usually better than stacking several overlapping apps.

Asking customers for reviews

Most happy customers do not leave reviews unless asked. A well-timed request makes a big difference.

  • Time it after the product arrives. Asking before delivery makes little sense. Allow time for the customer to use the product, which varies by item.
  • Keep it simple. Link directly to a short review form, ideally pre-filled with the product.
  • Ask for specifics. Prompts such as "How was the fit?" or "What did you use it for?" encourage more useful reviews.
  • Invite photos. Customer photos can be especially helpful for fashion, homeware and gifts.
  • Follow up once. A single reminder is reasonable; repeated chasing is not.

Ask all customers, not just those you expect to be happy. Selectively inviting only satisfied customers to review can give a misleading picture. Our ecommerce email marketing guide covers post-purchase emails in more detail.

Incentives

Offering a reward for leaving a review is a grey area that needs care. If you offer any incentive, it should not depend on the review being positive, and incentivised reviews should be clearly disclosed. Some platforms and search engines have their own rules about incentivised reviews too. UK consumer protection rules on fake and misleading reviews have tightened, so take legal advice before running any incentive scheme. This guide is not legal advice.

Designing the review section

Reviews should be easy to find and easy to scan. Good practice on product pages includes:

  • A summary near the product title. Average star rating and number of reviews, linking down to the full reviews.
  • A rating breakdown. How many reviews gave each star rating, so shoppers can see the spread.
  • Sorting and filtering. Options such as most recent, highest rated, lowest rated and with photos.
  • Useful attributes. For some products, ratings for fit, comfort or quality add helpful detail.
  • Verified buyer labels. Where your system can confirm the reviewer bought the product.
  • Readable layout. Clear dates, names or initials, and comfortable text sizes on mobile.
  • Accessible star ratings. Ratings should have a text equivalent, such as "4 out of 5 stars", so screen reader users get the same information.

If a product has no reviews yet, avoid an empty, awkward section. A simple invitation to be the first to review is fine. Our product descriptions guide covers the rest of the product page.

Handling negative reviews

Negative reviews are inevitable, and handled well they can actually build trust. Shoppers often read the low ratings to see what could go wrong and how the shop responds.

  • Do not hide or delete genuine negative reviews simply because they are unfavourable.
  • Respond calmly and helpfully, acknowledging the problem and explaining what you are doing about it.
  • Take conversations about personal details offline, inviting the customer to contact you directly.
  • Learn from patterns. If several reviews mention the same issue, update the product, description or packaging.

Sometimes a negative review reveals that the product description was unclear. Fixing the description can prevent future disappointment.

Moderation

You will need a moderation policy. It is reasonable to remove reviews that are spam, offensive, contain personal information, are clearly about a different product or are fake. It is not reasonable to remove reviews simply because they are critical. Publish a short review policy explaining what you will and will not publish, and apply it consistently.

Fake reviews and UK law

Writing fake reviews, commissioning them, or presenting reviews in a misleading way can breach UK consumer protection law. The rules have been strengthened in recent years, with the Competition and Markets Authority given greater enforcement powers, and businesses are expected to take reasonable steps to prevent fake reviews and misleading presentation. Never post reviews pretending to be customers, and be careful with practices such as merging reviews across different products or suppressing negative ones. The specifics are detailed and evolving, so check current CMA guidance or take legal advice for your situation.

Reviews and SEO

Customer reviews add fresh, relevant text to product pages, often using the words real shoppers use. Product structured data can include review and rating information, which may make pages eligible for star ratings in search results, although Google decides whether to show them. Make sure structured data matches the reviews visible on the page and follows Google's guidelines; marking up reviews you do not display, or self-written reviews, can cause problems.

Check that your review system outputs content search engines can see, rather than loading it in a way that hides it. Our schema markup guide and product page SEO guide cover the technical side.

Measuring the impact

Track how many customers leave reviews after being asked, how review counts and ratings change over time, and how products with reviews perform compared with those without. Read the reviews themselves too; they are some of the most direct feedback you will get.

Common mistakes

  • Never asking customers for reviews.
  • Asking before the product has arrived.
  • Hiding or deleting genuine negative reviews.
  • Offering rewards only for positive reviews.
  • Using star graphics with no text alternative.
  • Marking up reviews in structured data that are not shown on the page.
  • Choosing a review tool that slows product pages noticeably.

FAQs

How do I get more product reviews?

Ask every customer after their product has arrived and had time to be used, with a simple link to a short form and one polite reminder.

Should I delete negative reviews?

No, not if they are genuine. Respond helpfully instead. Removing reviews only because they are critical can mislead shoppers.

Can I offer a discount for reviews?

Incentives need care: they must not depend on a positive review and should be disclosed. Take legal advice before running any scheme.

Will reviews show stars in Google?

Structured data can make pages eligible, but Google decides whether to show star ratings. Make sure markup matches visible reviews.

Does WooCommerce include product reviews?

Yes, basic reviews are built in. Extensions can add features such as photos and review request emails.

What is the difference between reviews and testimonials?

Product reviews are about specific items and appear on product pages. Testimonials are about your business generally.
