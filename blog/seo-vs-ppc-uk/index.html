SEO vs PPC: Which Should a UK Business Invest In First?

SEO builds traffic you do not pay per click for; PPC buys visibility now. Compare cost, speed and risk to decide which suits your UK business first.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • PPC can put you in front of searchers within days; SEO usually takes months but keeps working without a cost per click.
  • The right choice depends on your margins, how urgently you need enquiries and how competitive your search results are.
  • Most businesses end up using both: PPC for speed and testing, SEO for lasting visibility and lower long-term cost per lead.

SEO and PPC both put your business in front of people searching on Google, but they work in very different ways. PPC (pay-per-click advertising such as Google Ads) buys visibility straight away and stops when you stop paying, while SEO (search engine optimisation) builds organic rankings slowly that keep bringing visitors without a charge for each click.

Neither is better in every situation. The right answer depends on how quickly you need enquiries, what each customer is worth to you, how much competition you face and how long you plan to stay in the market. This guide compares the two side by side so you can decide where to put your first pound, and when it makes sense to run both.

SEO vs PPC: the short answer

If you need leads this month, have a clear offer and can measure enquiries, PPC is usually the faster route. If you can wait several months and want a source of traffic that does not rise in cost with every visitor, SEO is the stronger long-term investment. Many UK businesses start with a modest PPC budget to win early enquiries and learn which searches convert, while putting SEO foundations in place at the same time.

How SEO works and what it costs

SEO is the work of making your website the best answer for the searches your customers make. That covers technical health (can search engines crawl and understand the site?), relevance (does each page match a real query?) and authority (do other sites and sources trust you?). For local businesses it also includes your Google Business Profile, reviews and local citations.

The main costs are time and expertise. You either do the work yourself, hire a freelancer or use an agency. Our guide to how much SEO costs in the UK breaks down typical fee models. The upside is that once a page ranks, each extra visitor costs nothing directly. The downside is that results are slower, less predictable and depend on factors you do not control, such as algorithm updates and what competitors do.

SEO also feeds newer search features. Well-structured, accurate content is more likely to be quoted in AI-generated summaries, which we cover in our guide to AI Overviews and SEO.

How PPC works and what it costs

With PPC you bid to appear for chosen keywords and pay when someone clicks your ad. Google Ads is the largest platform in the UK, and Microsoft Ads covers Bing and partner sites. You set daily budgets, choose locations, schedule ads for the hours that suit you and send people to a specific landing page.

There are two costs: the ad spend paid to the platform, and the time or fee for managing the account. Click prices vary widely by industry, location and competition, so check current figures in your own account rather than relying on averages. Our guides to Google Ads costs in the UK and PPC management fees explain how to budget for both.

PPC is only as good as the page it sends people to and the tracking behind it. Without conversion tracking you cannot tell which keywords bring enquiries and which simply spend money.

When PPC is the better first move

  • You need enquiries quickly. A new business, a new location or a seasonal push cannot always wait months for rankings.
  • Your offer is time-limited. Events, sales and launches suit ads you can switch on and off.
  • Your website is new. New domains often take time to earn organic visibility, so ads bridge the gap.
  • You want to test demand. PPC shows which searches and messages lead to calls or sales before you invest in content around them.
  • Your margins can absorb click costs. High-value services and products can often pay for competitive clicks; low-margin items may struggle.

When SEO is the better first move

  • Clicks in your market are expensive. If paying for every click makes each lead unprofitable, organic traffic becomes more valuable.
  • Your customers research before buying. Longer buying journeys suit helpful guides, comparison pages and FAQs that rank over time.
  • You serve a local area. Map results and local organic listings are often within reach for a well-optimised small business; see our local SEO guide.
  • You plan to be around for years. Rankings are an asset that keeps working, while ad traffic ends the day the budget does.
  • Your budget is tight but you have time. Much early SEO work, such as fixing page titles and writing better service pages, needs effort more than money.

Comparing the cost per lead over time

A fair comparison looks at cost per enquiry or sale across a year, not just the first month. PPC tends to have a fairly steady cost per lead: if clicks cost the same and your conversion rate holds, each lead costs roughly the same in month twelve as in month one. SEO usually looks expensive at the start, because you pay for work before rankings arrive, then the cost per lead falls as organic traffic grows.

These patterns are general. Your own figures depend on your market, the quality of the work and how well your website converts. Track both channels in GA4 with consistent goals so you can compare like with like.

Why most businesses use both

SEO and PPC support each other. PPC search term reports show which exact phrases lead to enquiries, which helps decide what content to create for SEO. SEO pages that rank well can let you reduce bids on the same terms. Ads can cover competitive head terms while organic pages target longer, more specific searches. And when a ranking drops after an update, ads can protect your lead flow while you fix the cause.

Both channels also rely on the same landing pages. A clear, fast page with a strong call to action improves ad results and organic conversion together; our guide to landing page design covers what to include.

A simple way to decide

  1. Work out what a customer is worth. Know your average order or job value and your margin.
  2. Check search demand and competition. Look at who appears for your main searches today, in ads and organically.
  3. Set your timeline. Decide whether you need leads in weeks or can build over months.
  4. Fix measurement first. Set up GA4 and conversion tracking so either channel can be judged on real results.
  5. Start small and review. Run a focused PPC test, begin SEO foundations, then shift budget towards whichever brings cheaper enquiries.

FAQs

SEO vs PPC: which is better for a UK business?

Neither is better for everyone. PPC suits businesses that need enquiries quickly and can afford click costs, while SEO suits businesses that can wait for results and want lower long-term cost per lead. Many use both, with PPC for speed and SEO for lasting visibility.

Is SEO cheaper than PPC?

Over the long term SEO often costs less per lead because you do not pay for each click, but it needs upfront investment and takes longer to work. PPC has a clearer, more immediate cost that continues as long as you run ads.

How long does SEO take compared with PPC?

PPC ads can appear within days of launch once the account is approved. SEO usually takes 3-6 months to show meaningful movement, and competitive markets can take longer.

Does running Google Ads help my SEO rankings?

Google states that advertising does not directly influence organic rankings. However, ad data can show which searches convert, which helps you decide what content to create for SEO.

Should a small business start with SEO or PPC?

If you need enquiries soon, a small, well-tracked PPC campaign is often the quickest start. At the same time, fix basic SEO such as page titles, service pages and your Google Business Profile, as these cost time more than money.

Can I stop PPC once my SEO is working?

You can reduce PPC once organic rankings bring steady enquiries for the same searches, but many businesses keep some ads running for competitive terms, new offers or to protect lead flow if rankings change.
