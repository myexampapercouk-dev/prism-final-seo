Why Danish businesses choose WordPress

WordPress suits many Danish organisations because it is flexible, widely supported and easy for non-technical staff to edit. It works well for:

  • Service and B2B firms. Consultancies, engineering companies, design firms and technology businesses that publish case information, news and guides.
  • Exporters. Manufacturers and design brands selling abroad that need clear English pages alongside Danish ones.
  • Online shops. Retailers using WooCommerce to sell products while keeping content and shop in one system.
  • Hospitality and tourism. Restaurants, hotels and attractions that need booking links, menus and seasonal updates.
  • Organisations with many editors. Associations and companies where several people update different sections.

The trade-off is that WordPress needs care. Themes and plugins must be chosen carefully and kept up to date, or the site becomes slow and vulnerable. A good agency plans for that from the start.

Danish and English on one WordPress site

WordPress does not handle multiple languages on its own, so a multilingual plugin or a multisite setup is usually needed. We choose the approach based on how many languages you need, how different each version will be and who will edit them. For most Danish businesses with two languages, a well-supported multilingual plugin with language folders, such as one for Danish and one for English, is the simplest option to manage.

Each language gets its own URLs, titles and meta descriptions, with hreflang tags so search engines show the right version. Danish includes letters such as æ, ø and å, so we check fonts, forms and URLs handle them properly. Our multilingual website design guide covers the structure in more depth, including when separate sites make more sense.

Prism writes English copy in-house. For Danish pages we give your team or a native-speaking translator a clear brief for each page, then check the finished pages in the build.

WooCommerce for Danish online shops

If you sell online, WooCommerce lets you run a shop inside WordPress. For Danish retailers, the main points are:

  • Prices and VAT. Prices shown with the correct VAT and currency, set up with your accountant's guidance.
  • Payments. A payment provider that supports the methods your customers expect, which in Denmark often includes cards and MobilePay. We connect the provider you choose and test the checkout.
  • Delivery options. Clear shipping rates, collection points where your carrier offers them and honest delivery times.
  • Selling abroad. If you sell into other EU countries, the shop needs the right VAT handling and translated product pages.

Our guide to WooCommerce development covers shop builds in more detail. If your catalogue is large or you want less maintenance, Shopify may be a better fit, and we will say so during discovery.

Security, hosting and maintenance

Most WordPress problems come from outdated plugins, weak passwords and cheap hosting. We reduce the risk from the start:

  • A lean plugin list, with each plugin chosen for a clear reason and from a reliable developer.
  • Hosting with EU data centres where you prefer, daily backups and staging environments.
  • Editor roles so staff can only change what they need to.
  • Two-factor login for administrators and limits on login attempts.
  • Regular updates to WordPress, themes and plugins, tested on staging before going live.

Our guides to WordPress security and WordPress hosting explain the details, and our post on website maintenance sets out what ongoing care should include.

Consent, privacy and accessibility

Danish websites fall under the GDPR for personal data, and the EU ePrivacy rules mean analytics and marketing cookies normally need consent before they load. On WordPress, that means choosing a consent tool that genuinely blocks scripts until a visitor agrees, rather than one that only shows a banner. We configure it so analytics and advertising tags respect each choice.

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing products and services, including a wide range of online shops. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but building to WCAG AA, the level referenced by the harmonised standard EN 301 549, is good practice for any business. Our European Accessibility Act guide covers the basics. Legal wording should come from your legal adviser, who has final approval, and official guidance should be checked before launch.

Common WordPress problems we see

When Danish businesses ask us to rebuild or rescue a WordPress site, the same issues tend to appear:

  • Page builder bloat. Heavy builders and premium themes that load scripts on every page and slow mobile visits.
  • Plugin overload. Dozens of plugins, some abandoned, causing conflicts and security risks.
  • A half-finished English version. Missing pages and a switcher that sends visitors back to the home page.
  • No updates since launch. Old versions of WordPress and PHP that put the site at risk.
  • No backups. Or backups stored on the same server as the site, which do not help if the server fails.

Most of these are fixed with planning and regular care rather than a bigger budget.
