A LinkedIn Ads agency for a Swiss business should help you reach the right decision-makers by job role, industry and company size, then turn that attention into qualified leads at a cost your sales team can live with. In Switzerland that also means planning for several languages, a high-value but small audience and careful tracking.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that runs paid social and search campaigns remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Zurich, Geneva, Basel or anywhere else in Switzerland. Our paid media service works by video call, shared reports and your own ad accounts, with Switzerland one hour ahead of UK time. We write ads in English and work with your team or a translator on German, French and Italian versions.

Why LinkedIn Ads suit Swiss B2B companies

Switzerland has a strong base of financial services, insurance, pharmaceuticals and life sciences, engineering, software, commodities trading, consulting and international organisations. Many of these companies sell expensive products or services to a small number of buyers, and the decision often involves several people. LinkedIn is useful here because it lets you target by the details that matter in B2B:

  • Job function and seniority. Reach heads of procurement, finance directors or IT leaders rather than everyone in a company.
  • Industry and company size. Focus on the sectors and employee bands your sales team actually closes.
  • Named accounts. Upload a list of target companies for account-based campaigns.
  • Language and region. Separate audiences for German-speaking, French-speaking and Italian-speaking Switzerland, plus English for international teams.

Working across German, French, Italian and English

Many Swiss companies operate in English internally, but their buyers often prefer to read ads and landing pages in their own language. A campaign aimed at Zurich finance teams may work best in German, while one aimed at Geneva or Lausanne may need French. Some audiences, such as international organisations, multinationals and start-ups, respond well to English.

Prism writes ad copy and landing page briefs in English. For German, French or Italian versions we work with your team or a native-speaking translator, giving them the structure, offer and character limits for each format, then check the finished ads in the account before launch. Swiss German spelling conventions, such as not using the sharp s, are worth flagging to your translator early.

Tracking, consent and data protection

Good LinkedIn campaigns depend on knowing which ads lead to real enquiries. That usually means installing the LinkedIn Insight Tag, setting up conversion events and, where possible, feeding offline outcomes such as qualified leads back into reporting. Our LinkedIn Insight Tag setup guide walks through the steps.

Switzerland has its own data protection law, and if you target or track people in the EU, the GDPR and the EU ePrivacy rules on cookies also come into play. In practice, tracking tags and retargeting normally sit behind a consent banner, and lead gen forms need a clear privacy notice. We set up tags, consent and forms technically, but the legal wording and final approval rest with your legal adviser. For regulated sectors such as banking, insurance or healthcare, ad copy also needs sign-off from your compliance team. Requirements change, so check current official guidance.

Landing pages and offers that work for Swiss buyers

LinkedIn clicks are often expensive, so the page people land on has to work hard. Swiss B2B buyers tend to expect precise, factual information and a professional presentation. The offers that usually perform best are practical: a short guide, a benchmark checklist, a demo, a consultation or a webinar.

  • Keep the landing page in the same language as the ad.
  • State who the offer is for, what they get and what happens after they submit the form.
  • Show prices or price ranges where your sales process allows it.
  • Make the page fast on mobile, as many LinkedIn users browse on their phones.

Our guide to landing page design covers layout, copy and form design in more detail.

Common LinkedIn Ads mistakes we see

  • Audiences that are too broad or too narrow. Broad audiences waste budget; very narrow ones in a small market stop delivering.
  • One language for the whole country. A single German campaign misses French-speaking buyers and vice versa.
  • Leaving audience expansion switched on by default. This can push ads to people outside your target roles.
  • Measuring form fills only. Without feedback from sales, campaigns optimise for volume rather than quality.
  • Creative that never changes. Small audiences tire of the same ad quickly, so creative needs regular refreshing.

Fees, ad spend and timelines

Our fees are set out on our pricing page. FAQs

Are you a LinkedIn Ads agency based in Switzerland?

No. Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Switzerland. We manage campaigns remotely by video call and shared reports, one hour behind Swiss time.

Can you run LinkedIn Ads in German and French?

Yes. We plan and build campaigns for each language region. We write in English, and German, French or Italian copy comes from your team or a native-speaking translator, which we check before launch.

How much does a LinkedIn Ads agency in Switzerland cost with your studio?

Management is £350, £750 or £1,500 a month depending on scope, quoted in pounds with other currencies on request. Ad spend is paid separately to LinkedIn.

How long before LinkedIn Ads produce results?

Setup takes a few weeks, and paid results typically take three to six months to settle as campaigns gather data and your sales cycle plays out.

Do we need cookie consent for the LinkedIn Insight Tag?

Usually yes, especially where you track EU visitors. We set up the tag behind your consent banner, and your legal adviser should confirm the wording for Swiss and EU rules.

Who owns the LinkedIn ad account?

You do. Campaigns run in your own account with your payment method, and we work with access you grant and can remove at any time.
