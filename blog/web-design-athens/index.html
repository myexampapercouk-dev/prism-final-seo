Web Design for Athens Businesses: Fast, Bilingual Websites Built Remotely

Need web design in Athens? How we design and build fast Greek and English websites for Athens businesses remotely, with costs, timelines and compliance notes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

  • A good website for an Athens business loads fast on mobile, works properly in Greek and English, makes the next step obvious and has consent, privacy and accessibility basics in place.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Greece; we design and build websites remotely in English, with Greek copy from your team or a translator.

Web design for an Athens business should produce a website that loads quickly on a phone, reads naturally in Greek and, where needed, English, and makes it easy for visitors to call, book or buy. It should also have the compliance basics in place from launch, including cookie consent, a clear privacy notice and accessible design.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Athens or anywhere else in Greece. Athens is two hours ahead of the UK, which still leaves plenty of shared working hours for calls and reviews. We work in English. Greek copy is written or checked by your team or a professional translator. Our website and e-commerce development service explains how our projects run.

What Athens businesses need from a website

Athens has a broad mix of businesses: shipping and logistics firms in and around Piraeus, professional services in the city centre, a growing technology and start-up scene, and a large hospitality and tourism sector serving visitors all year round. Each needs something slightly different from its website, but the basics are the same.

  • Speed on mobile. Most visitors arrive on a phone. A slow site loses them before they read anything.
  • Clear structure. Visitors should understand what you offer and how to contact you within seconds.
  • The right languages. Greek for local customers, English for international clients and visitors, and sometimes more.
  • Easy editing. Your team should be able to update text, images and posts without calling a developer.
  • Search-ready foundations. Clean code, sensible URLs and correct language settings so the site can rank.

Designing for Greek and English

Greek uses its own alphabet, which brings a few practical design points. Not every web font includes complete Greek characters, so a font that looks good in English can fall back to a different, mismatched font on Greek pages. We choose fonts with full Greek support and test every page in both languages. Greek words and phrases are often longer than their English equivalents, so menus, buttons and headings need room to breathe.

URLs need a decision too. Some Greek sites use Greek characters in their addresses, while others use a Latin-script transliteration. Both can work, but the approach should be consistent across the site and agreed before launch, because changing it later means redirecting every page.

Each language should have its own pages, linked through language tags so search engines show the right version to each visitor. Automatic translation widgets rarely help search visibility and can produce awkward Greek. Our guide to multilingual website design explains the setup. Page copy comes from your team or a translator; we provide structures and briefs in English, then place and format the text.

Choosing a platform

Most Athens businesses are well served by one of three options:

  • WordPress. Flexible and widely supported, good for service businesses, content-heavy sites and smaller shops using WooCommerce. Our guide to WordPress website costs explains what affects price.
  • Shopify. A strong choice for online shops that want reliable hosting and checkout without managing servers. Our comparison of Shopify and WooCommerce helps you decide.
  • Lean custom builds. Useful for simple, very fast sites with few editing needs.

For shops selling to Greek customers, confirm which payment methods your chosen provider supports and set up shipping zones for the mainland and the islands, which often have different delivery times and costs.

Hotels, tourism and hospitality

Hospitality is one of the largest sectors in Athens, and hotel and restaurant websites have particular needs: strong photography, a clear route to the booking engine, practical information about location and transport, and versions in the languages your guests speak. Our guide to hotel and B&B website design covers the details, and our hospitality marketing page explains how the website fits with search and social campaigns.

Speed and accessibility

Speed affects both visitors and search rankings. We size images properly, keep plugins and scripts to a minimum and recommend hosting with servers in Europe. We test pages on mid-range phones over mobile data, not just on a fast office connection.

Accessible design means good colour contrast, clear headings, labelled forms, keyboard-friendly menus and text alternatives for images. It helps everyone, including older visitors and people using a phone in bright sunlight. Our guide to website accessibility covers the practical checks we make.

Compliance basics for Greek websites

These notes are general information only. Please follow official guidance from the relevant authorities and take advice from your own legal adviser.

  • GDPR. Contact forms, bookings, newsletters and analytics collect personal data. You need a clear privacy notice in each language, a lawful basis and sensible retention. Greece's data protection authority publishes guidance.
  • Cookie consent. Under the ePrivacy rules as applied in Greek law, non-essential cookies such as analytics and advertising tags generally need prior consent, and rejecting should be as easy as accepting. Our post on cookie banner design covers the practical side.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer products and services, including e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but accessible design helps every visitor. The expected standard is EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA. See our overview of the European Accessibility Act for websites.

How a remote web design project works

  1. Discovery call. We discuss your goals, audience, languages, content and any existing site.
  2. Sitemap and wireframes. We agree the structure and the layout of key pages.
  3. Design. We design the homepage and main templates in Greek and English for your feedback.
  4. Build on staging. We build the site on a private link you can review on your own devices.
  5. Content. Your team or translator supplies Greek copy; we place and format it.
  6. Testing and launch. We test forms, speed, devices, languages and consent, set up redirects and go live.
  7. Training. We show your team how to edit pages and agree any ongoing care.

Choosing a web design partner in Athens

Whoever you work with, these questions help you compare proposals:

  • Will the Greek and English versions have their own pages and URLs?
  • Have the chosen fonts been tested with full Greek characters?
  • Will you own the domain, hosting account and all site files?
  • How will cookie consent and accessibility be handled before launch?
  • What training is included, and what does ongoing care cost?

FAQs

What does web design in Athens cost?

With us, small business websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and online shops £1,500 to £3,500, quoted in pounds. Euro quotes are available on request, and hosting and translation are separate.

Do you have an office in Athens?

No. Prism Works Studio is UK-based and works remotely. We have no office in Athens or elsewhere in Greece, and we work with Greek clients by video call, email and shared staging links.

Can you build a website in Greek and English?

Yes. We build separate Greek and English pages with fonts that support Greek characters. We work in English, so Greek copy comes from your team or a professional translator.

How long does a website take to build?

Most small sites take three to five weeks and online shops five to twelve weeks, depending on scope and how quickly content and feedback arrive.
