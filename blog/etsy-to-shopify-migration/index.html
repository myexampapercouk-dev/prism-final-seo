Moving From Etsy to Shopify: A Guide for UK Sellers

Thinking of moving from Etsy to Shopify? What changes, how to move products and customers, keeping Etsy running, traffic, policies and SEO for UK sellers.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Etsy brings marketplace traffic; your own Shopify store brings control over branding, customer relationships and presentation, but you must drive visitors yourself.
  • Many sellers run both for a period, keeping Etsy sales going while the Shopify store builds an audience.
  • Moving products is the easy part; building traffic, writing your own policies and earning trust take the most planning.

Etsy is where many UK makers and small creative businesses start selling online. It is quick to set up, buyers already go there looking for handmade, vintage and personalised items, and the marketplace handles much of the technical side. As a shop grows, some sellers start to want more: their own brand front and centre, more control over how products are presented, a direct relationship with customers and less dependence on one marketplace's rules and fees.

Shopify is a common next step. This guide explains what changes when you move, how to plan the move, whether to keep your Etsy shop, and what it takes to build traffic to your own store. It is general guidance, and both platforms' features and fees change, so check current details.

What actually changes

The difference between Etsy and Shopify is not just the software. It is the business model.

  • Traffic. On Etsy, buyers find your listings through Etsy's search and recommendations. On Shopify, nobody arrives unless you bring them through search engines, social media, email, advertising or word of mouth.
  • Branding. Etsy shops share a common layout. On Shopify, the whole site can reflect your brand, from design to navigation to tone.
  • Customer relationship. On your own store, customers buy from you directly, and with appropriate consent you can build an email list and market to them.
  • Responsibility. You write your own policies, manage your own payment setup, handle customer service directly and are responsible for compliance with UK consumer and data protection law on your site.
  • Costs. Etsy charges listing and transaction-based fees. Shopify charges a subscription plus payment processing and any paid apps or themes. Which works out cheaper depends on your sales volume and setup, so compare current fees for your situation. See our Shopify website cost guide for the cost components.

Should you keep your Etsy shop?

Closing Etsy on the day the Shopify store launches is risky, because the new store will usually have little traffic at first. Many sellers run both channels for a period, or permanently. Etsy continues to bring in marketplace buyers while the Shopify store builds its own audience.

Running both means managing stock across two places. If you sell one-of-a-kind items, an item could sell on both before you update the other. Options include keeping some ranges exclusive to one channel, updating stock promptly by hand, or using an integration that connects the two. Check that any integration handles your variants and personalisation correctly.

Be aware of Etsy's rules about directing buyers away from the marketplace. Check Etsy's current seller policies before adding links or messages about your own website in listings or communications.

Moving your products

Product data can be exported from Etsy and imported into Shopify, either via a spreadsheet or using a migration app. However, it is rarely a straight copy, and the move is a good opportunity to improve things.

  • Variants. Etsy's variation structure does not always map neatly to Shopify's options and variants, so check each product after import.
  • Personalisation. Etsy's personalisation box needs replacing with custom fields on the Shopify product page, through theme customisation or an app, so the details are captured with each order.
  • Titles. Etsy titles are often stuffed with keywords to suit marketplace search. On your own store, clearer, more readable product names usually work better, with keywords used naturally in titles and descriptions.
  • Descriptions. Rewrite descriptions to suit your brand voice and answer buyers' questions. Avoid copying the exact same text in both places where you can. Our guide to product descriptions helps.
  • Images. Re-upload the highest-resolution versions available and consider adding more lifestyle and detail shots.
  • Collections. Group products into collections that suit browsing on your own site, rather than relying only on Etsy's shop sections.

Our guides to variants and bundles and category page design cover setup in more detail.

Reviews and customer data

Reviews are one of Etsy's strengths, and sellers naturally want to bring them across. Some review apps can import reviews, but check Etsy's terms and the app's approach, and only display genuine reviews with their original context. Never edit reviews to change their meaning or invent new ones. Our guide to product reviews covers collecting reviews on your own store.

Customer information collected through Etsy is subject to Etsy's policies and to UK data protection law. Do not assume you can add Etsy buyers to your marketing list. Check current Etsy policies and UK GDPR guidance from the Information Commissioner's Office, and build your own email list with proper consent on the new store.

Policies and legal pages

On Etsy, some aspects of the buying framework are set by the marketplace. On your own store, you need your own terms and conditions, privacy policy, cookie information, delivery information and returns policy. UK consumer law generally gives online buyers a right to cancel, with exceptions that can apply to personalised or made-to-order items. Take advice or check current guidance to make sure your policies are accurate, rather than copying another site's. Our guides to delivery and returns pages and cookie banners cover presentation.

Designing a store that earns trust

On Etsy, buyers rely partly on the marketplace's familiarity. On a new standalone store, they need other reasons to trust you. That includes a clear about page explaining who you are and how you make your products, visible contact details, straightforward policies, secure checkout and genuine reviews. Good photography and a consistent brand help too. Our guides to website trust signals and writing an about us page explain what to include.

Building traffic to your own store

This is the part that most determines whether the move is worthwhile. Common channels include:

  • Search engines. Well-structured collection and product pages, useful content and good technical foundations can help your store rank over time. See our Shopify SEO guide.
  • Existing audiences. If you have followers on social media, point them to your own store in line with each platform's rules.
  • Email. Build a list from day one with clear consent. Our ecommerce email marketing guide explains the basics.
  • Packaging inserts. For orders fulfilled through your own store, a card with your website can encourage repeat purchases. Check Etsy's rules before including such material in Etsy orders.
  • Paid advertising. Google Shopping and social ads can bring traffic quickly but need budget and careful management. See our Google Shopping ads guide.
  • Craft fairs and events. If you sell in person, Shopify POS can connect in-person sales to your online store.

Expect this to take time. A pre-launch plan helps; our guide to ecommerce pre-launch marketing covers building interest before the store opens.

Measuring whether the move is working

Set up analytics on the new store from launch so you can see where visitors come from, which products they view and where they drop out of the checkout. Compare trends over months rather than days, since a new store's traffic usually builds gradually. Our GA4 setup guide explains the basics of tracking an online store.

A sensible order of work

  • Decide whether you will run both channels and how you will handle stock.
  • Choose a domain and set up business email. See our domain and email setup guide.
  • Set up the Shopify store, theme, payments, shipping and tax settings, taking advice on VAT if relevant.
  • Import and then clean up products, rewriting titles and descriptions.
  • Write policies and legal pages.
  • Test the full purchase journey, including personalised orders.
  • Launch, then build traffic steadily while Etsy continues.

FAQs

Can I move my Etsy listings to Shopify?

Yes, listings can be exported from Etsy and imported into Shopify via spreadsheet or an app. Check variants, personalisation and images after import.

Should I close my Etsy shop when I open on Shopify?

Many sellers keep Etsy running while the new store builds traffic. Plan how you will manage stock across both.

Can I bring my Etsy reviews to Shopify?

Some review apps can import reviews. Check Etsy's terms and only display genuine reviews without altering them.

Can I email my Etsy customers about my new store?

Not automatically. Etsy's policies and UK data protection law apply, so check current rules and build your own consented list.

Is Shopify cheaper than Etsy?

It depends on your sales volume, plan, payment fees and apps. Compare current fees for both platforms based on your own figures.
