How we build a Google Ads account

A well-built account makes management easier and cheaper. Our usual structure looks like this:

  1. Conversion tracking first. We set up tracking for calls, forms, bookings and purchases through Google Ads and Google Analytics before spending anything.
  2. Keyword research. We focus on high-intent terms, such as service plus location, and build negative keyword lists to block irrelevant searches.
  3. Campaign structure. Separate campaigns for each core service, so budgets and bids match the value of each.
  4. Location targeting. We target the parishes and towns you actually serve, and use presence-based targeting so ads reach people in your area.
  5. Ad copy and assets. Clear, specific ads with call, location and sitelink assets.
  6. Landing pages. Each campaign points to a relevant page with a clear offer and an easy way to get in touch.

What to budget for Google Ads

There are two costs: the ad spend you pay Google directly, and the management fee you pay the agency. Ad spend is always paid separately to our fees and goes straight from your card to Google.

The right ad budget depends on how competitive your keywords are, how many services and areas you target, and how much a new customer is worth. Legal and home services keywords tend to cost more per click than retail or hospitality searches. Managing campaigns week by week

Paid search is never finished. Each week we:

  • Review search terms and add negatives to stop wasted spend.
  • Adjust bids and budgets towards the campaigns producing leads.
  • Test new ad copy and landing page changes.
  • Check that tracking is still firing correctly.
  • Watch for seasonal shifts, such as storm season demand or football weekends.

Each month you get a plain-English report showing spend, conversions, cost per lead and what we changed. Campaigns can bring traffic from the first day, but they usually need several weeks of data before performance settles. Broader paid and organic results typically take 3 to 6 months to mature.

Common Google Ads mistakes that waste budget

When we review existing accounts, a handful of problems account for most wasted spend. Fixing them is usually the first job in any takeover:

  • No conversion tracking, or the wrong conversions. Counting page views or button clicks as leads makes campaigns look better than they are and sends automated bidding in the wrong direction.
  • Broad keywords without negatives. A roofing company can easily pay for searches about roofing jobs, roofing courses or DIY repairs unless those terms are blocked.
  • Location settings that are too wide. Default settings can show ads to people merely interested in Baton Rouge rather than people actually there. For local services, that wastes money quickly.
  • Every ad pointing to the home page. Visitors who searched for a specific service should land on a page about that service, with a clear way to call or book.
  • Unanswered calls. Call ads only work if someone picks up. We often suggest ad schedules that match the hours your team can answer the phone.
  • Set and forget. Search behaviour, competition and costs change constantly. Accounts that are not reviewed every week drift and waste budget.

We also check for smaller issues, such as disapproved ads, missing assets, duplicate keywords competing with each other and automatically applied recommendations that were never reviewed. Google regularly suggests changes that increase spend, and not all of them suit a local business.
