PPC for SaaS Companies: A UK Guide to Trials, Demos and Competitor Terms

PPC for SaaS companies in the UK: match campaigns to trial or demo funnels, bid on competitor terms with care and use LinkedIn to reach buyers by role.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Optimise SaaS PPC to qualified trials, demos and paying customers, not just form fills, by feeding CRM data back into the ad platforms.
  • Google Search captures existing demand while LinkedIn reaches buyers by role and company, so most B2B SaaS teams need both in different proportions.
  • Competitor and alternative terms can be valuable, but use fair, accurate ad copy and avoid using trade marks in ways that breach platform policy.

PPC for SaaS companies in the UK works when every campaign is tied to a specific funnel step, either a free trial or a demo request, and when the ad platforms are optimised towards qualified pipeline rather than raw sign-ups. In practice that means Google Search for high-intent and competitor terms, LinkedIn for reaching named roles and accounts, and a tight feedback loop from your CRM.

This guide covers the paid side. For organic search, see our SaaS SEO agency guide, and for a general view of costs, our article on PPC management costs in the UK.

Trial or demo: choose the funnel before the campaign

Product-led SaaS with a self-serve free trial or freemium plan behaves very differently from sales-led SaaS where a demo comes first. Your keywords, landing pages, bids and conversion goals should follow that choice.

FactorFree trial or freemiumDemo-led
Typical buyerIndividual or small teamDepartment or company
Primary conversionSign-up, then activationDemo booked, then attended
Landing pageShort, product-focused, fast sign-upProblem-focused, proof, short form
Best signal to optimiseActivated or paying usersSales-qualified opportunities
Main channelGoogle Search, sometimes MetaGoogle Search plus LinkedIn

Many companies run both: a trial for smaller customers and a demo route for larger ones. If so, split campaigns and pages so the platforms do not blend two very different conversion types into one.

Why sign-ups alone mislead the algorithm

Smart bidding learns from whatever you call a conversion. If that is every trial sign-up, the algorithm will happily find cheap sign-ups from students, competitors and people who never log in again. The fix is to send deeper signals back:

  • Track trial activation (for example, connecting an integration or inviting a colleague) as a separate conversion.
  • Import qualified lead, opportunity and closed-won stages from your CRM using offline conversion imports.
  • Assign values so a demo from a large company is worth more than a single-seat trial.
  • Use enhanced conversions where appropriate, with a privacy notice and consent setup that fit UK GDPR.

Google Ads structure for SaaS

Category and problem terms

Searches like "project management software", "HR software UK" or "automate invoice reminders" show clear intent but are often expensive and competitive. Group them tightly and send each to a page that speaks to that use case, not a generic homepage.

Brand terms

Bid on your own name. It is usually cheap, protects you from competitors appearing above you and lets you control the message. Keep it in a separate campaign so it does not flatter the performance of non-brand activity.

Competitor and alternative terms

People searching "X alternative" or "X vs Y" are often unhappy with a current tool or comparing options, which makes them valuable. A few rules:

  • Google generally allows bidding on competitor names as keywords, but using trade marks in ad text can be restricted if the owner files a complaint.
  • Keep claims factual and verifiable; comparative advertising is covered by the CAP Code and ASA rulings.
  • Send traffic to an honest comparison page that explains when your tool is and is not the right fit.
  • Expect lower click-through rates and Quality Scores, and judge these campaigns on pipeline, not cost per click.

LinkedIn vs Google for SaaS

The two platforms do different jobs. Google captures people already searching for a solution. LinkedIn finds people who match your ideal customer profile by job title, seniority, industry and company size, whether or not they are searching today.

QuestionGoogle AdsLinkedIn Ads
Demand typeExisting intentCreating and shaping demand
TargetingKeywords and audiencesJob title, function, company, seniority
Typical cost per clickVaries widely by termUsually higher
Best offersTrial, demo, pricingGuides, webinars, demos for warm audiences
Account-based marketingLimitedStrong, with company list uploads

A common pattern is to use Google for bottom-funnel demand and LinkedIn to warm up target accounts with useful content, then retarget engaged people with a demo offer. Our article on LinkedIn ads for B2B lead generation goes deeper on formats and lead forms.

Landing pages that convert SaaS clicks

A SaaS landing page should match the ad message, state the outcome in one line, show the product, address the main objection (price, setup time, security, integrations) and make the next step obvious. For demo pages, keep forms short and say what happens next. For trial pages, remove anything that slows sign-up. Our guide to landing page design has a full checklist, and for UK buyers, mentioning UK data hosting or UK GDPR alignment, where true, can remove friction.

Retargeting and nurturing paid traffic

Most SaaS visitors do not convert on their first click, particularly on demo-led products. Retargeting lets you stay in front of people who visited pricing, comparison or feature pages. Keep audiences specific: someone who read the pricing page is warmer than someone who skimmed a blog post, and they should see a different message.

  • Pricing page visitors: a demo or trial offer with a short answer to the main cost objection.
  • Trial users who did not activate: setup help, a short video or an onboarding call, often better sent by email than by ads.
  • Comparison page readers: a clear summary of why customers switch, with fair and accurate claims.
  • Existing customers: exclude them from acquisition campaigns, or show them upgrade and add-on messages instead.

Retargeting relies on cookies and tracking, so make sure your cookie banner and privacy notice reflect what you use, in line with PECR and UK GDPR. Consent mode settings in Google and similar controls on LinkedIn help respect visitors who decline.

Budgets and expectations

Ad spend is paid separately from management fees and depends on your market. Competitive software categories can carry high click costs, so starting with a narrow set of high-intent terms and building out is usually more efficient than going broad on day one. Allow time for learning: with long B2B sales cycles, it may take a few months before CRM data shows which campaigns create real revenue.

Review search term reports weekly in the early months. SaaS keywords attract a surprising amount of irrelevant traffic, such as people looking for jobs at a competitor, login pages, free templates or support for a tool they already use. Adding these as negative keywords early protects budget for the searches that lead to trials and demos.

SaaS PPC checklist

  • Decide whether each campaign drives trials or demos.
  • Track activation and CRM stages, not just sign-ups.
  • Import offline conversions and assign values by deal size.
  • Separate brand, non-brand and competitor campaigns.
  • Build honest comparison pages for competitor traffic.
  • Check trade mark use in ad text against platform policy.
  • Match each ad group to a focused landing page.
  • Use LinkedIn for target roles and accounts, then retarget.
  • Add negative keywords for jobs, free, login and support searches.
  • Review pipeline by campaign monthly, not just cost per lead.

FAQs

What is the best PPC approach for SaaS companies?

Tie each campaign to a single funnel step, trial or demo, and optimise towards qualified pipeline using CRM data. Combine Google Search for existing demand with LinkedIn for targeting specific roles and accounts.

Is it legal to bid on competitor names in the UK?

Bidding on competitor names as keywords is generally permitted by Google, but trade mark use in ad text can be restricted, and comparative claims must be fair and accurate under advertising rules. Take legal advice if unsure.

Should SaaS companies use LinkedIn or Google Ads?

Usually both. Google captures people searching now; LinkedIn reaches decision makers by job title and company. The right split depends on your deal size and how well known your category is.

Why are my trial sign-ups cheap but revenue low?

The algorithm is probably optimising for any sign-up. Track activation and import CRM stages so bidding learns which clicks become paying customers.

How much should a SaaS company spend on PPC?

It depends on click costs in your category and your customer value. Start with a focused budget on high-intent terms, measure pipeline, and scale what works. Management fees are separate from ad spend.

How long before SaaS PPC shows results?

Clicks and sign-ups appear quickly, but with longer sales cycles it often takes a few months to see which campaigns generate revenue. Paid results typically settle within three to six months.
