Google Shopping Agency UK: Feed, Merchant Center and Campaign Management

What a Google Shopping agency manages, from product feeds to Merchant Center and campaigns, how to compare management options and what to ask before hiring.

By the Prism Works Studio team · Updated 7 November 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most Shopping performance is decided by the product feed, so ask any agency how they manage titles, attributes and errors.
  • Merchant Center health (approvals, policies, shipping and returns) is ongoing work, not a one-off setup task.
  • Judge agencies on feed process, campaign structure and reporting by product margin, not on promises of ROAS.

A Google Shopping agency in the UK manages the three things that decide Shopping results: your product feed, your Merchant Center account and the campaigns that bid on your products. When you hire one, the most useful question is how they improve the feed, because Google uses feed data rather than keywords to decide when your products appear.

This article is for store owners deciding whether to hire help and how to judge the options. What does a Google Shopping agency do?

Shopping is a narrower discipline than general PPC. The work splits into feed management, Merchant Center management and campaign management, with tracking underneath all three.

Feed management: where the gains usually are

Your feed is the product data Google reads: title, description, price, availability, images, brand, GTIN, product type and more. Feed improvements can matter more than bid changes because they help decide which searches you can appear for.

Titles

Platform exports often use short, branded titles such as a product name with no context. Shoppers search with attributes: brand, product type, material, colour, size, gender. A good agency restructures titles so the most searched words appear first, within Google's guidelines, and tests the change rather than assuming.

Attributes and identifiers

GTINs, brand, condition, colour, size, material and Google product category all help matching. Missing identifiers limit reach. For own-brand products without GTINs, the feed must declare that correctly.

Custom labels

Custom labels let campaigns group products by things Google does not know: margin band, best seller, seasonal, clearance, new arrival. These labels are what make a profitable structure possible.

Feed tools and rules

Many stores use a feed app or supplemental feeds in Merchant Center to apply rules without editing the store. Shopify and WooCommerce both have options; our Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison touches on how each handles product data. Ask the agency who owns the rules and whether you can see them.

Merchant Center management

Merchant Center is where products get approved or rejected. It needs ongoing attention, not a single setup.

  • Diagnostics. Errors (price mismatch, missing images, unavailable landing pages) remove products from ads. Warnings quietly limit performance.
  • Policies. Misrepresentation suspensions are often triggered by missing contact details, unclear returns or checkout problems. Your site should have clear UK delivery and returns information.
  • Shipping and returns. Delivery costs and times set in Merchant Center should match the delivery options shown on your site.
  • Free listings. Products can also appear in free listings, which is extra reach from the same feed.
  • Promotions and reviews. Merchant promotions and product ratings can improve click-through where eligible.

The causes of disapprovals often sit on the website rather than in the ads. Our website and ecommerce development service can cover those fixes when they are in scope, and our Merchant Center setup guide explains the account basics.

Campaign structure that reflects your margins

Google's default is to push stores into Performance Max, which can work well but hides detail. A thoughtful agency chooses structure based on catalogue size, margins and data volume. Our Performance Max guide goes deeper on that campaign type.

Common structures include:

  • Margin-based split. High, medium and low margin products in separate campaigns with different targets.
  • Hero products. Best sellers isolated so they get guaranteed budget.
  • Brand vs generic. Searches containing your brand separated from generic searches, so brand demand does not inflate results.
  • Zombie products. Products with no impressions grouped and tested on their own to find whether any deserve budget.

Negative keywords, search term reviews and device or location adjustments still matter where the campaign type allows them.

What Google Shopping management includes each month

Google Shopping management is ongoing work rather than a one-off setup. The exact routine varies with your catalogue and spend, but a managed account generally involves:

  • Feed upkeep: checking new products, price and stock accuracy, titles, attributes and custom labels.
  • Merchant Center checks: reviewing diagnostics and resolving disapprovals before they spread.
  • Search term and placement reviews: where the campaign type shows them, removing irrelevant traffic.
  • Budget and target changes: moving spend towards profitable lines and adjusting around sales and seasonal stock.
  • Tracking checks: comparing reported purchase values with your store's real orders.
  • Reporting: what changed, what it did and what is planned next, by product and category.

How to compare Google Shopping agencies

Ask every agency the same questions so the answers are comparable.

  1. How will you improve our feed in the first month, and who maintains it after?
  2. What do you do when products are disapproved?
  3. How will you structure campaigns for our margins and catalogue size?
  4. Will you report by product, category and margin, not just total ROAS?
  5. Do we keep ownership of Merchant Center, Google Ads and any feed tools?
  6. What is included in the monthly fee, and is ad spend billed directly to us?

When you might not need a specialist

A store with a small catalogue, low spend and a clean feed can often run Shopping well in-house. Hiring help makes more sense when:

  • You have hundreds or thousands of products and frequent stock changes.
  • Merchant Center keeps flagging errors or suspensions.
  • Spend has grown but profit has not.
  • You are moving platforms or redesigning, which can break the feed and tracking. Our guide to a redesign without losing rankings covers the organic side of that risk.

Shopping also works best alongside organic product visibility; an ecommerce SEO agency can improve the same product pages your ads send traffic to.

What the first 90 days should look like

A clear onboarding plan is one of the best ways to tell a capable agency from a vague one. Ask for it in writing before you sign. A sensible sequence looks like this.

Weeks 1 to 2: audit and access

The agency should get access to Google Ads, Merchant Center, GA4 and your store, then review conversion tracking, feed quality, Merchant Center diagnostics and existing campaign performance by product. You should receive a written list of findings ranked by likely impact, not a generic template.

Weeks 3 to 6: fix the foundations

Tracking problems come first, because bidding depends on accurate purchase values. Feed work follows: title restructuring for priority categories, missing attributes, custom labels for margin and best sellers, and fixes for disapproved products. Shipping and returns settings in Merchant Center should be checked against what the site actually offers.

Weeks 7 to 12: restructure and learn

Once data is trustworthy, campaigns can be restructured around margin bands, hero products and brand versus generic demand. Expect some volatility while automated bidding relearns. Changes should be made gradually, with notes on what changed and why, so results can be read properly.

By the end of the first quarter you should understand which products and categories make money through Shopping, which do not, and what the plan is for each. That understanding is worth more than any single headline number.

Questions to ask yourself before hiring

Agencies can only work with what the business gives them. Before you hire, be clear on your gross margins by category, which products you want to push, your stock position for the next season, and how much you can spend while campaigns learn. Knowing your margin is especially important, because a return on ad spend that looks healthy can still lose money on low-margin lines.

Google Shopping hiring checklist

  • Agency explains a specific feed improvement plan.
  • Merchant Center diagnostics reviewed during the pitch or audit.
  • Structure proposed around margin and best sellers.
  • Conversion tracking checked against real orders.
  • Reporting at product and category level.
  • You own every account and feed tool.
  • Fee scope written down; ad spend billed directly.
  • No guaranteed ROAS or sales promises.

FAQs

What does a Google Shopping agency UK do?

It manages your product feed, keeps Merchant Center healthy and runs the Shopping or Performance Max campaigns that show your products. Good agencies also check tracking against your actual sales and report by product and margin.

What is Google Shopping management?

It is the ongoing work of keeping your product feed accurate, your Merchant Center account healthy and your campaigns focused on profitable products, with tracking and reporting to show what is working.

Is feed management really that important?

Yes. Shopping ads do not use keywords in the way search ads do. Google matches searches to your feed data, so titles, attributes and identifiers decide which searches you can appear for.

Why are my products disapproved in Merchant Center?

Common causes are price or stock mismatches, missing identifiers, image problems, unavailable landing pages and policy issues such as unclear returns or contact details. The Diagnostics tab lists each issue.

Should I use Performance Max or Standard Shopping?

It depends on your data volume and how much control you need. Performance Max suits accounts with enough conversions and good assets; Standard Shopping gives more visibility of search terms and bids. Many stores test both.

How long until Google Shopping improvements show?

Feed fixes can sometimes affect impressions fairly quickly, but paid results typically take three to six months to settle as campaigns learn and structure is refined. No agency can promise a set outcome.

Is ad spend included in an agency fee?

It should not be. Ad spend is paid to Google directly, usually on your own card, and the agency fee covers management. Always confirm this in writing.
