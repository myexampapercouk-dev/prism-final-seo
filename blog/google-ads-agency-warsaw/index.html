What a Google Ads agency in Warsaw should handle

Warsaw is Poland's business capital, with a large professional services sector, regional headquarters of international firms, a fast-growing technology scene, busy e-commerce brands and thousands of local service businesses. Google Ads can work for all of them, but only when the account is built around real buyer intent.

  • Account structure. Campaigns and ad groups organised by service, product line or location so budgets go where they earn most.
  • Polish keyword research. Polish is heavily inflected, so the same word appears in several forms. Keyword lists and negative keywords need to reflect that.
  • Ads that match intent. Responsive search ads with clear benefits, prices where appropriate and strong calls to action.
  • Landing pages. Each ad should lead to a page that answers the search directly, in Polish.
  • Tracking and consent. Conversion tracking for calls, forms and sales, with Google consent mode configured alongside your cookie banner.
  • Ongoing optimisation. Search term reviews, bid strategy adjustments and regular testing.

Polish search behaviour and keyword planning

Polish grammar changes word endings depending on context. Someone might search "księgowa Warszawa", "biuro rachunkowe w Warszawie" or "usługi księgowe Warszawa Mokotów". Broad and phrase match handle some of this variation, but a good account still reviews search terms closely to catch irrelevant traffic and add negative keywords.

We use Polish location and language settings for research, then review the lists with your team, who understand how customers describe your services. Landing pages that convert Polish visitors

Paying for clicks only makes sense if the page converts. Compliance notes for Polish advertisers

This is general information, not legal advice. Check official guidance or speak to your own adviser.

  • GDPR. Polish businesses are subject to the EU General Data Protection Regulation. Lead forms, call tracking and customer lists used for advertising all involve personal data and need a lawful basis and a clear privacy notice.
  • Cookie consent. Under EU ePrivacy rules, advertising and analytics cookies generally need consent before they are set. Google consent mode should reflect the visitor's choice. Our cookie banner design article covers the practical side.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing services, including e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt. EN 301 549, which refers to WCAG at level AA, is the usual technical reference.
  • Google's own policies. Some sectors, such as financial services, healthcare and gambling, face extra Google advertising rules or certification. In regulated sectors, approval of ad and landing page content rests with your compliance team.

Choosing a Google Ads partner in Warsaw

Whichever agency you choose, ask:

  • Will the account be in your name, so you keep the data if you move on?
  • How will conversions be tracked, and how does consent mode work on your site?
  • Who reviews search terms, and how often?
  • How is Polish copy written and checked?
  • What does reporting include, and what will they change each month?

A local Warsaw agency may suit you if you prefer Polish-language meetings. A remote UK studio can suit businesses comfortable working in English, especially those also advertising in the UK or other European markets.

Common Google Ads mistakes we see in Poland

Many accounts we review share the same avoidable problems. The most common is broad targeting without enough negative keywords, which lets ads show for loosely related Polish searches that never convert. Because Polish word forms vary so much, search term reports need checking weekly in the early months, not once a quarter.

Another frequent issue is sending all traffic to the home page. A visitor searching for a specific service in Warsaw should land on a page about that service, with a clear way to get in touch. Tracking is the third weak spot: if form submissions, calls and purchases are not recorded properly, or consent mode is missing, automated bidding has little reliable data to learn from.

Finally, many accounts target the whole of Poland when the business only serves Warsaw and nearby towns. Tight location settings, sensible ad schedules and budgets split by service usually reduce wasted spend before any bid changes are needed.
