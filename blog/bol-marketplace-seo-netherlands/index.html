Bol Marketplace SEO: Improving Product Listings for Dutch and Belgian Shoppers

Selling on bol in the Netherlands or Belgium? How bol listing SEO works: titles, attributes, images, EANs, reviews and Dutch content, with our fees in pounds.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Bol marketplace SEO is the work of making your products easier to find and more likely to be bought on bol, the large online marketplace serving shoppers in the Netherlands and Belgium. It comes down to complete, accurate product data: clear Dutch titles, every relevant attribute filled in, good images, helpful descriptions and steady reviews, backed by reliable stock, pricing and delivery.

Our SEO and generative engine optimisation service covers marketplace listings as well as websites, and we work by video call and shared documents, with the Netherlands and Belgium one hour ahead of UK time. We write in English and work with your team or a translator on Dutch listing copy.

How search works on bol

Like other large marketplaces, bol has its own internal search and its own ranking logic, which bol does not publish in full. What is clear is that the platform wants to show shoppers relevant, well-described products that are in stock, fairly priced, delivered quickly and well reviewed. Listings with missing data, weak images or poor service records struggle to compete, however good the product is.

Bol organises products around a shared catalogue. Products are usually matched by their EAN barcode, so several sellers can offer the same item on one product page. That has two consequences. First, accurate EANs are essential: a wrong or missing barcode can put your offer on the wrong page or stop it appearing at all. Second, when you sell an item that other sellers also offer, competition for that page depends heavily on price, delivery promise and seller performance, not only on content.

Dutch product content, written properly

Bol shoppers in the Netherlands and Flanders browse in Dutch, so product content needs to read naturally in Dutch, not as a rough translation. Search terms also differ: shoppers may use everyday words that differ from the formal product names a manufacturer uses, and Dutch and Flemish usage are not always identical. Keyword research for bol should start from how real shoppers describe the product.

Prism writes English copy in-house. For bol listings we prepare a brief for each product or product family, covering the title structure, priority search terms, attributes to complete and the questions the description must answer. Your team or a native Dutch-speaking translator writes the final copy, and we check it against the brief before it goes live. Our guide to writing ecommerce product descriptions covers the principles we apply on any platform.

Machine translation alone is risky on a marketplace. Inaccurate wording can lead to wrong expectations, more returns and weaker reviews, all of which can hold a listing back.

Images and product data

Images often decide whether a shopper clicks at all. A clean main image on a plain background, followed by detail shots, size references and lifestyle images, helps shoppers understand the product quickly. Always follow bol's current image requirements, which can change. Our ecommerce product photography guide explains how to brief a shoot that works across your own shop and marketplaces.

Attributes deserve as much care as copy. Shoppers on bol use filters heavily, and a product missing a size, colour or compatibility field may simply not appear when those filters are applied. We audit listings for missing or inconsistent attributes and set up a simple process so new products launch with complete data.

Offers, delivery and seller performance

Content is only part of the picture. On a shared product page, the offer shown first usually depends on price, delivery speed, stock and seller performance. Late deliveries, cancellations and slow replies to customer questions can weaken your position. We do not manage your logistics, but we review these factors with you so content work is not undermined by service problems.

Bol also offers its own fulfilment option and advertising tools for sellers. Whether either fits depends on your products, margins and operations. Rules and requirements change, including the requirements for sellers based outside the Netherlands and Belgium, so check bol's current seller terms before you plan.

Bol, Amazon and your own shop

Many brands selling into the Netherlands and Belgium use more than one channel. Amazon also operates marketplaces for the Netherlands and Belgium, and your own online shop gives you control over branding, customer data and margins. Each channel has trade-offs:

ChannelStrengthsTrade-offs
BolStrong recognition with Dutch and Belgian shoppersMarketplace fees, shared product pages and platform rules
AmazonFamiliar checkout for shoppers who already use itFees, competition on shared listings and its own rules
Your own shopFull control of brand, data and customer relationshipYou must drive all of the traffic yourself

Our post on selling on Amazon versus your own website explains the wider choice. If you run a Shopify store alongside bol, our guides to Shopify for Dutch brands and Shopify Markets for Europe cover the store side.

Compliance points to keep in mind

Product pages must describe items accurately, and EU consumer, product safety and labelling rules apply to what you sell and how you describe it. If you also run your own shop, the GDPR and the EU ePrivacy rules on cookie consent apply to that site, and the European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing online shops, with an exemption for microenterprises providing services and WCAG AA, as referenced by EN 301 549, as the usual benchmark. These notes are general, and your legal adviser should confirm what applies to your business.
