Facebook Ads for Small Business in the UK: A Practical Starter Guide

Facebook ads for small business work best with one clear offer, a tight audience, simple creative and proper tracking. Here is how UK owners can start well.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with one product or service, one clear offer and one goal, rather than trying to advertise everything at once.
  • Set up the Meta pixel and conversion tracking before you spend, so you can see which ads actually bring sales or enquiries.
  • Plan for several weeks of testing and keep refreshing creative; small budgets go further when the account stays simple.

Facebook ads for small business work best when you keep things simple: one clear offer, a sensible audience, honest and eye-catching creative, and tracking that tells you which ads lead to sales or enquiries. You do not need a large budget to start, but you do need patience, because the first few weeks are about learning rather than profit.

Facebook ads are bought through Meta Ads Manager, which also covers Instagram. This guide is written for UK owners of small businesses, such as shops, trades, clinics, salons and local services, who want to try paid social without wasting money.

Are Facebook ads worth it for a small business?

For many small businesses, yes, but not for every one. Facebook and Instagram are good at putting an offer in front of people who were not actively searching for it. That makes them a strong fit for products with visual appeal, local services people use regularly, events, and offers with a clear hook.

They are a weaker fit when people only look for you in an emergency, such as an emergency plumber, where search ads tend to work better. Our guide to SEO for small business covers the organic side of being found when people search.

Facebook ads for small business: a step-by-step setup

Here is the order we recommend for getting started without wasting budget.

  1. Set up Business Manager. Create a Meta Business account so your page, ad account and pixel belong to the business, not a personal profile. This matters if staff or suppliers change.
  2. Install tracking. Add the Meta pixel to your website, and ideally the Conversions API too, so Meta can see purchases, form fills or bookings. Make sure your cookie banner and privacy notice reflect this in line with UK GDPR and PECR.
  3. Choose one goal. Pick the action that matters most, such as purchases, leads or booked appointments.
  4. Pick one offer. Lead with a single product, service or promotion rather than a general "we do everything" message.
  5. Define your audience. Start with location, plus broad interests or your own audiences such as website visitors.
  6. Create a few ads. Two to four genuinely different creative ideas are enough to begin testing.
  7. Set a budget you can hold. Choose a daily amount you can leave running for several weeks without panic.
  8. Review weekly. Look at cost per result, not likes or clicks, and change one thing at a time.

Choosing your audience

Small businesses often make their audience too narrow. Stacking lots of interests and age bands can leave Meta with too few people to find buyers among. A better starting point is usually:

  • Location first. For local businesses, target the area you genuinely serve, and check the setting that controls whether you reach people living there or just interested in it.
  • Broad enough to learn. Let Meta's system find likely customers within a reasonably sized audience, then judge by results.
  • Your own audiences. People who have visited your website, engaged with your page or are on your customer list (where you have the right to use it) are often your most responsive group.

Some categories, such as housing, employment, credit and certain health or financial products, have special rules and restricted targeting. If you are in a regulated sector, keep claims general and make sure your own compliance team approves the wording.

Creative that works on a small budget

You do not need a studio shoot. Many effective small business ads are filmed on a phone. What matters is clarity.

  • Hook in the first second or two. Show the product, the problem or the result straight away.
  • Use real people and places. Your shop, your van, your team at work (with their permission) feel more trustworthy than stock images.
  • Say who it is for. "For dog owners in Bristol" is clearer than a generic headline.
  • Make the next step obvious. Book, shop, call or claim, with one clear call to action.
  • Follow advertising rules. Ads must be honest and not misleading under the CAP Code, so avoid claims you cannot back up.

Plan to refresh ads regularly. When the same people see the same ad too often, results usually fade.

How much to spend

There is no minimum that guarantees results, and no fixed price, because Meta's auction sets costs. The most useful approach is to decide what you can afford to pay for a customer, then set a budget that buys enough results each week to learn from. When to bring in help

Running ads yourself is a good way to learn, but it takes time each week. Signs that it may be worth getting support include spend that is growing, results that have stalled, or not knowing whether the ads are profitable at all. Measuring what matters

Facebook's reporting shows plenty of numbers, and it is easy to focus on the wrong ones. Reach, likes and link clicks show that people are seeing your ads, but they do not pay the bills. The measures that matter most for a small business are usually cost per lead or sale, how many of those leads turn into paying customers, and whether the money you put in comes back over time.

Keep a simple weekly record: what you spent, how many results Meta reported, and how many real enquiries or orders arrived. Over a few weeks that record will tell you far more than any single day's figures. It also makes it easier to spot problems early, such as a tracking tag that has stopped firing after a website update, or an ad that has started to tire.

Finally, ask new customers how they found you. Some people see an ad on their phone, then search for you later or visit in person, and that journey will not always show in the reports.

FAQs

Are Facebook ads for small business worth it?

They can be, especially for visual products, local services and clear offers. They work best with tracking in place, a focused offer and a few weeks of testing. For urgent, search-led needs, search ads or SEO may be a better first channel.

How much should a small business spend on Facebook ads?

There is no set figure. Work out what you can afford to pay for a customer, then set a budget you can sustain for several weeks that is large enough to produce regular results to learn from.

Do Facebook ads also show on Instagram?

Yes. Campaigns created in Meta Ads Manager can run across Facebook, Instagram and other Meta placements. You can let Meta choose placements or select them yourself.

Should I boost posts or use Ads Manager?

Boosting is quick but limited. Ads Manager lets you choose objectives, audiences, placements and tests properly, which is usually better once you are spending money regularly.

How long do Facebook ads take to work?

You can get traffic quickly, but finding a profitable mix of audience, offer and creative usually takes several weeks of testing, with results typically building over three to six months.

Can I target people only in my town?

Yes. You can target by location, including a radius around an address. Check whether you are reaching people who live there or people who have simply shown interest in the area.
