Managing Google Shopping Feeds and Campaigns: What Good Management Involves

What good Google Shopping management involves: product feed quality, Merchant Center health, campaign structure, search terms, reporting and regular checks.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 7 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Good Google Shopping management starts with the product feed, because Shopping ads are built from your product data rather than from keywords you write.
  • Campaign structure should reflect commercial differences such as margin, stock and seasonality, not simply copy the layout of your online shop.
  • Ongoing work means regular feed and account checks, search term reviews and reporting tied to profit, not one-off set-up.

Good Google Shopping management means looking after two connected things: the product feed that tells Google what you sell, and the campaigns that decide how much you bid and where your budget goes. In practice, it involves keeping product data accurate and complete, resolving Merchant Center issues quickly, structuring campaigns around commercial priorities, reviewing search terms and reporting on profit rather than clicks alone. It is an ongoing routine, not a set-up task you complete once.

This guide is for UK ecommerce owners and marketing managers who run Shopping ads themselves, or pay someone else to, and want to know what good looks like. If you are comparing outside suppliers, our guide to choosing a Google Shopping agency covers that decision. If you are new to the format, begin with our Google Shopping ads guide and our walkthrough of Google Merchant Center set-up.

Why the feed comes first

With a standard search campaign, you choose keywords and write adverts. Shopping works differently. Google uses the information in your product data to decide which searches your products may appear for and what the listing shows. A weak feed can limit your campaigns however carefully they are built, which is why strong management often begins here.

A product feed is a structured list of your products and their attributes. It may come from your ecommerce platform, a file or another connection to Merchant Center. Whichever method you use, somebody must take responsibility for its quality.

What good feed management involves

Accurate core data

The basics must match what customers see on your site. Prices, availability, currency, delivery details and links should all agree with the landing page. Mismatches can lead to disapprovals as well as disappointed shoppers, so a feed should update often enough to reflect changes in your stock and pricing.

Useful product titles

Titles have a strong influence on how your products are matched and whether people click. A good title describes the product in the words shoppers use, and may include details such as brand, product type, material, size or colour where relevant. Internal product names and marketing phrases tend to be less helpful. The right order varies by category, so it is worth reviewing your important products individually.

Clear descriptions

Descriptions should explain the product accurately and include useful details such as features, materials and intended use. Avoid promotional text that Google's specifications do not allow, such as delivery offers or calls to action. Check current guidance if you are unsure.

Good images

Your main image is often the first thing a shopper notices. It should show the product clearly and meet Google's image requirements, which restrict things like added promotional text and watermarks. Additional images can help where they show the product from other angles or in use.

Identifiers and categories

Product identifiers such as GTINs, brand and manufacturer part numbers help Google understand exactly what you sell, and may be required for certain products. Do not invent identifiers. If your products genuinely do not have them, as with some handmade or own-label goods, check how Google asks you to handle this. Google product category and your own product type field can also support relevance and reporting.

Variants and detailed attributes

Products sold in several colours or sizes should be set up so that each variant is correctly represented and linked to the right page. In categories such as clothing, attributes like gender, age group, size and colour can be especially important.

Custom labels

Custom labels let you tag products with information Google does not otherwise have, such as margin band, best sellers, seasonal items or clearance stock. They do not appear to shoppers, but they can make campaigns far easier to organise. Good managers plan them around how the business actually makes money.

Rules and supplemental data

You will not always be able to change your source data quickly. Merchant Center's data options, such as rules and supplemental feeds, can let you improve titles or add attributes without altering your site. These should be documented, so that nobody is puzzled later by why feed data differs from the shop.

Keeping Merchant Center healthy

Merchant Center is where product issues surface, and regular checks should be part of the routine. Good management includes:

  • Monitoring product disapprovals and warnings, and tackling the most commercially important first.
  • Investigating account-level issues promptly, since these can affect all your products.
  • Making sure site requirements are met, including clear contact details, returns information, delivery terms and secure checkout.
  • Checking that delivery and tax settings are correct for each country you sell to. UK prices shown to consumers should generally include VAT, but ask your accountant if your position is unusual.
  • Reviewing whether products are excluded by mistake, for example because of a broken rule or a stock feed error.

Google's policies and interface change over time, so anyone managing the account should check current documentation rather than relying on memory. Restricted categories such as healthcare products or alcohol can have extra requirements that must be checked before you advertise.

What good campaign management involves

A clear campaign type decision

You may run Standard Shopping campaigns, Performance Max campaigns or both. Standard Shopping offers more direct control and clear reporting on search terms. Performance Max can show ads across a range of Google surfaces and relies more heavily on automation and your conversion data. The right choice depends on your data, your goals and how much control you need. A good manager should be able to explain why they chose a particular approach and how they will judge it.

A structure built on commercial logic

Copying your shop's menu into campaigns is easy, but it is not always useful. Products in the same category can have very different margins, prices and sales histories. Better structures often group products by things that should change how you bid, such as:

  • Margin or profit contribution.
  • Best sellers and products that need more visibility.
  • Seasonal or clearance products.
  • Brand and non-brand demand, where it makes sense to separate these.

The structure should also be simple enough for each group to gather meaningful data. Splitting a small account into too many segments can leave each one short of information.

Bidding based on real goals

Automated bidding works best when it has reliable conversion data and a target that reflects your business. A target that looks at revenue alone can favour low-margin products, so it helps to know what return you actually need after product costs, delivery, returns and fees. A good manager will ask you for those figures, or at least for an estimate, rather than choosing a target arbitrarily.

Search term and negative keyword work

You cannot pick Shopping keywords directly, but you can see search terms where they are available and exclude irrelevant ones with negative keywords. Regular reviews help to reduce wasted spend and can reveal title improvements or gaps in your range. Search terms can also indicate whether a product is matching the right intent.

Budget and stock awareness

Budget should be moved towards what the business wants to sell. If a product is out of stock, about to be discontinued or has poor margin, it may not deserve the same support. Good managers talk to whoever controls stock and promotions, so that campaigns reflect upcoming sales and supply problems.

Accurate conversion tracking

Every decision depends on the conversion data. Purchases should be recorded once, with the correct value, and figures should broadly agree with your ecommerce platform. Consent settings can also affect what is measured, so take advice if you are unsure what applies to your site. For a related checklist, see our PPC audit checklist.

A sensible management routine

The exact rhythm depends on the size of your account, but the following gives a sense of how the work might be organised.

RhythmTypical tasks
Frequent checksSpend and sales anomalies, account warnings, feed fetch errors, major disapprovals
Regular reviewsSearch terms, product performance, budget pacing, stock and promotion changes
Periodic workTitle and image improvements, custom label updates, structure and target reviews
Seasonal planningPreparing feeds and budgets for peak periods, promotions and new ranges

How to judge your Shopping management

Whether you manage the account in-house or use an agency, these questions can show how well it is being looked after:

  • Do you know which products are making money, and which are spending without returning enough?
  • When did someone last improve your product titles or images?
  • Can you see why the campaigns are organised as they are?
  • Are you told about disapprovals before they become a serious problem?
  • Do reports speak about profit, or at least about return in relation to your margins, rather than only clicks and impressions?
  • Do you own the Google Ads and Merchant Center accounts, or at least hold full access?

Be careful about any promise of a guaranteed return. Shopping performance depends on your prices, range, competition, delivery terms and website as well as on the management of your ads. Our guide to ecommerce PPC agencies looks at the wider picture.

FAQs

How often should a Google Shopping feed update?

Often enough that prices and availability stay accurate. How frequently that is depends on how often your stock and prices change. A fast-changing range will need more frequent updates than a small, stable catalogue.

Is Performance Max better than Standard Shopping?

There is no single answer. It depends on your conversion data, your goals, your budget and how much visibility and control you want. Some retailers use one, some use both, and any change should be measured carefully.

Why are my products disapproved?

Possible reasons include data that does not match the landing page, missing attributes, image problems and policy issues. Merchant Center normally explains the specific problem, and its help documentation sets out how to fix it.

Can I improve results by editing product titles?

It can help, because titles influence matching and clicks, but results vary. Change important products first, keep a record of what you changed and review the effect over a sensible period.

Do I need an agency to manage Shopping?

Not always. A smaller retailer with time and a modest range can manage it in-house. Outside help tends to be more useful as your range grows, or where the feed and campaigns need more attention than your team can give them.

Next steps

Pick your commercially most important products, then check their titles, images, availability and Merchant Center status. Compare what you find with how those products are grouped and bid on in your campaigns.
