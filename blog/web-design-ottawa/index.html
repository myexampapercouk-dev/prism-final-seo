Web Design for Ottawa Businesses: How a Remote Website Project Works

Looking for web design in Ottawa? How a remote website project runs, what bilingual and accessibility planning involves, and what to check before you hire.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • An Ottawa business can commission a strong website from a remote studio, provided discovery, content, approvals and ownership are agreed in writing at the start.
  • Bilingual content and accessibility are worth planning before design begins in Ottawa, because adding French pages or fixing accessibility later costs more time.
  • Judge any web designer on process, clarity about who owns the finished site and honesty about timelines, not on a polished sales deck.

Web design for an Ottawa business should give you a fast, clear website that turns visitors into enquiries, and it can be delivered remotely as well as locally. Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works with Canadian businesses online, with no office in Ottawa, and this guide explains how a remote website project runs, what Ottawa businesses should plan for and how to judge any web designer before you sign.

Whether you run a professional services firm in Centretown, a trades business serving Kanata and Orléans, or a company selling to government and enterprise buyers, the fundamentals are the same: a clear structure, good content, solid technical foundations and a site you genuinely own.

What good web design in Ottawa looks like

Ottawa has an unusual mix of buyers. Federal departments, technology companies, consultancies, healthcare providers and local service businesses all sit side by side, and many sell across the river into Gatineau. A good website for this market usually does a few things well:

  • Says what you do in seconds. A clear headline, a plain summary of your services and an obvious next step on the homepage.
  • Builds trust quickly. Real photos, accurate business details, credentials you genuinely hold and case studies only where you have permission to share them.
  • Works on every device. Many visitors arrive on a phone, so layouts, buttons and forms must work at small sizes. Our guide to mobile website design covers the detail.
  • Loads fast. Compressed images, lean code and sensible hosting matter for both visitors and search engines.
  • Is ready for search. Page titles, headings, structured data and an internal link structure planned from day one rather than bolted on.

Our website and ecommerce development service covers design, build, launch and the technical SEO foundations that go with them.

Planning a bilingual website for Ottawa and Gatineau

Because Ottawa sits on the Ontario and Quebec border, many businesses here serve both English and French speakers. Some are required to communicate in French in certain situations, and others simply win more work when francophone customers can read about their services in their own language. Either way, bilingual content is far easier to plan at the start than to retrofit.

Key decisions include whether every page needs a French version or only the most important ones, how language switching works, how URLs are structured for each language and who writes or reviews the French content. Machine translation alone is rarely good enough for service pages that need to persuade. Our guide to multilingual website design covers URL structures, language switchers and how search engines handle each version.

If your business sells into Quebec, French-language rules there may affect your website and marketing. Those rules have changed in recent years, so check current official guidance from the Quebec government and take your own advice on what applies to you. We can build the structure; the legal judgement rests with you and your advisers.

Accessibility and privacy for Ontario websites

Accessibility is not optional for many Ottawa organisations. Under the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA), certain organisations in Ontario have website accessibility obligations depending on their size and sector. Even where the rules do not strictly apply, an accessible site is clearer for every visitor and tends to be easier for search engines to understand. Our guide to website accessibility explains the practical design and build steps, which follow the same international WCAG guidelines.

Privacy matters too. Contact forms, analytics and marketing tags collect personal information, and federal privacy law (PIPEDA) sets expectations for how that is handled. Email marketing in Canada is also covered by anti-spam legislation (CASL). We build consent and tracking with these in mind, but please check current guidance from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and the relevant regulators, and take your own advice on your obligations.

How a remote web design project runs from Ottawa

Most of a website project is research, writing, design and development, all done online. What makes a remote project work is a clear process and good communication. Our guide to hiring an overseas web design agency goes deeper into the questions to ask.

Content is often the biggest cause of delay on any website project, local or remote. Our guide to preparing website content before the build explains what to gather early so the project keeps moving.

How long does a website take?

A typical brochure website takes three to five weeks from discovery to launch, and an ecommerce site usually takes five to twelve weeks depending on product numbers, integrations and payment setup. Bilingual sites take longer because there is more content to write, review and test. Our guide to how long it takes to build a website breaks down where the time goes.

The fastest projects are the ones where content and decisions arrive on time. If your organisation needs several people to approve each page, build that into the timeline from the start rather than discovering it halfway through.

How much does web design cost for an Ottawa business?

Our published prices are in pounds sterling, and you can see them on our pricing page. A small business website is typically £1,000 to £2,000 and an ecommerce site is typically £1,500 to £3,500, depending on scope. Canadian dollar quotes are available on request, and we will always confirm exactly what is included before any work starts.

When comparing quotes from any provider, check what each one actually covers. Some include copywriting, French content, accessibility testing, analytics setup and redirects from your old site; others charge separately. A cheaper quote that excludes these can end up costing more.

  • Is the content written for you, or do you supply it?
  • Are French pages included, and who writes or reviews them?
  • Is accessibility tested, and against which standard?
  • Are redirects from your old site planned to protect search rankings?
  • What happens after launch, and is ongoing support optional?

Who owns the website when it is finished?

You should. That means your business holds the domain, the hosting account, the content management system admin login and the analytics accounts, with the agency given access rather than the other way round. If a provider insists on keeping control of your domain or hosting, treat that as a warning sign. Our guide to website ownership and handover lists what you should receive at launch.

Choosing a web designer in Ottawa: a quick checklist

  1. Look at the process, not just the portfolio. Ask how they run discovery, content and approvals.
  2. Ask about bilingual and accessibility experience. These are specific skills, and an Ottawa site often needs both.
  3. Check search foundations. Titles, headings, redirects and structured data should be standard, not extras.
  4. Confirm ownership in writing. Domain, hosting and logins should belong to you.
  5. Agree communication early. A regular call slot, response times and one point of contact on each side.
  6. Be wary of guarantees. Nobody can guarantee rankings or a set number of leads from a new website.

FAQs

Can a UK studio handle web design for an Ottawa business?

Yes. Prism Works Studio works with Canadian businesses remotely, with no office in Ottawa. Discovery, design reviews and training run over video calls, and you review the site on a staging link at every stage.

How much does web design in Ottawa cost?

Our published prices are in pounds sterling: a small business website is typically £1,000 to £2,000 and an ecommerce site £1,500 to £3,500. Canadian dollar quotes are available on request, and the final figure depends on scope.

Does my Ottawa website need to be in French as well as English?

It depends on your customers, your sector and whether you sell into Quebec. Many Ottawa businesses benefit from French content even where it is not required. Check current official guidance for your situation and plan the structure before design starts.

Does AODA apply to my website?

AODA website requirements apply to certain Ontario organisations depending on size and type. Check current guidance from the Government of Ontario for your organisation. Building to WCAG accessibility guidelines is good practice either way.

How long does it take to build a website?

A brochure site typically takes three to five weeks and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks. Bilingual sites and projects with several approvers usually take longer.

Will I own my website after launch?

Yes. We set up the domain, hosting, content management system and analytics in your name, or transfer them to you at handover, and provide documentation so your team can manage the site.
