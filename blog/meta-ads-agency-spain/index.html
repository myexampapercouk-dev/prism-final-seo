Why Meta Ads work well in Spain

Social media use is high across Spain, and Instagram in particular plays a big part in how people discover fashion, beauty, food, travel, home goods and local services. Meta Ads let you reach people by location, interests and behaviour, and retarget those who have already visited your site or engaged with your content.

Spain is also a varied market. Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Seville, Málaga, Bilbao and the islands each have their own character, and some regions have co-official languages such as Catalan, Basque and Galician. Tourism shapes demand in coastal areas, while large cities have strong year-round ecommerce and local service markets. Good campaigns reflect those differences rather than treating Spain as one audience.

Spanish-language creative and localisation

Ads that read like a direct translation tend to underperform. Spanish buyers respond to natural language, local references and offers that make sense in the Spanish market, such as delivery times, returns and payment options people expect. If you target Catalonia, the Basque Country or Galicia, it can also be worth testing ads in the regional language alongside Spanish.

Because Prism writes in English, the usual workflow is that we prepare the creative brief, angles and structure, and your team or a translator writes or adapts the Spanish copy. We then check the ads against the brief, set up the tests and track what works.

Many brands that sell in Spain also sell in Portugal, France, Italy or the UK. Combining countries in one campaign can save time but often hides what is really happening in each market. Our guide to Meta Ads in multiple countries explains when to split and when to combine. If France and Germany are on your list, see our pages on Meta Ads in France and Meta Ads in Germany.

Tracking, consent and privacy

Spain follows the GDPR and its own data protection and cookie rules. In practice, tracking pixels should only fire after a visitor gives consent, and your cookie banner should make refusing as simple as accepting. This affects how much data Meta receives, so we pair the pixel with the Conversions API where appropriate and keep reporting honest about what can and cannot be measured.

We provide general marketing support, not legal advice. Your own team and advisers are responsible for privacy notices, consent setup and compliance decisions, and should refer to official guidance from the Spanish data protection authority.

Combining Meta Ads with Google Ads

Meta Ads create demand, while Google Ads capture people who are already searching. Running both lets you reach new audiences on Instagram and Facebook, then appear when they later search for your brand or product. Our Google Ads agency Madrid page explains our search approach in Spain's capital.

Seasonality in the Spanish market

Spanish demand shifts through the year. Summer holidays change behaviour in big cities and boost coastal tourism, while the January and July sales periods are important for retail. Christmas runs later than in some markets, with Three Kings Day on 6 January a major gifting moment. Hospitality brands should plan around peak travel seasons; our page on hotel marketing in Barcelona covers that sector in more depth. We plan budgets and creative around these moments so campaigns are ready before demand peaks.

Creative that tends to work in Spain

There is no single formula, but some patterns are worth testing early. Short vertical videos and Reels-style content usually suit Instagram audiences, especially for fashion, beauty, food and lifestyle brands. Real people using the product, clear before-and-after visuals and simple demonstrations often outperform polished studio images. For local services, ads that show the team, the premises or the neighbourhood can build trust quickly.

Offers should feel relevant to Spanish buyers. Free delivery thresholds, easy returns, delivery timescales within Spain and familiar payment options can all lift conversion rates. Mentioning the city or region in headlines can help local campaigns stand out. We set up structured tests so each round of creative teaches you something, rather than changing everything at once and guessing what made the difference.

Common Meta Ads mistakes we see

  • Translated creative from another market: copy and visuals that clearly come from elsewhere rarely build trust.
  • Broken or missing tracking: without consent-aware pixel and Conversions API setup, the algorithm has little data to optimise with.
  • Too many small ad sets: budgets spread thinly across many audiences slow down learning.
  • No retargeting: most visitors do not buy on their first visit, so following up matters.
  • Judging too early: switching campaigns off after a few days often wastes the learning already gathered.
