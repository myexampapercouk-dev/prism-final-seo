Web Design for Bahrain Businesses: Process, Cost and What to Ask

Looking for web design in Bahrain? How a remote UK studio plans, builds and launches bilingual sites for Manama businesses, with timelines and pricing.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good Bahrain business website is fast on mobile, clear in Arabic and English where your customers need both, and built so you own the domain, hosting and content.
  • Working with a remote studio can suit Bahrain businesses well when the process is written down, meetings are planned around both time zones and approvals are clear.
  • Our small websites start from £1,000 to £2,000 and e-commerce sites from £1,500 to £3,500, with other currencies quoted on request.

Web design for a Bahrain business means building a site that loads quickly on mobile, explains what you offer in the language your customers search in, and turns visits into calls, WhatsApp messages, bookings or sales. The right partner can be local or remote, as long as the process is clear, you own everything at the end and the site is built for how people in Bahrain actually browse and buy.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for businesses across the Gulf, including Bahrain. We have no office in Bahrain, so every project runs through video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can review at any time. This guide explains how we approach website and e-commerce development for Bahraini businesses, what it costs in pounds and what to ask any studio before you sign.

Web design in Bahrain: what a good site needs

Bahrain is a compact, well-connected market. Customers in Manama, Muharraq, Riffa and the rest of the kingdom often compare several providers on their phones before they make contact, and many businesses also serve customers from Saudi Arabia and the wider Gulf. A site that works for this audience usually has a few things in common.

  • Mobile first. Most visits will come from phones, so layouts, menus and forms are designed for small screens before desktop. Our guide to mobile website design covers the details.
  • Clear language choices. Some businesses need full Arabic and English versions, others need English with key pages in Arabic. The decision should follow your customers, not habit.
  • Fast loading. Heavy sliders and oversized images slow pages down and cost enquiries. We build to pass Core Web Vitals where the content allows.
  • Easy contact. Click-to-call, WhatsApp links, short forms and booking tools placed where people expect them.
  • Trust signals. Real photos, clear service descriptions, accurate business details and policies that answer common questions.

Arabic, English or both

Language is the biggest early decision for most Bahrain projects. A bilingual site is not simply a translated copy. Arabic pages read right to left, which affects layout, navigation, icons and forms, and Arabic copy often needs adapting rather than translating word for word so it sounds natural to local readers.

We usually recommend one of three approaches:

ApproachBest forThings to plan
English onlyB2B firms and services aimed mainly at expatriates or international clientsClear, simple English and strong local signals such as service areas
English with key Arabic pagesBusinesses testing demand from Arabic-speaking customersWhich pages to translate first and how visitors switch language
Full bilingual siteRetail, clinics, education, property and consumer servicesRight-to-left layouts, separate URLs per language, hreflang tags and ongoing upkeep in both languages

Our guide to bilingual Arabic and English websites goes deeper into right-to-left design, fonts and SEO for each language. If you also sell into other markets, see our wider notes on multilingual website design.

How a remote web design project works

Working with a studio in another country is straightforward when the process is written down. Our projects follow the same stages whether the client is in London or Manama.

  1. Discovery call. We talk through your goals, customers, languages, competitors and must-have features.
  2. Proposal and plan. You receive a written scope, timeline and fixed price in pounds, so there are no surprises later.
  3. Content and structure. We agree the sitemap and page outlines. You supply or approve copy, photos and details such as opening hours and service areas.
  4. Design. We share designs for the key pages, collect feedback in one place and revise.
  5. Build on staging. The site is built on a private staging address you can review on your own phone and laptop.
  6. Testing and launch. We check forms, speed, mobile layouts, both languages and tracking, then go live.
  7. Handover. You receive logins, a short guide and access to everything in your own name.

Bahrain is a few hours ahead of the UK, and the working week can differ, so we agree regular meeting slots at the start that suit both sides. Most feedback happens in writing, which also gives you a clear record of every decision. If you are weighing up local and remote options, our guide to hiring an overseas web design agency sets out the questions worth asking.

Platforms we use

We choose the platform based on what you need to do day to day, not what is easiest for us.

  • WordPress suits service businesses, clinics, consultancies and content-led sites that need flexible pages and a blog.
  • Shopify suits most online shops, including those selling across the Gulf, thanks to its checkout, multi-currency and multi-language options.
  • WooCommerce can suit stores that already run on WordPress or need specific customisation.

Whichever platform we use, the site, domain and hosting accounts are set up in your name. Our guide to website ownership and handover explains why that matters if you ever change provider.

Payments, delivery and local details

If you sell online in Bahrain, your checkout needs payment options your customers trust, clear delivery information for Bahrain and any other Gulf countries you serve, and prices shown with the correct tax treatment. Rules on VAT, e-commerce, consumer protection and data handling apply in Bahrain, and they change over time, so we always ask clients to check current official guidance and confirm details with their accountant or adviser. We build the pages and settings; responsibility for compliance stays with the business.

Local details also matter for search. Consistent business information, a properly set up Google Business Profile and pages that mention the areas you serve all help people find you. For businesses that also want ongoing visibility, our SEO and GEO service covers technical health, content and AI search.

Questions to ask any web design studio

Whether you choose a studio in Manama, the UK or elsewhere, these questions help you compare proposals fairly.

  • Who will own the domain, hosting, code and content when the project ends?
  • How will Arabic and English versions be structured, and who writes or checks the Arabic copy?
  • What is included in the price, and what counts as extra?
  • How many rounds of design feedback are included?
  • How will you test speed and mobile layouts before launch?
  • What support is available after launch, and what does it cost?
  • How will we communicate, and how quickly will you reply?

A studio that answers clearly and in writing is usually one that will run a smooth project. Be cautious of any provider that keeps the domain in its own name or will not explain what happens if you leave.

After launch

A new website is a starting point. Most sites benefit from regular updates, security patches, small content changes and a periodic review of what visitors do. We offer support and marketing retainers for clients who want help after launch, and we are equally happy to hand over a site you can run yourself.

If you plan to advertise, set up conversion tracking from day one so you can see which campaigns bring enquiries. Our paid media service covers Google, Meta, TikTok and YouTube Ads for clients who want campaigns managed alongside the site.

FAQs

How much does web design in Bahrain cost?

Our small business websites are typically £1,000 to £2,000 and e-commerce sites £1,500 to £3,500, priced in pounds. Bilingual builds and extra features can add to this, and other currencies are quoted on request after a discovery call.

Do you have an office in Bahrain?

No. Prism Works Studio is based in the UK and works remotely with Bahrain clients through video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can review at any time.

Can you build an Arabic and English website?

Yes. We plan right-to-left layouts, separate language URLs and hreflang tags. We recommend that Arabic copy is written or checked by a fluent speaker so it reads naturally.

How long does a website take to build?

A small business website usually takes 3 to 5 weeks and an e-commerce site 5 to 12 weeks. Bilingual content and how quickly feedback comes back are the main things that affect timing.

Will I own my website?

Yes. We set up the domain, hosting and platform accounts in your name and hand over all logins at launch, so you can keep working with us or move elsewhere.

Do you handle VAT and legal pages for Bahrain?

We build the pages and checkout settings, but rules change and depend on your business, so please check current official guidance and confirm the details with your accountant or legal adviser.
