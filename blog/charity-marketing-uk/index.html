Digital Marketing for UK Charities: A Practical Guide

Charity digital marketing in the UK: use Google Ad Grants well, build clear fundraising pages, grow search visibility and keep fundraising compliant.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Google Ad Grants can give eligible charities free search advertising, but the account needs careful structure and tracking to stay compliant and useful.
  • Fundraising and volunteer pages should be fast, focused and easy to complete on a phone, because that is where much charity traffic arrives.
  • UK GDPR, PECR and the Code of Fundraising Practice shape how charities collect data and ask for support, so check guidance before you launch campaigns.

Charity digital marketing in the UK works best when you focus on a few things done well: a search-friendly website with clear donation, volunteer and service pages, Google Ad Grants used properly if you are eligible, email that respects consent, and tracking that shows which activity raises money or reaches beneficiaries. Small teams get further by doing three channels well than by spreading thin across ten.

This guide is general and practical. It is not legal or regulatory advice; decisions on fundraising compliance rest with your trustees and any advisers you work with.

Start with goals, not channels

Charities usually juggle several goals at once: donations, regular giving, event sign-ups, volunteers, service users finding help, and campaigning. Each needs different pages and different measures. Write them down and rank them before choosing tactics, because a donation campaign and a helpline awareness campaign should be run quite differently.

Google Ad Grants: the basics

Google Ad Grants is a programme that gives eligible non-profits free search advertising credit on Google. In the UK, organisations typically apply through Google for Nonprofits, and eligibility, monthly limits and rules are set by Google and can change, so always check the current programme terms before planning around it.

Grant accounts come with conditions that ordinary Google Ads accounts do not have. Historically these have included a maximum bid cap (unless you use certain automated bidding strategies), a minimum click-through rate, rules on single-word and very generic keywords, and requirements for meaningful conversion tracking. Accounts that fall out of compliance can be suspended.

Making a grant account work

  • Structure around real intent: separate campaigns for services, volunteering, donations and information content.
  • Track meaningful conversions: donations, volunteer applications, sign-ups and calls, not just page views.
  • Prune poor keywords: remove terms with very low click-through rates that drag the account down.
  • Use helpful content: grant ads often perform well when they point to genuine advice pages that answer a question.
  • Review monthly: check compliance and performance regularly, not once a year.

Many charities also run a separate paid Google Ads account alongside the grant, for example to compete on high-value donation terms where the grant bid limits make it hard to appear. Paid budgets are separate from any management fee. Our guide to Google Ads agencies in the UK explains how paid accounts are typically managed.

Fundraising pages that convert

Most charity traffic now arrives on a phone, often from social media or email. A donation page that is slow or confusing loses people who have already decided to give.

  • Lead with one clear ask and one image that tells the story.
  • Explain what different amounts can help fund, but keep examples accurate and approved.
  • Offer one-off and monthly options, with the monthly choice explained simply.
  • Include a Gift Aid declaration where eligible, following HMRC wording requirements.
  • Offer familiar payment methods such as card, Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal if your platform supports them.
  • Keep the form short and say clearly how data will be used.

SEO for charities

Search is often the main way people in need find a charity. Someone searching "debt advice Birmingham" or "carers support group near me" is looking for help right now. Clear service pages, a well-maintained Google Business Profile for each location, and plain-English advice content can serve them well and build trust with donors at the same time.

Charities also tend to earn links naturally: local news, councils, partner organisations, universities and funders. Make sure those links point to useful pages, and fix technical issues so the site can be crawled easily. Our local SEO guide and SEO for small businesses article both apply closely to small charities.

Email, consent and the Fundraising Regulator

Email is often the most cost-effective way to keep supporters engaged, but charities should be careful with consent. In general terms:

  • PECR usually requires consent to send marketing emails, including fundraising appeals, to individuals. The soft opt-in for existing customers was historically limited to commercial sales; recent legislation has extended a version of it to charities, so check current ICO guidance before relying on it.
  • UK GDPR applies to how you collect, store and use supporter data. The ICO publishes guidance aimed at charities.
  • The Code of Fundraising Practice, overseen by the Fundraising Regulator in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, sets standards for fundraising, including treating donors fairly and respecting their wishes. Scotland has its own Scottish Fundraising Adjudication Panel.
  • The Fundraising Preference Service lets people stop contact from specific charities, and you should act on requests you receive.

Take advice from a specialist if you are unsure. It is far easier to set up sign-up forms correctly than to rebuild a list later.

Social media and content

Social channels work well for storytelling, events and community fundraising, but organic reach on many platforms is limited. Focus on one or two platforms where your supporters already are, post consistently, and use stories with consent from the people featured. Safeguarding comes first: avoid identifying vulnerable people without proper permission.

Longer-form content such as impact reports, guides and campaign pages can be repurposed into email and social posts. Our content marketing guide explains how to plan this efficiently.

Volunteer recruitment online

Volunteer searches are often local and specific: "volunteer at a food bank in Leeds" or "befriending volunteer near me". A dedicated page for each role, with location, time commitment, any checks required and a short application form, tends to perform far better than a single general volunteering page. These pages also suit Ad Grants campaigns, because the intent is clear and the conversion is easy to track.

Tell applicants what happens next and how long it usually takes. Slow follow-up is one of the most common reasons volunteers drop out.

Measuring what matters

Set up GA4 with events for donations, volunteer applications, sign-ups and key calls, and connect your donation platform where possible so you can see which channels raise money. Our GA4 setup guide explains the basics. Report to trustees on outcomes, not vanity metrics.

Share a short monthly dashboard with staff and trustees showing donations, volunteer applications and service enquiries by channel. Even simple reporting helps small teams decide where to spend limited time.

Charity digital marketing checklist

  • Agree and rank goals: donations, regular giving, volunteers, services.
  • Check eligibility and current terms for Google Ad Grants.
  • Track meaningful conversions in GA4 and the grant account.
  • Make donation pages fast, mobile-friendly and short.
  • Include Gift Aid wording where relevant.
  • Review consent wording against current ICO guidance.
  • Check fundraising activity against the Code of Fundraising Practice.
  • Claim and maintain Google Business Profiles for each location.
  • Publish clear service and advice pages for people seeking help.
  • Report outcomes to trustees quarterly.

FAQs

What does charity digital marketing in the UK involve?

It covers your website, search visibility, Google Ad Grants or paid ads, email, social media and tracking, all aimed at raising funds, recruiting volunteers and helping people find your services, within UK data and fundraising rules.

Is Google Ad Grants really free?

The advertising credit is free for eligible charities, but the account needs time and skill to manage and must meet Google's ongoing rules. Check the current programme terms for limits and requirements.

Can charities email supporters without consent?

Generally you need consent for marketing emails, including appeals. Rules have recently changed to extend a form of soft opt-in to charities, so check current ICO guidance or take advice before relying on it.

Who regulates charity fundraising in the UK?

The Fundraising Regulator oversees the Code of Fundraising Practice in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, while Scotland has its own arrangements. The Charity Commission and the ICO also have relevant roles.

What should a charity donation page include?

One clear ask, one-off and monthly options, a Gift Aid declaration where eligible, familiar payment methods, a short form and clear information on how data will be used.

How can a small charity improve its Google ranking?

Create clear pages for each service and location, maintain a Google Business Profile, publish helpful advice content and ask partners and local organisations to link to relevant pages.
