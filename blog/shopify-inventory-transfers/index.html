Shopify Inventory Transfers: Moving Stock Between Locations

How to use Shopify inventory transfers to move stock between shops, warehouses and stockrooms, with tips on receiving, counting and keeping records accurate.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Inventory transfers record stock moving between your Shopify locations so each location shows what it really holds.
  • Create the transfer when stock leaves, then receive it carefully at the destination, noting any shortfalls or damage.
  • Clear location names, regular counts and one person responsible for transfers keep the numbers trustworthy.

If you sell from more than one place, stock rarely stays where it started. Products arrive at a warehouse or back room, get moved to a shop floor, travel to a pop-up market and sometimes come back again. Each time stock moves, the numbers in Shopify need to follow it. Otherwise your website may promise items that are sitting in the wrong building, or your shop staff may turn customers away when the item is in a box down the road.

Shopify handles this through locations and inventory transfers. This guide explains what transfers are, when to use them, how to create and receive them, and the habits that keep multi-location stock accurate for UK small businesses running shops, stockrooms or small warehouses.

What locations and transfers mean in Shopify

A location in Shopify is any place where you keep stock, fulfil orders or sell in person. That might be a retail shop, a home office, a rented storage unit or a warehouse. Each location keeps its own inventory count for every product variant you stock there.

An inventory transfer is a record of stock moving from one location to another. Rather than simply lowering the count in one place and raising it in another by hand, a transfer ties the two changes together. It shows what was sent, where it went, and what actually arrived. That paper trail is useful when numbers do not add up later.

Shopify has developed its transfer tools over time, and the exact screens and options can vary by plan and by whether you use point of sale. It is worth checking the current help documentation for your plan, but the principles below apply whichever version you see.

When you need inventory transfers

Transfers are useful whenever stock physically moves between two places that Shopify tracks separately. Typical situations include:

  • Restocking a shop from a back room or warehouse.
  • Sending stock to a temporary location such as a market stall, event or pop-up.
  • Rebalancing stock between two shops when one sells a line faster than the other.
  • Bringing unsold stock back to a central store after a season or event.
  • Moving stock to a new location when you open a site or close one.

If you only have one location, you do not need transfers. Incoming stock from suppliers is a different process, usually handled by adjusting inventory or through purchase order style records, depending on how your store is set up.

Setting up your locations first

Transfers only work well if your locations are set up clearly. Before moving any stock, review your location list in Shopify settings.

Use names your team will recognise

Give each location a plain, specific name. "High Street shop" and "Unit 4 stockroom" are easier to work with than "Location 2". Staff creating or receiving transfers should be able to tell at a glance where stock is going.

Decide which locations fulfil online orders

Each location can be set to fulfil online orders or not. A stockroom might ship web orders while a shop only sells in person. These settings affect which stock counts towards what customers can buy on your website, so think carefully before changing them. Our guide to Shopify inventory management covers the wider setup.

Check product tracking

Inventory tracking needs to be switched on for each variant you want to transfer. Products that are not tracked will not show meaningful counts, so a transfer cannot keep them accurate. It is a good moment to tidy up variants too; our article on Shopify product variants explains how to structure them.

How to create an inventory transfer

The general flow in Shopify admin looks like this, although wording may differ slightly:

  1. Go to the inventory or transfers area in your Shopify admin.
  2. Create a new transfer and choose the origin location, where stock is leaving, and the destination location, where it is going.
  3. Add the products and variants being moved, with the quantity of each.
  4. Add a reference or note if helpful, such as the date, the van run or the event name.
  5. Save the transfer, and mark it as ready to ship or in transit when the stock physically leaves.

Try to create the transfer at the moment stock is packed, not days afterwards from memory. Counting items into a box while entering them into the transfer is the simplest way to avoid mistakes.

Using barcodes

If your products have barcodes, scanning them while building the transfer is faster and more reliable than searching by name. This is especially helpful when variants look similar, such as the same top in several sizes. Adding barcodes to variants is worth the effort if you move stock often.

Receiving a transfer at the destination

Receiving is where accuracy is won or lost. When the stock arrives, someone at the destination should open the transfer in Shopify and count what is actually in the box against what was sent.

  • Accept items that arrived in the right quantity and condition.
  • Record shortfalls if fewer items arrived than were sent.
  • Reject or note items that arrived damaged, so they are not counted as sellable stock.

Once received, the destination location's inventory increases by the quantity accepted. You can usually receive a transfer in stages if stock arrives in more than one delivery.

Resist the temptation to receive everything with one click without counting. If a box went missing or a size was swapped, accepting the full quantity hides the problem and your counts drift away from reality.

What happens to stock while it is in transit

Between sending and receiving, stock is neither available at the origin nor at the destination. Depending on your setup, Shopify may show it as incoming at the destination. This matters for online availability: if a product's only stock is in transit, your website may show it as out of stock until it is received.

For short moves, such as across a car park, this barely matters. For longer moves, think about timing. Moving your best seller on a busy Saturday morning could make it briefly unavailable online. Planning transfers for quieter times can avoid that.

Common transfer problems and how to avoid them

Stock moved without a transfer

The most frequent source of trouble is someone carrying stock to another location without recording it. The origin still shows the stock, the destination does not, and both counts are wrong. Make it a rule that nothing leaves a location without a transfer.

Transfers left open

Transfers that are created but never received leave stock stuck in limbo. Review open transfers regularly and chase any that should have arrived.

Manual adjustments that fight the transfer

If staff adjust counts by hand to fix a number, and then the transfer is received as well, the stock can be counted twice. Agree on who is allowed to make manual adjustments and ask them to add a reason each time. Our guide to Shopify staff accounts and permissions explains how to limit who can change inventory.

Wrong variant selected

Picking the wrong size or colour when building the transfer leads to one variant being overstated and another understated. Barcodes help, and so does a quick second check before marking the transfer as sent.

Transfers and point of sale

If you sell in person with Shopify POS, accurate transfers are what let staff check stock at another location for a customer. When counts are reliable, a shop assistant can say with confidence that an item is available at your other site, or arrange to have it moved. Our article on Shopify POS for UK retail covers in-person selling in more detail.

Depending on your setup, staff may be able to create or receive transfers from the POS app as well as the admin. Check what your plan allows and train shop staff on the receiving step in particular.

Keeping records you can rely on

Transfers form part of your stock history. Good habits make that history useful:

  • Add a short note to each transfer explaining why it happened.
  • Run regular stock counts at each location and compare them with Shopify.
  • Investigate gaps promptly while people still remember what happened.
  • Keep one person responsible for checking open transfers each week.

Accurate stock records can also matter for your accounts and year-end stock valuation. How stock should be valued and reported depends on your circumstances, so speak to your accountant about what records they need.

FAQs

Do I need inventory transfers if I only have one shop?

No. Transfers are for moving stock between two or more Shopify locations. With a single location, you simply adjust inventory when stock arrives or is written off.

Can I edit a transfer after creating it?

Usually you can edit a transfer before it is marked as sent or received. Once stock has been received, it is generally better to record a correction than to rewrite history. Check Shopify's current documentation for your plan.

Why does my product show out of stock online when I have stock?

The stock may be at a location that does not fulfil online orders, or it may be in transit on an open transfer. Check each location's settings and any open transfers.

Can I transfer stock to a pop-up or market stall?

Yes, if you set the pop-up up as a location. Transfer stock out before the event and transfer any unsold items back afterwards, so each location stays accurate.

What should I do if items arrive damaged?

Do not accept damaged items as sellable stock. Record them as rejected or note the damage when receiving, then decide separately whether to write them off or return them.

How often should I count stock at each location?

There is no single right answer. Many businesses count fast-moving lines more often than slow ones. Choose a rhythm you can keep up and compare results against Shopify each time.
