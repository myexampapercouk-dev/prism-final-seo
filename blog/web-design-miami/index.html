Web Design Miami: How Remote Website Design Works for Miami Businesses

Need web design in Miami? A remote studio can plan, design and build a fast, bilingual-ready website for your Miami business. See the costs and how it works.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Good web design for a Miami business starts with clear goals, mobile-first layouts, fast pages and content planned for both English and Spanish speakers where that fits your audience.
  • Miami's hospitality, real estate, health and trade businesses all need sites that load quickly, explain services clearly and make booking or enquiring easy.
  • We are a UK-based studio designing websites remotely, with small sites from £1,000 to £2,000, builds typically in three to five weeks and other currencies quoted on request.

If you are looking for web design in Miami, the right partner is the team that can build a fast, clear website that turns visitors into bookings, calls and enquiries, wherever that team is based. A remote studio can plan, design and build sites for Miami restaurants, hotels, real estate firms, clinics, contractors and professional services, as long as the process is well organised and grounded in your customers.

We are a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for US businesses. We have no Miami office and no US office. This guide explains what web design for a Miami business should include, how to plan for a bilingual audience, how a remote project runs and what it costs.

What web design in Miami should include

Miami's economy leans on tourism, hospitality, real estate, international trade, finance, healthcare and a strong creative scene, alongside the trades and home services every growing city needs. Whatever the sector, a good website project usually covers the same ground:

  • Discovery: goals, audiences, services, competitors and what a good enquiry looks like.
  • Site structure: a clear page plan, so visitors and search engines can find each service quickly.
  • Design: layouts built for mobile first, with a look that suits your brand and your market.
  • Content: clear copy for each page, written or refined with your input.
  • Build: a fast, secure site on a platform you can update yourself.
  • SEO foundations: titles, headings, structured data, redirects and a clean technical set-up.
  • Launch and handover: testing, go-live and training, with all logins in your name.

Most Miami visitors will arrive on a phone, often while out and about, comparing options or looking for directions. Our guide to mobile website design explains what that means for layouts, buttons and page speed.

Designing for a bilingual Miami audience

Spanish is widely spoken across Miami-Dade, and many businesses serve customers who prefer English, Spanish or both. If that applies to you, the website should be planned for it from the start rather than patched later. That usually means:

  • Deciding scope: whether the whole site needs both languages, or only key service and contact pages.
  • Proper translation: copy written or reviewed by fluent speakers rather than relying on automatic translation alone.
  • Clear language switching: an obvious switcher that keeps visitors on the equivalent page.
  • Separate URLs per language: so search engines can index and show the right version to the right searcher.
  • Layouts with room to grow: Spanish text often runs longer than English, so buttons and headings need flexibility.

Our guide to multilingual website design covers language structure, translation workflow and SEO in more depth. Some Miami businesses also serve buyers across Latin America and the Caribbean, which can widen the plan to cover currency notes, time zones and international contact options.

Think about forms and follow-up as well. If enquiries arrive in Spanish, someone on your team needs to be ready to reply in Spanish, and confirmation emails, booking reminders and error messages should match the language the visitor chose. Small details like these make a bilingual site feel genuinely welcoming rather than half-translated.

Speed, accessibility and trust

A Miami website has to work hard in a crowded market. Three qualities matter more than any design trend:

  • Speed: compressed images, lean code and good hosting, so pages load quickly on mobile data.
  • Accessibility: readable text, good contrast, keyboard-friendly navigation, alt text and properly labelled forms.
  • Trust: clear contact details, real photography where possible, transparent pricing or process information and genuine reviews.

Accessibility is good practice for every visitor, and US businesses often ask about the Americans with Disabilities Act. Requirements and how they apply to websites can vary, so check official guidance or speak to your own adviser about your obligations. Our guide to website accessibility explains the practical design and build steps we follow. Privacy rules, cookie notices and any sales tax on online sales also vary by state, so get advice on anything specific to your business.

We use AI-powered tools to speed up parts of the process, such as first drafts of page outlines, image optimisation and testing checklists. Every design decision and every page of copy is reviewed by a person before it goes anywhere near your live site.

How a remote web design project works for a Miami business

Miami is on Eastern Time, usually five hours behind the UK, which gives a comfortable overlap from your morning into our afternoon. Designs are shared as clickable previews, feedback is gathered in writing and the build happens on a private staging site you can check at any time. A typical project runs like this:

  1. Discovery call: goals, audiences, languages, pages and examples you like.
  2. Proposal and plan: scope, page list, timeline and price, agreed in writing.
  3. Content gathering: copy, photos, logos and any existing assets.
  4. Design: homepage and key page designs, refined with your feedback.
  5. Build and testing: development on a staging site, checked across devices and browsers.
  6. Launch and handover: go-live, redirects from any old site, training and documentation.

Domain, hosting and every login stay in your name. Our guide to website ownership and handover explains what you should receive at the end of any project, and our article on how to hire an overseas web design agency covers contracts, payments and communication.

How much web design costs for a Miami business

Prices depend on the number of pages, features, languages and integrations. Our fees are in pounds, and we can quote US dollars or other currencies on request:

  • Small website: £1,000 to £2,000, suited to service businesses with a focused set of pages.
  • Ecommerce website: £1,500 to £3,500, depending on products, payments and integrations.
  • Timeline: websites typically take three to five weeks, and ecommerce builds five to twelve weeks.
  • Ongoing support: optional monthly retainers from £350 a month for updates, SEO and marketing.

FAQs

How do I choose a web design company in Miami?

Look for a team that starts with discovery, agrees scope and price in writing, shows designs before building, tests on mobile and hands over every login in your name. Ask how they handle content, languages and launch.

Do you have an office in Miami?

No. We are a UK-based studio and work remotely with US businesses. We have no Miami office or any other US office, and we work through video calls, shared previews and written feedback.

Can you build a website in English and Spanish?

Yes. We can plan and build a site with English and Spanish versions, with separate pages for each language and a clear switcher. Translation should be written or reviewed by fluent speakers.

How long does web design in Miami take?

Most service websites take three to five weeks, and ecommerce builds take five to twelve weeks. Bilingual content and late content delivery can extend the timeline.

How much does a website cost for a Miami business?

Our small websites are £1,000 to £2,000, and ecommerce sites are £1,500 to £3,500. Prices are in pounds, and we can quote US dollars or other currencies on request.

Will my new website meet ADA requirements?

We follow accessibility good practice in design and build, but we cannot give legal advice on the ADA. Check official guidance or speak to your own adviser about what applies to your business.
