How to Set Up Enhanced Conversions in Google Ads: A UK Guide

Enhanced conversions in Google Ads use hashed first-party data to measure conversions more accurately. Learn how to set them up, test them and stay compliant.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Enhanced conversions send hashed customer details, such as an email address, alongside a conversion to help Google match it to an ad click.
  • You can set them up through the Google tag, Google Tag Manager or an API or upload, depending on your site and how leads are handled.
  • Your privacy policy, consent set-up and customer data terms must support it, so check current Google and ICO guidance first.

To set up enhanced conversions in Google Ads, turn the feature on in your conversion settings, accept Google's customer data terms, then choose how customer details such as email addresses will be collected from your conversion page: automatically by the Google tag, through Google Tag Manager, or via code. The data is hashed before it is sent, and Google uses it to match more conversions back to ad clicks.

For UK small businesses, enhanced conversions can help fill gaps left by browser privacy changes and cookie consent, which means your bidding and reporting are based on more complete data. This guide explains what they are, the set-up options, how to test them and the privacy points to check. Google changes the interface and requirements from time to time, so always follow the current Google Ads help pages for exact steps.

What enhanced conversions are

Standard Google Ads conversion tracking relies on information stored in the browser when someone clicks an ad. If that information is lost, for example because cookies are blocked or the visitor changes device, the conversion may not be linked back to the click.

Enhanced conversions add another way to make that link. When a conversion happens, such as a form submission or purchase, the tag collects first-party details the customer has just given you, typically an email address and sometimes a phone number, name or address. These are hashed using a one-way algorithm before being sent, and Google compares them with hashed data from signed-in Google accounts to attribute the conversion.

There are two main forms:

TypeWhat it coversTypical use
Enhanced conversions for webConversions that happen on your website, such as purchases or form fillsOnline shops, service businesses with enquiry forms
Enhanced conversions for leadsLeads captured on your site that convert later offline, such as a signed contractB2B, trades and services with a sales process after the enquiry

If you have not set up basic tracking yet, start with our Google Ads conversion tracking guide. Enhanced conversions build on top of working conversion actions.

Before you start

  • Working conversion actions. Your existing conversions should already be recording correctly.
  • Customer details on the conversion. The conversion must involve the customer giving you an email address or similar, so a click on a phone number alone will not work.
  • Privacy policy and consent. Your privacy notice should explain that you share data with advertising partners for measurement, and your consent set-up should reflect that.
  • Customer data terms. Google requires you to accept its customer data policies before enhanced conversions can be turned on.

How to set up enhanced conversions in Google Ads

Choose the method that matches how your tracking is installed.

MethodBest forEffort
Google tag, automatic detectionSimple sites where the email is visible on the page or in a form when the conversion firesLow, but less control
Google tag, manual with code or CSS selectorsSites where automatic detection misses the dataModerate, may need a developer
Google Tag ManagerSites already using GTM for Ads tagsModerate, flexible and testable
Google Ads API or uploadEnhanced conversions for leads or larger set-upsHigher, usually involves a CRM

A typical Tag Manager set-up looks like this:

  1. In Google Ads, go to the conversion settings and turn on enhanced conversions, accepting the terms.
  2. Choose Google Tag Manager as the method.
  3. In Tag Manager, open your Google Ads conversion tag and enable the option to include user-provided data.
  4. Create a user-provided data variable. Choose automatic collection, or map fields manually using data layer values or page elements such as the email input.
  5. Make sure the data is available at the moment the conversion tag fires. If your thank you page no longer contains the email, push it into the data layer when the form submits.
  6. Preview, test and publish.

Our Google Tag Manager setup guide covers the basics of containers, triggers and the data layer if any of those terms are unfamiliar.

Enhanced conversions for leads

If your real goal is a sale that happens after an enquiry, such as a quote accepted over the phone, enhanced conversions for leads can help. The basic idea is:

  1. When someone submits your website form, the tag sends a hashed email address with the lead conversion.
  2. Your CRM keeps the same email address with the lead record.
  3. When the lead becomes a customer, you upload or send that conversion, with the hashed email, to Google Ads.
  4. Google matches it back to the original click and reports it as an offline conversion.

This gives bidding a much better signal than form fills alone, especially for B2B or high-value services where many enquiries never turn into work. It does require a reasonably organised CRM and a consistent way of recording lead status.

How to test enhanced conversions

  • Use Tag Manager Preview to complete a test conversion and check the conversion tag includes user-provided data.
  • Check the diagnostics area for enhanced conversions in Google Ads. It reports whether data is being received and flags common issues. Allow time for it to update.
  • Make sure the email captured is the customer's, not a placeholder or your own test address left in the code.
  • Confirm conversions are not being counted twice after the change.

Privacy and consent in the UK

Enhanced conversions involve sending personal data, even though it is hashed, to Google for advertising measurement. That has implications under UK GDPR and PECR. In general you should:

  • Explain in your privacy notice that you share data with advertising partners for measurement. Our guide to website terms and privacy policies explains what these documents usually cover.
  • Respect the choices people make in your cookie banner, including through Consent Mode settings where you use them.
  • Only send data you are entitled to use for this purpose.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the ICO's current guidance and Google's current customer data policies, and take advice if you are unsure. If you work in a regulated sector, approval of how customer data is used rests with your own compliance team.

Is it worth it for a small business?

For most UK small businesses already running Google Ads with conversion tracking, enhanced conversions are worth considering. The set-up is usually a few hours of work, and better data helps automated bidding strategies make better decisions. Our guide to Google Ads bidding strategies explains why conversion data quality matters so much for Smart Bidding.

It is less useful if very few of your conversions involve a customer giving their details, such as accounts that rely mainly on calls from ads. In that case, focus on call tracking and keeping your core conversion actions clean.

Common enhanced conversions mistakes

  • Turning it on without mapping any data. The setting alone does nothing if the tag cannot find an email address when the conversion fires.
  • Capturing the wrong field. Automatic detection can occasionally pick up a newsletter box or a staff address in the footer instead of the customer's details.
  • Ignoring diagnostics warnings. If Google reports low match rates or missing data, investigate rather than assuming it will fix itself.
  • Forgetting to update privacy documents. The technical set-up and your published privacy notice should tell the same story.

FAQs

How do I set up enhanced conversions in Google Ads?

Turn on enhanced conversions in your conversion settings, accept the customer data terms, pick a method such as the Google tag or Google Tag Manager, and make sure customer details like email address are available when the conversion fires. Then test and check the diagnostics.

Is the data sent to Google hashed?

Yes. The customer data is hashed with a one-way algorithm before it is sent. You still need to cover this in your privacy notice and consent set-up.

Do enhanced conversions replace standard conversion tracking?

No. They add to your existing conversion actions to help more conversions be matched. You still need a working conversion tag.

Will enhanced conversions work for phone calls?

Not usually on their own, because a phone call does not involve the customer typing an email address into your website. Calls are tracked through call conversion actions instead.

How long does it take to see data?

The diagnostics area typically needs some days of conversions before it shows a clear status. Low-volume accounts may need longer to see meaningful information.

Do I need a developer?

Not always. Automatic detection or Tag Manager can work without code changes on simple sites. If your form does not expose the email when the conversion fires, a developer may need to add it to the data layer.
