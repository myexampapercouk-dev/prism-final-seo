Beauty Salon Marketing UK: How to Fill Your Booking Diary

Beauty salon marketing UK guide: local SEO, Google Business Profile, Instagram, paid ads and rebooking tactics that help salons fill appointments.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Salon clients search locally and judge on photos and reviews, so your Google Business Profile matters most.
  • Make booking effortless: one tap from Instagram, Google or your website to a confirmed appointment.
  • Rebooking and retention fill more of the diary than chasing new clients alone.

Beauty salon marketing in the UK works best when it makes your salon easy to find locally, easy to trust from photos and reviews, and easy to book in a few taps. For most salons, that means a strong Google Business Profile, a mobile-friendly website with online booking, consistent Instagram content and a rebooking routine that keeps loyal clients coming back.

This guide is for owners of beauty salons, nail bars, lash and brow studios, aesthetics-adjacent beauty businesses and hair salons who want a practical plan rather than a long list of platforms to post on.

What makes salon marketing different

Beauty services are visual, personal and repeat. A client chooses based on the look of your work, how the salon feels, what others say about you and whether there is a slot that suits them. Once they trust you, they can return for years. That means marketing has two jobs: winning first appointments and protecting the relationship afterwards.

Competition is usually local. A client wants a salon close to home or work, so you are often competing with a handful of businesses within a few miles, plus mobile therapists and home-based studios. Clear positioning helps: specialist treatments, a calm environment, late opening, a particular style or a strong reputation for one service.

Google Business Profile and local search come first

Searches like "nail salon near me" or "lash lift in [town]" lead straight to the map results. A strong profile includes:

  • The most accurate primary category, such as beauty salon, nail salon or hair salon, plus relevant secondary ones.
  • A full list of services with clear descriptions.
  • Recent, well-lit photos of your work and the salon itself.
  • A booking link that goes straight to your booking system.
  • Regular reviews, with polite replies to all of them.

A website that books, not just informs

Many salons rely on social media alone, but a website gives you something you own, pages that can rank for each treatment and a place to show prices and policies clearly. A good salon website usually has:

  • A page for each main treatment, with what it involves, how long it takes and aftercare notes.
  • Prices or "from" prices, so clients are not surprised.
  • Booking buttons on every page, linked to your booking system.
  • Clear policies on deposits, cancellations and patch tests where relevant.
  • Fast loading and simple navigation on mobile.

Instagram and short video

Instagram remains a natural showcase for beauty work, and short video on Reels and TikTok can reach new local clients. Content that tends to work includes:

  • Short process videos showing a treatment from start to finish.
  • Close-up photos of finished work in good natural light.
  • Explainers answering common questions, such as how long a lash lift lasts or how to look after gel nails.
  • Glimpses of the salon atmosphere and your team, if they are happy to appear.

Only post client photos with clear permission, and keep edited images honest. Filters that change how a result looks can mislead and may breach advertising rules. Put the booking link in your bio and make it one tap to a slot.

Paid ads to fill quiet days

Paid social is useful for filling midweek gaps, promoting a new treatment or building a list of local clients. Target a sensible radius, use real photos or video of your work and send people to a booking page rather than a general homepage. Track bookings, not just clicks.

Google Ads can also work for higher-value treatments where people search with clear intent. Keep location targeting tight and use negative keywords to avoid training courses or product searches. Rebooking, reminders and retention

The fastest way to a full diary is often your existing clients. Practical habits include:

  • Offering to book the next appointment before the client leaves.
  • Sending reminders timed to each treatment, such as infills or root touch-ups.
  • A simple loyalty or referral offer that rewards regulars.
  • A short follow-up message after a first visit asking how they found it and inviting a review.

Email and SMS marketing must follow UK GDPR and PECR, so collect clear consent and make it easy to opt out. Appointment reminders are different from marketing messages, but it is still sensible to explain how you use contact details.

Compliance points to keep in mind

Some treatments carry specific rules, for example around patch testing, age restrictions or local licensing for certain procedures. Advertising claims about results should be honest and evidenced, in line with the CAP Code. This guide is general information only; the salon owner is responsible for checking the rules that apply to their treatments and signing off any claims. If you offer non-surgical aesthetic treatments, take extra care, as rules in this area have been changing.

Measuring progress

Track bookings by source where your booking system allows, new versus returning clients, rebooking rate, no-shows and revenue per available hour. Google Analytics 4 can track booking button clicks and completed bookings if set up properly; see our GA4 setup guide. SEO and paid results typically take three to six months to settle.

A simple monthly marketing routine

Salon owners are busy, so a routine you can keep up beats an ambitious plan you abandon. A realistic monthly rhythm might look like this:

  1. Week one: check your diary for the coming month and identify quiet days or treatments with spare capacity.
  2. Week two: plan and batch-create a few weeks of social content, focusing on the treatments you want to fill.
  3. Week three: add new photos and a post to your Google Business Profile, and reply to any outstanding reviews.
  4. Week four: review bookings by source, rebooking rate and ad results, then adjust next month's plan.

Treat seasonal moments as planning anchors. Wedding season, school proms, Christmas parties and summer holidays all shift demand for particular treatments. Promote the right services a few weeks before demand peaks, not on the day.

Common salon marketing mistakes

  • Relying only on Instagram. If your account has a problem or reach falls, enquiries can dry up. A website and Google profile give you more stable foundations.
  • Hiding prices. Clients who cannot see a rough price often move on to a salon that shows one.
  • Too many steps to book. Every extra click or message loses people. Link directly to a booking page.
  • Discounting by default. Constant discounts can attract bargain hunters who never rebook. Use offers for specific purposes, such as quiet slots or new treatments.
  • Ignoring reviews. Unanswered reviews, especially critical ones, can put off new clients.

FAQs

What is the best beauty salon marketing UK approach for a new salon?

Set up a complete Google Business Profile with a booking link, build a simple website with treatment pages, post your work consistently on Instagram and ask every happy client for a review. Add paid ads once booking and follow-up work smoothly.

Do salons need a website if they have Instagram?

A website is strongly recommended. You own it, it can rank in Google for treatment searches and it gives clients a clear place to see prices, policies and booking.

How do I get more salon bookings on quiet days?

Use targeted Meta ads or messages to existing clients promoting specific slots, and make rebooking part of every appointment so the diary fills further ahead.

How do I get more Google reviews for my salon?

Ask at the end of a good appointment and follow up with a short link. Do not offer incentives for reviews, and reply to every review politely.

Can I use before and after photos in salon ads?

Often yes, with the client's clear permission and without editing that misrepresents results. Advertising rules apply, so check claims and leave final sign-off with the salon.
