What Dundee businesses need from a website

Dundee has reinvented itself over recent decades. The waterfront regeneration, the V&A Dundee museum, a well-known games and digital sector, two universities, a strong life sciences presence and a growing visitor economy sit alongside traditional trades, retailers, care services and professional firms. Whatever the sector, the website basics are similar.

  • Mobile speed. Most local searches happen on phones, and a slow page loses people quickly.
  • Plain-language service pages. One focused page per main service helps visitors find answers and helps search engines understand what you offer.
  • A clear next step. Tap-to-call, a short form or a booking link, placed where people look for it.
  • Credible trust signals. Real photography, accurate contact details and accreditations you genuinely hold.
  • Room to grow. A structure that lets you add services, locations or an online shop later without starting again.

Local SEO for Dundee searches

People search for phrases like "electrician Dundee", "café Broughty Ferry" or "accountant Tayside". We build every site with local SEO foundations so it has a fair chance of appearing for those searches:

  • Titles and headings using the words customers actually type.
  • Consistent business name, address and phone details.

  • Fast, mobile-friendly templates.

Scotland-specific points to keep in mind

Most website rules are UK-wide, but a few practical points are worth noting for Scottish businesses. Some legal, property and professional services work differently under Scots law, so service descriptions should reflect how things work in Scotland rather than borrowing wording from English templates. If you serve customers on both sides of the border, make that clear. Keep it general on the site and check specific wording with your own adviser or professional body.

The data protection and electronic marketing rules are UK-wide:

  • UK GDPR. If your site collects personal data, you need a clear privacy notice explaining what you collect and why.
  • PECR. Non-essential cookies, such as analytics and advertising tags, generally need consent before they load, and marketing emails have separate consent rules.
  • Accessibility. Good contrast, readable text, keyboard navigation and descriptive image text help everyone use the site.
  • Sector rules. Regulated sectors have extra rules on website claims, and approval of that wording rests with you.

The Information Commissioner's Office publishes official guidance on data protection and cookies, and it is the best place to start.

How to prepare for your website project

The fastest projects are the ones where the groundwork is done early. Before the discovery call, it helps to gather a few things together. None of this needs to be perfect, but having it ready saves weeks later on.

  • A list of your services. Write down every service you offer, which ones earn the most and which ones you want more of.
  • Your best customers. Describe who they are, where they come from and the questions they ask before they buy.
  • Competitor websites. Note two or three local competitors and what you like or dislike about their sites.
  • Photos and logos. Real photos of your premises, team and work are far more convincing than stock images.
  • Access details. Make sure you can log in to your domain registrar, current hosting and Google Business Profile, or know who can.
  • Existing content. If you have brochures, price lists or old web pages, share them so useful material is not lost.

We will turn this into a written brief that sets out the pages, features and timeline, so everyone agrees on the scope before design starts.

Measuring whether the website works

A website should be judged on what it does for the business, not only on how it looks. We set up analytics and conversion tracking at launch, with consent respected, so you can see how many people visit, where they come from and how many go on to call, enquire, book or buy. If a key page attracts visits but few enquiries, that is usually the first place to improve the copy, layout or call to action.
