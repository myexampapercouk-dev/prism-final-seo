Shopify for UK Artists and Print Sellers

How UK artists and print sellers can sell on Shopify: originals vs editions, sizes and framing, print on demand, product pages, packaging, VAT and SEO.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Originals, limited editions and open editions behave differently in stock and pricing, so model them deliberately in Shopify.
  • Size and framing options are the main variants for prints, and shoppers need help picturing scale on a wall.
  • Your own Shopify store gives more control over presentation and customer relationships than a marketplace, alongside the extra work of driving traffic.

Selling art online is different from selling most products. A one-off original painting cannot be restocked. A limited edition print has a fixed number of copies, and buyers care about which number they receive. Open edition prints may be produced on demand in several sizes, with or without frames. And buyers are often making an emotional, considered purchase, wanting to understand the work, the artist and exactly how it will look in their home.

This guide covers how UK artists, illustrators, photographers and print studios can set up a Shopify store that handles these differences well. It focuses on structure and presentation rather than any particular art style.

Why your own store, and how it fits with marketplaces

Many artists start on marketplaces such as Etsy, which bring built-in traffic. Your own Shopify store offers more control over design, presentation, pricing structure and customer relationships, and lets you build a brand around your work rather than within a marketplace's listing format. The trade-off is that you need to bring visitors yourself through search, social media, email and other channels.

Many sellers run both. If you are thinking about moving across, our guide to Wix vs Shopify and Shopify vs Squarespace may help you compare platforms, as portfolio-style builders are a common alternative for artists.

Originals, limited editions and open editions

The most important structural decision is how to model the different kinds of work you sell.

  • Originals. Each original is a single product with a stock quantity of one. Once sold, you can keep the page live and marked as sold to preserve the portfolio and its search value, or hide it. Showing sold work can demonstrate demand and range, but make sure shoppers can easily find what is still available.
  • Limited editions. The edition size sets a fixed stock level. State the edition size, paper, printing method and whether prints are signed and numbered. If buyers can choose a specific number, that needs custom handling; most sellers allocate numbers in order.
  • Open editions. These can be reprinted, so stock may be unlimited or managed per size. They usually sit at a lower price point and suit a wider range of sizes and framing options.

Collections can separate these clearly, such as originals, limited editions and prints, while tags or metafields store details like medium, year, edition size and dimensions. Be accurate: do not describe an edition as limited unless it genuinely is, and do not reissue a limited edition in the same form.

Sizes and framing as variants

For prints, size and framing are typically the main options. Shopify variants handle combinations such as A4 unframed, A3 framed in black and so on. A few practical points:

  • Keep options manageable. Every size multiplied by every frame colour can create a long list. Offer the combinations that make sense rather than every possibility.
  • Give exact dimensions. State the paper size, image size and, for framed prints, the outer frame size including any mount.
  • Update images per variant. When a shopper selects a framed option, show that framed version.
  • Explain aspect ratios. Not every artwork fits standard paper sizes, so say whether there will be a border.

Our guide to product variants and bundles covers how to structure options cleanly.

Helping buyers picture the work at home

Scale is a common uncertainty when buying art online. A print that looks large on a phone screen may be smaller than expected. Ways to help include:

  • Room setting images showing the work on a wall with familiar objects for scale.
  • Photos of the actual print or original, including close-ups of texture, paper and brushwork.
  • A simple size comparison showing each print size side by side.
  • Clear notes on colour, since screens display colours differently.

If you use mock-up images rather than photos of the actual piece in a room, it is fair to make that clear. Our product photography guide covers capturing work accurately.

Telling the story behind the work

Art buyers often want to connect with the artist and the piece. Product descriptions can cover what inspired the work, the materials and process, and anything that makes it distinctive, alongside the practical details. An about page that introduces the artist or studio, the way you work and where you are based in the UK helps buyers feel confident. Our guides to writing an about us page and product descriptions offer structure.

Print on demand vs printing yourself

Artists generally choose between producing prints themselves or through a local printer and holding stock, or using a print on demand service connected to Shopify that prints and ships each order. Each approach has trade-offs:

  • Holding stock gives control over quality, packaging and presentation, but ties up money and needs storage and time for fulfilment.
  • Print on demand avoids stock and can offer many sizes, but you have less control over quality and packaging, margins differ, and you rely on a third party for delivery times.

If you use print on demand, order samples to check quality, and make sure delivery times shown on your site reflect the service's production and shipping times. Some artists use a mix, fulfilling originals and limited editions themselves while open editions are printed on demand.

Packaging and delivery

Art needs careful packaging. Prints are usually shipped flat with rigid backing or rolled in tubes, and framed work and originals need more protection, especially glass. Shopify shipping profiles let you set different rates for small prints, large framed pieces and originals. Consider whether to offer insured or tracked delivery for higher value items. Our guides to packaging and unboxing and delivery and returns pages go into detail.

On returns, buyers usually have cancellation rights for online purchases, though exceptions can apply, for example to items made to the customer's specification. Check current consumer rules and set out your policy clearly.

Selling internationally

Art can appeal to buyers abroad, and Shopify supports multiple currencies and international shipping. Sending framed work overseas can be costly and risky, so some artists offer international delivery only on unframed or rolled prints. Customs, duties and VAT can apply when shipping outside the UK. See our guide to selling internationally from a UK online store, and take advice on your tax position.

A note on VAT and copyright

VAT treatment of works of art can involve specific rules, and whether you need to register depends on your turnover and circumstances. Speak to an accountant or check current HMRC guidance rather than assuming. Similarly, if you sell prints of work that includes others' imagery, characters or trademarks, check you have the rights to do so. As the creator, you generally hold copyright in your own original work, and selling a print does not usually transfer it, but take advice if you plan licensing arrangements.

SEO for art and print stores

People search for art in many ways: by subject, such as coastal prints; by style, such as abstract or botanical; by room, such as kitchen wall art; by medium; and by location-based subjects, such as prints of particular UK cities or landscapes. Collection pages organised around these themes, with genuine introductory text, can match that intent.

  • Give each artwork a descriptive title rather than only a poetic one, or include descriptive terms in the page title.
  • Write alt text describing what each image actually shows.
  • Avoid duplicate descriptions across sizes and editions of the same work.
  • Keep sold originals live where they still help visitors and search, with clear links to available work.

FAQs

Is Shopify good for selling art prints?

Yes, Shopify handles size and framing variants, limited stock for editions and shipping profiles well. Presentation and imagery matter as much as the platform.

How do I sell limited edition prints on Shopify?

Set stock to the edition size and state the edition size, paper and signing details clearly. Do not reissue the same limited edition once sold out.

Should I keep sold originals on my website?

Many artists keep sold work visible as a portfolio, clearly marked as sold. Make sure available pieces are easy to find.

Can I use print on demand with Shopify?

Yes, print on demand services can connect to Shopify. Order samples to check quality and show realistic delivery times.

Do I need to charge VAT on art?

It depends on your turnover and circumstances, and works of art can involve specific VAT rules. Check current HMRC guidance or speak to an accountant.
