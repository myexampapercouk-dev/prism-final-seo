Shopify Website Cost UK: Build, Theme, Apps and Running Costs Explained

Shopify website cost UK: what drives the price of a build, which costs are one-off or monthly, and why themes, apps and plans are quoted separately.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Shopify website has two kinds of cost: the one-off build and the ongoing running costs of plan, apps and payments.
  • Our ecommerce builds typically cost £1,500 to £3,500, with Shopify plan, theme and app costs quoted separately because they vary.
  • The biggest price drivers are catalogue size, design complexity, integrations and data migration, not the platform itself.

A Shopify website in the UK has two parts to its cost: the one-off build, which at Prism Works Studio typically falls between £1,500 and £3,500 for an ecommerce site, and ongoing running costs such as the Shopify plan, any paid theme, apps and payment fees. Those third-party costs vary with your choices and your sales, so we quote them separately rather than bundling them into one number.

This guide explains what you are paying for, what pushes the price up or down, and how to budget without nasty surprises. For a platform-neutral view, see our ecommerce website cost guide.

What is included in a Shopify build

A Shopify build is more than installing a theme. A typical project covers:

  • Discovery and planning. Understanding your products, customers, delivery and returns, and what the store must do on day one.
  • Theme selection or design. Choosing a theme that fits your catalogue and customising it to your brand.
  • Store structure. Collections, navigation, filters and the page templates you need.
  • Product setup. Importing or entering products, variants, images and descriptions.
  • Payments, shipping and tax. UK delivery options with carriers such as Royal Mail, Evri or DPD, VAT settings and payment methods, including buy now pay later options like Klarna or Clearpay if they suit your audience.
  • Legal pages and compliance. Privacy, cookies and terms set up with UK GDPR and PECR in mind; final wording rests with you and your advisers.
  • SEO foundations. Titles, meta descriptions, clean URLs, redirects if you are moving platform, and analytics. Our Shopify SEO guide explains why these matter.
  • Testing and launch. Checking the buying journey on mobile and desktop before going live.

What makes a Shopify website cost more

Two Shopify stores can look similar and cost very different amounts to build. The main drivers are:

Catalogue size and complexity

Ten simple products are quick to set up. Several hundred products with many variants, bundles or personalisation options take far longer, especially if the data needs cleaning first.

Design

Customising a well-chosen theme is efficient. Heavily bespoke layouts, custom sections and unusual interactions take more design and development time.

Integrations

Connecting stock systems, accounting software, ERP, marketplaces or fulfilment partners adds work. Each integration needs setting up and testing, and some need paid apps.

Migration

Moving from another platform means transferring products, customers and order history where possible, and redirecting every old URL so you keep your search rankings. Our SEO migration checklist covers that process.

Content

If you provide finished product copy and photography, the build is faster. If those need writing or improving, that is extra time well spent, because product content drives both rankings and sales.

Running costs: plans, themes and apps

Shopify is a hosted platform, so you pay a subscription rather than separate hosting. Shopify sets its own plan prices and changes them from time to time, so we always check current pricing with you and quote it separately from our work.

Themes can be free or paid. A free theme is often a perfectly good starting point for a smaller store. A premium theme may save build time if it already does what you need.

Apps are where running costs creep up. Reviews, subscriptions, upsells, wishlists, email and search tools often charge monthly, sometimes based on usage. Before adding any app we ask whether the feature is needed now, whether the theme already does it, and whether an app will slow the store down. A lean set of apps is cheaper and faster.

Payment processing fees depend on your plan and payment provider. They scale with sales, so budget them as a percentage of revenue rather than a fixed sum.

Shopify vs other options on cost

Shopify suits most retailers who want a reliable, hosted store with low maintenance. WooCommerce can suit stores that need more control or already run on WordPress, but you take on hosting, updates and security. Neither is automatically cheaper; it depends on your catalogue and the features you need. Our Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison and WooCommerce agency guide set out the trade-offs.

Larger, high-volume retailers sometimes outgrow standard Shopify plans. We also handle moves to Shopify Plus; see our Shopify Plus migration guide if that is on your horizon.

One-off costs vs ongoing costs

It helps to split your Shopify budget into two columns. The first column holds one-off costs: the design and build, any premium theme, initial product photography and copywriting, and data migration from an old platform. Once these are paid, they are done, although you may choose to invest again later for a redesign or new features.

The second column holds ongoing costs: the Shopify plan, monthly app subscriptions, payment processing fees, your domain renewal, and any marketing or support you buy. These continue for as long as the store runs, and several of them grow as you sell more. Looking at both columns over the first year gives a far more accurate picture than comparing build quotes alone.

When you compare quotes from different providers, check what each one includes. One quote may include product setup and SEO redirects; another may leave those for you to do. A lower build price is not a saving if you then have to pay for the missing pieces separately or lose search traffic after launch.

Questions to ask before you commit

  • Who owns the Shopify account, the theme licence and the domain? You should.
  • Which apps are recommended, and what does each one do that the theme cannot?
  • How will existing URLs be redirected if you are moving from another platform?
  • What training or handover is included so you can manage products and orders yourself?
  • What support is available after launch if something needs fixing?

How long a Shopify build takes

Our ecommerce builds typically take 5 to 12 weeks. A simple store with a ready catalogue sits at the shorter end. Large catalogues, integrations and migrations take longer. The biggest delays usually come from waiting for product data, images and decisions, so having those ready keeps both time and cost down.

How to keep your Shopify budget under control

  1. Write down must-haves and nice-to-haves. Launch with the essentials and add extras once the store is earning.
  2. Prepare your product data. A clean spreadsheet of products, prices, variants and images saves hours.
  3. Start with a good theme. Customise rather than rebuild from scratch unless you have a clear reason.
  4. Be strict with apps. Every app has a running cost and can affect speed.
  5. Plan for marketing. A store with no traffic earns nothing. Budget for SEO, paid ads or email from day one; our ecommerce marketing guide explains the options.

FAQs

What is the Shopify website cost UK businesses should budget for?

Budget for a one-off build, which at Prism Works Studio typically costs £1,500 to £3,500 for an ecommerce site, plus ongoing third-party costs for the Shopify plan, apps, any premium theme and payment fees. Those vary, so we quote them separately.

Can I build a Shopify store myself?

Yes, and many small sellers start that way. An agency adds value when you need a stronger design, a larger catalogue set up properly, integrations, migration from another platform or SEO foundations done right from the start.

Do I have to pay for a Shopify theme?

No. Free themes can work well for smaller stores. A paid theme can make sense if it already includes features you would otherwise build or add with apps.

Why are Shopify apps quoted separately?

Apps are charged by their developers, often monthly and sometimes based on usage. Prices vary and change, so we list the apps we recommend and let you see their costs separately from our build fee.

How long does it take to build a Shopify website?

Our ecommerce builds typically take 5 to 12 weeks, depending on catalogue size, design, integrations and how quickly product content is ready.

Is Shopify cheaper than WooCommerce?

Not automatically. Shopify bundles hosting into a subscription; WooCommerce needs separate hosting and more maintenance. The total depends on your catalogue, features and apps or plugins.
