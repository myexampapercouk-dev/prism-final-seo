Shopify Agency UK: How to Choose the Right Partner for Your Store

Choosing a Shopify agency in the UK? Learn what a good agency does, the questions to ask, typical build costs and timelines, and the red flags to avoid.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good Shopify agency starts with your products, margins and customers, not with a theme, and should explain exactly what is in scope before work begins.
  • Compare agencies on process, ownership of the store, SEO migration care and aftercare, not just on the headline build price.
  • A focused Shopify build for a UK small business typically takes 5 to 12 weeks, and our published ecommerce site price is £1,500 to £3,500.

A Shopify agency in the UK designs, builds and improves Shopify stores so they sell well to UK shoppers, from theme setup and product structure to payments, delivery options and search visibility. The right one for you is the agency that can explain your scope, costs, timeline and who owns what in plain English before you sign anything.

Shopify makes it easy to open a store, which is exactly why so many stores underperform. The platform handles hosting, security and checkout, but the decisions that drive sales still need to be made by someone: how products are grouped, how pages are written, how delivery and returns are presented, and how the store is found on Google. This guide explains what a Shopify agency actually does, how to compare agencies, what you should expect to pay and how to avoid the common mistakes.

Shopify agency UK: what does one actually do?

Agencies vary, but most Shopify work falls into a handful of areas. Knowing them helps you check whether a quote covers what you need or leaves gaps that become extra invoices later.

  • Discovery and planning: understanding your range, margins, customers and competitors, then planning the store structure and key pages.
  • Design and theme setup: choosing or customising a theme so it reflects your brand and works on mobile, where most UK shoppers browse.
  • Product and collection structure: organising products, variants, filters and collections so people (and search engines) can find things quickly.
  • Payments, shipping and tax settings: configuring UK payment options, delivery rates and carriers such as Royal Mail, Evri or DPD, and VAT settings for your accountant to confirm.
  • Apps and integrations: adding only the apps you need for reviews, email, subscriptions or stock, and keeping the store fast.
  • SEO foundations: clean URLs, titles, descriptions, structured data and redirects if you are moving from another platform. Our guide to Shopify SEO in the UK goes deeper.
  • Launch and aftercare: testing orders end to end, going live, then fixing issues and improving the store once real customers arrive.

If you are still deciding on the platform itself, read our comparison of Shopify vs WooCommerce for UK businesses before you brief anyone.

When a Shopify agency makes sense

Not every store needs an agency. If you sell a handful of products and are comfortable with a standard theme, you may be fine on your own for a while. An agency usually earns its fee when one or more of these is true:

  • You are moving from another platform and cannot afford to lose search rankings or order history.
  • You have a large or complex range with many variants, bundles or B2B pricing.
  • Your current store gets visitors but few sales, and you are not sure why.
  • You want the store to look and feel like your brand rather than a template.
  • You simply do not have the time to learn Shopify properly while running the business.

What a typical Shopify project looks like

Most well-run Shopify projects follow a similar order, whatever the size of the store.

  1. Discovery (week 1): agree goals, audit any existing store, review competitors and gather content and product data.
  2. Structure and design (weeks 1 to 3): sitemap, collection structure, key page layouts and theme choices signed off.
  3. Build (weeks 2 to 8): theme setup, product import, apps, payments, shipping rules, legal pages and tracking.
  4. Content and SEO (alongside build): titles, descriptions, collection copy and redirects.
  5. Testing (final 1 to 2 weeks): test orders, mobile checks, speed checks and accessibility basics.
  6. Launch and aftercare: go live, monitor orders and search data, fix issues quickly.

Simpler stores sit at the five-week end; migrations and larger catalogues take longer. If you are replacing an existing site, read how to redesign a website without losing rankings before you set a launch date.

Shopify agency checklist

Use this checklist before you sign with any agency.

  • The store, domain and payment accounts will be registered in your business name.
  • You have a written scope listing pages, features, apps and number of design revisions.
  • The timeline includes time for you to supply content and approve designs.
  • There is a redirect plan if you are moving from another platform or domain.
  • Payments, shipping rates and VAT settings will be checked with you (and your accountant).
  • Privacy policy, cookie consent and terms are covered, in line with UK GDPR and PECR.
  • GA4 and conversion tracking are set up and tested before launch.
  • Monthly costs for Shopify and every app are listed so there are no surprises.
  • The aftercare period and what it covers are written down.
  • You know who to contact and how quickly they will respond after launch.

After launch: growing the store

A new store is a starting point. Sales usually come from steady work after launch: improving product pages, building collection content that ranks, running Google Shopping campaigns and making checkout smoother. Our guide to Google Shopping ads in the UK explains one of the main growth channels. You can also see how we approach online retail on our ecommerce sector page.

When you speak to agencies, ask how they would split effort between the build and the months that follow. A balanced plan protects your investment in the store.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing on price alone. The cheapest quote often leaves out redirects, tracking or aftercare.
  • Too many apps. Each one adds cost and can slow the store down.
  • Skipping product data work. Messy titles and variants hurt search and filtering.
  • Launching without tracking. Without analytics you cannot tell what is working.
  • No plan for delivery and returns messaging. UK shoppers want clear costs and timings before checkout.

FAQs

How do I choose a Shopify agency UK businesses can trust?

Ask for a written scope, confirm the store will be in your name, check how redirects and tracking are handled, and make sure aftercare is defined. Agencies that answer these questions clearly are usually easier to work with.

How much does a Shopify store cost to build?

It depends on your range, design and integrations. Our published ecommerce site price is £1,500 to £3,500. Shopify plan fees, app subscriptions and ad spend are separate.

How long does a Shopify build take?

Most ecommerce builds take 5 to 12 weeks. Smaller catalogues on a standard theme are quicker; migrations and complex ranges take longer.

Can a Shopify agency move my store from another platform?

Yes. A careful migration covers products, customers, order history where possible, and a full redirect plan so search rankings are protected.

Do I need a Shopify agency after launch?

Not always. Some businesses manage the store themselves; others keep ongoing support for SEO, ads and improvements. Our retainers start at £350 a month.

Is Shopify good for UK small businesses?

Shopify suits many UK small businesses because hosting, security and checkout are handled for you. WooCommerce can suit businesses wanting more control. Compare both before deciding.
