Multilingual websites done properly

Most Geneva sites need at least French and English, and some add German or Italian for wider Swiss reach. The technical setup matters as much as the words. Each language should have its own URL, hreflang tags should connect equivalent pages, and the language switcher should take visitors to the same page in the other language rather than the home page.

We write the English copy and brief your translator on tone, terms and page purpose. We avoid publishing machine translations without review, because awkward French on a Geneva site undermines trust fast. If budgets are tight, we can launch with core pages in both languages and add secondary pages later.

Privacy, cookies and accessibility

Switzerland has its own data protection law, and many Geneva businesses also serve customers in the EU, so European rules often apply as well. These notes are general, so please check official guidance or ask your own adviser.

  • GDPR may apply if you offer goods or services to people in the EU or monitor their behaviour. Forms, analytics and ad tags all collect personal data.
  • ePrivacy and cookie consent rules in the EU generally require consent before non-essential cookies are set. We build sites so analytics and ad tags respect your consent banner.
  • The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing digital services sold into the EU, such as e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt. The harmonised standard EN 301 549 points to WCAG level AA, which we use as our design target.

How a remote website project works

Switzerland is usually one hour ahead of the UK, so our working hours overlap well. A typical project runs like this:

  1. Discovery call. We discuss your goals, audience, languages and the pages you need.
  2. Sitemap and wireframes. We agree the structure and the content each page needs.
  3. Design. We share designs for review and refine them with your feedback.
  4. Build. We develop the site on a private preview link you can test.
  5. Content and translation. Your team or translator supplies French copy, and we load and check it.
  6. Launch. We handle redirects, analytics, consent setup and search console checks.

A small website usually takes three to five weeks. E-commerce builds usually take five to twelve weeks depending on the number of products and integrations.

Common website problems we fix for Geneva firms

Many Geneva businesses come to us with a site that already exists but no longer works hard enough. The same issues come up again and again, and most of them are straightforward to fix once they are spotted.

  • Half-translated pages. The home page is in French and English, but service pages, forms and error messages are only in one language. We map every page and every string so nothing is missed.
  • Slow image-heavy layouts. Large photos of offices, lakeside views or products load slowly on mobile data. We resize and compress images and load them only when needed.
  • Unclear calls to action. Visitors cannot quickly see how to book a meeting, request a proposal or call. We add one clear primary action per page.
  • Outdated plugins and themes. Old WordPress sites often carry plugins nobody uses. We remove what is not needed and update the rest, which improves speed and security.
  • Weak contact details. Address, phone and opening hours differ between the site and online listings. We make them consistent so search engines and visitors trust them.

Sometimes a full rebuild is the better option, and sometimes a focused refresh of key pages is enough. We explain both options honestly, with costs and timelines, so you can decide what fits your budget.

We also think about who will edit the site after launch. Most Geneva teams want to update news, team pages or opening hours without calling a developer. We set up simple editing in WordPress or Shopify and walk your team through it on a short call, so changes in either language are easy to make.

Finally, we plan for growth. A site built with a clear structure makes it easier to add new services, new languages or a small shop later without starting again. That saves money over the life of the website and keeps your search rankings stable as the site expands.
