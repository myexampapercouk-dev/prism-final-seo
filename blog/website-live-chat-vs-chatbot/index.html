Website Live Chat vs Chatbot: Choosing the Right Option for Your Business

The differences between staffed live chat and chatbots on a business website or online store, when each makes sense, and how to set chat up well.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Live chat is staffed by people; chatbots answer automatically. Many sites use a mix of both.
  • Chat only helps if it is answered promptly or clearly explains when a reply will come.
  • Good FAQ content, clear contact options and honest handover to a person matter more than the tool itself.

A chat window in the corner of a website can be a helpful way for visitors to ask a quick question before buying or enquiring. It can also be an irritation: a box that pops up uninvited, a bot that cannot understand simple questions or a "chat now" button that leads to nobody. Whether chat helps depends on choosing the right type for your business and running it properly.

This guide explains the difference between staffed live chat and chatbots, when each makes sense, the questions to ask before adding chat and how to set it up so it supports visitors rather than getting in their way.

Live chat and chatbots: what is the difference?

Live chat

Live chat connects a website visitor to a real person, usually someone from your team, who replies in real time through a chat window. It feels personal and can handle nuanced questions, but it needs people available to answer.

Chatbots

A chatbot answers automatically. Simpler bots follow set paths, offering buttons or menu options that lead to pre-written answers. More advanced bots use AI to interpret questions written in natural language and respond from a set of information such as your help content. Chatbots can be available at any hour, but their answers are only as good as the information and rules behind them.

Hybrid setups

Many businesses combine the two. A bot handles common questions and gathers basic details, then hands over to a person for anything more complex, or takes a message when nobody is available.

When live chat tends to suit

  • Considered purchases where shoppers want reassurance before buying, such as higher-value products, made-to-order items or technical equipment.
  • Service businesses where a quick conversation can turn a browsing visitor into an enquiry.
  • Businesses with someone available during the hours visitors are most active, and the capacity to respond promptly.
  • Questions that vary widely and would be hard to predict or script.

When a chatbot tends to suit

  • Repetitive questions about delivery times, returns, opening hours or order status that have clear, consistent answers.
  • Out-of-hours coverage, when visitors arrive in the evening or at weekends and you cannot staff chat.
  • Routing, where the bot's job is to point people to the right page, form or team rather than answer everything itself.
  • Gathering details before a person picks up the conversation, saving time on both sides.

When you may not need chat at all

Chat is not essential for every website. If visitors rarely have questions before acting, if your team cannot respond promptly or if most enquiries are better handled by a call or a detailed form, chat may add cost and complexity without much benefit.

Often the better first step is to make sure answers are already on the site. A clear FAQ page, detailed delivery and returns pages and well-designed contact forms can resolve many questions before anyone needs to ask. Chat then becomes a helpful extra rather than a patch for missing information.

Questions to ask before adding chat

  1. Who will answer, and when? Be realistic about the hours you can cover and how quickly you can reply.
  2. What do visitors actually ask? Review emails, calls and form submissions to understand common questions.
  3. Where should chat appear? Every page, or only on pages where questions are likely, such as product, pricing or checkout pages?
  4. What happens out of hours? Will chat hide, show a message form or hand over to a bot?
  5. Where do conversations go? Chats should reach the right person and be recorded so nothing is lost.
  6. How will it affect page speed? Chat scripts add weight to every page they load on.

Setting up chat well

Set clear expectations

If chat is staffed, show when someone is available. Outside those hours, say clearly when a reply will come and offer a way to leave a message. A visitor told "we usually reply within one working day" is better served than one left waiting in an empty chat.

Make bots honest

If visitors are talking to a bot, make that clear. Give an obvious way to reach a person, or to leave contact details, when the bot cannot help. Few things frustrate visitors more than a bot that loops through the same unhelpful options.

Keep answers accurate

A bot that gives wrong information about prices, delivery or returns can cause real problems. Base answers on content you control and review regularly, and update them whenever policies change. With AI-driven bots in particular, test a wide range of questions and limit them to your own information so they do not make things up.

Do not interrupt too eagerly

Chat windows that pop open as soon as a page loads can feel intrusive, especially on mobile where they may cover content. Consider a discreet button that visitors can open when they want it, or a gentle prompt only after a visitor has spent some time on a relevant page. Many of the principles in our guide to pop-ups done well apply here too.

Design for mobile

On small screens, chat widgets can block buttons, menus or the checkout. Check that the chat button does not cover important elements and that the chat window is easy to open and close. Our guide to mobile website design covers related principles.

Keep an eye on speed

Chat tools usually load scripts from a third party, which can slow pages down. Ask your developer whether the chat script can load after the main content, or only once a visitor interacts with the chat button. Our guide to website speed optimisation explains why this matters.

Accessibility

Chat widgets should be usable by everyone. Check that the chat can be opened, used and closed with a keyboard, that it works with screen readers, that text has sufficient contrast and that it does not trap focus. Our guide to website accessibility covers the wider principles.

Privacy and data

Chat conversations often contain personal information such as names, email addresses and order details. Think about where that data is stored, who can access it and how long it is kept, and mention chat in your privacy information. Some chat tools also set cookies, which may need to be reflected in your consent setup. Data protection rules can be nuanced, so check guidance from the Information Commissioner's Office or take advice if you are unsure. For regulated sectors, such as healthcare or financial services, take extra care over what can be discussed through chat.

Platform options

Shopify, WordPress and WooCommerce sites can add chat through apps and plugins, and Shopify offers its own messaging option for stores. Choosing a tool depends on your platform, the features you need, how it connects with your email or helpdesk and its effect on performance. It is worth trialling a tool and testing it on real devices before committing.

Measuring whether chat helps

After launch, review how chat is being used. Useful questions include:

  • How many chats start, and on which pages?
  • What are the most common questions? Could they be answered on the page instead?
  • How quickly are chats answered, and how many go unanswered?
  • Do visitors who chat go on to buy or enquire?

Recurring questions are valuable feedback. If many people ask the same thing about sizing, delivery or pricing, that information probably needs to be clearer on the site itself.

Common mistakes

  • Showing "chat now" when nobody is available to reply.
  • Bots with no way to reach a person or leave a message.
  • Chat windows that pop open immediately and cover content on mobile.
  • Out-of-date bot answers about prices or policies.
  • Heavy chat scripts loading on every page with no regard for speed.

FAQs

Is live chat better than a chatbot?

Neither is better in every case. Live chat suits nuanced questions when staff are available; chatbots suit repetitive questions and out-of-hours cover. Many businesses use both.

Does live chat slow down a website?

Chat scripts add weight, but loading them after the main content or only on interaction can reduce the impact.

Do I need to tell visitors they are talking to a bot?

Being clear is good practice and helps set expectations. Visitors should also have a way to reach a person.

Can a chatbot use AI to answer questions?

Yes, some can. They should be limited to your own accurate information and tested carefully, since AI-generated answers can sometimes be wrong.

Should chat appear on every page?

Not necessarily. Showing it on pages where visitors are most likely to have questions can be more helpful and less distracting.
