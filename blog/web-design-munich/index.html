What Munich visitors expect from a website

Buyers in Munich tend to research carefully before they get in touch. A site that works well for them usually has:

  • Clear, specific information. What you offer, who it is for, how it works and what happens next.
  • Visible company details. German businesses are expected to publish a legal notice, often called an Impressum, with company and contact details. Your legal adviser can confirm what yours must contain.
  • Privacy information that is easy to find. A clear privacy notice linked from every page.
  • Fast pages on mobile. Many first visits happen on a phone, often between meetings or on public transport.
  • Simple contact routes. Short forms, a visible phone number or email and clear expectations about response times.

We design each page so that the most important information sits near the top and the next step is always obvious.

GDPR, cookie consent and accessibility

Any website aimed at people in Germany needs to take data protection, consent and accessibility seriously. The notes below are general. Please check the official guidance and your own legal adviser for anything specific to your business.

  • GDPR. The General Data Protection Regulation applies to personal data you collect, such as form submissions and analytics identifiers. You need a lawful basis, a clear privacy notice and sensible retention rules.
  • Cookie consent. Under the ePrivacy rules, non-essential cookies and similar trackers generally need consent before they are set. A consent banner should make accepting and rejecting equally easy, and your tags should respect the choice.
  • European Accessibility Act. From June 2025, the EAA applies accessibility requirements to many consumer-facing products and services, including a lot of e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but accessible design is still good practice. The harmonised standard, EN 301 549, points to WCAG at level AA.

We build to WCAG AA as standard, using proper headings, colour contrast, keyboard access and alt text. Our website accessibility guide explains the checks we run, and our cookie banner design guide shows how to set up consent without hurting the user experience. Final responsibility for compliance rests with you and your advisers.

Building your site to be found

A beautiful website does little if nobody finds it. We build every Munich site with search in mind from day one.

  • Keyword-led page plan. Each service or product gets its own page targeting the phrases your buyers use.
  • Language versions done properly. Separate URLs for German and English, with hreflang tags so search engines show the right version.
  • Local signals. Consistent business details and a Google Business Profile, if you have a real address customers can visit.
  • Speed. Compressed images, minimal scripts and good hosting so pages load quickly.
  • Content structure. Clear answers to common questions, which helps both search engines and AI search tools.
