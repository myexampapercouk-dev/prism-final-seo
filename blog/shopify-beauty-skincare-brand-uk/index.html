Shopify for UK Beauty and Skincare Brands: Building a Store Customers Trust

How UK beauty and skincare brands can sell well on Shopify: product pages, ingredients, shade selection, claims, subscriptions, bundles and regulation.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Beauty shoppers want ingredients, usage guidance and honest results information, so product pages need to do more than look good.
  • Cosmetic claims and product information are regulated in the UK, so check wording carefully and take advice where needed.
  • Replenishment, bundles and routines suit Shopify well and can encourage repeat purchases when planned around real customer needs.

Beauty and skincare are categories where customers often research carefully before buying. They want to know what is in a product, whether it suits their skin, how to use it and what to realistically expect. They also buy repeatedly once they find something that works. A good online store supports both stages: helping a new customer choose with confidence and making it easy for a returning customer to reorder. Shopify is widely used by UK beauty brands, from small indie skincare labels to larger cosmetics businesses. This guide covers the decisions that shape a Shopify beauty store.

It is written for founders and ecommerce managers at UK skincare, cosmetics, haircare, body care and fragrance brands. It is not aimed at salons; if you run a salon, our guide to beauty salon marketing is a better fit.

Structuring the catalogue

Beauty catalogues tend to include single products, sizes, shades, sets and refills. Plan how each is modelled before building:

  • Sizes, such as 30ml and 50ml, usually work well as variants.
  • Shades for foundations, lipsticks and similar can be variants with swatches, though a large shade range may need a dedicated shade selector.
  • Sets and bundles may be separate products or built using bundle functionality, depending on how stock needs to be tracked.
  • Refills can be variants or separate products linked from the main product page.

Our guide to product variants and bundles covers the options in more detail.

Navigation that matches how people shop

Beauty shoppers browse by product type, but also by skin type, concern and routine step. Consider navigation and collections such as:

  • Product type: cleansers, serums, moisturisers, SPF, make-up.
  • Skin type or concern: dry skin, oily skin, sensitive skin, and so on, worded carefully (see the section on claims below).
  • Routine step: cleanse, treat, moisturise, protect.
  • Collections and ranges.
  • Sets, gifts and travel sizes.

Filters by skin type, concern, key ingredient and price help shoppers narrow choices, provided the product data behind them is consistent. Use metafields to store attributes like skin type and key ingredients so filters and product pages draw from the same source. Our guide to ecommerce site search covers making products easy to find.

Product pages for beauty

A strong beauty product page answers the questions a shopper would ask at a counter. Elements to consider:

  • Clear imagery of packaging, texture and, for make-up, swatches on a range of skin tones.
  • What it is and who it is for, in plain language.
  • Key ingredients and what they are included for, described accurately.
  • Full ingredients list in the standard format, easy to find.
  • How to use, including when in a routine and how much to apply.
  • Size, shelf life after opening and storage.
  • Warnings and patch test advice where relevant.
  • Reviews, ideally with skin type information. See our guide to product reviews.

Tabs or accordions keep long information tidy without hiding it from search engines or shoppers. Our guide to product descriptions covers structure and tone.

Shade selection

For make-up brands, choosing the right shade online is one of the biggest barriers to purchase. Helpful approaches include clear swatches on several skin tones, descriptions of undertones, comparison with other shades in your range and a simple shade-finding quiz. Keep imagery consistent and colour-accurate across shades, because inconsistent photography makes shades hard to compare. Interactive tools should be tested on mobile and kept light so they do not slow the page.

Claims and regulation

Cosmetic products sold in Great Britain are subject to specific rules, including product safety requirements, a responsible person, notification and labelling requirements, and rules on the claims you can make. Advertising claims, including on your website and social media, also fall under the UK advertising codes overseen by the ASA. In broad terms:

  • Claims should be truthful, supported by evidence and not misleading.
  • Cosmetics should not be presented as treating or curing medical conditions; that can move a product into medicines regulation.
  • Environmental and "clean" or "natural" claims should be specific and supportable; the CMA has published guidance on green claims.
  • Before and after images should not be exaggerated or digitally altered in a misleading way.

The rules are detailed and differ in Northern Ireland, so check current guidance from the Office for Product Safety and Standards, the ASA and the CMA, or take professional advice. This is a general overview, not legal advice.

Routines, bundles and cross-selling

Skincare is often used as a routine, which makes it suited to bundles and complementary product suggestions. Options on Shopify include:

  • Curated sets, such as a starter routine or travel kit.
  • Build-your-own bundles with a discount.
  • "Complete the routine" suggestions on product pages.
  • Free samples or minis added at checkout, if your margins allow.

Keep suggestions genuinely relevant. Recommending products that do not suit the customer's needs can undermine trust.

Subscriptions and replenishment

Products that run out on a predictable cycle, such as cleansers, moisturisers and SPF, can suit subscriptions or replenishment reminders. Shopify supports subscriptions through apps that use its subscription features. Plan the details carefully: delivery frequency options, how customers skip, change or cancel, and how discounts apply. Subscription terms must be clear, and UK rules on subscription contracts have been changing, so check current requirements. Our guide to ecommerce subscriptions covers this in more depth.

A simpler alternative is a replenishment email timed to when a product is likely to run out, with consent handled properly under UK GDPR and PECR. Our guide to ecommerce email marketing explains the principles.

Delivery and returns

Beauty products raise specific delivery and returns questions. Liquids and aerosols may have courier restrictions, especially for international shipping. Glass packaging needs secure packing. On returns, many brands do not accept opened products for hygiene reasons, but UK consumer rules on cancellation and faulty goods still apply, and the position on sealed hygiene products is specific. State your policy clearly and check the current rules. Our guide to delivery and returns pages explains what to include.

Packaging and brand experience

Beauty is a category where packaging and presentation often matter to customers. The unboxing experience, inserts explaining how to use products, and sustainable packaging choices can all reinforce the brand. Our guide to packaging and unboxing covers the practical side, and our brand strategy service page explains how we approach brand work.

Theme and performance

Choose a theme that shows imagery well, supports swatches, tabs and metafields, and stays fast on mobile. Beauty stores often add apps for reviews, quizzes, subscriptions and loyalty, and each app can add code to the site. Review apps regularly and remove those you do not need. Our guides to Shopify theme customisation and Shopify speed optimisation go further.

Getting found and growing

Beauty is highly competitive in search and social. On the store, unique copy for products and collections, accurate structured data and clear information about ingredients and concerns help search visibility. Our Shopify SEO guide covers the basics. Many beauty brands grow through paid social, Google Shopping, creator content and loyalty schemes. Our guides to Google Shopping ads and loyalty programmes may help, and our paid media service page explains how we approach advertising.

Shopify beauty store checklist

  • A clear structure for sizes, shades, sets and refills.
  • Navigation by product type, skin type, concern and routine.
  • Product pages with ingredients, usage and accurate imagery.
  • Claims checked against current rules and guidance.
  • Bundles and routines that make sense for customers.
  • Subscription or replenishment options with clear terms.
  • Delivery and returns policies suited to cosmetics.

FAQs

Is Shopify good for skincare brands?

Yes, Shopify handles variants, bundles, subscriptions via apps and rich product content well, which suits most skincare and beauty brands.

What should a skincare product page include?

Clear images, who the product is for, key and full ingredients, how to use it, size and shelf life, warnings and genuine reviews.

Can I sell skincare subscriptions on Shopify?

Yes, through subscription apps that use Shopify's subscription features. Make terms clear and check current UK rules on subscription contracts.

What claims can I make about skincare products?

Claims should be truthful, evidenced and not suggest medical treatment. Check guidance from the ASA and product safety regulators, or take advice.

Do I have to accept returns of opened beauty products?

The rules on cancellation for sealed hygiene products are specific, and faulty goods are always covered. Check current rules and state your policy clearly.
