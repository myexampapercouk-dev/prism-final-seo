How we build WordPress sites

WordPress can be quick and solid or slow and fragile. The difference usually comes from the theme, the number of plugins and how carefully the site is set up. Our approach:

  • A lightweight theme or block-based build rather than a heavy multipurpose theme loaded with features you will never use.
  • Few, well-maintained plugins, each chosen for a clear reason.
  • The block editor set up for your team, with reusable patterns so pages stay consistent.
  • Image handling and caching configured so pages load quickly on mobile.
  • Accessibility basics such as headings in order, colour contrast, alt text and keyboard-friendly forms.
  • SEO foundations including clean URLs, titles, descriptions, an XML sitemap and schema where useful.

Before launch, you review everything on a staging site. After launch, we give your team a short training session and written notes. Our guide to web design in Ottawa covers design choices in more detail.

Bilingual WordPress sites

Many Ottawa organisations serve English and French audiences, and some have obligations to do so. On WordPress, that usually means a multilingual plugin or a multisite setup, each with trade-offs. We help you choose based on how much content you have and who will edit it.

We set language switchers, separate URLs for each language and the tags that tell search engines which version is which. The French text itself comes from your team or a professional translator; we do not write or translate French. If you have official language requirements, check them with your own adviser, as approval of content rests with you.

Fixing slow or broken WordPress sites

Many sites we see are not new builds. They are older WordPress sites that have grown slow, insecure or hard to edit. Common problems include:

  • Too many plugins, some no longer updated.
  • Page builders that add heavy code to every page.
  • Large images uploaded straight from a camera.
  • Out-of-date WordPress core, themes and PHP versions.
  • Broken forms that quietly stop sending enquiries.

We start with a review, list the issues in plain English and suggest whether fixes or a rebuild make more sense. When we rebuild, we map redirects from old URLs to new ones so search rankings are protected as far as possible.

Security, privacy and consent

We keep WordPress sites updated, backed up and monitored, with strong admin passwords set by your team, limited user roles and two-factor sign-in where possible. On privacy, PIPEDA sets general federal rules for how private-sector organisations handle personal information, and CASL covers marketing emails and texts. Some organisations may also have provincial or sector rules to follow.

These notes are general information only. Please check official guidance and speak to your own adviser. We build cookie banners, consent checkboxes and privacy page layouts using wording you approve.

Using AI-powered tools in WordPress work

We use AI-powered tools to speed up audits, suggest internal links, draft alt text for review and summarise content gaps. Everything is checked by a person before it goes live, and nothing is published without your approval.

Hosting, updates and ongoing care

A WordPress site is never finished. Core, theme and plugin updates arrive regularly, and some of them fix security issues. Skipping updates for months is one of the most common reasons sites break or get compromised. Our care work covers:

  • Regular updates to WordPress core, themes and plugins, tested before they go live where possible.
  • Daily or weekly backups stored away from the main server.
  • Uptime and security monitoring, with quick action when something goes wrong.
  • Small content changes and fixes your team does not have time for.
  • A monthly note on what was done and anything that needs your decision.

On hosting, we recommend managed WordPress hosting from a reputable provider rather than the cheapest shared plan. Some organisations prefer Canadian data centres for their own policy reasons, and we can work with that choice. The hosting account stays in your name, so you are never locked in.

Choosing a WordPress partner

When comparing agencies, ask who owns the site and hosting, how many plugins they plan to use, how updates are handled and what happens if you leave. A good partner answers in writing. We are open about being UK-based and remote, about our prices and about the fact that French content comes from your team or a translator.
