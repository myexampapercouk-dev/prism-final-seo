Removals Company Website Design: From Survey Request to Moving Day

Website design for UK removals companies: quote and survey forms, home and office move pages, storage, packing services, insurance wording and trust.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A removals website is mostly a quote-gathering tool, so the quote or survey form deserves more design attention than any other part of the site.
  • Moving customers are often stressed and comparing several firms, so clear explanations of what is and is not included build confidence.
  • Insurance, liability and storage terms should be described carefully and accurately, with full details in written terms rather than marketing copy.

Moving home is often described as one of life's more stressful events, and people looking for a removals firm usually have a lot on their mind: completion dates that might slip, boxes still to pack, children or pets to organise. They will often contact several companies, compare quotes and choose the one that seems most organised and trustworthy. A removals website has one main job: to make getting a quote easy and to make your firm feel like the safe pair of hands.

This guide is about the website itself for UK removals companies, from man-and-van operators to firms handling home, office and international moves. For promotion beyond your own site, such as reviews, directories and local advertising, see our guide to tradesman marketing in the UK.

The quote form is the centre of the site

Almost every removals visitor wants a price. How you gather the information for that price shapes your enquiry rate and how much back-and-forth you need. Common approaches include:

  • A short initial form with moving from and to postcodes, property size, rough date and contact details, followed by a call or survey.
  • A detailed inventory form where customers list rooms and large items. More effort for the customer but a better basis for an estimate.
  • A survey request, in person or by video call, for larger moves.

Many firms combine these: a short form first, then an option to add more detail or book a survey. Splitting a longer form into steps, with a progress indicator, can make it feel less daunting on a phone. Useful fields might include:

  • Current and new postcodes.
  • Property type and number of bedrooms.
  • Access details, such as floors, lifts, parking restrictions or narrow streets.
  • Whether the date is fixed or flexible, and whether they are waiting on completion.
  • Services needed: packing, dismantling furniture, storage, disposal.
  • Specialist items such as pianos, antiques or large appliances.

Be clear about what happens next and roughly how quickly you respond. Our guide to website contact forms has more on reducing drop-off, and your privacy notice should explain how you use the details collected, particularly addresses.

Explaining how quotes work

Removals pricing confuses many customers. A page explaining your approach helps, for example whether you give fixed quotes or estimates, what can change the price (extra items, access problems, waiting time on completion day), and whether prices include VAT. If you require a deposit, explain how it works and refer to your terms for cancellation or postponement. Rather than publishing figures, explain the factors and invite people to request a quote.

Pages for different types of move

Different moves bring different questions, so separate pages are worth having:

  • Home removals. The typical domestic move, explaining how moving day works from arrival to unloading.
  • Office and commercial moves. Out-of-hours moves, IT equipment, minimising downtime and working with building managers.
  • Small moves and man and van. Single items, student moves and flats, if you offer them.
  • Long-distance and international moves. If you offer them, explain your coverage. For international moves, customs and documentation can be complex, so say you will advise and avoid detailed statements of rules that can change.
  • Packing services. Full packing, fragile-only packing and packing materials.
  • Storage. If you offer storage, explain the type (for example containerised or self-access), how access works and what customers need to know about insurance while goods are stored.

A moving day explainer

Customers who have not moved for years often do not know what to expect. A clear walkthrough reduces anxiety and questions: when the team arrives, what they need from the customer, what happens with keys and completion delays, how furniture is protected and what happens at the new property. Only describe what you actually do. If your process varies by job, say so rather than presenting a fixed sequence.

A downloadable or printable moving checklist can be helpful and gives people a reason to return to your site. Keep it practical and avoid any advice that strays into legal or conveyancing territory.

Insurance and liability wording

This is one area where care matters. Customers often ask whether their belongings are insured during the move. The honest answer depends on your own cover and terms, and sometimes on the customer's home insurance too. On the website:

  • Describe the cover you offer in plain terms and point to your full written terms for limits and exclusions.
  • Avoid broad phrases such as "fully insured" unless you can explain exactly what that means.
  • Explain whether items packed by the customer are treated differently from items you pack, if that applies.
  • Suggest customers check their own home insurance for cover during a move.

If you are a member of a trade association that has a code of practice, you can mention it, but only if you are a current member and in line with the association's rules on logos and wording.

Trust details for removals

Customers hand over almost everything they own, so trust details carry a lot of weight:

  • Photos of your actual vehicles, team and storage facility, rather than stock images.
  • A physical base or depot address if you have one, which reassures people you are an established business.
  • Clear business details, including company registration if you are a limited company.
  • Genuine reviews, linked to where people can read them.
  • Straightforward terms and conditions that are easy to find.

Booking and dates

Many moves depend on completion dates that are not confirmed until shortly before. Your site can address this by explaining how you handle provisional bookings, date changes and moves that slip on the day. Some firms show a general availability calendar; others prefer to confirm by phone. If you use an online booking or deposit system, our guide to online booking systems covers the main options. Make sure any deposit terms are clearly linked before payment.

Mobile and speed

A short frequently asked questions section on each service page also helps. Movers commonly wonder whether they need to empty drawers, what happens to fridges and freezers, whether you move plants or garden items, and what you will not carry, such as hazardous materials. Answering these honestly, based on your own policies, saves phone calls and shows customers you have thought about the details that worry them.

People often look for removals firms on their phones while dealing with estate agents and solicitors. The quote form must work well on a small screen, with large tap targets and fields that use the right keyboard for postcodes and phone numbers. Fast loading matters too; see our guide to website speed optimisation.

Local and regional search

Removals searches usually include a place name. An areas covered page and a strong Google Business Profile help, and our guides to local SEO and Google Business Profile explain how. If you cover long-distance moves, route-based content can be useful only where you have genuinely useful things to say. Thin pages for every town pair add little.

Choosing a platform

A removals site needs reliable forms, clear service pages and possibly a booking or deposit system. WordPress, Squarespace and Wix can all manage this. WordPress gives the most flexibility for complex multi-step quote forms; the simpler builders are easier to update yourself. Think about who will edit the site and how complicated your quote process is before choosing.

FAQs

What should a removals quote form ask?

Typically postcodes, property size, access details, date flexibility, services needed and any specialist items, with contact details. Keep essential fields short and let people add detail optionally.

Should a removals website show prices?

Moves vary so much that many firms explain how they price rather than listing figures. If you show starting prices, be clear about what they include.

How should I describe insurance on my website?

Plainly and accurately, pointing to your written terms for limits and exclusions, and suggesting customers check their own home insurance.

Do I need separate pages for office moves?

If you do commercial work, yes. Businesses care about downtime, IT equipment and out-of-hours moves, which are different from home movers' concerns.

Is a video survey option worth offering?

It can make quoting easier for customers who are far away or short on time. If you offer it, explain how it works and how to book.
