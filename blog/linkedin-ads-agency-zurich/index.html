Why LinkedIn makes sense for Zurich businesses

Zurich is home to a large concentration of banks, insurers, consultancies, technology firms, software companies and professional service providers. Many of these sell to other businesses, often with long sales cycles and high contract values. In that setting, the ability to show ads only to finance directors, IT leads or procurement teams at companies of a certain size is very useful.

LinkedIn clicks are expensive compared with other platforms. That is acceptable when one new client is worth a lot, but it means campaigns must be planned carefully. What a LinkedIn Ads agency in Zurich does

  • Audit and setup: reviewing your Campaign Manager account, company page and billing, and fixing structure problems.
  • Tracking: installing the Insight Tag, setting up conversions and, where suitable, the Conversions API, so leads are measured properly.
  • Audience planning: building audiences by job function, seniority, industry, company size and matched company lists.
  • Offer and creative: deciding what you will offer, such as a guide, a benchmark, a webinar or a consultation, and writing English ad copy.

  • Reporting: monthly reports showing spend, leads, cost per lead and what we will test next.

Targeting in a multilingual market

Switzerland has several official languages, and Zurich is mainly German-speaking, but many professionals in the city work in English. That gives you a choice. Some firms run English ads aimed at international roles, while others run German ads for local decision-makers. Testing both is often wise.

LinkedIn lets you target by profile language as well as location. We can set up separate campaigns for English and German audiences so results stay clear. All German wording should come from a native speaker on your team or a translator. Our guide to multilingual website design covers how to make sure landing pages match the language of each ad.

Account-based marketing is another strong option in Zurich. If you have a list of target companies, LinkedIn can match it and show ads only to people working there. That works well for firms selling to banks, insurers or large employers.

Landing pages and lead quality

A LinkedIn lead is only valuable if someone follows it up quickly and it fits your ideal client. We recommend that every campaign has a clear follow-up process, and that leads flow into your CRM or inbox straight away.

If you send traffic to your website, the landing page should match the ad, explain the offer clearly and keep the form short. For B2B buyers, credibility matters, so show your services, process and contact details clearly. Our website development service can build or improve landing pages, with small websites from £1,000 to £2,000 and a typical timeline of three to five weeks.

Privacy and regulated sectors

Swiss data protection law and, where you target EU users, GDPR both apply to how you collect and use personal data. In general, tracking tags should respect your consent setup, and lead forms should link to a clear privacy policy.

Many Zurich firms work in financial services, insurance or health, where advertising rules are stricter. We can follow your guidance and keep claims factual, but we do not give legal or compliance advice. Approval of all ad wording rests with your compliance team, and you should check official guidance from the relevant Swiss authorities or your own adviser. Common mistakes we fix in LinkedIn accounts

When we audit LinkedIn accounts for B2B firms, the same problems come up again and again. Fixing them often improves results before any extra budget is spent.

  • Audiences that are too broad: targeting everyone in Switzerland with a manager title wastes money. Tighter audiences by function, seniority and industry usually perform better.
  • Audience expansion left on: this setting can push ads to people outside your chosen audience. We review it campaign by campaign.
  • The LinkedIn Audience Network left on by default: it shows ads on partner sites and apps, which may not suit high-value B2B offers. We test it rather than assume it helps.
  • No conversion tracking: without the Insight Tag and clear conversion events, it is impossible to know which ads produce leads.
  • One ad per campaign: running a single ad gives LinkedIn nothing to compare. Two to four variations per campaign is a better starting point.
  • Weak offers: "Contact us" rarely works with cold audiences. A useful guide, checklist or short consultation usually earns more interest.
  • Slow follow-up: leads that wait days for a reply often go cold. Lead gen form results should reach your team straight away.

We also look at your company page. Prospects often click through to it before they respond, so it should explain what you do, show recent posts and link to your website. An empty or outdated page can undermine even a well-targeted ad.

Finally, we check how campaigns are grouped. Clear naming and separate campaigns for each audience and language make reporting simple and help you see exactly where budget goes. This kind of tidy structure is what makes later testing quick and reliable, and it means anyone on your team can read the account and understand it.
