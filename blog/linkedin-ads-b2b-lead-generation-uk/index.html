LinkedIn Ads for B2B Lead Generation: A Practical Guide for UK Companies

How UK B2B companies use LinkedIn Ads for lead generation: targeting, ad formats, Lead Gen Forms, budgets, offers that convert and measuring cost per lead.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • LinkedIn’s strength is professional targeting by job title, seniority, industry and company, which suits B2B offers with a clear audience.
  • Clicks cost more than on many platforms, so the offer, creative and follow-up process decide whether it pays off.
  • Measure cost per qualified lead and pipeline, not cost per click, and connect LinkedIn leads to your CRM.

LinkedIn Ads let B2B companies put their message in front of specific professionals: by job title, seniority, function, industry, company size and even named companies. For UK businesses selling to other businesses, that precision makes LinkedIn one of the most useful paid channels for lead generation, provided the offer and follow-up are strong enough to justify higher click costs.

This guide explains how LinkedIn Ads work, which formats and targeting options to use, how to budget and how to judge whether your campaigns are worth it.

Why LinkedIn works for B2B

On most ad platforms, you target people by interests and behaviour, then hope they are decision-makers. On LinkedIn, people describe their own jobs. That means you can reach, for example, finance directors at UK manufacturing companies with 50 to 500 employees, or HR managers in the education sector, without guessing.

The trade-off is cost. Clicks on LinkedIn are typically more expensive than on Meta or many Google searches, so campaigns need a clear audience, a strong offer and a sales process ready to follow up quickly. For context on where LinkedIn fits among other channels, see our paid media service.

Keep audiences focused but not tiny. Very narrow audiences can be expensive and slow to deliver; very broad ones waste money on people who will never buy. LinkedIn's audience size estimates help find a sensible middle ground.

Ad formats worth using

Single image ads

The workhorse format: an image, headline and short introductory text in the feed. Simple to produce and easy to test.

Document ads

Let people preview a guide or report in the feed and download the full version via a form. Good for content-led lead generation.

Video ads

Useful for explaining a product, introducing your team or sharing a short insight. Keep them short and add captions, as many people watch without sound.

Conversation and message ads

Delivered to LinkedIn inboxes. They can work for event invitations or high-value offers but should be used sparingly and relevantly.

Thought leader ads

Promote posts from individual employees, such as a founder or specialist, which can feel more personal than company page ads.

Lead Gen Forms vs landing pages

LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms open inside LinkedIn and pre-fill details from the user's profile. They usually produce more leads at a lower cost per lead, because there is less friction. The trade-off is that some leads are less engaged, because submitting is so easy.

Landing pages on your own website take more effort to complete but often produce more qualified leads and let you tell a fuller story. Many companies test both: Lead Gen Forms for content offers such as guides, and landing pages for higher-intent offers such as demos or consultations. Offers that convert on LinkedIn

People on LinkedIn are not usually searching for a supplier at that moment, so the offer has to earn attention. Offers that tend to work include:

  • Practical guides, templates or benchmarks relevant to the audience's job.
  • Webinars and events with a clear, useful topic.
  • Assessments, audits or calculators that give a personal result.
  • Demos or consultations, best aimed at warm audiences who already know you.

Matching the offer to the audience's stage matters. Cold audiences rarely book a demo from a first ad; warm audiences who have read your guide might.

Budgeting for LinkedIn Ads

LinkedIn has minimum daily budgets, and because clicks cost more, a meaningful test needs enough budget to generate a reasonable number of leads. Rather than spreading a small budget across many audiences, focus on one well-defined audience and one strong offer, then expand once you have results.

Measuring LinkedIn lead generation

Cost per click and click-through rate are useful diagnostics, but the numbers that matter are further down the funnel:

  1. Cost per lead, for each campaign and offer.
  2. Lead quality: how many leads match your ideal customer profile.
  3. Meetings or opportunities generated from those leads.
  4. Pipeline and revenue, where your CRM allows you to track it.

Install the LinkedIn Insight Tag, set up conversion tracking, and connect Lead Gen Forms to your CRM so leads are not lost in a spreadsheet. Respond quickly: a lead contacted the same day is far more likely to convert than one contacted a week later.

LinkedIn and account-based marketing

For companies with a defined list of target accounts, LinkedIn is a natural fit for account-based marketing. Upload a company list, show tailored ads to the buying group in those companies, and coordinate with sales outreach. This is especially effective for higher-value B2B services where a handful of new clients makes a big difference.

Writing LinkedIn ads that get noticed

LinkedIn feeds are busy, and most people scroll quickly. A few principles help your ads stand out for the right reasons:

  • Speak to one audience. An ad written for finance directors will outperform one written for "business leaders" in general.
  • Lead with the problem or outcome the reader cares about, not with your company name.
  • Be specific. "A 12-point checklist for preparing your first ISO audit" is more compelling than "Download our guide".
  • Keep introductory text short. Put the key message in the first line, before the "see more" cut-off.
  • Use real visuals. Genuine photos of your team or product, or clear previews of the guide, tend to feel more credible than stock imagery.
  • Test variations. Run two or three versions of each ad and let the data show which works.

A simple campaign structure

For most UK B2B companies starting out, a two-stage structure works well. The first campaign targets your ideal customer profile with a genuinely useful piece of content, such as a guide or benchmark, using Lead Gen Forms. The second retargets people who engaged with the first campaign or visited your website, with a higher-commitment offer such as a consultation, demo or event invitation. This mirrors how B2B buyers actually behave: learn first, then talk to suppliers.

Common LinkedIn Ads mistakes

  • Leaving LinkedIn Audience Network on by default without reviewing where ads appear.
  • Targeting too broadly, or too narrowly to deliver.
  • Asking cold audiences for a demo straight away.
  • Generic creative that could be from any company.
  • No process for following up leads quickly.
  • Judging campaigns on cost per click rather than lead quality.

Combining LinkedIn with search and content

LinkedIn works best as part of a wider B2B plan. Search ads and SEO capture people actively looking for a solution, while LinkedIn builds awareness and generates demand among people who are not searching yet. FAQs

Are LinkedIn Ads worth it for B2B?

For many B2B companies, yes, because of the professional targeting. They work best when the offer is strong, the audience is well defined and leads are followed up quickly.

Why are LinkedIn Ads so expensive?

LinkedIn's audience is professional and highly targetable, and competition for decision-makers is high, so clicks typically cost more than on many other platforms.

Should I use LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms or a landing page?

Lead Gen Forms usually produce more leads at lower cost; landing pages often produce more qualified leads. Testing both for different offers is a sensible approach.

What is a good cost per lead on LinkedIn?

It depends on your sector, audience and deal value. Judge it against how many leads become customers and what those customers are worth.

Can I target specific companies on LinkedIn?

Yes. You can upload a list of company names to target their employees, which is the basis of account-based marketing on LinkedIn.
