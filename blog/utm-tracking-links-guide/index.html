UTM Tracking Links: How to Tag Your Marketing So You Know What Works

UTM tracking links show which emails, posts and ads send visitors to your site. Learn how to build, name and organise them so your GA4 reports stay clean.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • UTM tracking links add short labels to a web address so analytics tools can tell you which email, post, QR code or ad sent each visitor.
  • A simple written naming convention, used consistently in lower case, matters more than any tool and keeps your GA4 reports readable.
  • Never put personal data in UTM values, never tag internal links on your own site, and check that auto-tagged ad platforms are not being overwritten.

UTM tracking links are ordinary web addresses with a few extra labels on the end that tell your analytics tool where a visitor came from, such as a particular newsletter, Instagram post or printed flyer. Used consistently, they turn a vague "direct" or "social" figure in GA4 into a clear answer about which piece of marketing actually brought people to your website.

Most UK small businesses we speak to run several channels at once: an email newsletter, a few social posts a week, perhaps a local magazine advert or a QR code on a van. Without tagging, much of that traffic blends together. This guide explains what the labels mean, how to build links by hand, how to name them so reports stay tidy, and the mistakes that quietly spoil the data.

What are UTM tracking links?

UTM stands for Urchin Tracking Module, a name left over from the analytics product that later became Google Analytics. A UTM link is your normal page address followed by a question mark and a set of parameters. When someone clicks it, your analytics tool reads those parameters and records them against the visit.

For example, a link in your spring newsletter might point to your booking page with labels saying the source was your newsletter, the medium was email and the campaign was a spring offer. In GA4 you can then see sessions, enquiries and sales attributed to that campaign rather than lumped in with everything else. If you have not yet set GA4 up properly, start with our GA4 setup guide, because tags are only useful if the analytics underneath is working.

The five UTM parameters explained

There are five common parameters. Three are the ones you will use almost every time, and two are optional.

ParameterWhat it describesExample valueUse it?
utm_sourceWho or where sent the trafficnewsletter, facebook, local-magazineAlways
utm_mediumThe type of channelemail, social, paid-social, print, qrAlways
utm_campaignThe specific promotion or pushspring-offer-2026Always
utm_contentWhich version or placement was clickedheader-button, footer-linkWhen testing variations
utm_termA keyword, mostly for paid searchemergency-plumberRarely for small businesses

The medium value deserves the most care. GA4 groups traffic into default channel groups such as Email, Organic Social and Paid Social partly by reading the medium. If you invent unusual medium names, visits may fall into an "Unassigned" bucket. Check Google's current help pages on default channel groups before settling on your medium values, as the rules can change.

How to build a UTM link step by step

  1. Pick the destination page. Send people to the most relevant page, not always the homepage. A dedicated offer page or a well-built landing page usually converts better and makes the numbers easier to read.
  2. Choose your three core values. Decide source, medium and campaign using your naming convention (more on that below).
  3. Add the parameters. After the page address, add a question mark, then each parameter and value joined with an equals sign, separated by ampersands. If the address already contains a question mark, use an ampersand to add yours instead.
  4. Use a builder if you prefer. Google offers a free campaign URL builder, and many email and social scheduling tools have tagging built in. Check each tool's current help pages to see how it handles tags.
  5. Test the link. Click it, check the page loads correctly, then look at the GA4 realtime report to confirm the visit appears with the right source and medium.
  6. Record it. Paste the finished link into your tracking sheet with the date, channel and owner.

Long tagged links look messy in print or on social posts. You can shorten them with a link shortener or a redirect on your own domain, but always test that the tags survive the redirect. Some redirects strip parameters, which means your effort disappears without warning.

Create a naming convention before you start

The most common problem we see is not a missing tag but inconsistent ones. GA4 treats "Facebook", "facebook" and "fb" as three different sources, so a year of reports ends up split across spellings. A one-page naming convention prevents this.

  • Lower case only. Values are case-sensitive, so pick lower case and stick with it.
  • Hyphens, not spaces. Spaces get encoded into odd characters. Use hyphens between words.
  • A fixed list of sources and mediums. Write down the allowed values, for example email, social, paid-social, print, qr and referral, and do not invent new ones on the fly.
  • Descriptive campaign names. Include the offer and a date, such as summer-sale-2026 or open-day-march, so you can still understand them next year.
  • One owner. Whoever builds links checks the sheet first, so two people do not tag the same newsletter differently.

A shared spreadsheet is enough for most small businesses. Columns for date, destination page, source, medium, campaign, content, final link and notes will cover nearly everything.

Where small businesses should use UTM links

Tag any link you control that lives outside your own website and is not already tracked automatically. Typical examples include:

  • Email newsletters and automated email sequences, especially if your email tool does not add tags itself.
  • Organic social posts, link-in-bio pages and pinned posts.
  • QR codes on flyers, menus, vehicles, shop windows and packaging inserts.
  • Links in your Google Business Profile, so you can separate local listing visits from other organic search. Our Google Business Profile guide covers the profile itself.
  • Sponsorships, directory listings and partner websites where you can choose the link.
  • Printed adverts in local magazines that point to a short, memorable redirect.

For QR codes in particular, tagging is often the only way to see whether a print run did anything at all, because a scan otherwise looks like a direct visit.

When not to use UTM links

There are two important exceptions.

Do not tag internal links. Never add UTM parameters to links from one page of your website to another, such as a homepage banner pointing to a product page. Doing so can restart the session and overwrite the real source, so a visitor who arrived from Google search suddenly appears to come from your own banner.

Be careful with auto-tagged ad platforms. Google Ads normally uses auto-tagging, which passes richer data than manual UTMs. Adding manual tags on top can cause conflicts, depending on your settings. Meta and other platforms have their own options for URL parameters. Check each platform's current help pages and decide on one approach per platform. If you run paid campaigns, our paid media service includes setting tracking up so the platforms and GA4 agree.

Reading UTM data in GA4

Once tagged visits start arriving, you will find them in GA4's acquisition reports. The traffic acquisition report can be switched to show session source, medium or campaign, and you can add a secondary dimension to combine them. Exploration reports let you go further, for example comparing which campaign produced the most enquiries.

Visits alone are not the goal. Make sure the actions that matter, such as a form submission, booking or purchase, are set up as key events, so you can see which tagged links lead to real outcomes. Our guide to website analytics for small business explains which numbers are worth watching and which are noise, and our SEO reporting guide shows how campaign data fits into a monthly report.

Expect some gaps. Privacy settings, cookie choices and blocked scripts mean analytics never captures every visit. UTM data shows you trends and comparisons between your own campaigns rather than a perfect headcount.

Privacy and UTM links

UTM values end up in analytics tools, browser histories and sometimes in shared screenshots, so treat them as public. Never put an email address, name, phone number, customer ID or anything else that identifies a person into a parameter. Google's own policies prohibit sending personal data to Google Analytics, and UK GDPR expectations point the same way.

Your analytics also depends on consent. If your cookie banner lets visitors refuse analytics, some tagged visits will not be recorded, or will be modelled. That is expected. Our guide to cookie banner design covers how to ask for consent clearly, and the ICO's guidance on cookies and similar technologies is worth reading directly.

FAQs

What are UTM tracking links?

They are normal web addresses with extra labels added after a question mark. Analytics tools read those labels to record which source, channel and campaign sent each visitor to your website.

Do UTM links affect SEO?

Used on external links such as emails and social posts, they do not usually cause SEO problems. Avoid using them on internal links, and make sure your pages have canonical tags so tagged versions are not treated as separate pages.

Which UTM parameters do I need?

Source, medium and campaign cover most needs. Add content when you want to compare two links in the same email or post. Term is mainly for paid search and rarely needed by small businesses.

Should I add UTM tags to Google Ads links?

Usually Google Ads auto-tagging is the better option because it passes more detail to GA4. Mixing manual tags with auto-tagging can cause conflicts, so check Google's current help pages and choose one approach.

Why does my tagged traffic show as direct?

Common causes are a redirect or link shortener stripping the parameters, a typo in the tag, or consent settings preventing analytics from recording the visit. Test the full journey and check realtime reports.

Can I put customer names in UTM tags?

No. Never include names, email addresses or other personal data. Values can be seen in analytics, browser histories and shared links, and analytics platform policies prohibit sending personal data.
