Email Marketing Agency for Canadian Businesses: Flows, Campaigns and CASL

Looking for an email marketing agency in Canada? What good email work includes, how CASL consent shapes it, and how to choose a provider that fits your brand.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Strong email marketing starts with automated flows such as welcome, abandoned cart and post-purchase, then adds regular campaigns to segmented lists.
  • Canada's anti-spam legislation (CASL) shapes how you collect consent, identify yourself and offer unsubscribes, so check current official guidance and take your own advice.
  • Choose an email marketing agency on strategy, list ownership, consent practices and clear reporting, not on how many emails it promises to send.

An email marketing agency helps Canadian businesses turn subscribers into repeat customers by building automated flows, sending well-targeted campaigns and keeping consent and list practices in line with Canada's anti-spam legislation (CASL). Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that supports Canadian businesses remotely, with no office in Canada, and this guide explains what good email marketing includes, how CASL shapes the work and how to choose an agency you can trust.

Email remains one of the few marketing channels you genuinely own. Social platforms change their algorithms and ad costs rise, but a healthy list of people who have chosen to hear from you is an asset that grows over time. That is true whether you run an online store shipping across Canada, a clinic with returning patients, a B2B firm nurturing long sales cycles or a hospitality business filling quieter weeks.

What an email marketing agency in Canada should actually do

Email marketing is more than writing a monthly newsletter. A capable agency should cover strategy, setup, content and measurement:

  • Platform setup and deliverability. Connecting your email platform to your website or store, authenticating your sending domain and keeping lists clean so messages reach the inbox. Our guide to business domain and email setup explains the foundations.
  • Automated flows. Welcome series, abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase and win-back emails that run in the background. Our guide to abandoned cart recovery covers one of the most valuable.
  • Segmentation. Sending different messages to new subscribers, first-time buyers, loyal customers and lapsed contacts rather than one email to everyone.
  • Campaigns and content. Planned campaigns for launches, seasonal moments and useful content, written in your brand voice. See our guide on how to write an email newsletter.
  • List growth. Sign-up forms, pop-ups and lead magnets that attract the right subscribers with clear consent. Our email list building guide covers the tactics.
  • Reporting. Revenue or enquiries per email, click rates, unsubscribes and list growth, with clear next actions.

Email sits within our digital PR and content service, and for online stores it connects closely with our ecommerce work, because flows depend on clean product and customer data.

Email marketing and CASL: what Canadian businesses should know

Canada's anti-spam legislation is widely regarded as one of the stricter email marketing regimes. In broad terms, it sets rules around consent to send commercial electronic messages, identifying who the message is from and giving recipients a simple way to unsubscribe. It distinguishes between express consent and implied consent in certain situations, and implied consent can be time-limited.

This is general information only. CASL and its guidance are detailed, they apply to more than just email, and penalties for breaches can be significant. Check current official guidance from the Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC) and the Government of Canada, and take your own legal advice on your obligations. We build sign-up forms, consent records and unsubscribe handling with these rules in mind, but the legal judgement stays with you and your advisers.

Automated flows to build first

If you are starting from scratch, flows usually deliver more value than one-off campaigns because they run every day without extra effort. A sensible order for most online stores is:

  1. Welcome series. Introduce your brand, set expectations and guide new subscribers towards a first purchase or enquiry.
  2. Abandoned cart and checkout. Remind people who left items behind, with clear product images and an easy return to checkout.
  3. Post-purchase. Thank customers, share care or setup tips and ask for a review at the right moment.
  4. Browse abandonment. Follow up on products viewed but not added to the cart, where consent allows.
  5. Win-back. Re-engage customers who have not bought for a while before they drift away.

Service businesses can adapt the same idea: a welcome series for new enquiries, appointment reminders, follow-ups after a consultation and periodic useful content. Email marketing alongside your other channels

Email works best when it is connected to the rest of your marketing rather than run in isolation. Paid social and search ads can bring new visitors to a sign-up offer, and email then nurtures them over weeks or months at a far lower cost per contact. Our comparison of email marketing and social media explains how the two complement each other. For online stores, loyalty schemes, reviews and post-purchase emails all reinforce one another, and our guide to ecommerce loyalty programmes covers how to reward repeat customers without eroding margins.

Tracking matters too. Tagging email links consistently lets you see in your analytics which campaigns and flows lead to sales or enquiries, rather than relying only on the email platform's own reports. That joined-up view makes it far easier to decide where the next pound or dollar of effort should go.

How to choose an email marketing agency in Canada

  • Who owns the list and platform account? It should always be you.
  • How do you handle consent? Ask how sign-up forms, imported lists and consent records will be managed with CASL in mind.
  • What will you build first? A good answer prioritises flows and deliverability before sending more campaigns.
  • How do you measure success? Look for revenue or enquiries per email, not just open rates, which are less reliable than they once were.
  • Who writes the emails? And how is your brand voice captured and approved?

Be wary of anyone offering to sell you a purchased list or to email contacts who never agreed to hear from you. Beyond the legal risk, it damages deliverability for every future email you send.

FAQs

What does an email marketing agency in Canada do?

It sets up and manages your email platform, builds automated flows, plans and writes campaigns, grows your list with clear consent and reports on the revenue or enquiries email generates, while keeping practices aligned with CASL.

Do you have an office in Canada?

No. Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio with no Canadian office. We support Canadian businesses remotely through video calls, shared documents and regular reporting.

Can you make sure our email marketing complies with CASL?

We build forms, consent records and unsubscribe handling with CASL in mind, but we cannot give legal advice. Check current official guidance from the CRTC and take your own legal advice on your obligations.

How much does email marketing support cost?

Email work can be included within our retainers at £350, £750 or £1,500 a month, quoted in pounds sterling with Canadian dollar figures on request. Platform subscription costs are paid separately.

Which email flows should we set up first?

For most online stores, start with a welcome series, abandoned cart, post-purchase and win-back flows. Service businesses can adapt these into enquiry follow-ups, reminders and nurture sequences.

Can we email customers from an old list?

Only if you have appropriate consent for those contacts. Imported or old lists are a common CASL risk, so review how consent was gathered and take your own advice before sending.
