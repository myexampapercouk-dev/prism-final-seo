Ecommerce SEO UAE: How to Get Your Online Store Found in Search

Ecommerce SEO for UAE online stores: category and product pages, Arabic and English search, technical fixes, what an agency should do and our monthly fees.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Ecommerce SEO in the UAE starts with a clean technical set-up, then category and product pages built around how shoppers search in English and Arabic.
  • Category pages usually carry the most commercial search demand, so they deserve unique copy, sensible filters and strong internal links.
  • Our SEO retainers are Launch £350, Growth £750 and Scale £1,500 a month, delivered remotely from the UK, with results typically in 3-6 months.

Ecommerce SEO in the UAE means making your online store's category and product pages appear when shoppers across the emirates search for what you sell, in English and in Arabic, and turning those visits into orders. It combines technical fixes, keyword research, better category and product content and steady internal linking, measured by revenue rather than rankings alone.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that provides SEO and GEO services for online stores, working remotely with UAE retailers. We have no office or staff in the UAE. We write in English, and Arabic content is written with your team or a translator. This guide explains how ecommerce SEO works for a UAE store, what to prioritise and what our retainers cost.

Why ecommerce SEO in the UAE is different

UAE shoppers have plenty of choice. Large marketplaces, regional retailers and international brands all compete for the same searches. A smaller store can still win, but it has to be precise about where it competes:

  • Marketplaces dominate head terms. Broad product searches are often led by big marketplaces. Independent stores usually do better on specific, longer searches, such as a product type plus a material, size, use or brand.
  • Two languages, different searches. Arabic and English shoppers often use different words for the same product. Keyword research needs to be done in each language, not translated.
  • Delivery and returns matter. Shoppers want to know delivery times and costs across the emirates before they buy. Clear delivery information supports both conversions and search visibility.
  • Mobile shopping. Most product browsing happens on phones, so page speed and mobile layouts have a direct effect on both rankings and sales.

Technical SEO for online stores

Online stores create technical problems that brochure sites rarely face. Filters and sorting options can generate thousands of near-identical URLs. Out-of-stock and discontinued products can leave dead ends. Language versions can compete with each other if they are not linked correctly. We check each of these and fix them in priority order.

Speed matters too. Large product images, heavy apps and scripts slow down mobile pages. If you run Shopify, our Shopify speed optimisation guide explains the usual culprits. Whatever platform you use, compressed images, fewer scripts and a lean theme help both shoppers and search engines.

Category pages carry the most weight

For most stores, category pages match the searches with the most buying intent, such as a product type in a particular style or for a particular use. Yet many category pages contain nothing but a grid of products. We add a short, useful introduction, logical filters, a few answers to common questions and links to related categories. Our guide to ecommerce category page design shows how to balance SEO content with a clean shopping layout.

The category structure itself matters as well. If shoppers search for a sub-type often enough, it may deserve its own category page rather than a filter. Keyword research helps decide where that line falls.

Product pages that rank and sell

Product pages should answer the questions a shopper would ask in a store: what it is, what it is made of, what size it comes in, how it is used and how quickly it arrives. Copying the manufacturer's description means your page looks the same as every other retailer's. Our guides to product page SEO and writing ecommerce product descriptions explain how to make each page distinct.

Structured data for products, prices, availability and reviews you genuinely have can help your listings show more detail in results. Prices on your store should be shown in the local currency your customers pay in.

Arabic and English store versions

A bilingual store needs separate URLs for each language, properly linked so search engines show the right version to the right shopper. Product names, category labels and filter values should all be translated by a person, not a plugin. We plan the structure, handle the technical set-up and write English content, while Arabic copy is written with your team or a translator. Our guide to a bilingual Arabic and English website covers the planning in more detail.

Content beyond the shop

Buying guides, comparisons and care guides capture people earlier in their search and link naturally to categories and products. A small number of genuinely useful guides is better than a stream of thin blog posts. We plan these around real questions shoppers ask and link each one to the relevant category.

AI answer tools increasingly recommend products and stores. Clear product information, honest comparisons and well-structured FAQs give these tools more to work with, and we build that into our SEO work.

Common ecommerce SEO mistakes we see

When we audit UAE online stores, a handful of problems come up again and again:

  • Empty category pages. A grid of products with no text gives search engines little reason to rank the page.
  • Duplicate product copy. Manufacturer descriptions shared across many retailers make it hard for any one store to stand out.
  • Filter pages indexed without control. Thousands of filter combinations can dilute crawling and create thin, duplicate pages.
  • Machine-translated Arabic. Automatic translation often reads poorly and misses the words shoppers actually use.
  • Deleted products with no redirect. Removing products without redirecting them wastes any links and rankings they had earned.
  • Hidden delivery information. If delivery times and costs only appear at checkout, shoppers leave and search engines see a weaker page.

Fixing these is often the quickest way to improve organic sales before any new content is written.
