The Quebec City search market

Quebec City is the provincial capital and a major centre for government, health care, education, insurance, tourism and a growing technology sector. The historic old town draws visitors throughout the year, with strong peaks around summer festivals and the winter carnival. Surrounding areas such as Lévis, Sainte-Foy, Charlesbourg, Beauport and L'Ancienne-Lorette add a large suburban population.

Most residents search in French, while tourists, students and some business buyers search in English. That split shapes everything about a campaign, from keyword research to ad copy and landing pages. A campaign that only targets English searches will miss most of the local market, while a French campaign that sends clicks to an English page will waste money.

Language and Quebec rules

Quebec has language legislation that affects how businesses communicate commercially, including on websites and in advertising. In general terms, French needs a prominent place in commercial communication aimed at Quebec customers. The rules were updated in recent years and the details matter.

We are not legal advisers and keep this note general. The business is responsible for meeting Quebec language requirements, and we recommend checking official guidance from the Office québécois de la langue française or speaking to your own adviser. In practice, we structure campaigns so French searches lead to French ads and French landing pages, with English versions running alongside where appropriate.

Campaign types that suit Quebec City businesses

Search campaigns are usually the core, because they reach people actively looking for a service. For local service firms, such as renovation companies, clinics, lawyers and accountants, search ads with call options and location assets work well. Our guide to Google Ads assets explains these extras.

Hotels, restaurants and tourism businesses can use search to reach visitors planning a trip, with campaigns adjusted for seasonal peaks. Retailers selling online may benefit from Shopping and Performance Max campaigns, provided product feeds and tracking are in good shape. B2B firms often focus on narrower, high-intent searches and pair Google Ads with LinkedIn, as covered in our guide to LinkedIn Ads vs Google Ads for B2B.

Targeting the right area

Location settings are one of the most common sources of wasted spend. By default, Google may show ads to people who are interested in a location rather than physically in it. For a local service business that is often a mistake. For a hotel, it can be exactly right, because travellers search from elsewhere.

We set location targeting to match the business model, whether that is the Quebec City area only, the wider Capitale-Nationale region or travellers searching from other provinces and countries. Our guide to Google Ads location targeting covers the settings in detail.

Tracking that shows real results

Without accurate tracking, it is impossible to know which campaigns are worth the money. We set up conversion tracking for the actions that matter, such as phone calls, form submissions, booking requests and online purchases. Where appropriate, we use enhanced conversions to improve accuracy while respecting privacy rules.

Our guides to Google Ads conversion tracking and enhanced conversions explain the setup. Quebec has its own privacy law, which affects how personal information and tracking are handled, so businesses should review their consent approach with their own adviser.

Cutting wasted spend

Many accounts we review waste budget on irrelevant searches, overly broad keywords and ads that send traffic to the home page. Regular negative keyword reviews, tighter match types, dedicated landing pages and sensible bidding make a big difference. Our negative keywords guide and bidding strategies guide explain the methods we use.

Landing pages matter as much as the ads. If a French search leads to a slow or unclear page, even a perfect ad will struggle. We can improve or build landing pages through our website work, with French text supplied by your team.

Budgets and seasonality

Quebec City has strong seasonal patterns. Tourism, hospitality and some retail businesses see clear peaks in summer and around the winter carnival, while trades such as roofing, landscaping and renovation follow the weather. Snow removal, heating and winter services flip the pattern entirely. A fixed monthly budget that ignores these swings often overspends in quiet months and runs out when demand is highest.

We plan budgets around the business calendar, increasing spend ahead of known peaks and trimming it when demand falls. Ad schedules can also focus spend on the hours when calls are answered, which matters for small teams that cannot take enquiries late at night. For new accounts, we usually suggest starting with a modest budget focused on the highest-intent services, then expanding once tracking shows which searches bring profitable work.

Budget size also shapes structure. A smaller budget works best concentrated on a few tightly themed campaigns, rather than spread thinly across every service, language and area. As results become clear, we add campaigns for further services or neighbouring areas such as Lévis.
