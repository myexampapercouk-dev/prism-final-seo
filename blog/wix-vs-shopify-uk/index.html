Wix vs Shopify for UK Businesses: Which Platform Should You Sell On?

Wix vs Shopify for UK businesses: selling features, design, payments, apps, SEO, growth and costs compared, so you can choose the right platform for your shop.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify is built around selling products, while Wix is a general website builder with ecommerce added to it.
  • Wix often suits service businesses and small catalogues; Shopify usually suits businesses where online sales are the main source of revenue.
  • Think about where you expect to be in two or three years, because moving platform later usually means a rebuild.

Wix and Shopify both let UK businesses build a website and take payments online without writing code, so they often end up on the same shortlist. They come from different starting points, though. Wix began as a general website builder and added ecommerce as one of many features. Shopify was built from the ground up for selling products online, and everything else revolves around the shop. That difference shapes almost every comparison point.

This guide is for UK owners deciding where to build or rebuild an online shop. It compares the two on selling features, design, payments, apps, search, growth and running costs, then sets out a few scenarios to help you decide.

The short answer

Your situationLikely fit
Online product sales are, or will be, your main source of revenueShopify
You sell services, bookings or a handful of products alongside themWix
You want to sell across several channels, such as social media and marketplacesShopify
You want a single drag-and-drop tool for a small business website with a little sellingWix
You expect your catalogue, order volume or team to grow significantlyShopify

How the platforms differ

Wix

Wix is a hosted builder known for its flexible drag-and-drop editor. You can place elements almost anywhere on the page, choose from a large library of templates and add features such as bookings, events, blogs and online stores from the same dashboard. For businesses whose website does many jobs, that all-in-one approach is convenient.

Shopify

Shopify is a hosted ecommerce platform. Products, inventory, orders, customers, discounts, shipping and payments are at its core, and themes and apps are designed around them. It also offers point-of-sale tools for physical retail and connections to other sales channels. Content pages and blogs are available, but they are not the main focus.

Selling features

Both platforms cover the basics: product pages, variants such as size and colour, stock tracking, discount codes, shipping rates, tax settings and order management. The differences show as a shop becomes more involved.

  • Catalogue and variants. Shopify is built to handle large catalogues, many variants and bulk editing. Wix handles smaller catalogues comfortably. Our guide to product variants and bundles covers the decisions involved.
  • Inventory and fulfilment. Shopify has deeper tools for multiple locations, fulfilment workflows and integrations with warehouses and couriers.
  • Checkout. Shopify's checkout is designed and continuously developed specifically for conversion, and it is familiar to many shoppers. Wix's checkout is perfectly usable for smaller stores.
  • Multichannel selling. Shopify connects to a wide range of channels, including Google, social platforms and marketplaces, so stock and orders can be managed in one place.
  • Subscriptions and B2B. Both offer options, but Shopify's ecosystem tends to give more choice. See our guide to ecommerce subscriptions if repeat orders matter to you.

Design and editing

Wix's editor gives a lot of visual freedom. That suits people who want to arrange a page exactly as they picture it, but it also makes it easier to create layouts that look good on desktop and awkward on a phone, so mobile views need checking carefully.

Shopify themes are built from sections and blocks. There is less pixel-level freedom, but layouts tend to stay consistent and work well on mobile. Deeper changes are possible through theme code, and a theme can be customised extensively by a developer. Our Shopify theme customisation guide explains what is involved.

Most UK shoppers browse on their phones, so whichever you choose, design for mobile first. Our mobile website design guide covers the essentials.

Payments in the UK

Both platforms offer their own payment processing in the UK, along with options such as card payments, digital wallets and third-party gateways. Fees and available methods vary by plan and change over time, so check each platform's current UK pricing. With Shopify, using a third-party payment provider instead of Shopify's own processing may add an extra transaction fee, so factor that into comparisons.

Shoppers in the UK generally expect to pay by card and digital wallets, and many look for PayPal or buy-now-pay-later options. If you offer buy-now-pay-later, be aware that how it is promoted can be subject to financial promotion rules, so check current guidance from the FCA or take advice.

Apps and integrations

Wix has its own app market and a growing range of built-in business tools. Shopify's app ecosystem is larger and more focused on commerce, covering reviews, subscriptions, loyalty, shipping, accounting, product feeds and more.

More apps is not automatically better. Each one may add a monthly cost, slow pages or overlap with others. A good build uses the platform's built-in features first and adds apps only where they earn their place.

SEO and content

Both can rank well in Google, and both cover the essentials: editable titles and descriptions, clean URLs, sitemaps, redirects and structured data for products. Shopify has some fixed URL patterns, such as product and collection paths, that cannot be fully changed. Wix gives a little more freedom over page URLs. Neither limitation stops a well-run shop ranking.

What matters most is the work done on top: unique product and category descriptions, helpful content, good internal linking and fast pages. Our guides to Shopify SEO and product page SEO explain the details.

Speed and reliability

Both platforms handle hosting and security for you, which removes a lot of technical work. Speed then depends mainly on your theme or template, image sizes and the number of apps and scripts you add. Our Shopify speed optimisation guide covers the common causes of slow stores.

Growth and scalability

This is where the platforms diverge most. Shopify is designed to grow with a retail business, from a first product to a large catalogue with high order volumes, and it offers an enterprise tier, Shopify Plus, for larger brands. If you are planning international selling, multiple warehouses, wholesale or significant growth in orders, Shopify generally gives more headroom. Our guide to selling internationally covers what that involves.

Wix suits businesses where the shop is one part of a broader website and order volumes stay moderate. Many small businesses never need more.

Running costs

Both charge a monthly or annual subscription, with ecommerce available on specific plans, plus payment processing fees on each sale. The total cost of ownership also includes paid apps, premium themes or templates, and any design or development help. Exact prices change regularly, so compare each platform's current UK pricing pages rather than relying on figures quoted elsewhere. Our Shopify website cost guide breaks down the main cost areas, and our pricing page explains how we price our own work.

Some simple scenarios

A yoga teacher or beauty salon selling classes or treatments, with a few gift vouchers and products, may well be happier on Wix, where bookings and the website sit together.

A clothing or homeware brand with dozens or hundreds of products and plans to advertise and grow will usually be better served by Shopify, where inventory, variants, checkout and multichannel selling are stronger.

A maker selling a small range online could go either way. If the shop is likely to become the core of the business, starting on Shopify can avoid a migration later.

For a related comparison, read our guide to Shopify vs Squarespace.

Moving from Wix to Shopify

Many businesses start on Wix and later move to Shopify as sales grow. Products, customers and orders can often be exported and imported, though not everything transfers cleanly, and the design is rebuilt. Protecting search visibility with a full URL map and 301 redirects is essential. Our SEO migration checklist sets out the steps.

FAQs

Is Shopify better than Wix for ecommerce?

For businesses where product sales are the main focus, Shopify is usually stronger because it is built around selling. Wix can be a good fit for smaller shops and service businesses.

Is Wix cheaper than Shopify?

It depends on the plans, apps and payment options you use. Compare total costs, including processing fees and apps, using each platform's current UK pricing.

Can Wix handle a large online shop?

Wix can run a sizeable store, but Shopify generally offers more depth for large catalogues, high order volumes and multichannel selling.

Is Wix or Shopify better for SEO?

Both can rank well. Content, structure, product descriptions and speed matter far more than the platform choice.

Can I move my Wix store to Shopify?

Yes. Products and customers can often be migrated, but the design is rebuilt and redirects must be set up to protect rankings.

Which is easier to use, Wix or Shopify?

Wix's drag-and-drop editor feels easier for general page design. Shopify's admin is easier for managing products, stock and orders.
