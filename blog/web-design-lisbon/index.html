Web Design for Lisbon Businesses: Working With a Remote UK Studio

Need web design in Lisbon? What a Portuguese business website needs, how a remote UK studio works in your time zone, languages, timelines and pricing in pounds.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Web design for a Lisbon business usually means planning for Portuguese and English visitors, strong mobile pages and an easy route to book, enquire or buy.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Portugal; we work remotely in the same time zone as Lisbon and write in English, working with your team or a translator on Portuguese pages.
  • Small websites typically take three to five weeks and cost £1,000 to £2,000, priced in pounds with euro and other currencies quoted on request.

Web design for a Lisbon business should give Portuguese and international visitors a fast, trustworthy website that explains what you offer and makes it simple to book, enquire or buy. The best results come from planning structure, languages, content and technical setup together, before any design work starts.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites for businesses across Europe, including Portugal. We have no office in Lisbon and will not suggest otherwise. Lisbon and the UK share the same time zone for most of the year, which makes remote working straightforward: calls happen during normal working hours, and you can review progress on a private staging site whenever suits you.

Web design Lisbon: what local businesses usually need

Lisbon's economy mixes tourism and hospitality, technology companies, start-ups, professional services, property, education and a growing international community. Many businesses serve Portuguese customers and English-speaking visitors or residents at the same time. A good website reflects that without becoming confusing.

  • A clear language plan. Decide which pages need Portuguese, English or both, and how visitors switch between them.
  • Mobile-first design. Visitors, especially tourists, often find you on a phone while out and about.
  • Direct booking or enquiry routes. Booking forms, contact forms, click-to-call and map links that work first time.
  • Search-friendly structure. Pages built around what people search for, with clean URLs, headings and descriptions.
  • Accessibility and privacy basics. Readable text, keyboard-friendly navigation and a respectful consent setup.

Our website and ecommerce development service includes all of these as standard parts of the build.

Portuguese and English versions done properly

A two-language website needs more than an automatic translation widget. Each version should be written for its readers, with its own page titles and descriptions, and search engines should be told clearly which page serves which language. Planning this at the start avoids rebuilding menus or changing URLs later.

Prism writes in English. For Portuguese content, we work with your own team or a native-speaking translator or copywriter you choose, and we build the site so both versions are easy to edit and keep in step. If your audience includes Brazilian visitors as well as Portuguese ones, it is worth deciding early which variety of Portuguese the site uses. Our multilingual website design guide explains the structural options and their trade-offs.

Websites for Lisbon hotels, restaurants and tourism businesses

Hospitality and tourism businesses in Lisbon compete hard for visitors who are comparing options on their phones, often in English. A site that loads quickly, shows real photography, answers practical questions and lets people book directly can reduce reliance on third-party platforms and their commissions.

We can integrate booking tools, menus, opening times, maps and enquiry forms, and make sure each page works well on mobile. Our guide to website online booking systems compares common approaches, and our hospitality sector page explains how we support hotels, restaurants and venues more widely.

Websites for start-ups and international firms in Lisbon

Lisbon attracts many technology firms, remote teams and international companies. Their websites often need to work for investors, partners, recruits and clients across several countries, usually led in English. Priorities include clear positioning, strong service or product pages, case study templates you can fill with your own work, careers pages and fast performance.

For these businesses, the structure of the site matters as much as its look. A short website discovery workshop helps agree audiences, messages and page priorities before design begins, which saves time and revisions later.

How a remote website project runs

  1. Discovery call. Goals, audiences, languages, current site, budget and deadlines.
  2. Written scope. Pages, features, languages, responsibilities and a fixed price in pounds.
  3. Content plan. A sitemap and page outlines, so Portuguese and English content can be prepared alongside design.
  4. Design. Key pages designed and shared for feedback, then applied across the site.
  5. Build on staging. A private version of the site you can test on your own phone and laptop.
  6. Testing and launch. Forms, bookings, speed, mobile layouts, redirects and tracking checked before go-live.
  7. Handover. Training and documentation so your team can update content confidently.

Content is the most common cause of delay, particularly with two languages. Our guide to preparing website content before the build explains what to gather early. Photography, menus, prices and service descriptions are worth collecting before design starts, so pages can be built with real content rather than placeholders.

Mobile design, accessibility and privacy

Most Lisbon visitors will see your site on a phone first, so we design mobile layouts from the start rather than shrinking a desktop design. Our mobile website design guide covers what makes a phone visit work well.

Accessibility requirements are growing for businesses selling certain products and services to consumers in the EU. Our European Accessibility Act guide gives an overview. Privacy rules also affect cookies and tracking for visitors in Portugal. These notes are general information only; check current official guidance from Portuguese and EU authorities, and take legal advice where your situation needs it.

Questions to ask any web designer for a Lisbon business

  • Who writes the Portuguese content, and who checks it before launch?
  • How will the language versions be structured for visitors and search engines?
  • Can I test the site on a staging link before it goes live?
  • What is fixed in the price, and what would count as extra work?
  • Who owns the domain, hosting and website files after handover?
  • What support is available once the site is live, and how is it priced?

Clear, written answers to these questions are a better guide than location alone. A partner who explains the process plainly is usually easier to work with, wherever they are based.

FAQs

Do you offer web design in Lisbon?

Yes, remotely. Prism Works Studio is based in the UK and has no office in Portugal. We design and build websites for Lisbon businesses through video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can review throughout, usually in the same time zone.

Can you build a website in Portuguese?

We build the site to support Portuguese and English versions. We write in English, and Portuguese pages are written or translated by your team or a native-speaking translator, then added and checked as part of the project.

How much does a website cost for a Lisbon business?

Our small business websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites £1,500 to £3,500, depending on scope. We price in pounds and can quote euro on request. Translation and third-party costs are usually separate.

How long does it take to build a website?

A small business website typically takes three to five weeks and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks. Two-language sites can take longer if translated content arrives late, so we agree content dates at the start.

Can you add direct booking to a hotel or restaurant website?

Yes. We can integrate booking tools you already use or help you choose one, then design booking pages that work smoothly on mobile. Booking software subscriptions are paid separately to the provider.

Will my website comply with EU accessibility and privacy rules?

We build with accessibility and consent in mind. Whether specific rules apply depends on your business and what you sell, so check current official guidance and take legal advice where needed.
