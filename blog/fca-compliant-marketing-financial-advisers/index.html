FCA-Compliant Digital Marketing for Financial Advisers: A Practical Guide

Digital marketing for UK financial advisers within FCA rules: financial promotions, Consumer Duty, websites, SEO, social media and ads, explained simply.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Marketing from regulated firms is generally treated as a financial promotion: it must be fair, clear and not misleading, and approved through your compliance process.
  • Educational content, clear fee information and well-structured adviser websites support both compliance and lead generation.
  • Build compliance sign-off into the workflow from the start, so campaigns are not delayed or rewritten at the last minute.

Financial advisers can market themselves effectively online while staying within FCA rules. The key is to treat every website page, social post, ad and email as a potential financial promotion that must be fair, clear and not misleading, and to build compliance approval into the process rather than bolting it on at the end.

This guide covers the principles that shape digital marketing for IFAs, wealth managers and mortgage brokers, and how to apply them to websites, SEO, content, social media and paid ads. It is general information, not compliance or legal advice. Your compliance team or network has the final say.

The rules that shape financial adviser marketing

Financial promotions

Communications that invite or induce someone to engage in financial services activity are financial promotions. For regulated firms, the FCA's rules require them to be fair, clear and not misleading. In practice that means balanced information, prominent risk warnings where relevant, no exaggerated claims and nothing that obscures important information.

Consumer Duty

Consumer Duty raised expectations on how firms communicate. Communications should help customers understand products and services and make good decisions, and firms should test whether their communications actually achieve that. Clear language, transparent fees and easy-to-find information matter more than ever.

Platform policies

Google, Meta and LinkedIn each have their own financial services advertising policies, which can require verification or restrict certain products. These sit on top of FCA rules.

Data protection

Enquiry forms, cookies and email marketing fall under UK GDPR and PECR. You need a clear privacy notice, appropriate consent for marketing emails and a compliant cookie banner.

Building an adviser website that converts and complies

An effective adviser website answers the questions prospective clients have before they pick up the phone:

  • Who you help, such as retirees, business owners, families or professionals in a particular field.
  • What you do: retirement planning, pensions, investments, protection, mortgages or estate planning.
  • How you charge, explained clearly. Transparent fee information supports Consumer Duty and builds trust.
  • Who the advisers are, with genuine qualifications and experience.
  • How the process works, from first meeting to ongoing reviews.
  • Your regulatory status, displayed as your compliance team requires.

Good design matters too. Many prospective clients are older and may browse on a phone or tablet, so readable text, simple navigation and accessible forms are essential. Our website development work for advice firms puts these first.

SEO and content for advice firms

People research financial decisions carefully, often starting with questions such as "how much do I need to retire?" or "should I consolidate my pensions?". Educational content that explains topics clearly can attract these searchers and build trust long before they enquire.

The line to watch is between general information and personal advice. Content should explain options, considerations and risks in general terms, and invite readers to seek advice for their own situation. Keep figures such as allowances and thresholds current, and date your content so readers know when it was reviewed.

Local SEO matters for most practices: a complete Google Business Profile, consistent details across directories and pages for the areas you serve. AI assistants increasingly answer financial questions too, so clear, accurate content also supports generative engine optimisation. Our SEO and GEO service covers both.

Social media for financial advisers

Social media works best for advisers as a credibility channel: commentary on news, explainers, practice updates and community involvement. LinkedIn is particularly effective for reaching business owners and professionals.

Remember that a short post can still be a financial promotion. Avoid statements that imply guaranteed returns, cherry-pick performance or omit important risks. Agree with your compliance team which kinds of posts need individual approval and which can follow pre-approved templates.

Paid advertising

Paid search reaches people actively looking for advice, which makes it efficient when done well. Before launching, check platform verification requirements for financial services, make sure the ad copy and landing page say the same thing, and ensure the landing page includes the information and disclosures your compliance team requires. A compliance-friendly marketing workflow

  1. Agree the rules up front. Ask your compliance team or network for their requirements, templates and approval process.
  2. Plan content in batches. A monthly content plan lets compliance review in one go rather than piecemeal.
  3. Keep an approval record. Store approved versions and dates, as your compliance process requires.
  4. Build in review dates. Content with figures or rules should be checked periodically and updated.
  5. Test understanding. Under Consumer Duty, check that communications are understood, for example by asking clients for feedback.

Working this way removes most of the friction between marketing and compliance, and avoids campaigns being delayed at the last minute.

Content ideas that work for advice firms

Good adviser content is useful, specific and general enough not to be personal advice. Ideas that tend to work well include:

  • Plain-English explainers of common topics, such as how pension consolidation works and what to consider before doing it.
  • Guides to what happens in a first meeting with an adviser, which reduce anxiety and encourage enquiries.
  • Annual reminders, such as end of tax year checklists, reviewed and updated each year.
  • Answers to the questions clients ask most often, which also mirror what people type into search engines and AI assistants.
  • Commentary on relevant changes, published promptly once your compliance team has approved it.

Each piece should end with a clear, low-pressure next step, such as booking an initial conversation, and link to the relevant service page on your website.

Reviews and testimonials

Client reviews can build trust, but treat them with care. Only publish genuine reviews, never edit them to change their meaning, and check with your compliance team how reviews and testimonials can be used in your marketing, particularly anything that refers to investment performance.

Common mistakes

  • Using past performance prominently without balance or context.
  • Vague or hidden fee information.
  • Copying generic content that does not reflect your actual services.
  • Publishing social posts without the approval your firm requires.
  • Landing pages that do not match the ads that lead to them.
  • Out-of-date allowances or thresholds left on old blog posts.

Measuring what matters

For advice firms, the most useful measure is qualified enquiries: people who match your ideal client and are ready for a conversation. Track enquiries by source, note which become clients, and use that to decide where to focus. Traffic and followers are only useful if they lead there. A simple question on your enquiry form, such as "how did you hear about us?", adds valuable context to the data in Google Analytics.

Expect SEO and content to take months to build, while well-targeted paid search can produce enquiries sooner. Many firms find a combination works best: paid search for immediate demand, content and local SEO for steady growth, and LinkedIn for visibility with professional audiences.

FAQs

Is a financial adviser's website a financial promotion?

Generally, marketing content from regulated firms is treated as a financial promotion. Check the specifics with your compliance team or network, who should approve it.

Can financial advisers use social media for marketing?

Yes. Social media is widely used by advisers, but posts can be financial promotions, so they need to be fair, clear and not misleading and follow your firm's approval process.

What does Consumer Duty mean for marketing?

It means communications should help customers understand products and services and make good decisions, with clear language, transparent fees and information that is easy to find.

Can financial advisers run Google Ads?

Yes, subject to Google's financial services policies, which may require verification. Ads and landing pages must also meet FCA requirements.

Can a marketing agency guarantee FCA compliance?

No. An agency can prepare content carefully and follow your process, but responsibility for approving financial promotions rests with the regulated firm.
