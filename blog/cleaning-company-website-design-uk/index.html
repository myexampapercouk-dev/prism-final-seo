Cleaning Company Website Design: Quotes, Service Pages and Trust Built In

Cleaning company website design for UK domestic and commercial cleaners: service pages, instant quote forms, recurring bookings, areas and trust details.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Domestic and commercial cleaning buyers want different things, so give each its own route through the site.
  • A quote form that asks about property size, frequency and access lets you price accurately without a site visit for every enquiry.
  • Trust matters because cleaners work in homes and premises, so explain vetting, insurance and key handling honestly.

Cleaning is built on trust. Customers are letting people into their homes or business premises, often when they are not there, and they need to feel confident before they enquire. At the same time, many cleaning enquiries are fairly price-driven, and people want to know quickly whether you cover their area and roughly what a clean will cost. A good cleaning company website balances reassurance with speed.

This guide focuses on the website: structure, quote and booking forms, service pages and the details that build confidence. For promotion beyond the website, such as local listings, leaflets and reviews, see our guide to cleaning company marketing in the UK.

Domestic or commercial, or both

The first structural decision is whether you serve homes, businesses or both, because these customers think differently.

  • Domestic customers care about reliability, the same cleaner each time, flexibility, price and trust. They often decide quickly and on a phone.
  • Commercial customers, such as offices, shops, clinics or schools, care about consistency, out-of-hours working, compliance, contracts and how issues are handled. Decisions may involve more than one person and take longer.

If you do both, split them clearly from the homepage, for example with two prominent routes: home cleaning and business cleaning. Mixing them on one page tends to make the site feel vague to both audiences.

Pages a cleaning business typically needs

  • Home. What you clean, where, and a clear quote button.
  • Service pages. One for each main service, such as regular domestic cleaning, end of tenancy, deep cleans, after-builders cleans, office cleaning, carpet cleaning or window cleaning.
  • Areas covered. Towns and districts you serve.
  • How it works. What happens from first enquiry to the first clean.
  • About and trust. Who you are, how cleaners are chosen and checked, insurance and policies.
  • Quote or booking. A form or booking system, plus phone and email.
  • FAQs. Supplies, keys, pets, cancellations and what happens if something goes wrong.

Service pages for each type of clean

Different cleans attract different customers. Someone searching for end of tenancy cleaning is in a very different situation from someone wanting a weekly cleaner. Give each core service its own page that explains:

  • Who it is for and when they might need it.
  • What is usually included and what is not, described as a general guide rather than a fixed promise, so expectations are realistic.
  • Whether you bring equipment and products, or use the customer's.
  • How long it typically takes and what you need from the customer, such as access or parking.
  • A clear way to request a quote for that service.

Checklists are popular on cleaning sites because they make the work tangible. If you use one, keep it accurate to what your team actually does, and note that the final scope is agreed with each customer.

End of tenancy pages

End of tenancy cleans are often linked to deposit returns. Be careful not to promise that a clean guarantees a deposit back, since that depends on the landlord or agent, the inventory and the tenancy deposit scheme's process. You can explain what you clean and any re-clean policy you genuinely offer, and leave deposit decisions to the parties involved.

Quote forms that let you price properly

Pricing cleaning often depends on the size of the property, its condition, how often you visit and any extras. A good quote form collects enough to give a realistic figure without making the customer fill in a questionnaire.

For domestic work, useful fields might include:

  • Postcode, to check coverage.
  • Type of clean.
  • Number of bedrooms and bathrooms.
  • Frequency, such as weekly, fortnightly or one-off.
  • Preferred days and times.
  • Extras such as oven, inside windows or ironing, if you offer them.
  • Pets and any access notes.

For commercial work, ask about the type of premises, approximate size, number of washrooms and kitchens, frequency, preferred hours and any specific requirements. Many commercial jobs will still need a visit or a call before a firm price.

Some cleaning companies use an instant price calculator. It can work well for standard domestic cleans if your pricing is consistent, but it can create problems if the price shown is unrealistic for a particular property. If you use one, make clear that the figure is an estimate and may change after you confirm details. Our website contact forms guide has more on designing forms people complete.

Online booking and recurring cleans

For repeat domestic customers, online booking can be convenient. Specialist booking and scheduling tools for cleaning businesses can handle recurring appointments, reminders and payments, and can usually be linked from or embedded in your website. Our online booking systems guide explains the main options.

Before adding self-service booking, think about how you allocate cleaners and whether you can honour bookings made at short notice. Sometimes a quick quote form followed by a personal call is a better first step, with booking tools used for existing customers.

Building trust

Because cleaners work inside people's homes and businesses, trust details carry a lot of weight. Be specific and honest.

  • Vetting. Explain how you recruit and check cleaners, such as references, right to work checks and, where appropriate and lawful, criminal record checks. Only describe checks you actually carry out.
  • Insurance. Mention public liability insurance if you hold it, and any other cover relevant to your work.
  • Keys and access. Explain how keys, codes and alarm details are stored and returned.
  • Consistency. Say whether customers usually get the same cleaner, and what happens when that cleaner is off.
  • Problems. Explain how complaints or breakages are handled.

Real photos of your team, with their permission, are more reassuring than stock images of smiling people with spray bottles. Genuine reviews from customers, used with consent, also help. Our guide to website trust signals covers how to present credibility well.

Privacy and personal data

Cleaning companies often hold sensitive information: home addresses, key safe codes, alarm codes and details of when people are away. Your website's forms and any booking system should handle this securely, and your privacy notice should explain what you collect and why. The ICO publishes guidance for small businesses on data protection. Avoid asking for access codes through a general website form; collect those through a more secure route once a booking is confirmed.

Areas covered

Coverage is one of the first things people check. List the towns and districts you serve in text on a dedicated page and mention your main areas on the homepage. A postcode check at the start of the quote form also helps people find out quickly whether you can help.

If you build individual area pages, make each one genuinely useful, perhaps by mentioning the kinds of properties you clean there or local services offered. Duplicate pages with only the town name changed add little. Our local SEO guide covers how area pages and map listings fit together.

Design priorities for cleaning websites

A cleaning website should feel fresh, clear and organised, which is a reasonable reflection of the service itself. Practical priorities:

  • A quote button visible at all times on mobile.
  • Short, scannable pages with clear headings.
  • Plenty of white space and a restrained colour palette.
  • Real team and work photos, compressed so pages load quickly.
  • Readable fonts and good contrast for accessibility.

Before-and-after photos can be persuasive for deep cleans and after-builders work. Take them from the same angle and lighting, and avoid showing anything that identifies the customer or their home without permission.

Commercial cleaning sections

If you clean businesses, give that side of the site the depth it needs. Commercial buyers may want to understand your experience with similar premises, how you supervise and check quality, how you handle out-of-hours work and security, and what documentation you can provide, such as risk assessments or method statements. Keep claims accurate and avoid mentioning certifications or standards you do not hold.

Choosing a platform

Squarespace and Wix suit smaller cleaning businesses wanting an easy site to update. WordPress offers more flexibility for multiple service and area pages and for integrating booking tools. Whatever you choose, check that your booking or quoting tools work smoothly with it. Our article on website builders versus custom websites explains the trade-offs.

FAQs

Should a cleaning company show prices on its website?

Many show starting prices or estimates for standard cleans. Make clear the final price depends on property size, condition and frequency.

Is an instant quote calculator a good idea?

It can be for standard domestic cleans with consistent pricing. Always label results as estimates that may change once details are confirmed.

Should domestic and commercial cleaning be on separate pages?

Yes, usually. The audiences care about different things, and separate routes make the site clearer.

What trust information should a cleaning website include?

Accurate details of how cleaners are checked, insurance you hold, how keys are handled and how problems are resolved, along with genuine reviews.

Can I take recurring bookings through my website?

Yes, using scheduling tools that link from or embed in your site. Make sure you can reliably staff the bookings that come in.

Should I ask for key safe codes on my quote form?

It is better not to. Collect sensitive access details through a more secure route once a booking is confirmed.
