Soft Play Centre Marketing in the UK: Filling Sessions and Parties

Soft play centre marketing in the UK works when parents can find you locally, see sessions and prices at a glance, and book play or parties online in minutes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Parents decide quickly on a phone, so session times, prices, age ranges and online booking must be obvious on every page.
  • Birthday parties are often the most valuable bookings, so give them their own page with packages and an easy enquiry route.
  • Local search, school holiday planning and social ads aimed at nearby parents usually beat broad awareness campaigns.

Soft play centre marketing in the UK is about making it effortless for local parents to find you, check what is on and book. Most decisions happen on a phone, often the same day, so session times, prices, age ranges and online booking need to be clear wherever a parent first meets your business.

This guide is for independent soft play centres, indoor play barns, toddler play cafés and family activity centres across the UK. It covers how parents choose, what your website must include, how to market birthday parties, and where local SEO and paid media fit. We are a UK studio that builds websites and runs search and paid campaigns, and we have written this as practical guidance rather than a pitch.

How parents choose a soft play centre

Parents rarely spend long deciding. A rainy Saturday morning, a half-term day or a toddler who needs to burn off energy can lead to a quick search and a booking within minutes. The questions on their minds are simple:

  • Is it open today, and when are the sessions?
  • How much does it cost for my children, and do adults pay?
  • Is it suitable for my child's age, and is there a separate baby or toddler area?
  • Do I need to book, or can I just turn up?
  • Is there food, parking, baby changing and step-free access?
  • Is it clean and well supervised?

Every one of these should be answered on your website and your Google Business Profile without the parent needing to call.

What your website must include

Session times and prices on one clear page

Create a single page with term-time and school holiday sessions, prices for each age group, adult entry if relevant, and any off-peak or toddler-only sessions. Use a simple table that reads well on mobile. Our guide to website tables on mobile explains how to keep them readable on small screens.

Online booking

Online booking with time slots helps parents plan, reduces queues and lets you manage capacity. Most leisure booking systems offer a widget or booking link that can be embedded on your site. Make the booking button prominent and keep the journey as short as possible. Our article on website online booking systems explains the options and trade-offs.

Practical visitor information

Parents want to know about parking, buggy storage, baby changing, accessibility, socks rules, food and drink, allergen information and what happens if a child is unwell. A practical "plan your visit" page answers these and saves your staff repeated phone calls. Describe only what you genuinely provide, and keep the information up to date.

Real photos

Show the play frame, toddler area, café and party rooms as they really are, well lit and clean. Avoid photographing identifiable children unless you have clear written permission from parents. Wide shots of empty or quiet areas work well and avoid privacy concerns.

Safety and supervision

Explain your approach to supervision, cleaning and safety in plain terms. Parents notice when a centre is open about this. If you hold a food hygiene rating, displaying it accurately is a useful trust signal. Our guide to website trust signals has more ideas.

Marketing birthday parties

Parties are often the most valuable bookings for a soft play centre and they can be planned weeks in advance. Give parties their own page with:

  • Each party package, what is included, the number of children covered and the duration.
  • Food options, with a clear note on allergens and dietary needs.
  • Party room photos and what parents need to bring.
  • Available days and times, and how far ahead to book.
  • An online booking or enquiry form that asks for the child's age, preferred date and number of guests.

A dedicated party page also gives you something specific to promote in ads and social posts. Our guide to landing page design explains how to build pages that convert this kind of focused interest.

Planning around the school calendar

Demand in this sector follows the school year and the weather. Plan your marketing calendar around half-terms, Easter, the summer holidays and Christmas, and remember that term dates vary between local authorities and between England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Publish holiday session times well in advance, update your Google Business Profile hours, and schedule social posts and email reminders a week or two before each break.

Quieter weekday mornings in term time can be filled with toddler sessions, baby groups, home education meetups or SEN-friendly quiet sessions if you run them. Promote these separately to the right parents rather than mixing them into general posts.

Paid media for family attractions

Meta platforms are often effective for reaching local parents with specific, date-led messages: half-term sessions, a new party package, a character event. Keep the radius realistic, since most families travel only a short distance for soft play. Ads must follow platform rules on targeting and content, and advertising to parents rather than children is the sensible approach.

Search ads can capture parents searching for soft play or children's party venues in your town. Keep keywords local, add negatives for jobs and equipment sales, and send traffic to the session or party page rather than the homepage. Track bookings and party enquiries as conversions. Our paid media service explains how we plan and manage campaigns, with ad spend paid separately.

Reviews and repeat visits

Reviews strongly influence parents choosing a centre they have not visited. Ask for a review after a visit or party, using a link in a follow-up email where parents have agreed to hear from you. Reply to every review, thank people warmly and respond calmly to complaints. Repeat visits can be encouraged with a simple loyalty scheme, off-peak offers or a holiday newsletter, sent only to families who have opted in.

Measuring results

Track online session bookings, party bookings and enquiries, calls from your website and from your Google Business Profile. Compare busy and quiet periods to see which promotions genuinely filled sessions. SEO improvements usually take three to six months to show clearly, while ads can bring bookings within days.

FAQs

What is the best soft play centre marketing in the UK?

For most centres it is a complete Google Business Profile, a mobile-friendly website with session times, prices and online booking, a strong party page, and local social ads planned around school holidays.

Should a soft play centre take bookings online?

Usually yes. Online booking helps parents plan, reduces queues and lets you manage capacity. Many centres keep some walk-in availability alongside booked sessions.

How do we get more birthday party bookings?

Create a dedicated party page with clear packages, food and allergen information, photos and an easy booking or enquiry form, then promote it through local search, social posts and ads aimed at nearby parents.

Can we use photos of children on our website?

Only with clear written permission from parents or carers. Many centres use photos of the play areas and party rooms without identifiable children to avoid privacy concerns.

Are Facebook and Instagram ads worth it for soft play?

They can work well for date-led promotions such as half-term sessions or party offers when aimed at parents within a realistic travel distance and tracked on bookings.

How far in advance should we market school holidays?

Publish holiday session times and update your listings a few weeks ahead, then send reminders about a week or two before the break. Check local term dates, as they vary by area.
