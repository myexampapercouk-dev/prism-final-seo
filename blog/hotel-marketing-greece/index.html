Hotel Marketing in Greece: How to Win More Direct Bookings

Hotel marketing in Greece explained: the website, SEO, Google Ads and social ads that win direct bookings from abroad, plus GDPR and cookie consent basics.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Hotel marketing in Greece is about winning bookings from travellers who are planning months ahead, usually from another country, and turning as many of those bookings as possible into direct reservations on your own website. The hotels that do this well pair a fast, multilingual website and booking engine with search, paid ads and social content timed to when their main source markets start planning.

Why Greek hotel marketing is different

Most Greek hotels face three conditions at once. First, demand is strongly seasonal, especially on the islands, so marketing has to start well before the first guest arrives. Second, a large share of guests are international, which means you are competing in several languages and several search markets. Third, online travel agencies are powerful, and every booking they take costs commission.

None of this means you should abandon the agencies. They bring visibility, and for many properties they fill rooms that would otherwise stay empty. The aim is balance: use them for reach, then give guests good reasons to book with you directly, both the first time and when they return. Our guide to increasing hotel direct bookings covers the principles in more depth, and they apply just as well to a boutique hotel in Crete as to one in Cornwall.

The website comes first

Every channel sends people to your website, so it needs to work hard. A good hotel site loads quickly on a phone, shows rooms and rates clearly, answers practical questions about transfers, ferries and parking, and makes the booking engine easy to find on every page. Strong photography and short video matter more for hotels than for almost any other business.

Greek hotels usually need the site in several languages. English is the common default, with German, French, Italian and Dutch often added depending on where your guests come from. Each language should have its own pages and URLs rather than an automatic translation widget, so search engines can index them properly. Our guide to multilingual website design explains how to set this up, including how to signal each language version to search engines.

If your site is a few years old, check how it performs on a mid-range phone on mobile data. Many guests browse while commuting or relaxing, and a slow page or a booking engine that is hard to use on a small screen loses bookings quietly.

SEO for Greek hotels

Search engine optimisation helps travellers find you when they search for places to stay in your area. The searches that matter are often specific: a boutique hotel in a named village, a family hotel with a pool near a particular beach, or an adults-only property on a given island. Pages that answer those needs directly, in each target language, have a much better chance of ranking than a single general page.

Useful content for a hotel site includes room pages with honest detail, a page about the local area, guides to getting there, and seasonal information such as when the property opens and closes. Structured data for your hotel, consistent contact details and a well-maintained Google Business Profile all support visibility. Our hotel SEO guide and our post on international SEO go into more detail.

SEO takes time. Results typically build over three to six months, which is another reason to start well before the season.

Google Ads and social advertising

Google Ads lets you appear when people search for your hotel's name, your destination or your type of stay. Brand campaigns are often the first priority, because intermediaries may bid on your hotel's name and take bookings you would otherwise receive directly. Location targeting matters too: you usually want to reach people in your source markets, not people already on the island. Our guide to Google Ads location targeting explains the settings.

Meta, TikTok and YouTube ads work earlier in the journey. A short video of a sunset terrace or a quiet pool can prompt someone to start planning. These campaigns work best with clear audiences in each market, landing pages in the right language, and budgets that rise as planning season approaches. Our paid media service covers search and social campaigns together, and ad spend is always paid separately from our fees.

Planning around the season

For seasonal properties, the calendar shapes everything. A simple plan might look like this:

  • Late autumn and winter. Review the past season, update the website, refresh photography and plan content for each language.
  • Early in the year. Start brand search campaigns and social ads in your main source markets as planning begins.
  • Spring. Push offers for shoulder months, email past guests and keep availability up to date.
  • Summer. Focus on last-minute availability, reviews and gathering consent-based email sign-ups for next year.

City hotels in Athens and Thessaloniki tend to have a longer season, with conference, city-break and business travel spread across the year, so their plan is usually steadier.

AI-powered tools for hotel marketing

AI-powered tools can help with first drafts of room descriptions, ideas for ad variations and summaries of guest reviews. We use them to speed up routine work, but every piece of copy is checked by a person, and translated content should always be reviewed by a fluent speaker before it goes live. Facts such as prices, amenities and distances need to be accurate, so they are never left to an automated tool.

Compliance basics for Greek hotel websites

These notes are general information only. Please follow official guidance from the relevant authorities and take advice from your own legal adviser.

  • GDPR. Booking forms, enquiry forms, newsletters and analytics collect personal data. You need a clear privacy notice, a lawful basis for each use and sensible retention. Greece's data protection authority publishes guidance.
  • Cookie consent. Under the ePrivacy rules as applied in Greek law, non-essential cookies such as analytics and advertising tags generally need prior consent, and rejecting should be as easy as accepting. Our post on cookie banner design covers the practical side.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer services, including e-commerce and some booking services. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but accessible design helps every guest. The expected standard is EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA. See our overview of the European Accessibility Act for websites.

Consent matters for advertising too. If your tags only fire after consent, your reporting will show fewer conversions than actually happen, and campaigns need to be judged with that in mind.
