Window Cleaner Marketing in the UK: How to Build Fuller, Tighter Rounds

Window cleaner marketing in the UK works best when local search, round-focused leaflets, easy booking and regular reviews fill the streets you already clean.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Density beats reach: market hardest in the streets and estates you already clean so every new customer makes your round more profitable.
  • A well-kept Google Business Profile, a simple website with a quote form and steady reviews do most of the work for a domestic round.
  • Commercial contracts, gutter and fascia cleaning and conservatory roofs are worth their own pages because they attract different searches and bigger jobs.

Window cleaner marketing in the UK is mostly about density: winning more customers in the streets you already clean so your round gets tighter, your travel time drops and each day earns more. The window cleaners who grow steadily tend to combine a tidy Google Business Profile, a simple website with an easy quote form, targeted local leaflets and a habit of asking every happy customer for a review.

This guide is for sole traders building a first round, small teams adding vans, and established businesses that want more commercial work or higher-value add-ons such as gutter, fascia and conservatory cleaning. We cover where customers look, what your website needs, when paid ads make sense and how to keep the customers you win.

How people choose a window cleaner

Most domestic customers find a window cleaner in one of three ways. They notice a van or ladder on their street and ask the neighbours. They post in a local community group asking for recommendations. Or they search on their phone for something like "window cleaner near me" or "window cleaner [town]" and pick from the map results. Moving house is a common trigger, as is a previous cleaner retiring or becoming unreliable.

Commercial buyers behave differently. A shop owner, office manager, letting agent or property manager is more likely to search for "commercial window cleaning [town]", compare two or three websites and ask about insurance, access methods and invoicing before committing. That is why it pays to treat domestic and commercial as two separate audiences in your marketing, even if the same team does both.

The general principles in our guide to marketing for tradespeople apply here too. This post focuses on what is specific to running a round.

A window cleaning business has a modest average job value but excellent repeat potential, so the cost of winning a customer matters less than how long they stay and how close they live to your existing work. The table below is how we would usually prioritise channels.

Make your Google Business Profile work hard

For a domestic round, your Google Business Profile is often the most valuable marketing asset you have. Set it up as a service-area business, list the towns and districts you genuinely cover, and choose the most accurate primary category. Add your services individually: window cleaning, conservatory roof cleaning, gutter clearing, fascia and soffit cleaning, solar panel cleaning if you offer it.

Photos matter more than many cleaners expect. Upload pictures of your liveried van, your water-fed pole system in use, a sparkling conservatory roof, and before-and-after shots of gutters and fascias. Post an update every week or two, for example a note that you have spaces on a particular estate this month. Our Google Business Profile guide walks through the full setup, and it is worth learning how to reply to reviews in a way that reassures future customers.

A website that turns searches into customers

Window cleaners do not need a large website, but they do need a clear one. Many potential customers will check your site after seeing your van or hearing your name, so it needs to answer their questions quickly on a phone.

  • Areas covered. A plain list of towns, villages and estates you clean, so people know immediately whether you serve them.
  • How it works. How often you visit, whether customers need to be in, how you access the back, and what happens if it rains.
  • Price guidance. Many cleaners give typical ranges by property type. Even a rough guide filters out mismatched enquiries.
  • Payment options. Explain how customers pay, whether by bank transfer, card link or another method you use.
  • Quote form. Name, address or postcode, property type and services wanted. Keep it short.
  • Insurance and methods. State that you hold public liability insurance if you do, and describe whether you use ladders, poles or both.

Our guide to website contact forms covers how to keep forms short enough that people actually finish them. If you want a site built properly from the start, our website development service covers small, fast service-business sites.

Separate pages for add-on and commercial services

One page that says "we clean windows, gutters, conservatories and more" will struggle to rank for any of those searches. Give each meaningful service its own page with its own photos and questions answered.

  • Gutter clearing and fascia cleaning: explain how you clear gutters, whether you use vacuum systems or cameras, and how you show the customer the result.
  • Conservatory roof cleaning: cover how you clean safely without damaging seals or panels.
  • Commercial window cleaning: shops, offices, schools and blocks of flats. Mention access methods, scheduling outside opening hours and invoicing on account.
  • Solar panel cleaning: if you offer it, describe your method and tell customers to check their panel manufacturer's guidance on cleaning.

These pages also help with search engines and AI assistants, because they give a clear, specific answer to a specific question. Our guide to service page design explains the structure we use for pages like these.

Leaflets and door hangers done properly

Printed marketing still works for window cleaners because the product is visible from the street. The trick is targeting. Rather than dropping leaflets across a whole town, focus on the streets next to and between your existing customers. A new customer three doors down from an existing one is worth far more than one on the other side of town.

Good practice includes a short line such as "we already clean windows on your street every four weeks", a clear way to book (a short web address or QR code that goes straight to your quote form), and a simple offer that does not devalue your service. Some cleaners leave a polite card after each visit at neighbouring houses. Whatever you do, keep a note of which streets you have covered so you can see what brings results.

Reviews and referrals

Trust is the deciding factor in this trade. Customers are letting someone into their garden, often while they are at work. Reviews from neighbours carry real weight, so make asking routine: a short message after the first clean and again after a few months, with a direct link to your Google review form.

Referrals are just as powerful. Some window cleaners offer an existing customer a free clean when a neighbour signs up. If you do this, keep the terms simple and make sure the reward is something you can afford on every referral. Our guide to website trust signals covers how to present reviews, insurance details and photos so they reassure new visitors.

When paid ads make sense

For a pure domestic round, Google Ads can be hard to justify because the average clean is small and customers expect local, personal service. Paid search tends to work better for commercial contracts, higher-value services like gutter clearing and conservatory roof cleaning, or when you are launching in a new area and want to build density quickly.

If you run ads, keep the location tight around your round, add negative keywords for job seekers, equipment buyers and DIY searches, and send clicks to a specific service page rather than your homepage. Keeping the customers you win

Retention is where a round becomes a business. Customers drop off when visits become unpredictable, when payment is awkward, or when they feel taken for granted. A few habits help:

  1. Send a text or message the day before you visit so customers know when you are coming.
  2. Make paying easy and consistent, with a clear reminder for anyone who has not paid.
  3. Note any special requests, such as gate codes or dogs in the garden, so every visit feels personal.
  4. Offer add-on services once a year, for example a gutter clear in autumn.
  5. Ask for feedback occasionally and act on it.

Customer data needs care. If you keep names, addresses and phone numbers, you are handling personal data, so check current ICO guidance on UK GDPR, and if you send marketing messages, check the rules on electronic marketing under PECR before you start.

Growing beyond one van

When you add a second van or take on staff, marketing needs a little more structure. Track where each new customer came from, whether that is your Google profile, a leaflet, a referral or your website. A simple spreadsheet is enough. That record tells you where to spend the next pound and which areas are worth building up.

FAQs

What is the best window cleaner marketing in the UK?

For most rounds, the best window cleaner marketing combines a well-kept Google Business Profile, a simple website with a quote form, targeted leaflets on streets you already clean and a steady flow of reviews. Paid ads are more useful for commercial work and add-on services.

Do window cleaners need a website?

Yes, even a small one. Many people look you up after seeing your van or getting a recommendation, and a clear site with areas covered, how visits work and a short quote form turns that interest into a booking.

How do I get more window cleaning customers on my round?

Focus on density. Leaflet and door-drop the streets around existing customers, ask happy customers for reviews and referrals, and keep your Google profile updated with photos and posts about the areas you serve.

Are Google Ads worth it for window cleaners?

They can be for commercial contracts, gutter clearing, conservatory roofs and new-area launches. For small domestic cleans the cost per customer is often too high unless you target tightly and track results carefully.

Should I put prices on my window cleaning website?

Many cleaners give guide prices by property type, with a note that the final price depends on size and access. This reduces time spent on enquiries that would never have suited your pricing.

How can I win commercial window cleaning contracts?

Create a dedicated commercial page explaining access methods, scheduling, insurance and invoicing, then reach out to local shops, offices and property managers with a short, specific introduction and a link to that page.
