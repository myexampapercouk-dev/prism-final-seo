Designing for Norwegian visitors

A website that works well in Oslo usually has a few things in common:

  • Clear, direct copy. Visitors want to know what you do, who it is for and what it costs, without marketing fluff.
  • Language choice. Many Oslo businesses need Norwegian pages for local customers and English pages for international clients. Prism writes in English. Norwegian content comes from your team or a translator, and we build the site so both languages work properly. Our guide to multilingual website design explains how.
  • Prices in kroner. Show prices and delivery costs in Norwegian kroner where you sell to Norwegian customers.
  • Mobile first. Pages must load fast and work smoothly on a phone.
  • Trust signals. Clear contact details, organisation details and real information about your team and process.

Speed, SEO and launch

A new website should not lose the search traffic your old one earned. Before launch we map old URLs to new ones and set up redirects, so rankings carry across. Read our guide to a website redesign without losing rankings for the full process.

We also build for speed. That means compressed images, limited plugins, light themes and sensible hosting. Our guide to Core Web Vitals covers the measures Google uses to judge page experience.

After launch, many clients add SEO or paid ads to bring in more traffic. That is optional and separate from the build.

Privacy, cookies and accessibility

A new website is a good moment to get the basics right. The notes below are general information, not legal advice. Please check official guidance or speak to your own adviser.

  • GDPR. Norway is part of the European Economic Area, so the GDPR applies to personal data collected through your website, such as contact forms and newsletter sign-ups. We build forms that only collect what you need and link to your privacy notice.
  • Cookie consent. Norwegian and wider European rules generally require consent before non-essential cookies, such as analytics and advertising tags, are set. We can add a consent banner and set up analytics so it respects visitor choices. Our guide to cookie banner design has more detail.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act has applied to many consumer-facing digital products and services in the EU since June 2025, with a general exemption for microenterprises that provide services. The usual technical reference is EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA. Norway also has its own universal design rules for websites. Check official guidance on how these apply to your business. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act for websites explains the basics.

We design and build with WCAG AA in mind on every project, because it makes sites easier for everyone to use. Compliance decisions rest with you and your adviser.

What to prepare before your project starts

Projects run faster and cost less when a few things are ready before design begins. You do not need all of them on day one, but the sooner they are in place, the smoother the build will be.

  • Your goals. What should the website do: generate enquiries, sell products, take bookings or support existing customers?
  • Your content. Text for key pages, in English, Norwegian or both. If a translator is involved, agree timings early so content is ready when pages are built.
  • Photos and logos. Good photos of your team, premises or products make a big difference. We can advise on stock images where needed.
  • Access details. Your domain, hosting, analytics and any existing website accounts, shared securely by your team.
  • Examples you like. A few websites you admire, and a few you do not, help us understand your taste quickly.

If content is the biggest gap, we can write English page copy as part of the project, and brief your translator so the Norwegian version keeps the same structure and keywords.

Our web design process

  1. Discovery call. We talk about your business, customers, goals and budget.
  2. Proposal. You receive a fixed scope, price in pounds and timeline.
  3. Sitemap and content plan. We agree the pages and what each one needs to say.
  4. Design. We design key pages for desktop and mobile and refine them with your feedback.
  5. Build. We build the site on WordPress or Shopify and add your content.
  6. Testing. We check speed, forms, mobile layouts, accessibility basics and tracking.
  7. Launch and training. We go live, set up redirects and show your team how to edit the site.

A typical website takes three to five weeks. An e-commerce site takes five to twelve weeks, depending on products, integrations and content.
