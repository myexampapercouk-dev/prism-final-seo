Shopify Agency Saudi Arabia: Building a Store for Saudi Shoppers

Choosing a Shopify agency in Saudi Arabia: Arabic and English storefronts, payments, delivery, store build timelines and our published ecommerce prices.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Shopify store for Saudi shoppers needs a well-tested Arabic and English storefront, payment and delivery options that suit local buyers, and fast mobile pages.
  • Check which payment providers, apps and shipping partners support Saudi Arabia before you build, as availability changes and must be confirmed directly.
  • Our ecommerce builds cost £1,500 to £3,500 and typically take five to twelve weeks, delivered remotely from the UK, with other currencies quoted on request.

A Shopify agency in Saudi Arabia should build you a fast, mobile-first store that works properly in Arabic and English, offers the payment and delivery options Saudi shoppers expect and is set up so you can manage products and orders yourself. The right partner checks what Shopify and its apps support in the Kingdom before building, rather than discovering gaps after launch.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds ecommerce websites, including Shopify stores, remotely for brands in Saudi Arabia and the wider Gulf. We have no office or staff in the Kingdom. We write in English, and Arabic content is produced with your team or a translator. This guide covers what a Saudi Shopify build involves, how to compare agencies and what our builds cost.

Why Shopify suits many Saudi brands

Shopify is a hosted platform, so you do not manage servers, security patches or software updates. That makes it a practical choice for many direct-to-consumer brands, retailers moving online and businesses that want a store they can run without a developer on call. Its theme system supports right-to-left layouts, its admin is straightforward and its app ecosystem covers reviews, subscriptions, loyalty and more.

It is not right for every business. Very complex B2B pricing, unusual product configurators or deep integration with older systems can push you towards other platforms. Our comparison of Shopify and WooCommerce sets out the trade-offs, and the same reasoning applies in Saudi Arabia.

Arabic storefronts done properly

A Saudi store usually needs Arabic as a first-class language, not an afterthought. That means more than translating product titles. Navigation, buttons, cart messages, checkout text, order emails and policy pages all need to read naturally in Arabic. Layouts must flip correctly for right-to-left reading, and icons, arrows and sliders need checking so they point the right way.

We build the store structure and test the right-to-left layout, and the Arabic copy is written or reviewed by your team or a native-speaking translator. Our guide to a bilingual Arabic and English website explains how to plan the two languages, and Shopify's own translation and market settings are covered in our notes on Shopify Markets, which apply in principle to Gulf markets too.

Payments and delivery in the Kingdom

Saudi shoppers often expect a choice of payment methods, which may include local debit card schemes, digital wallets, buy now pay later options and in some cases cash on delivery. Which of these you can offer depends on Shopify's current support in Saudi Arabia, the gateway or app you choose and your own business registration. Availability changes, so we confirm options directly with the providers during discovery rather than assuming.

Delivery needs the same care. You will want sensible shipping zones, clear delivery times by city and a courier who can handle your order volume and returns. Our guides to Shopify shipping profiles and choosing payment gateways were written for UK stores, but the setup steps are the same.

Regulation and tax

Online stores in Saudi Arabia must follow local rules on ecommerce, consumer protection, data protection and VAT, including how prices and invoices are shown. These rules are updated over time. Check current official guidance from the relevant Saudi authorities and take advice from a local accountant or lawyer. We configure tax settings, policy pages and consent tools technically, but compliance decisions rest with your business and its advisers.

How we build a Shopify store for a Saudi brand

  1. Discovery. We agree goals, products, languages, payment and delivery options and confirm what is available in Saudi Arabia.
  2. Structure and design. We plan collections, navigation and key templates, designing mobile first in both reading directions.
  3. Build. We set up the theme, apps, payments, shipping and tracking. Our Shopify store setup guide lists the settings we check.
  4. Content. You or your translator supply Arabic copy; we load products and check every template in both languages.
  5. Testing. We place test orders, check emails, test on real phones and review speed.
  6. Launch and training. We launch, hand over accounts and train your team to run the store.

After launch: bringing shoppers to the store

A new store does not attract visitors on its own. Before launch, it is worth planning how the first customers will find you, because the build decisions you make now affect how well marketing works later.

  • Search. Clean collection URLs, unique product descriptions in both languages and structured data give the store a fair chance in organic results. Our ecommerce SEO checklist lists what to get right from day one.
  • Paid social and search. Many Saudi brands rely on social platforms and Google Shopping for early sales. Installing tracking pixels and a product feed during the build saves rework later.
  • Email and messaging. Capturing customer consent at checkout lets you follow up with order updates, restock alerts and offers, within the rules on marketing consent.
  • Reviews. Genuine product reviews help shoppers who are new to your brand decide with confidence.

We can plan these during discovery so the store launches ready to measure, even if the marketing itself starts a little later.

Local agency or remote studio?

A Saudi-based agency can meet you in person, may have Arabic designers and writers in-house and will know local couriers and payment providers well. A remote studio can offer a fixed, published price and experience of building stores for several markets. We work through video calls, shared project boards and clear written updates, with a few hours' time difference between the UK and Saudi Arabia. If in-person meetings are essential, a local agency may suit you better.
