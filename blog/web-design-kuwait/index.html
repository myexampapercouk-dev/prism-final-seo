Web Design for Kuwait Businesses: Bilingual Sites, Process and Cost

Looking for web design in Kuwait? How a remote studio builds fast, bilingual Arabic and English business websites, what it costs in £ and how long it takes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good Kuwait business website is fast on mobile, works properly in Arabic and English, and makes it easy for visitors to call, message or enquire.
  • We build sites remotely from the UK, with no office in Kuwait, and Arabic content is written with your team or a professional translator.
  • Small business websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000 and take 3 to 5 weeks; other currencies are quoted on request.

Web design for a Kuwait business means building a site that loads quickly on mobile, reads naturally in both Arabic and English, and turns visitors into calls, WhatsApp messages or enquiries. The design matters, but the structure, content and technical setup underneath it decide whether the site actually brings in work.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for businesses in Kuwait and across the Gulf. We have no office in Kuwait. Projects run through video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can review at any time. This guide explains what to expect from web design in Kuwait, how our website and ecommerce development service works, and what it costs.

What a Kuwait business website needs to do

Most visitors to a Kuwaiti business website arrive on a phone, often from a Google search, a social media profile or a link shared in a message. They want to know quickly what you offer, whether you serve their area, and how to get in touch. A site that is slow, cluttered or awkward in Arabic loses them in seconds.

A well-built site for a Kuwait business should:

  • Load fast on mobile data, with images compressed and scripts kept to what is needed.
  • Offer Arabic and English versions that each read as if they were written in that language.
  • Show clear calls to action: phone, WhatsApp, enquiry form or booking, depending on how you take work.
  • Explain your services on dedicated pages rather than one long page that tries to cover everything.
  • Be set up for search engines from day one, with clean URLs, titles, descriptions and structured data.
  • Be easy for your team to update without calling a developer for every small change.

Our guide to mobile website design covers the layout decisions that matter most when the majority of visitors are on a phone.

Bilingual Arabic and English design

Kuwait has an Arabic-speaking population and a large expatriate community, so many businesses need both languages. A bilingual site is more than a translated copy. Arabic reads right to left, so navigation, icons, forms, tables and image placement all need to mirror correctly. Fonts must support Arabic script well and stay readable at small sizes on mobile.

We build each language as its own set of pages with separate URLs, connected by a language switcher and correct hreflang tags so search engines show the right version to the right searcher. We write in English. Arabic pages are written with your team or a professional translator, and we recommend a fluent speaker reviews them before launch. Our article on bilingual Arabic and English websites explains the technical setup in more detail.

Web design Kuwait: our process

We follow the same clear sequence on every project, adjusted to the size of the site. You see progress throughout, and nothing goes live without your approval.

  1. Discovery call. We discuss your business, customers, services, competitors and goals, and agree the pages the site needs.
  2. Sitemap and content plan. We map each page to what customers search for and decide what content each one needs. Our guide to preparing website content before the build explains why this step saves time later.
  3. Design. We design key pages for mobile and desktop, in both languages where needed, and refine them with your feedback.
  4. Build. We develop the site on a staging address, set up forms, tracking and basic SEO, and test on real devices.
  5. Review and launch. You check every page, we make final changes, then we connect your domain and go live.
  6. Handover. We show your team how to make edits and give you full access to the site, domain and analytics.

Because we work remotely, we agree meeting times that suit both time zones. Kuwait is a few hours ahead of the UK, which usually leaves a comfortable overlap in the working day.

Working with a remote web design studio

Hiring a studio outside Kuwait is common, and it works well when the process is clear. The main questions are communication, ownership and support. Our guide to hiring an overseas web design agency walks through what to check before you sign.

With us, you can expect:

  • A named point of contact at the studio and agreed call times.
  • A shared project plan showing what is done and what is next.
  • A staging site you can open on your own phone at any point.
  • Full ownership of your domain, hosting account, content and analytics. Our article on website ownership and handover explains what you should always receive.

What we will not do is claim local experience we do not have. If your project depends on in-person visits, on-site photography or a local trade licence, you will need a local partner for those parts, and we are happy to work alongside them.

Search, speed and compliance from launch

A new website should be ready to rank from the day it goes live. We set up page titles, meta descriptions, headings, structured data, an XML sitemap and Google Search Console as part of every build. If you want ongoing search work after launch, our guide to SEO for Kuwait businesses explains how that continues.

Speed is part of the design, not an afterthought. We keep page weight low, use modern image formats and avoid heavy plugins. On compliance, rules on privacy, cookies, consumer information and online selling vary by country and change over time. We build sensible privacy and cookie features, but you should check current official guidance in Kuwait, and any market you sell into, and confirm requirements with your own legal adviser.

How to choose a web designer for your Kuwait business

Whether you hire us or another studio, these questions help you compare options fairly:

  • Have they built right-to-left Arabic layouts, and can they show how they handle mirroring?
  • Who writes the Arabic content, and who checks it?
  • Will you own the domain, hosting and site files outright?
  • What is included in the price, and what costs extra?
  • How is basic SEO handled at launch?
  • What support is available after the site goes live, and how is it charged?

Clear answers to these questions matter more than a polished pitch. A studio that is honest about what it does and does not do is easier to work with over the long term.
