Why Amazon.de needs its own approach

Germany is one of the largest ecommerce markets in Europe, and Amazon.de is a major starting point for product searches there. Sellers who already do well on Amazon.co.uk often assume a translated listing will perform the same way. It rarely does, for a few reasons:

  • Different search terms. German shoppers use their own words, compound nouns and spelling variants, and the most searched term is not always the direct translation.
  • Higher expectations of detail. German buyers tend to look for precise specifications, materials, dimensions and care information before they buy.
  • Different competitors. The products ranking well on Amazon.de are often not the same as those on Amazon.co.uk.
  • Language quality matters. Clumsy machine translation can put buyers off and lead to returns or poor reviews.

How German copy is produced

Prism writes in English. For Amazon.de we produce a keyword map and a listing brief for each product in English, showing which German terms belong in the title, bullets and backend fields, and what each section needs to say. Your team or a native-speaking German translator then writes the final copy from that brief.

We check the finished listing against the brief to confirm the priority keywords are in the right places, character limits are respected and nothing important was lost in translation. This approach keeps the search strategy and the language quality separate, so neither is compromised. If you do not have a German writer, we can suggest what to look for when you hire a translator, but the translation fee is arranged and paid separately.

Reviews, pricing and Buy Box basics

Search ranking on Amazon is influenced by more than text. Sales history, conversion rate, price competitiveness, stock levels and reviews all play a part. We cannot write or buy reviews, and nor should anyone, but we can help you make listings clearer so the right buyers purchase and fewer return the product.

  • Keep stock levels steady on Amazon.de, as running out can hurt ranking.
  • Price in euros with awareness of the German competitors in your category.
  • Answer common questions in the bullets and A+ content to reduce returns.
  • Use Amazon's own review programmes where your products qualify.

Sponsored Products and other Amazon ads can support new listings while organic ranking builds, with ad spend paid separately from our fees.

Compliance and regulatory notes

Selling into Germany involves product, packaging and consumer rules that sit outside listing SEO. Depending on your products, you may need packaging and electrical registrations, correct product safety information, German-language instructions and clear returns information. Amazon may ask for proof of some registrations before your listings go live.

If you also run your own online shop for German customers, the GDPR and the EU ePrivacy cookie rules apply to that site, and the European Accessibility Act, in force since June 2025 with an exemption for microenterprises, may apply to consumer ecommerce. Building to WCAG AA under EN 301 549 is good practice. We do not give legal or tax advice: approval for compliance, VAT and regulatory matters rests with your own advisers, and requirements change, so check current official guidance.

Amazon.de, your own shop or both?

Amazon.de gives access to a large audience, but you share the page with competitors and pay referral fees on each sale. Your own shop gives you control over branding, customer data and margins, but you have to bring the traffic yourself. Many brands use both. Common Amazon.de listing problems we see

  • Direct translations of UK listings. The words are correct, but they are not the words German shoppers search for.
  • English text left on images. Infographics and A+ modules still show English labels and imperial units.
  • Empty attribute fields. Products drop out of filtered searches because size, material or colour fields are missing.
  • Keyword stuffing. Titles crammed with terms read badly and can breach Amazon's style rules.
  • Backend terms duplicated from the title. Space is wasted on words that are already indexed.
