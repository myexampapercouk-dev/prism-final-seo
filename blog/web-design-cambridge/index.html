Web Design in Cambridge: Websites for Tech and Life Science Startups

Web design for Cambridge tech and life science startups: sites that grow with each funding stage, win specialist hires and speak to partners and investors.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Cambridge startup website usually needs to change shape as the company moves from stealth to fundraising to commercial launch, so it should be built to evolve.
  • Life science companies should check what they can say about products in development, as rules on promoting medicines and medical devices are strict.
  • Prism Works Studio works with Cambridge businesses remotely, planning, writing and building sites over video calls and shared drafts.

Web design in Cambridge for a tech or life science startup is less about a single launch and more about a site that keeps up with a fast-changing company. A business that begins as two founders and a holding page may, within a short time, be fundraising, hiring specialists, signing commercial partners and preparing a product launch. Each stage asks something different of the website. Companies around the city, from the science and business parks on the edges to offices near the station and the biomedical campus in the south, face that same pattern. Prism Works Studio works with Cambridge businesses remotely, so planning, writing and reviews happen over video calls and shared documents.

This guide looks at how a startup site should evolve over time, and at the specific care life science companies need to take.

A website for each stage of the company

Treating the website as a series of stages, rather than one big project, helps founders spend sensibly and avoid rebuilding from scratch every year.

StageWhat the site mainly needs to do
Stealth or very earlyEstablish the company name, a short statement of focus and a contact route, without revealing more than you want to
FundraisingExplain the problem, the approach and the team clearly enough that an investor's first look supports the conversation
HiringShow the mission, the people and the culture, and make applying straightforward
Partnering and pilotsDescribe the technology and its applications in enough depth for technical evaluators
Commercial launchSell the product: features, use cases, pricing or demo routes, documentation and support

Building on a flexible platform with a sensible page structure from the start means new sections can be added at each stage without a redesign. WordPress is a common choice for this because non-developers can add pages and posts, though the right platform depends on who will maintain the site. Our guide to website navigation structure covers how to plan menus that can grow.

What investors look for on a first visit

Investors will rarely decide anything from a website alone, but a confusing or out-of-date site can create doubt before a meeting. A clear site supports the pitch rather than replacing it.

  • A one-line description of what the company does that matches how founders describe it in person.
  • A clear problem statement and why existing approaches fall short.
  • The team, with relevant backgrounds and any advisers who have agreed to be named.
  • News and milestones that are current, so the site does not look abandoned.
  • A contact route that reaches a founder, not a generic inbox nobody checks.

Avoid putting confidential details, such as unpublished data or detailed financials, on public pages. If you want to share more with specific investors, a separate, controlled route is safer than hidden website pages.

Hiring specialists through your website

Competition for experienced engineers, scientists and commercial staff can be intense in and around Cambridge, and candidates often research a company carefully before applying. Your careers page is a sales page for the company as an employer.

  1. Explain the mission in a way that makes the work feel meaningful.
  2. Introduce the team with photos and short profiles, so candidates can picture colleagues.
  3. Describe how you work: lab or office arrangements, hybrid options, development approach and how decisions are made.
  4. List roles with clear responsibilities, requirements and the stages of the hiring process.
  5. Be accurate about benefits, equity and visa sponsorship, and only state what you actually offer.

Job adverts and applications involve personal data, so your privacy notice should cover how applicant information is handled under UK GDPR. Our guide to writing an about us page also helps with presenting the company story.

Life science websites: extra care with claims

Life science companies face constraints that most startups do not. Rules in the UK on advertising medicines and medical devices are strict, and the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency publishes guidance on what can and cannot be promoted, particularly to the public. A product still in development cannot simply be marketed as if it were approved.

As a general approach, which is not regulatory advice:

  • Describe your science and pipeline factually, and be clear about development stage.
  • Avoid language that suggests a product is safe or effective for a use it has not been approved for.
  • Consider whether some content is meant for healthcare professionals or investors and should be presented accordingly.
  • Have regulatory or legal advisers review product and pipeline pages before publishing, and again when anything changes.

Planning these review steps into the website timeline avoids last-minute delays. Our healthcare sector page and healthcare marketing guide cover related points.

Deep tech and software startups

Cambridge tech startups range from hardware and semiconductors to AI, software and platforms. For companies selling software, the website eventually becomes a core part of the sales process, with product pages, documentation, pricing or demo requests and onboarding. For hardware and deep tech companies, the website is more often a credibility tool for long, relationship-led sales.

Either way, technical buyers appreciate substance. Specification sheets, technical overviews, integration details and published papers can all help evaluators, provided they are kept current. Our SaaS website design guide goes into product-led sites in more detail, and our SaaS sector page explains how we approach the area.

Content that builds authority over time

Startups often underestimate how useful a small amount of well-chosen content can be. A few clear articles explaining the problem space, how your approach differs and what is happening in your field can attract the right visitors and give investors and candidates something to read before meeting you. Quality matters more than volume, and technical content should be reviewed by someone qualified before it goes live. Our content strategy guide covers how to choose topics.

Search for specialist audiences

Startups rarely compete for high-volume searches. Instead, the aim is to be found by the small number of people searching for a specific problem, technology or capability. That means clear, specific page titles, pages dedicated to each application or use case and accurate structured data for the organisation. Our B2B SEO guide and SaaS SEO guide explain the approach.

Speed, security and hosting

A startup website should load quickly, use HTTPS, keep software updated and limit who has administrator access. If you hold any sensitive data through the site, such as job applications or partner enquiries, think about where it is stored and who can see it. Our articles on website speed and website maintenance cover the practical side.

Partner and press pages

As a startup grows, more people arrive looking for specific material: a journalist after a company description and logo, a potential partner wanting a short technical overview, or an event organiser needing a founder biography and photo. A simple press and media page with an approved company description, brand assets, founder profiles and a press contact saves repeated emails. Partner pages can explain how you collaborate, the kinds of organisations you work with and how to start a conversation. Only name partners, customers or funders who have agreed to be named, and check any funder's rules on acknowledging support, as many grant schemes set out how this should be done.

Publishing news is worthwhile only if someone will keep it going. A short, current news section with genuine milestones is better than an ambitious blog that stops after three posts.

FAQs

When should a startup invest in a proper website?

Often around the time of fundraising or first hires, when outsiders start judging the company. Before that, a simple, accurate holding page with a contact route may be enough.

Should we keep our website vague while in stealth?

You can. A short statement of focus, the company name and a contact route establish credibility without revealing details you want to keep private.

Can a biotech startup describe its drug candidates online?

You can describe your science and pipeline factually, but promoting unapproved medicines is restricted. Check MHRA guidance and have advisers review product and pipeline pages before publishing.

Do we need a separate careers site?

Usually not. A well-planned careers section on your main site, with team profiles and clear role pages, is often enough for a growing startup.

How do we stop the website going out of date?

Choose a platform your team can edit, assign someone to own updates, and review news, team and role pages at regular intervals.
