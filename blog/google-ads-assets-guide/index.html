Google Ads Assets Explained: How to Add Sitelinks, Callouts and Call Assets

Google Ads assets add sitelinks, callouts, phone numbers and more to your search ads. Learn which assets suit a small business and how to set them up well.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Assets, once called extensions, add extra links, short selling points, a phone number, location and more to search ads, making them larger and more useful.
  • Most UK small businesses should start with sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets and a call or location asset, all written to match the landing pages behind them.
  • Google decides when assets show, so add a good range, keep them accurate, and review the asset performance figures rather than expecting every one to appear every time.

Google Ads assets are extra pieces of information, such as sitelinks, callouts, phone numbers and your business address, that can appear alongside your search ads to make them larger and more useful. To add them, open the assets area of your Google Ads account, choose an asset type, write it for your business, and attach it at account, campaign or ad group level.

Assets used to be called ad extensions, and you will still see that name in older guides. For small businesses running search campaigns, they are one of the simplest improvements available. They cost nothing extra to add, you still pay only for clicks, and they give searchers more reasons to choose you. This guide covers the main types, how to write them, and the mistakes that stop them working.

Why assets matter for small business search ads

A search ad on its own is a headline, a short description and a link. With assets, the same ad might also show four extra links to specific pages, a line of selling points such as free quotes or local engineers, a click-to-call button and your town. That takes up more space on the results page and answers more questions before the click.

Google uses assets as part of how it judges ad quality and expected performance, so a well-built set of assets can support your ad's position and the cost of clicks. Google decides which assets show for each search, and none are guaranteed to appear, but accounts with a sensible range give the system more to work with.

How to add Google Ads assets

The exact menu names move from time to time, but the process is broadly the same:

  1. Open the assets area. In your Google Ads account, find the section for assets, usually within the campaign or ads menus.
  2. Choose the asset type. Start with sitelinks, then callouts, structured snippets and call or location.
  3. Pick the level. Account-level assets apply everywhere. Campaign or ad group assets override them where more specific wording is better. A common approach is general assets at account level and tailored ones for each campaign.
  4. Write and save. Follow the character limits shown on screen, and preview how the ad might look.
  5. Set schedules where useful. Call assets can be scheduled to show only when someone is available to answer.

Google may also create some assets automatically from your website and existing ads. You can usually review or switch these off in the account settings, so check what is running.

Writing sitelinks that get clicked

Sitelinks are usually the most valuable asset. Each one links to a different page and can carry two short description lines.

  • Link to genuinely different pages. Four sitelinks all pointing to the homepage add little and can be disapproved. Choose distinct service pages, a pricing page, reviews, or a contact page.
  • Match the search intent. In a campaign for boiler repairs, sitelinks for boiler servicing, emergency call-outs and finance options are more relevant than a link to your about page.
  • Use descriptions. The extra lines give Google more to show and make each link clearer.
  • Add more than the minimum. Google can show different combinations, so six to eight relevant sitelinks per campaign gives it choice.

Every sitelink sends people to a landing page, so those pages need to deliver. Our landing page design guide and service page design guide explain what a page needs to turn a click into an enquiry.

Callouts and structured snippets

Callouts are short phrases that sit under your ad, such as Free No-Obligation Quotes, Gas Safe Registered Engineers or Next Day UK Delivery. They are not clickable, so their job is to persuade. Keep each one short, specific and true. Every claim must be accurate and supported on your website; the UK advertising codes apply to search ads as much as anywhere else.

Structured snippets use a fixed header, such as Services, Types, Brands or Destinations, followed by a list of values. A florist might use Types: wedding flowers, funeral tributes, bouquets, subscriptions. Choose the header that genuinely fits and list concrete items rather than slogans.

Call and location assets for local businesses

For trades and local services, phone calls are often the most valuable lead. A call asset puts your number on the ad and, on mobile, a button to ring you. Use a number that is answered during the hours the asset is scheduled, and consider using Google's call reporting so calls can be counted as conversions. Our Google Ads conversion tracking guide covers how to measure calls and forms properly.

Location assets pull your address from your Google Business Profile, so the two accounts need linking and your profile details must be accurate. If your profile needs attention, our Google Business Profile guide is a good place to start. Location assets pair naturally with sensible geographic targeting, which our location targeting guide explains.

Common mistakes with assets

  • Setting them once and forgetting them. Offers end, opening hours change and pages get renamed. Outdated assets damage trust and can trigger disapprovals.
  • Repeating the ad text. If your headline says Free Quotes, a callout saying the same wastes space. Use assets to add new information.
  • Broken or redirected sitelinks. After a website redesign, check every sitelink URL still works.
  • Prices that do not match the website. Price assets must reflect what customers will actually pay. If prices vary a lot, leave this asset out.
  • Calls outside opening hours. An unanswered call is a wasted click. Schedule call assets carefully.
  • Judging assets too soon. Give new assets a few weeks of data before deciding they do not work.

Reviewing asset performance

Google Ads reports how often each asset shows and how it performs, though the figures are best read as a guide because assets appear in combinations. Look for sitelinks that are rarely shown or rarely clicked and test better alternatives, and keep the strongest callouts. FAQs

What are Google Ads assets?

They are extra details that can appear with your ads, such as sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, phone numbers, locations and images. They were previously called ad extensions and are free to add, though you still pay for clicks.

Do Google Ads assets cost extra?

No. There is no charge for adding assets. You pay for clicks as normal, and a click on a sitelink or call button is charged in the same way as a click on the main ad, within Google's current billing rules.

Why are my assets not showing?

Google decides whether to show assets for each search based on expected usefulness, ad position and available space. Assets can also be limited by eligibility, disapprovals or scheduling. Check the status column and Google Ads' current help pages if an asset never appears.

How many sitelinks should I add?

Add enough to give Google a choice, often six to eight relevant sitelinks per campaign, each pointing to a different useful page. Fewer, well-written sitelinks are better than many vague ones.

Should assets be at account or campaign level?

Both can work. Account-level assets are a sensible default, and campaign or ad group assets let you tailor messages where a campaign needs different links or selling points. More specific levels take priority over broader ones.

Are Google Ads assets the same as ad extensions?

Yes. Google renamed ad extensions to assets, and the core types such as sitelinks, callouts and call assets work in much the same way. Older guides may still use the extensions name.
