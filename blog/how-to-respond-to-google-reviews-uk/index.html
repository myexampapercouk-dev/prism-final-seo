How to Respond to Google Reviews as a UK Small Business

Reply to every Google review promptly, personally and calmly. Here is how UK small businesses can answer good, bad and fake reviews without hurting trust.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Reply to every review, positive or negative, within a few working days and in a calm, specific voice.
  • Never share customer details or argue in public; move complaints to a private channel.
  • Flag reviews that break Google policy, but do not rely on removal as your main strategy.

To respond to Google reviews well, reply to every review within a few working days, thank the customer by referring to something specific they said, and keep complaints calm, short and moved to a private channel. Doing this consistently shows future customers that a real business is listening, which matters as much as the star rating itself.

This guide walks UK small business owners through the practical side: where to reply, what to write for different kinds of review, how to handle fake or abusive ones, and how to build a routine that does not eat your week. Google changes its interface from time to time, so check the current Google Business Profile help pages for exact menu names before you start.

Why replying to reviews matters

Reviews sit right next to your name in Google Search and Maps. When someone compares three local businesses, they often read the reviews and the replies before they ever visit a website. A thoughtful reply tells them three things: you are still trading, you care about service, and you handle problems like an adult.

Replies also feed into the wider picture of your local presence. Google has said that review activity is one of the signals it considers for local results, alongside relevance, distance and prominence. We cover the bigger picture in our local SEO guide for UK businesses, but the short version is that an active, well-managed profile tends to do better than a neglected one.

Where to reply

You can usually reply from three places, as long as you are signed in with an account that manages the profile:

  • Google Search: search your business name while signed in and use the management panel that appears.
  • Google Maps app: open your business in the app and look for the reviews section under your managed profile.
  • Business Profile manager: businesses with several locations often manage reviews from the multi-location dashboard.

If you have not yet claimed and verified your profile, do that first. Our Google Business Profile optimisation guide explains the set-up, categories and photos. Make sure more than one trusted person in the business has access, so replies do not stop when someone is on holiday.

How to respond to Google reviews: the basic formula

Most good replies follow the same simple pattern. Keep it to two to four sentences in most cases.

  1. Thank them, using their first name if they have shown it publicly.
  2. Mention something specific from their review so it does not read like a template.
  3. Add one useful detail, such as a related service, a seasonal opening time or a fix you have made.
  4. Sign off with the business name or a role, never a personal mobile number or private email.

Avoid stuffing keywords such as your town and service into every reply. It reads badly to humans and there is no good reason to believe it helps. Write for the next customer reading, not for a search engine.

Reply templates by review type

Templates save time, but edit every one so it fits the review. The table below gives a starting point.

Handling negative reviews

A negative review feels personal, especially in a small business. Wait until you are calm before replying, and ideally ask a colleague to read your draft. Then work through these steps:

  • Check the facts. Look up the job, order or booking so your reply is accurate.
  • Apologise for the experience, even if you disagree with parts of the account. You can be sorry someone felt let down without admitting every claim.
  • Do not reveal personal details. Under UK GDPR, mentioning someone's order contents, address or health details in public could cause real problems. Keep it general.
  • Offer a private route, such as your main phone line or a general enquiries email, so the conversation moves out of public view.
  • Explain any change you have made, if relevant, without blaming staff by name.

If you later resolve the issue, some customers will update their review. You can politely ask, but do not pressure them or offer anything in return for changing it.

Fake, spam and policy-breaking reviews

Google has policies against fake engagement, conflicts of interest, offensive content and off-topic reviews. If a review breaks those rules, you can flag or report it through your profile, and Google usually offers a way to track the status of a report. Check the current review policy page for the categories that apply.

Removal is not guaranteed. While you wait, a short, factual reply is often wise, because readers will see the review either way. Keep it neutral: say you cannot find a record of the customer and invite them to get in touch. Never post angry accusations.

The CMA has taken a close interest in fake reviews and the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act brought in rules aimed at fake and incentivised reviews. That cuts both ways: do not buy reviews, do not write them for yourself, and do not offer discounts only to people who leave positive ones. Check the CMA guidance for the current position.

Getting more reviews to reply to

A reply routine works best with a steady trickle of fresh reviews. Most businesses simply forget to ask. Practical ways to ask include:

  • A short link or QR code on invoices, receipts and follow-up emails.
  • A polite request at the end of a job, when the customer is happiest.
  • A line in your order confirmation or delivery email, which our guide to ecommerce transactional emails covers in more depth.
  • A gentle prompt on your website, for example on a testimonials page that links to your profile.

Ask every customer, not just the ones you expect to be happy. Selectively asking only satisfied customers can fall foul of review rules and skews the picture anyway.

Building a weekly review routine

Reviews are easiest when they are a small, regular task rather than a pile you dread. A simple routine for a small business might look like this:

  1. Turn on review notifications for at least two people.
  2. Set a fixed slot, perhaps fifteen minutes on Monday and Thursday, to reply to anything new.
  3. Keep a shared document of approved phrases, sign-offs and a short escalation note for difficult reviews.
  4. Once a month, read all new reviews together and note recurring themes, such as parking, delivery times or communication.
  5. Feed those themes back into your website, for example by adding a clearer FAQ or updating your delivery page.

That last step is where reviews start paying back beyond reputation. Recurring questions are a strong hint about content your website is missing. Our guide to website trust signals explains how to bring that reassurance onto your own pages too.

What not to do

  • Do not copy and paste the same reply on every review.
  • Do not argue, use sarcasm or correct the customer's spelling.
  • Do not mention competitors or speculate about who wrote a review.
  • Do not include links, offers or sales pitches in replies to complaints.
  • Do not leave negative reviews unanswered for weeks; silence reads as indifference.

FAQs

How do I respond to Google reviews?

Sign in with an account that manages your Business Profile, find the review in Search, Maps or the profile manager, and choose the reply option. Thank the customer, mention something specific, and keep complaints calm and short. Check Google's current help pages for exact menu names.

How quickly should I reply to a Google review?

Aim for within a few working days. Quick replies show you are active, but for negative reviews it is better to wait a few hours and reply calmly than to rush an emotional response.

Should I reply to reviews with no text?

Yes, a short thank-you is enough. It costs seconds and shows anyone browsing your profile that every customer is acknowledged.

Can I delete a bad Google review?

Business owners cannot simply delete reviews. You can report ones that break Google policy, such as spam, conflicts of interest or offensive content, and Google decides whether to remove them.

Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Offering incentives for reviews goes against Google policy and UK rules on fake and incentivised reviews. Ask every customer politely instead, with no reward attached.

Can I edit my reply later?

In most cases you can edit or delete your own reply from the same place you posted it. This is useful if a complaint is resolved and you want to add a short update.
