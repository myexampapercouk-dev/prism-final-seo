What Pittsburgh businesses need from a website

Pittsburgh has a broad economy. Healthcare, education, engineering, technology, robotics, construction, professional services and hospitality all compete for attention, alongside a strong base of family-owned trades and shops in neighborhoods such as Shadyside, Lawrenceville, the Strip District, Squirrel Hill and the South Side. Many businesses also serve suburbs across Allegheny, Butler, Washington and Westmoreland counties.

Whatever the sector, most visitors want the same things. They want to know quickly whether you offer the service they need, whether you cover their area, roughly what to expect and how to get in touch. A website that hides those answers behind long introductions or slow pages loses people to the next search result.

Our design process

We keep the process simple and predictable so you always know what happens next.

  1. Discovery call. We learn about your services, customers, service area and goals, and agree what the site must do.
  2. Structure and content plan. We map the pages, decide what each one should rank for and list the content needed.
  3. Design. We create the look of key pages, using your brand colors and real photography where possible.
  4. Build. We build the site on a platform that suits you, usually WordPress for service businesses or Shopify for online stores.
  5. Testing and launch. We check speed, forms, mobile layout, redirects and tracking before going live.

Most small business sites take three to five weeks. E-commerce sites take five to twelve weeks depending on the number of products and integrations.

Choosing a platform

For most Pittsburgh service businesses, such as contractors, clinics, consultancies and law firms, WordPress is a flexible and familiar choice. It is easy to update and works well for content and local SEO. Our notes on a WordPress agency in Chicago explain what to look for in WordPress support in a similar Midwest market.

If you sell products online, Shopify is usually simpler to run, with reliable checkout, payments and inventory built in. Our guide to e-commerce web design for US businesses covers store design in more depth.

Whichever platform you choose, you should own the domain, hosting account and content. We set things up in your name so you are never locked in.

Design for conversion, not just looks

A beautiful website that does not bring enquiries is an expensive brochure. We design pages around the actions you want people to take. That means clear headlines, short paragraphs, obvious buttons and answers to the questions people ask before they call.

For existing sites, a review of where people drop off can be more useful than a full redesign. Our conversion rate optimization audit explains how we find and fix the weak points on a site.

Good structure also helps search. Logical headings, as covered in our heading tags guide, make pages easier to scan for both readers and search engines.

Redesigning without losing rankings

Many Pittsburgh businesses already have a website that ranks for something useful. A careless redesign can wipe that out by changing URLs without redirects or removing pages that bring traffic. Before any rebuild, we record your current pages, rankings and traffic, then plan redirects for anything that moves. Our SEO migration checklist walks through the steps.

After launch we check Search Console for errors and fix anything that slipped through. If you want ongoing search work after the build, our notes on an SEO agency in Pittsburgh explain how we approach local search in the city.

Questions to ask any Pittsburgh web designer

  • Will I own the domain, hosting and website files?
  • How will you protect my existing search rankings?
  • What happens after launch if something breaks?
  • Can I update text and images myself?
  • How will you measure whether the site is bringing enquiries?

Clear answers to these questions are a good sign. Vague answers are worth probing before you sign anything.

Preparing for your Pittsburgh website project

A little preparation makes any web project faster and cheaper. Before the discovery call, it helps to gather a few things together so the design and build can move without delays.

  • Your services. A simple list of what you offer, which services matter most and which bring the best customers.
  • Your service area. The neighborhoods, towns and counties you genuinely cover, such as the city itself, the North Hills or the Mon Valley.
  • Brand assets. Your logo files, any brand colors and fonts, and real photos of your team, premises or work.
  • Logins. Access to your domain registrar, current hosting, Google Business Profile, Search Console and analytics.
  • Examples. A few websites you like, and a note on what you like about them, whether layout, tone or simplicity.

We write website copy in English. If you want pages in another language, such as Spanish for part of your customer base, that content should come from your own team or a professional translator, and we can build the site structure to support it.

It is also worth thinking about what happens after launch. A website works best when someone keeps it current, adds new pages as services change and checks that forms and tracking still work. We can handle that as part of a monthly retainer, or hand the site over with simple guidance so your team can manage everyday updates.
