Shopify for UK Pet Product Brands

How UK pet product brands can use Shopify well: catalogue structure by pet and size, pet food labelling, subscriptions, product pages, delivery and SEO.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Pet shoppers browse by animal, size and life stage, so the catalogue structure should mirror how owners think about their pets.
  • Pet food, treats and some health products carry UK labelling and claims rules, so check current guidance before writing product copy.
  • Repeat purchases such as food, litter and treats make subscriptions and easy reordering worth planning from the start.

Pet products sit in an interesting corner of ecommerce. Some items are bought once, such as a bed, a crate or a harness, while others are bought again and again, such as food, treats, litter and poo bags. Owners are often emotionally invested, they want to know a product is safe and suitable for their animal, and they frequently need to get sizes right without the pet in the room. Shopify can handle all of this well, but the store needs to be set up with those habits in mind.

This guide covers how UK pet product brands can structure a Shopify store, write product pages that answer owners' questions, handle repeat purchases and approach the regulatory side with care. It is general guidance rather than legal advice, so check current rules for your specific products.

Is Shopify a good fit for pet brands?

For most small and growing pet brands, Shopify is a sensible choice. It is hosted, so you do not manage servers or security patches, and it handles variants, collections, discount codes and subscriptions through its own features and app ecosystem. If you are weighing it against other platforms, our Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison and Wix vs Shopify guide set out the trade-offs.

The platform matters less than the decisions you make inside it: how products are grouped, how sizing is explained, how repeat purchases work and how clearly safety and suitability information is presented.

Structure the catalogue around the animal

Pet owners rarely start by thinking about product types in the abstract. They think about their dog, their cat or their rabbit, and then what that animal needs. A navigation that reflects this tends to be easier to use.

  • Shop by pet first. Top-level collections such as dogs, cats and small animals let owners filter out irrelevant products immediately.
  • Then by need. Within each animal, group by food, treats, toys, beds, walking, grooming and health.
  • Then by size or life stage. Puppy, adult and senior, or small, medium and large breed, are common ways owners narrow choices.

Shopify collections can be manual or automated by tags and product attributes, and a single product can sit in several collections. A harness can appear under dogs, walking and small breed without duplicating the product. Filters on collection pages, built on product options and metafields, let shoppers narrow by size, material or flavour. Our guide to ecommerce category page design and website navigation structure go into more depth.

Sizing without the pet in the room

Collars, harnesses, coats, beds and crates all depend on getting the size right, and returns for the wrong size are frustrating on both sides. Good sizing information reduces that friction.

  • Give measurements, not just labels. Small, medium and large mean different things across brands. Show neck and chest girth ranges, back length or internal dimensions.
  • Explain how to measure. A short guide or diagram showing where to measure a dog's chest or neck helps owners get it right first time.
  • Offer breed guidance carefully. Example breeds for each size can help, but individual animals vary, so present them as a rough guide rather than a guarantee.
  • Show scale in photos. Images of the product on animals of different sizes help owners picture the fit.

Our article on ecommerce size guides covers presentation in more detail, much of which applies directly to pet products.

Product pages that answer owners' questions

Pet owners tend to want reassurance before buying, especially for anything that goes in or on their animal. A strong product page anticipates their questions.

  • Materials and construction. What is the toy or bed made from, is it machine washable, are there small parts?
  • Suitability. Which animals, sizes and ages is the product designed for, and who is it not suitable for?
  • Ingredients and feeding information for food and treats, presented clearly rather than buried in an image of the packaging.
  • Care and safety notes, such as supervising play with chew toys or checking fit regularly.
  • Delivery and returns information close to the add to basket button.

Shopify metafields are useful here. Rather than writing every detail into one long description, you can store structured information such as material, washing instructions, suitable sizes and feeding guide in separate fields and display them consistently across products. That also makes the information easier to keep up to date. For copy, our guide to ecommerce product descriptions covers structure and tone.

Pet food, treats and claims: tread carefully

If you sell pet food or treats, labelling and marketing are regulated in the UK. Rules cover matters such as the information that must appear on labels, how ingredients and analytical constituents are declared, and what claims can be made. Feed businesses may also need to register with their local authority. Supplements and products with health-related claims for animals raise further questions, and some products that make medicinal claims may fall under veterinary medicines rules overseen by the Veterinary Medicines Directorate.

We are not able to give legal advice, and the rules depend on exactly what you sell and how you describe it. Sensible steps include:

  • Checking current guidance from the Food Standards Agency, your local authority trading standards or feed team and, where relevant, the Veterinary Medicines Directorate.
  • Making sure the information on your product pages matches what is on the physical label.
  • Avoiding words that imply a product treats, cures or prevents a condition unless you have confirmed you are permitted to say so.
  • Taking professional advice if you are unsure whether a product or claim is allowed.

Product safety rules also apply to non-food items. If you import or manufacture toys, accessories or equipment, check your obligations as a producer or distributor.

Repeat purchases and subscriptions

Food, treats, litter and similar consumables are natural repeat purchases. Making reordering easy can benefit both customers and the brand.

  • Subscriptions. Shopify supports selling subscriptions through compatible apps, letting customers choose delivery frequency. Clear controls to pause, skip or cancel are important for trust, and UK consumer rules on subscriptions are evolving, so check current requirements. Our ecommerce subscriptions guide covers the essentials.
  • Customer accounts. Letting customers see past orders and reorder quickly helps those who prefer not to subscribe. See our guide to customer accounts and wishlists.
  • Bundles. Starter kits for a new puppy or kitten can combine several products sensibly. Our article on variants and bundles explains the options.
  • Email. Reminders timed around typical usage, set up with care and with consent where required, can prompt reorders. Our ecommerce email marketing guide covers the basics.

Delivery for bulky and heavy items

Large bags of food, crates and beds can be heavy or bulky, which affects delivery costs and options. Shopify lets you set shipping rates by weight, price or product, and you can create shipping profiles for items that need different handling. Be upfront about delivery charges and timescales for large items, and consider whether free delivery thresholds make sense given product weights. Our guide to delivery and returns pages covers how to present this information clearly.

Photography and trust

Images of real animals using products are often more persuasive than packshots alone, though clean product shots on a plain background are still useful for showing details. A mix usually works best: a clear main image, lifestyle shots showing scale and use, and close-ups of materials, stitching or fastenings. Our product photography guide covers the practicalities.

Trust also comes from clear contact details, honest product information, visible returns policies and genuine customer reviews. Never invent reviews or endorsements. Our guides to product reviews and website trust signals explain how to build credibility properly.

SEO for pet product stores

Pet owners search in specific ways: by animal, by size, by problem and by product type. Searches combining a product with a breed size or life stage, such as harnesses for small dogs or food for senior cats, show how specific intent can be. Collection pages targeting these combinations, written with genuinely useful introductory content, can help.

  • Write unique titles and descriptions for collections and products rather than relying on defaults.
  • Use descriptive image alt text that describes what the image shows.
  • Add structured data for products so search engines understand price and availability.
  • Create helpful guides, such as how to measure a dog for a harness, that answer real questions and link to relevant collections.

FAQs

Is Shopify good for selling pet products?

Yes, Shopify handles variants, collections, subscriptions and shipping profiles well, which suits most pet ranges. Success depends on clear sizing, product information and trust.

Do I need to register to sell pet food online?

Businesses that make, import or sell pet food may need to register as a feed business with their local authority. Check current guidance for your circumstances.

Can I say my pet product improves health?

Health and medicinal claims for animal products are restricted. Check current guidance from the relevant UK regulators and take advice before making such claims.

How should I organise a pet shop on Shopify?

Organising by animal first, then by need, then by size or life stage usually mirrors how owners shop. Products can sit in several collections at once.

Should I offer subscriptions for pet food?

Subscriptions can suit consumables like food and litter. Give customers easy controls to pause, skip or cancel, and check current UK subscription rules.
