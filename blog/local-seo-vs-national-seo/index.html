Local SEO vs National SEO: Choosing the Right Approach for a UK Business

Local SEO wins customers near you through Google Maps and town searches; national SEO competes UK-wide for broader terms. See which suits your business.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Local SEO targets people searching near you, using Google Business Profile, reviews, local pages and consistent business details.
  • National SEO targets searches with no local angle, which usually means more competition and a bigger need for strong content and links.
  • Choose based on where your customers are and how they search; many businesses need a local base with a national layer on top.

Local SEO helps you win customers searching near you, through Google Maps, the local results and searches that include a town or "near me". National SEO helps you compete for searches across the whole UK where location does not matter, such as an online shop or a remote service. Which one you need depends on where your customers are and how they search, and many businesses need a local foundation with some national work layered on top.

This guide compares local SEO vs national SEO on goals, tactics, competition, cost and timelines, then gives a simple way to decide. Search engines change how results are shown, so treat any specific feature described here as general guidance and check current official documentation.

What local SEO covers

Local SEO is about showing up when people search for something near them. When Google decides the searcher wants a local result, it often shows a map with a short list of businesses, followed by standard results. Getting into that map pack depends heavily on your Google Business Profile, your distance from the searcher, how relevant your profile is and how prominent your business appears across the web.

The main local SEO tasks are:

  • A complete, accurate Google Business Profile with the right categories, services, photos and opening hours.
  • A steady flow of genuine reviews, with thoughtful replies.
  • Consistent name, address and phone details across directories, known as local citations.
  • Website pages for your main services and the areas you cover, written for real people rather than stuffed with town names.
  • Local links and mentions, such as local press, associations, suppliers and community sponsorships.

What national SEO covers

National SEO targets searches where the person does not expect a local result. Someone searching for "vegan leather handbags" or "HR software for small businesses" wants the best answer, not the nearest one. You are competing with every relevant business in the UK, as well as large retailers, directories and publishers.

National SEO leans more on:

  • Thorough keyword research to find terms you can realistically win, often longer, more specific phrases.
  • Strong category, product or service pages, plus helpful guides that answer questions buyers ask before they purchase.
  • Technical health: fast pages, clean structure and indexing that works properly.
  • Links and mentions from relevant sites, which often come from digital PR and genuinely useful content.

Because the competition is wider, national SEO usually needs more content and more sustained effort before results show.

Competition, cost and timelines

Local SEO is often the quicker win for a local business, because you are only competing with firms in your area and the number of strong competitors is limited. A tidy profile, regular reviews and good service pages can make a noticeable difference in a reasonable time.

National SEO tends to take longer and cost more, because the bar for ranking is higher. More pages need writing, more links need earning and more technical work may be required. Across both, SEO results typically take three to six months to start showing, and competitive national terms can take longer. Our SEO cost guide explains what drives budgets.

Which businesses need which

Mostly local: trades, salons, clinics, restaurants, gyms, estate agents, solicitors and any business where the customer visits you or you visit them within an area. Local SEO is the priority, with national work only where it supports trust or brand.

Mostly national: online shops that ship across the UK, software companies, and service firms that work remotely. A Google Business Profile may still help with brand searches, but most growth comes from national rankings.

A mix: businesses with a physical base but a wider reach, such as a manufacturer, a training provider, a B2B consultancy or a shop that also sells online. These usually need local SEO for the home area and national SEO for products or specialist services. Our B2B sector page covers how that often works for professional firms.

If you operate from several branches, you are somewhere in between. Our multi-location SEO guide explains how to structure pages and profiles for each site.

Common mistakes

  • Creating dozens of near-identical town pages. Thin location pages with only the place name swapped rarely help and can make the site look low quality.
  • Chasing national head terms too early. A local firm targeting broad UK phrases can spend months with little to show.
  • Ignoring the business profile. For local searches, it often matters as much as the website.
  • Using a service-area address you do not operate from. Profiles should follow Google's current guidelines on addresses and service areas.
  • Weak service pages. Whether local or national, each main service needs a clear, useful page. Our guide to service page design covers what to include.

Measuring local and national SEO

The two approaches need slightly different measures. For local SEO, the most useful signals usually come from your Google Business Profile insights: calls, direction requests, website clicks and how often you appear for searches in your area. Rankings in the map results can vary street by street, so a single ranking check from your office tells you little. Look at trends over months and, most importantly, at the enquiries and bookings that come in.

For national SEO, Google Search Console is the main source. It shows which queries bring impressions and clicks, which pages earn them and where pages sit just outside the top results and could be improved. Pair that with your analytics so you can see whether organic visitors go on to buy, sign up or enquire.

In both cases, ask how new customers found you and record it. A short question on your enquiry form or at the till fills gaps that analytics cannot, especially for people who saw you in search and then phoned or walked in. Review the numbers quarterly, decide what is working and shift effort towards it.

How to decide your approach

  1. List where your best customers are based and how far you will travel or deliver.
  2. Check how people search for your service: with a place name, "near me", or with no location.
  3. Look at what currently ranks. If the results show a map, local signals matter.
  4. Start with the approach that matches most of your revenue, then add the other where it makes commercial sense.
  5. Track enquiries and sales, not just rankings, so you can see which work pays back.

FAQs

Local SEO vs national SEO: which does my business need?

If most customers are near you or you serve a defined area, start with local SEO. If you sell across the UK and location does not matter to searchers, focus on national SEO. Many businesses need both.

Is local SEO easier than national SEO?

Usually, yes, because you compete only with nearby businesses. Competitive local markets in large cities can still take sustained work.

Can I rank in towns where I do not have an office?

You can rank organic pages for areas you genuinely serve, but the map results tend to favour businesses close to the searcher. Follow Google's current guidelines on service areas.

Do online shops need local SEO?

Mostly not, unless they also have a physical shop, showroom or collection point. For most online shops, national SEO drives the bulk of organic sales.

How long does national SEO take?

SEO results typically take three to six months to start showing, and competitive national terms can take longer, depending on your site, content and links.

Do I need a Google Business Profile for national SEO?

It is not essential for national rankings, but if you have a real business address it can still help people searching your brand name and add trust.
