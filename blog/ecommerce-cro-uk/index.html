Ecommerce CRO UK: Conversion Rate Optimisation for Checkout, Product Pages and Testing

Ecommerce CRO in the UK means fixing checkout, product pages and site speed, then testing changes. See priorities, a testing approach and a full checklist.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with the checkout and product pages, where small fixes affect every order and every paid click.
  • Use research before testing: analytics, recordings, surveys and support questions show where shoppers get stuck.
  • Only A/B test when traffic allows; smaller UK stores should make evidence-based fixes and measure before and after.

Ecommerce conversion rate optimisation (CRO) for UK stores means finding where shoppers drop out and fixing it: usually the checkout, product pages, delivery information and site speed. The best order is research first, then fixes that remove friction, then testing once you have enough traffic to measure the difference.

CRO matters because it multiplies everything else. Every visitor from SEO, Google Shopping or Meta ads lands on the same pages, so a better-converting store makes each channel cheaper. This guide covers the UK specifics, where to start and how to test sensibly.

Why ecommerce CRO UK work starts with the basics

UK shoppers have clear expectations: delivery costs shown early, familiar carriers, easy returns, card and wallet payments, and often buy now pay later options. Stores that hide any of these lose people at the point of purchase. Before clever experiments, check that the basics are in place and working on mobile, where most browsing happens.

It also helps to agree what success means before any changes go live. For most stores the goal is more revenue per visitor rather than a higher conversion rate at any cost, because discounting heavily can lift conversion while cutting profit. Set a baseline for the main funnel steps, note any promotions or seasonal peaks in the period, and record every change with its date. That simple log makes it far easier to tell later which fixes actually made a difference and which coincided with something else.

Where to start: research before changes

Guessing is the most expensive form of CRO. Collect evidence first.

  • Funnel analysis in GA4. Compare product view to add to basket, basket to checkout, and checkout to purchase. The biggest drop is usually the first place to look.
  • Device and browser splits. A checkout that converts on desktop but not on a particular mobile browser often hides a bug.
  • Session recordings and heatmaps. Watch where people hesitate, rage-click or abandon. Set these tools up with UK GDPR and PECR consent in mind.
  • On-site surveys and exit polls. Ask shoppers what nearly stopped them buying.
  • Customer service questions. Repeated questions about sizing, delivery or returns point to missing page content.

Checkout optimisation

The checkout is where intent is highest, so friction here is costly.

  • Allow guest checkout. Forced account creation is a common reason for abandonment.
  • Show delivery costs and options before the final step. Surprise costs drive drop-off.
  • Offer the payment methods UK shoppers expect: cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal, and Klarna or Clearpay where they suit your price points and you are comfortable with the terms.
  • Use postcode lookup for addresses to speed up entry and reduce errors.
  • Keep fields to a minimum and use the right mobile keyboards.
  • Show trust signals near the pay button: secure payment, returns policy, delivery times.
  • Make error messages specific and keep entered data when validation fails.

Platform matters here. Shopify's checkout is standardised and fast; WooCommerce offers more flexibility but needs more care. Our Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison covers the trade-offs.

Product page optimisation

Product pages do the selling. Most improvements come from answering questions before the shopper has to ask.

  • Images and video. Several angles, scale, in-use shots and zoom. Video for products where movement or texture matters.
  • Clear price and stock. Price, offers and availability visible without scrolling.
  • Delivery and returns. State delivery cost, carrier (Royal Mail, Evri, DPD) and estimated dates near the add to basket button.
  • Sizing and specifications. Size guides, dimensions, materials and care instructions.
  • Reviews. Genuine customer reviews with photos, including the critical ones.
  • Sticky add to basket on mobile. Keep the main action in reach as people scroll.

Testing: when and how

A/B testing compares two versions of a page with real traffic. It is powerful but needs enough conversions to produce a reliable answer. Many smaller UK stores do not have that volume, and running underpowered tests produces false winners.

If you have high traffic

  1. Write a hypothesis based on research: changing X will improve Y because Z.
  2. Calculate the sample size needed before starting.
  3. Run the test for full weeks to cover weekday and weekend behaviour.
  4. Do not stop early because one version looks ahead.
  5. Record results, including losers, so you build knowledge over time.

If you have modest traffic

Make evidence-based fixes to obvious friction, then compare conversion rates before and after over a sensible period, adjusting for seasonality and promotions. Focus on bigger changes rather than small tweaks, since small effects are impossible to detect at low volume.

Prioritising CRO ideas

Research usually produces more ideas than you can act on. A simple scoring method keeps the work focused. Rate each idea from one to five on three questions, then work from the highest total down.

QuestionHigh score whenLow score when
ImpactIt affects a high-traffic page or the checkoutIt affects a rarely visited page
EvidenceAnalytics, recordings and surveys all point to itIt is a hunch or a competitor copy
EaseA settings change or small template editA rebuild or a new platform integration

Quick wins with strong evidence, such as showing delivery costs earlier or fixing a broken mobile form, should go first. Bigger projects, such as a redesign of the product template, can follow once the obvious friction has gone.

Measuring the right things

Overall conversion rate can mislead, because it moves with traffic mix. A burst of cheap social traffic lowers it even if the site improved. Track step-by-step rates (product view to basket, basket to checkout, checkout to purchase), average order value and revenue per visitor, split by device and channel. Revenue per visitor is often the most honest single measure, since it captures both conversion rate and order value.

Avoiding common CRO mistakes

  • Copying a competitor's design without knowing whether it works for them.
  • Adding pop-ups, countdown timers and urgency messages that erode trust, or that would not stand up under the CAP Code and consumer protection rules.
  • Installing apps for every idea until the site slows down.
  • Declaring a test winner after a few days.

CRO and paid traffic

Paid campaigns expose conversion problems quickly because every click has a cost. If your Google Shopping or Meta ads bring visitors who do not buy, landing page work often helps more than further ad changes. Ecommerce CRO checklist

  • GA4 funnel set up with add to basket, begin checkout and purchase events.
  • Biggest drop-off step identified by device.
  • Guest checkout available.
  • Delivery costs shown before checkout.
  • UK payment options and wallets offered.
  • Postcode lookup on address fields.
  • Product pages show delivery, returns, sizing and reviews.
  • Mobile Core Web Vitals passing on key templates.
  • Site search tested with common misspellings.
  • Returns policy and contact details easy to find.
  • Tests planned only where traffic supports them.

FAQs

What is ecommerce CRO UK?

Ecommerce CRO in the UK is the process of increasing the share of store visitors who buy, by researching where they drop out and improving checkout, product pages, delivery information and speed for UK shoppers.

What is a good ecommerce conversion rate?

It varies widely by sector, price point, device and traffic source, so benchmarks can mislead. Compare your own rate over time and by channel, and focus on improving the weakest step in your funnel.

Where should I start with conversion rate optimisation?

Start with the checkout and product pages, using GA4 funnel data to find the biggest drop-off. Fix obvious friction such as hidden delivery costs or forced account creation first.

Do I need A/B testing for CRO?

Not always. A/B testing needs enough conversions to be reliable. Smaller stores often get more from research-led fixes measured before and after.

Does site speed affect conversions?

Yes. Slow pages, especially on mobile, frustrate shoppers and can reduce sales. Compressing images and removing unused apps are common quick improvements.

Should I offer Klarna or Clearpay?

Buy now pay later options are familiar to many UK shoppers and can help at some price points. Weigh the fees and the regulatory context, and check the provider's rules before adding them.
