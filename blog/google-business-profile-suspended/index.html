Google Business Profile Suspended: Why It Happens and How to Get It Back

Google Business Profile suspended? Learn the common causes, how to check what went wrong, fix your listing and submit a clear appeal with the right evidence.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most suspensions come from a mismatch with Google's guidelines, such as a stuffed business name, an ineligible address or a recent edit that triggered a review.
  • Fix the problem on the profile first, gather evidence that the business is real and operates as listed, then submit one clear appeal.
  • Do not create a new profile while you wait. Keep your details accurate and consistent to reduce the chance of it happening again.

If your Google Business Profile is suspended, it usually means Google believes something on the listing breaks its guidelines for representing a business. To fix it, find the likely cause, correct the profile so it follows the current guidelines, gather documents that prove your business is real and operates as listed, and submit an appeal through Google's official process.

This guide is for UK small business owners who have found their profile missing from Maps, marked as suspended in their dashboard, or unable to be edited. It explains the common causes, the steps to take and how to lower the risk of it happening again. Google updates its policies and appeal tools regularly, so always check its current Business Profile help pages alongside this guide.

What a suspension looks like

There are broadly two situations, and the signs differ.

TypeWhat you seeWhat it means
Profile disabled or removedThe listing no longer appears on Search or Maps, and the dashboard shows a suspension or restriction noticeGoogle has taken the listing down and you need to appeal
Management restrictedThe listing still shows publicly, but you cannot edit it or respond to reviewsYour access is limited, often because of the account rather than the business
Edits not publishedChanges stay pending or are rejected, without a full suspensionGoogle is reviewing changes and may ask for verification

Sign in to the account that manages the profile and read any notice carefully. The wording often hints at what triggered the action.

Common reasons a profile is suspended

  • Keywords in the business name. Adding services or locations to the name, such as Smith Plumbing Emergency Plumber Leeds, when that is not the real trading name.
  • An ineligible address. Using a virtual office, mailbox, coworking desk you do not staff, or an address where you do not actually meet customers.
  • Service area business showing an address. If you travel to customers and do not serve them at your premises, Google expects the address to be hidden.
  • Several profiles for one business. Duplicate listings, or separate profiles for each service at the same location.
  • Big changes at once. Changing the name, address and category together can trigger a review.
  • Category or website mismatch. A profile that does not match the business described on the linked website.
  • Account problems. Issues with the Google account managing the profile, or a manager who has been flagged.
  • Regulated or sensitive categories. Some business types, such as locksmiths and certain trades, attract more scrutiny because of past abuse.

Sometimes a suspension is a mistake by an automated system. Even then, the fix is the same: make sure everything is accurate and appeal with evidence.

Google Business Profile suspended: what to do first

  1. Do not panic or create a new profile. Creating a replacement listing can make things worse and may be treated as a duplicate.
  2. Read the notice. Note the date, the wording and any policy mentioned.
  3. Review the guidelines. Read Google's current guidelines for representing your business on Google and compare them with your listing line by line.
  4. Think about recent changes. Did you or anyone else edit the name, address, phone number, hours or category shortly before the suspension?
  5. Check who manages the profile. Remove anyone who no longer needs access and make sure the owner account is one you control.

Fix the profile before you appeal

An appeal is more likely to succeed if the profile already follows the guidelines when Google reviews it. Where you still have edit access, or where Google lets you correct details as part of the appeal:

  • Change the business name to exactly what appears on your signage, paperwork and website.
  • Hide the address if customers do not visit you, and set a realistic service area instead.
  • Choose a primary category that describes the core of the business, not every service you offer.
  • Make sure the phone number and website are your own and match what appears elsewhere.
  • Remove any duplicate profiles you control, following Google's process for closing or merging them.

Consistency matters well beyond Google. Check that your name, address and phone number match on directories and your website too. Our guide to local citations explains how to tidy them up, and our Google Business Profile guide covers how a well-set-up listing should look.

Gather your evidence

Google may ask you to prove that the business exists at the address or in the area you claim. Evidence that is commonly useful includes:

EvidenceWhy it helps
Photos of permanent signage on your premisesShows a real, staffed location
Photos of branded vehicles or equipmentUseful for service area businesses
A recent utility bill or business rates billLinks the business name to the address
Business registration detailsCompanies House records or other registration show the trading name
Trade licences or membershipsSupport that you operate in the category you chose
A short video walkthroughGoogle sometimes asks for one to show the premises and the business in action

Make sure every document shows the same business name and address as the profile. Mismatches are a frequent reason appeals fail.

Submit the appeal

Google provides an official appeal or reinstatement route for suspended profiles, usually reached from the notice in your dashboard or through its Business Profile help pages. Use only Google's own tools. Be wary of anyone offering guaranteed reinstatement for a fee, and never share your Google account password with a third party. If someone needs to help, add them as a manager instead.

When you appeal:

  • Explain briefly what the business does and how it serves customers.
  • Say what you have changed to follow the guidelines, if anything.
  • Upload clear, relevant evidence rather than everything you have.
  • Submit one appeal and wait. Multiple appeals for the same issue can slow things down.

Review times vary. Keep an eye on the email address linked to the account for updates or requests for more information.

While you wait

A suspended profile can hit enquiries hard, especially for businesses that rely on calls from Maps. While the appeal is under review:

  • Make sure your website clearly shows your services, areas and contact details. Our guide to SEO for small businesses covers the essentials.
  • Keep listings on other directories accurate so people can still find and contact you.
  • Consider short-term local paid search to cover the gap. Our Google Ads for small business guide and location targeting guide explain how to keep spend focused on your area.

After reinstatement

When the profile is restored, check that your reviews, photos and details have come back as expected. Then reduce the risk of a repeat:

  • Make changes gradually and keep them accurate.
  • Limit who has manager access and review it every few months.
  • Keep the name exactly as you trade, with no added keywords.
  • Post regular, genuine updates. Our guide to Google Business Profile posts has ideas.
  • Keep a folder of evidence, such as signage photos and bills, so you are ready if Google asks again.

FAQs

Why is my Google Business Profile suspended?

Common causes include keywords added to the business name, an ineligible or virtual address, showing an address for a service area business, duplicate profiles, large edits made at once, or problems with the managing account. Compare your profile with Google's current guidelines to find the likely issue.

How long does a Google Business Profile appeal take?

It varies. Some appeals are reviewed within days, others take longer, particularly if Google asks for more evidence. Watch the email linked to the account and avoid sending repeat appeals.

Should I create a new profile if mine is suspended?

No. Creating a new listing can be treated as a duplicate and may make the situation worse. Fix the original profile and appeal through Google's official process.

Will I lose my reviews if my profile is suspended?

Reviews are usually hidden while a profile is suspended. If the profile is reinstated, they often return, but check once it is restored.

Can I use a virtual office address on Google Business Profile?

Generally no. Google expects the address to be a place where the business is staffed and serves customers, or for the address to be hidden for service area businesses. Check Google's current guidelines for your situation.

Can an agency get my profile reinstated?

Nobody outside Google can guarantee reinstatement. An agency can help correct the profile, prepare evidence and submit a clear appeal, but the decision always rests with Google.
