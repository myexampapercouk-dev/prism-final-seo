Marketing for Recruitment Agencies: How to Win Clients and Candidates

A practical overview of marketing for recruitment agencies: niche positioning, SEO, LinkedIn, paid ads, email and content for both clients and candidates.

By the Prism Works Studio team · Updated 7 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Marketing for recruitment agencies has two audiences, clients and candidates, so plan separate messages and pages for each.
  • A clear niche gives SEO, content and LinkedIn something specific to say.
  • Track placements and client enquiries by source, not just applications or followers.

Marketing for recruitment agencies has to win two audiences at once: employers who hire you and candidates who apply through you. A practical plan usually starts with a clear niche, then adds a website that serves both groups, consistent LinkedIn activity, targeted paid campaigns, useful content and email.

This overview is for owners of small and independent recruitment agencies who want a marketing plan rather than relying only on cold calls and job boards. It covers both audiences; for a closer look at the client side, read our guide to marketing to employer clients.

Two audiences, two journeys

Clients and candidates want different things. A hiring manager wants to know you understand their market, can find good people quickly and are easy to work with. A candidate wants relevant jobs, honest communication and someone who will represent them well. Mixing both messages on one page usually serves neither.

Plan separate paths:

  • Client side: service pages for each sector or role type, how you work, terms in general terms, and a clear way to brief you.
  • Candidate side: searchable jobs, sector pages, CV upload and helpful career content.

Pick a niche you can own

Generalist recruiters can find themselves competing with large networks and job boards on broad searches. A niche, such as construction, accountancy, IT contracting, education, hospitality or a regional focus, gives every channel something specific to say. It can make specific searches more realistic, content more relevant and your LinkedIn activity more recognisable. SEO for recruitment agencies

Search can bring both audiences, but they search differently.

  • Client searches include terms like "construction recruitment agency Manchester" or "finance recruiters London". These need strong service pages per sector and location, with real detail about the roles you fill and how you work.
  • Candidate searches include job titles plus locations. Job pages can rank if they are well structured, unique and kept up to date. Expired jobs should be handled sensibly, not left as dead pages.

LinkedIn: a natural channel for recruiters

LinkedIn is where many hiring managers and professional candidates can be reached. Consistent, useful posting from both the company page and individual consultants can build recognition. Content worth trying includes market commentary, salary and hiring trends you can genuinely evidence, practical advice for hiring managers and candidates, and live roles.

LinkedIn ads can target decision-makers by job title, industry and company size, which suits client acquisition for specialist agencies. Compare the cost per enquiry with other channels, and use a clear offer such as a market report or a hiring guide. Our LinkedIn ads guide covers setup and targeting.

Paid search and social for candidates

For volume roles such as warehouse, driving, care or hospitality, Meta ads and Google Ads can reach local candidates quickly. Keep application steps short and mobile-friendly. Make sure job ads comply with platform policies on employment advertising and avoid discriminatory targeting or wording. Equality law can apply to job adverts, so have wording checked and take advice where needed.

Google, Meta and LinkedIn campaigns are all part of our paid media service, and ad spend is paid separately.

Content that builds authority

Useful content helps you rank and gives consultants something worth sharing. Ideas include hiring guides for your sector, interview tips, salary benchmarking based on your own anonymised data (only if you can stand behind it), and explainers on topics like IR35 for contractors, kept general and pointing to official guidance. Our content strategy guide explains how to plan topics around real searches.

Website essentials

  • Fast, searchable job listings that work well on mobile.
  • Separate client and candidate navigation.
  • Clear calls to action: brief us, upload a CV, register for alerts.
  • Consultant or team pages only if your team is happy to appear.
  • A privacy notice explaining how candidate data is handled under UK GDPR.

Our recruitment agency website guide covers these features in more detail, and our small business website cost guide explains what affects rebuild costs. Job board integrations are worth agreeing during scoping, as each one can affect cost and testing.

Marketing to employers

Employer marketing tends to be slower and more relationship-led than advertising a role. It usually depends on evidence that you know the market, pages written for hiring managers, referrals and repeat business. Only share client feedback publicly with clear permission. Our employer marketing guide sets out how to approach each of these.

Marketing to candidates

Candidates mostly want to know three things: whether a role is relevant, whether it is real and what happens after they apply. State the location, working pattern and pay where you can. Reply to applicants, even briefly, because candidates talk and may also be future hiring managers. Keep a registered candidate list up to date, with permission, so a new role can be shared with suitable people before you spend on advertising it.

Reviews and reputation

Employers and candidates may both check what others say about you. A complete Google Business Profile and honest reviews can help, particularly for agencies with a local focus. Ask everyone fairly, never offer incentives or post fake reviews, and answer criticism calmly. Our Google Business Profile guide covers the setup.

Data and compliance

Recruitment involves a lot of personal data. Candidate details, CVs and marketing lists must be handled in line with UK GDPR, and email marketing to individuals must follow PECR. Employment agency conduct rules may also apply to how you advertise roles. This is general information only; the agency is responsible for compliance and for signing off all marketing.

Email and job alerts

Email gives you a list you control rather than relying on a platform. Candidate job alerts, filtered by sector and location, bring people back to your site whenever a relevant role appears. For clients, a short, regular update with market observations, available candidate profiles (anonymised and shared with permission) and hiring tips keeps you in mind between briefs.

Keep emails short, relevant and easy to unsubscribe from. Segment by sector and role type so people only receive what matters to them. Consent rules can differ between individual and corporate subscribers under PECR, so check how they apply to your lists.

Common recruitment marketing mistakes

  • Trying to appeal to everyone. Generic messages get lost against large job boards and national agencies.
  • Duplicate job adverts. Posting the same advert in many places without unique detail can weaken your own job pages in search.
  • Leaving expired jobs live. Candidates applying for filled roles have a poor experience and may not return.
  • Long application forms. Asking for too much information up front loses candidates, especially on mobile.
  • Measuring vanity numbers. Followers, impressions and raw applications can grow while placements stay flat.
  • Inconsistent consultant activity. LinkedIn works best with steady posting, not short bursts.

Building a simple quarterly plan

A practical approach is to set a quarterly focus. For example, one quarter might concentrate on improving sector service pages and launching job alerts, while the next adds a LinkedIn ads campaign aimed at a specific type of hiring manager. Each quarter, review which sources produced client briefs and placements, keep what worked and drop what did not. This keeps marketing manageable alongside billing and client work, and avoids trying everything at once.

Measuring what works

Track client enquiries, new briefs, candidate applications, shortlists and placements by source. Applications alone can mislead if they are low quality. Our GA4 setup guide explains how to track forms and applications. Give channels enough time to produce a fair picture, as SEO in particular tends to build gradually.

FAQs

What marketing works for a small recruitment agency?

A sensible starting point is a clear niche, separate client and candidate pages and consistent LinkedIn activity, supported by SEO and targeted ads as budget allows. Track placements by source, not just applications.

Is SEO worth it for recruitment agencies?

It can be, especially for niche agencies. Sector and location pages can attract clients, and well-structured job pages can attract candidates. Results tend to build gradually.

Do LinkedIn ads work for recruiters?

They can target decision-makers by job title, industry and company size, which suits client acquisition. Use a strong offer and track the cost of each enquiry carefully.

How do recruitment agencies attract candidates online?

Through searchable job pages, job alerts, LinkedIn posts and, for volume roles, Meta and Google ads with short mobile applications.

Should a recruitment agency have separate pages for clients and candidates?

Yes. The two audiences want different things, so separate pages and calls to action make the next step clearer for each.
