Restaurant Marketing in Thessaloniki: Filling Tables with Search, Social and Ads

Restaurant marketing in Thessaloniki explained: how restaurants, tavernas and cafes win diners with Google, a better website and ads, managed remotely by us.

Restaurant marketing in Thessaloniki is about making sure locals, students and visitors find your restaurant when they search for somewhere to eat, then giving them a clear reason to book or walk in. For most restaurants, tavernas, cafes and bars that means a complete Google Business Profile, steady reviews, a fast mobile website with menus and booking, and well-targeted social and search ads.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely. We have no office in Thessaloniki or anywhere in Greece. We support hospitality businesses that are comfortable working with a remote team through video calls, shared dashboards and written monthly reports, and we explain that before any work starts.

The Thessaloniki restaurant scene

Thessaloniki has a strong reputation for food, from traditional tavernas and mezedopoleia to bakeries, seafood restaurants along the waterfront and modern bistros in Ladadika and the city centre. It is also a major student city with a large university population, a busy port and a growing number of city-break visitors from across Europe and the Balkans.

That creates a large audience but also intense competition. Diners often decide where to eat on their phones, comparing maps results, photos, reviews and menus within minutes. A restaurant with an incomplete profile, few recent reviews or no visible menu can lose customers to a competitor a few streets away.

Google Maps and your Business Profile

For most restaurants, Google Maps is the single most important channel. When someone searches for a seafood restaurant, a taverna in Ladadika or brunch near Aristotelous Square, the maps results usually appear first. A strong profile includes the right primary category, accurate opening hours including holidays, a full menu, good photos of food and interior, booking links, attributes such as outdoor seating, and regular updates.

Reviews matter just as much. Ask happy guests to leave a review, respond to every review politely and keep information up to date. Our guide to Google Business Profile optimisation covers the setup in detail, and our local SEO guide explains how maps rankings work.

A website that turns searches into bookings

Many restaurants rely only on social media or a profile on a booking platform. A simple website of your own gives you more control. It should load fast on mobile, show the menu as text rather than a blurry image, include clear location and opening hours, offer online booking or a call button and highlight events, private dining and seasonal menus.

Greek and English versions help you reach both locals and visitors. Prism writes in English. Greek copy should come from your own team or a professional translator, and we build the site so each language has its own pages and search settings. Our guide to multilingual website design explains how this works. A small restaurant website with us typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes 3 to 5 weeks.

Social media and paid ads

Instagram is central for restaurants in Thessaloniki, and paid campaigns can extend your reach well beyond your followers. Meta Ads can target people within a set distance of the restaurant, visitors who have recently arrived in the city or audiences interested in specific cuisines. TikTok Ads can reach students and younger diners. Google Ads can capture people searching for a specific type of restaurant right now.

Prism does not film or edit video. Short clips and photos for social ads should come from your own team or a local photographer, and we plan the campaigns, audiences, budgets and testing around them. Ad spend is paid directly to the platforms, separately from our fee.

Common campaign ideas include promoting a new seasonal menu, filling quieter weekday evenings, advertising live music or events, pushing private dining and group bookings, and reaching visitors during busy tourist periods.

Types of venues we support

  • Tavernas and traditional restaurants: focused on maps visibility, reviews and visitor searches.
  • Seafood and waterfront restaurants: often targeting tourists and special occasions.
  • Cafes, bakeries and brunch spots: relying on daily foot traffic, Instagram and local searches.
  • Bars and cocktail venues: promoting events, late-night offers and group bookings. Alcohol advertising has its own platform policies and local rules, so check these with your adviser.
  • Restaurant groups: several venues needing consistent profiles, location pages and campaign management.

Timelines and seasonality

Profile and review improvements can make a difference within weeks. Paid campaigns can bring bookings within days of launch, though they need a few weeks of testing to become efficient. SEO and paid results typically build over 3 to 6 months. Plan around the city's rhythm: the university year, summer when many locals leave for the coast, busy festival and trade fair periods, and holiday peaks such as Christmas and Easter.

  1. Month one: audit of your profile, reviews, website and social accounts, plus a simple plan.
  2. Month two: profile and website fixes, review routine and first campaigns.
  3. Month three onwards: monthly reporting, seasonal campaigns and steady improvements.

Measuring what works

Good restaurant marketing should be measured, not guessed. We track the actions that matter most: calls from your Google Business Profile, direction requests, website booking clicks, completed online bookings, menu views and the cost of each booking from paid campaigns. Each month you receive a short report in plain English showing what changed, what worked and what we plan to do next.

Some results are harder to measure, such as a guest who saw an Instagram ad and walked in a week later. Simple habits help, such as asking new guests how they found you or using a specific offer code in each campaign. Over a few months these signals build a clear picture of which channels deserve more budget and which should be paused.

Working with a remote marketing team

Restaurant marketing works well remotely because most of it happens online. We work through access to your profiles and ad accounts, which stay in your name. Photos, videos, menu updates and in-person review requests stay with your team, and we guide you on what works best. Greece is two hours ahead of the UK, so calls fit easily before or after service.
