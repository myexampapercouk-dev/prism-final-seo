Google Business Profile Optimisation for UK Businesses

Google Business Profile optimisation for UK firms: pick the right categories, post weekly, add real photos, earn and answer reviews, keep details accurate.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Your primary category is the single biggest setting on your profile, so choose the most specific one that describes what you actually do.
  • Reviews, photos and posts work best as a steady weekly habit rather than a one-off burst before a busy season.
  • A profile only performs if it agrees with your website and directory listings on name, address, phone number and opening hours.

Google Business Profile optimisation for UK businesses comes down to six things: verify the listing, choose the most specific primary category, complete every field, add genuine photos, earn and reply to reviews, and keep posting and updating. Do those consistently and your profile gives Google clear, trusted signals for the local pack and Maps, which is where most nearby customers decide who to call.

This guide goes deeper on the profile itself. If you want the wider picture, including local landing pages, citations and links, read our local SEO guide for UK businesses first, then come back here to work through the profile field by field.

Why the profile matters more than most website pages

For searches such as "plumber near me" or "Italian restaurant Leeds", the map results usually sit above the standard organic listings. They read the star rating, glance at photos, check the opening hours and tap to call or get directions. That makes the profile a shop window in its own right, not just a supporting asset for your site.

Google has said publicly that local ranking is driven mainly by relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot change where a searcher is standing, but you can influence relevance (categories, services, description) and prominence (reviews, links, mentions and the strength of your website). The rest of this guide is about those two levers.

Setting up and verifying your profile properly

Start at the Google Business Profile manager and search for your business name first, because a listing may already exist from a customer suggestion or a previous owner. Claim that listing rather than creating a duplicate. Duplicates split reviews and confuse Google about which version is correct.

Verification methods vary by business and change over time. Google may offer a video recording, a phone or text code, email or a postcard. Follow whatever it offers and avoid editing key details while verification is pending, as changes can restart the process.

Storefront or service-area business

If customers visit you, show your address. If you travel to customers, such as electricians, mobile groomers or cleaners, set up as a service-area business, hide the address and list the towns or postcode districts you cover. Google guidelines say you should not list an address customers cannot visit, and virtual offices are a common cause of suspended listings.

Business name rules

Use your real-world trading name, as it appears on signage and paperwork. Adding keywords or towns to the name ("Smith Plumbing Manchester Emergency Plumber") breaks the guidelines and can lead to suspension once a competitor reports it.

Choosing categories, services and attributes

Your primary category tells Google what you are. Pick the most specific option available: "Wedding photographer" beats a generic "Photographer" if weddings are your main work. You can add secondary categories for genuine extra services, but each one should reflect something you really offer, not a keyword you hope to rank for.

Photos and videos that actually help

Real photos build trust far more than stock images. Upload a clear logo, a cover image, the outside of your premises (so people recognise it when they arrive), the inside, your team at work without names attached, and examples of your products or finished jobs. Restaurants and hotels should add menu items, rooms and the view from the table; trades should show before-and-after work.

Add a few new images each month rather than fifty at once. Fresh uploads keep the listing current, and customers can also add their own photos, so check those regularly and report anything offensive or irrelevant.

Google Posts: what to publish each week

Posts appear on your profile and sometimes in search results. They expire from prominent view after a while, so a weekly rhythm works well. Useful post types for UK businesses include:

  • Offers: seasonal deals, such as a January boiler service price or a Mother's Day set menu.
  • Updates: new services, recent projects, changes to hours over bank holidays.
  • Events: open days, tastings, workshops or school-holiday activities.
  • Products: featured items with a link to the relevant page on your site.

Every post should have a good photo and a clear button that leads to a relevant page, not just your homepage. Tag those links with UTM parameters so you can see the traffic in Google Analytics; our GA4 setup guide explains how to make sure that data is recorded correctly.

Reviews: earning them and replying well

Review count, rating and recency all influence how customers judge you, and they are widely believed to affect prominence too. The fix is not a tactic but a process: ask every happy customer, at the right moment, with a direct link.

  • Create your short review link from the profile dashboard and add it to receipts, follow-up emails and a QR code at the till.
  • Ask when the customer is most satisfied: after a job is signed off, after a meal, after delivery arrives.
  • Never offer discounts or gifts for reviews and never filter so only happy customers are asked. That breaks Google policy, and the Competition and Markets Authority has taken a firm line on fake and incentivised reviews in the UK.
  • Reply to every review within a few days. Thank positive reviewers by mentioning what they bought or had done. For negative reviews, stay calm, acknowledge the issue, and move the detail offline.

Questions, messages and bookings

Features on the profile change regularly, and Google has removed and reshaped tools such as chat and Q&A over the years. Where a questions section is shown, seed it with genuine common questions (parking, accessibility, whether you take card payments) and answer them yourself so the first answer is accurate. Monitor it, because anyone can post an answer.

Keep the profile consistent with your website

Google cross-checks your profile against your website and other listings. The name, address and phone number should match exactly across your site footer, contact page, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yell, Facebook and any industry directories. Add LocalBusiness structured data to your site so the details are machine readable. If you have several branches, give each its own location page and link each profile to its page.

A slow or confusing website also undermines the profile, because people who tap through and bounce do not convert. Our guides on website speed optimisation and landing page design cover the pages your profile points to.

Google Business Profile optimisation checklist

  • Claim and verify the listing; remove or merge duplicates.
  • Use your real trading name with no added keywords.
  • Set the most specific primary category and only genuine secondary ones.
  • Fill in services, products, attributes and a 750-character description.
  • Add regular and bank holiday hours, phone, website and booking links.
  • Upload real photos monthly: exterior, interior, work, products.
  • Publish one Google Post a week with a tracked link.
  • Send a review link to every customer and reply to every review.
  • Check the profile weekly for suggested edits from the public.
  • Match name, address and phone across your site and main directories.

FAQs

What is Google Business Profile optimisation for UK businesses?

It is the process of completing and maintaining your free Google listing so it appears in the map pack and Maps for relevant local searches. It covers categories, services, photos, posts, reviews and keeping your details consistent everywhere.

How long does it take to see results from a better profile?

Simple fixes such as the right primary category can make a difference within weeks, but reviews and prominence build over months. As with most SEO work, plan on three to six months before judging the overall trend.

Can I rank in a town where I have no address?

It is hard. Distance is a strong factor, so a service-area business usually ranks best near its verified location. Location pages on your website and links from local organisations can help you appear for searches that name other towns.

Should I add keywords to my business name?

No. It breaks Google guidelines and can lead to suspension if reported. Use your real trading name and put relevance into your categories, services and description instead.

How do I get rid of a fake or unfair review?

You can flag a review that breaks Google policy, for example spam, conflicts of interest or offensive content. Google decides whether to remove it. Reviews that are simply negative usually stay, so a calm public reply is your best response.

Do Google Posts help rankings?

Their direct ranking effect is debated. Their value is in showing customers that you are active, promoting offers and sending tracked traffic to the right page, which is reason enough to keep posting weekly.
