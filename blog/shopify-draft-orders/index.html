Shopify Draft Orders: A Practical Guide for Store Owners

How Shopify draft orders work, when to use them for phone, custom and trade orders, and how to send invoices, apply discounts and keep records tidy.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Draft orders let you create an order in the Shopify admin on a customer's behalf, then send an invoice or mark it as paid.
  • They suit phone and email orders, custom pricing, trade customers and orders that need manual adjustments.
  • Check stock, taxes and shipping carefully, because draft orders give staff more freedom than the normal checkout.

Most Shopify orders come through the online checkout: a customer adds products to their basket, enters their details and pays. But not every sale fits that pattern. A customer might phone in an order, ask for a custom quote or want to pay by bank transfer. For these situations, Shopify offers draft orders.

This guide explains what draft orders are, when they are useful, how to create one and what to watch out for so your records, stock and customer communication stay accurate.

What is a draft order?

A draft order is an order that you create in the Shopify admin, rather than one the customer places through your storefront. You choose the products, the customer, the shipping and any discounts, then either send the customer an invoice so they can pay online or record the payment yourself.

Until it is paid or marked as paid, a draft order stays in the Drafts section of your Orders area. Once completed, it becomes a normal order and appears alongside your other orders for fulfilment and reporting.

When draft orders are useful

Draft orders give you flexibility for sales that do not suit the standard checkout. Typical uses include:

  • Phone and email orders. A customer contacts you directly and you take the order on their behalf.
  • Custom or bespoke items. You can add custom line items with your own description and price for products that are not listed in your store.
  • Quotes. You can build an order and send it as an invoice for the customer to review and pay when ready.
  • Trade or wholesale customers. You can apply special pricing or discounts for particular customers.
  • Replacement orders. You can create an order to send a replacement item, applying a discount so the customer is not charged again.
  • Manual payments. Where you have agreed a payment method outside the online checkout, you can record the order and mark it as paid or payment pending.

How to create a draft order

The Shopify admin changes from time to time, but the basic steps are:

  1. Go to Orders and choose to create an order.
  2. Search for and add products. You can change quantities and select variants.
  3. Add custom items if needed, with a name, price and quantity, and choose whether they need shipping and are taxable.
  4. Find an existing customer or create a new one. The customer's details are used for the invoice and the order.
  5. Add discounts, either to individual items or to the whole order.
  6. Choose shipping. You can select one of your shipping rates or add a custom rate.
  7. Check taxes are being calculated as you expect.
  8. Add notes or tags if useful for your team.
  9. Decide how to collect payment: send an invoice, mark as paid or mark as payment pending.

You can save the draft at any point and return to it later.

Sending an invoice

When you send an invoice from a draft order, the customer receives an email with a link to a secure checkout page showing the items, prices and totals you set. They complete payment there using your normal payment methods.

You can add a custom message to the invoice email, which is useful for explaining the order or confirming anything you discussed. The email template itself can be edited in your notification settings, so it matches the tone and branding of your other emails.

Once the customer pays, the draft becomes a regular order. If they do not pay, the draft stays open, and you can resend the invoice or follow up.

Marking as paid or payment pending

If the customer has already paid in another way, for example by card over the phone through a method you have set up, you can mark the order as paid. If you have agreed that they will pay later, such as on account or by bank transfer, you can mark it as payment pending and record the payment when it arrives.

Take care with these options. Marking an order as paid does not take any money from the customer; it simply records that payment has been received. Make sure your bookkeeping and the actual payment match. Taking card details over the phone also brings security obligations, so check what applies to your setup before doing so.

Discounts and custom pricing

Draft orders let you apply discounts by amount or percentage, either to single line items or the whole order. You can also apply some existing discount codes. This flexibility is useful, but it relies on staff using it consistently.

It is worth agreeing simple internal rules: who can create draft orders, what level of discount is acceptable without approval and how discounts are explained to customers. Our guide to Shopify staff accounts and permissions explains how to control who can create and edit orders.

If you sell to trade customers regularly, draft orders may be only part of the picture. Depending on your Shopify plan, there may be built-in options for business-to-business selling that suit repeat trade customers better.

Stock and inventory

Creating a draft order does not always reserve stock in the way a completed order does. While a draft is open, the items may still be available to other customers. If stock is limited, you may want to reserve items in the draft, if that option is available, or complete the order promptly.

When a draft order is completed, inventory is updated as with a normal order. Check your stock levels after creating large or unusual orders. Our guide to Shopify inventory management covers how stock is tracked across locations.

Taxes and shipping

Shopify calculates taxes on draft orders based on your tax settings and the customer's address, as it would at checkout. If you add custom items, make sure you set whether they are taxable. For orders shipping outside the UK, check that taxes and duties are being handled correctly, as rules can be complex. If you are unsure how VAT applies to a particular order, it is sensible to check with an accountant or the guidance from HMRC.

For shipping, you can use your normal rates or add a custom rate. Custom rates are useful for unusual orders, such as large items or collections, but make sure the price reflects the real cost.

Keeping draft orders organised

Open drafts can build up over time, especially if you use them for quotes. A cluttered drafts list makes it harder to see what needs attention. A few habits help:

  • Use tags to mark the purpose of each draft, such as quote, phone order or replacement.
  • Add internal notes explaining any special pricing or arrangements.
  • Review open drafts regularly and follow up or delete those that are no longer needed.
  • Make sure the customer record is correct, so order history and marketing preferences stay accurate.

Customer consent and communications

When you create a new customer through a draft order, you may be asked about their marketing preferences. Only record consent if the customer has actually given it. In the UK, marketing communications are covered by data protection and electronic marketing rules, and the ICO provides guidance on what is required. If you are unsure, take advice and default to not subscribing people.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Marking an order as paid before the money has actually arrived.
  • Forgetting to set custom items as needing shipping, so no shipping charge is applied.
  • Applying discounts inconsistently between staff members.
  • Leaving drafts open so long that prices or stock levels have changed.
  • Entering customer details incorrectly, leading to delivery problems.

Draft orders also bypass some parts of your normal checkout experience, so review your Shopify checkout settings to understand which settings still apply when customers pay through an invoice.

FAQs

Can customers see draft orders?

Customers do not see a draft order until you send them an invoice. Once sent, they can view the items and totals through the invoice link and complete payment.

Can I edit a draft order after sending the invoice?

You can usually edit an open draft order and resend the invoice. Make sure the customer uses the latest invoice link so they see the correct details.

Do draft orders count in my sales reports?

Draft orders are not counted as sales until they are completed. Once paid or marked as paid, they become normal orders and appear in your reports.

Can I add products that are not in my store?

Yes. Custom line items let you add items with your own description and price, which is useful for bespoke work or one-off charges.

Should every phone order go through draft orders?

Draft orders are one option. Depending on your setup, Shopify POS or other tools may suit some in-person or phone sales better. Choose the approach that keeps your records accurate and simple for your team.
