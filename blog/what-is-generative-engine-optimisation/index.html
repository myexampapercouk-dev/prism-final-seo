What Is Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)? A Guide for UK Businesses

Generative engine optimisation (GEO) explained: how ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity choose sources, and practical steps to get your business cited.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • GEO is the work of making your business easy for AI assistants to understand, trust and cite in their answers.
  • It builds on SEO foundations: clear pages, consistent facts, structured data and authority from trusted websites.
  • Start with your most important pages: answer questions directly, state facts plainly and make sure they match everywhere online.

Generative engine optimisation (GEO) is the practice of making your business visible and cited in answers produced by AI tools such as ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Copilot and Perplexity. Where traditional SEO aims for a high position in a list of links, GEO aims for your brand, page or facts to be included in the answer itself.

For UK businesses, this matters because more people now ask an assistant "who are the best Shopify agencies for a small fashion brand?" or "how much does a small business website cost?" instead of scrolling through search results. This guide explains how AI answers are put together and what you can do, practically, to be part of them.

What is generative engine optimisation?

A generative engine is any system that writes an answer rather than simply listing pages. Some, like Google AI Overviews and Perplexity, search the web in real time and cite their sources. Others answer partly from what they learned during training and partly from live search. Either way, they need sources they can understand and trust.

GEO is about becoming one of those sources. In practice it overlaps heavily with good SEO, which is why we treat it as one discipline on our SEO and GEO service. The difference is mostly one of emphasis: GEO puts extra weight on clarity, consistency of facts and being mentioned by other trusted sites.

How AI assistants choose which sources to cite

No provider publishes a full recipe, and the systems change often, so be cautious of anyone claiming certainty. What is broadly understood is this:

  • Retrieval comes first. Tools that search the web usually run one or more searches, pull back candidate pages and then write an answer from them. If your page is not found and crawled, it cannot be cited.
  • Clear passages win. Assistants quote or summarise passages that directly answer the question. A paragraph that states the answer plainly is easier to use than one buried in marketing language.
  • Consistency builds confidence. If your website, Google Business Profile, directories and press coverage all describe your business the same way, the facts are easier to trust.
  • Authority still matters. Pages and brands mentioned on reputable websites are more likely to be retrieved and used.

GEO vs SEO: what is the difference?

AreaTraditional SEO focusExtra GEO emphasis
GoalRank highly in search resultsBe included and cited in AI-written answers
ContentPages targeting keywordsDirect answers to full questions, in clear passages
FactsAccurate on your own siteIdentical across your site, profiles and third-party mentions
TechnicalCrawlable, fast, indexedAlso accessible to AI crawlers you choose to allow; clean structured data
AuthorityLinks from relevant sitesLinks plus unlinked brand mentions, reviews and expert commentary
MeasurementRankings, clicks, conversionsAlso brand mentions in AI answers and referral traffic from AI tools

The overlap is large. A site with weak SEO rarely does well in AI answers, which is why GEO should be built on top of solid SEO rather than instead of it.

Practical GEO steps for UK businesses

1. Answer the question in the first two lines

For every important page, write an opening that answers the main question directly. If the page is about pricing, say what things cost. If it is about a service, say what it is, who it is for and where you provide it. Then add depth below.

2. State your key facts plainly and consistently

Your business name, what you do, the areas you serve, your contact details and, if you publish them, your prices should read the same on your website, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn and directory listings. Contradictions create doubt.

3. Use structured data

Schema markup such as Organization, Service, FAQPage, Article and BreadcrumbList helps machines understand what a page is about. It is not a magic switch, but it removes ambiguity. Only mark up what is genuinely on the page.

4. Write content that covers full questions

People ask assistants longer, more specific questions than they type into Google. Content that covers the follow-up questions too, such as cost, timescales, risks and alternatives, is more useful as a source. Good examples are comparison guides like Shopify vs WooCommerce for UK businesses and pricing explainers like how much SEO costs in the UK.

5. Earn mentions on trusted sites

Coverage in industry publications, local news, trade associations and respected directories helps both rankings and AI visibility. This is where digital PR and content pays off. Unlinked mentions still help assistants learn who you are.

6. Check your technical access

Make sure important pages are indexed, load quickly and are not hidden behind scripts that crawlers struggle with. Review your robots.txt to decide which AI crawlers you allow. Some businesses also publish an llms.txt file that summarises key pages for AI tools; it is a lightweight, emerging convention rather than a guaranteed ranking factor.

7. Keep content current

Out-of-date prices, services or contact details are a common reason for wrong answers about a business. Review key pages regularly and update the date when you make real changes.

How to measure GEO

Measurement is still developing. Useful signals include:

  • Referral traffic from AI tools in GA4 (for example from chatgpt.com or perplexity.ai).
  • Regular manual checks: ask the main assistants the questions your buyers ask and note whether, and how, your business appears.
  • Branded search growth in Search Console, which often rises when people discover you elsewhere.
  • Enquiries where customers say they found you through an AI tool. Add it as an option on your contact form.

Common GEO mistakes

  • Chasing tricks. Hidden text or prompts aimed at AI systems are unreliable and can damage trust.
  • Thin AI-written pages. Publishing lots of generic content rarely helps. Assistants already know generic information; they need specific, reliable sources.
  • Inconsistent facts. Different phone numbers, service lists or prices across the web confuse both people and machines.
  • Ignoring the basics. Slow, poorly structured sites are less likely to be retrieved at all.

An example: a GEO-ready service page

Imagine a page for a bookkeeping firm. A typical version opens with a slogan, then a paragraph about passion and excellence, then a list of services. An assistant asked "how much does a bookkeeper cost for a small limited company in the UK?" has little to work with.

A GEO-ready version opens with two sentences that say what the firm does, for whom and where, followed by a short section on how pricing works, a table of what is included at each level, a few plain-English answers to common questions and clear contact details. The same facts appear on the firm's Google Business Profile and directory listings. Nothing about it is a trick; it is simply easier to understand and quote.

Which businesses benefit most?

Any business whose customers research before buying can benefit, but some see it sooner. Software companies are frequently compared in AI answers, so SaaS businesses should prioritise comparison and use-case content. Professional services, including financial advisers, benefit from clear, factual explainers. Online retailers benefit when buying guides and product information are accurate and well structured.

Does GEO work for local businesses?

Yes. When someone asks an assistant for a recommendation nearby, the answer often draws on map listings, reviews and local websites. A complete Google Business Profile, consistent details across directories and clear location information on your website all help. For local businesses, GEO is mostly good local SEO done thoroughly.

Getting started

Begin with an honest review of your five most important pages and your business listings. Are the facts consistent, are the answers clear, and is the site technically sound? FAQs

What is generative engine optimisation in simple terms?

It is the work of making your business easy for AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews to find, understand and cite when they answer questions.

Is GEO replacing SEO?

No. GEO builds on SEO. The same foundations of crawlable pages, clear content and authority support both, and search engines remain a major source of visitors.

Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT answers?

Organic citations in AI answers cannot be bought. Advertising options are emerging on some platforms, but being cited as a source depends on your content and reputation.

Does structured data help with AI search?

It helps machines interpret your pages accurately, which supports both search and AI answers. It works best alongside clear visible content, not as a substitute.

How long does GEO take?

Changes to your own pages can be picked up quickly by tools that search live, but building authority through mentions and links takes months, much like SEO.
