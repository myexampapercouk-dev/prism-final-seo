Distillery Marketing in the UK: How Craft Distillers Grow Direct Sales, Tours and Trade

Distillery marketing in the UK works best with a strong brand story, a compliant online shop, bookable tours, local search, gift planning and email to fans.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • A craft distillery sells a story as much as a spirit, so a clear brand, honest production details and good photography do a lot of the selling.
  • Direct sales through your own shop, tours and tasting experiences usually earn better margins than wholesale, but online alcohol sales need age checks and careful wording.
  • Plan around gifting peaks such as Christmas and Father's Day, and keep in touch with fans through consented email so each release has a ready audience.

Distillery marketing in the UK is about turning interest in your spirit into direct sales, tour bookings and trade listings, while staying within the rules that apply to alcohol. The craft gin, whisky, rum and vodka producers that do well usually combine a distinctive brand, a well built online shop, bookable visitor experiences, local search visibility and a loyal email list.

This guide is for small and mid sized distilleries, cask and bottling brands, rum and gin makers selling online, and producers who open their doors for tours and tastings. We cover how buyers find spirits, what your website needs, how to market experiences, how paid campaigns work for alcohol and how to plan the year around gifting seasons.

How people discover a craft spirit

Most buyers come to a craft spirit in one of a few ways. Some search for a type of drink, such as a local gin, a single malt from a particular region or a spiced rum as a present. Some want an experience, such as a gin school, a distillery tour or a tasting evening near them. Others discover a bottle in a bar, farm shop or deli and then look it up to buy again.

That last group matters. If a customer cannot find your bottle online quickly after tasting it somewhere, the sale often goes to a better known brand.

Distillery marketing UK: build a brand people remember

Spirits are a crowded shelf. What sets a small distillery apart is usually its story: where it is based, the botanicals or grain it uses, the people and methods behind each batch and the character of the drink itself. Write this down plainly before you spend on advertising. A clear brand position makes every label, page and post more consistent, and our brand identity design guide explains how to shape it.

Be careful with claims. Only describe production methods, provenance and awards you can back up, and avoid suggesting your drink has health benefits, improves mood or helps social or sexual success. Those themes are restricted in alcohol marketing. Where a brand needs a fresh direction, our brand strategy service can help you set out positioning, tone and audience before any new design work starts.

Selling spirits online in the UK

A direct online shop lets you keep more margin and own the customer relationship. It also brings extra responsibilities. You need the right licences in place for retail sales, a clear age confirmation step, wording in your terms that sales are only to adults, and a delivery service that checks age on arrival where required. Keep statements on your website general and check current official guidance from your licensing authority and HMRC on duty and licensing before you launch.

Shipping glass is another challenge. Packaging needs to protect bottles, and not every courier service accepts alcohol. Our guide to food and drink ecommerce covers packaging, delivery choices and practical set up in more detail. Strong product photos make a real difference for spirits, and our guide to ecommerce product photography shares simple ways to show bottles, colour and labels clearly.

A typical small website costs £1,000 to £2,000, while an e-commerce site with age confirmation, gift options and booking costs £1,500 to £3,500 depending on features. E-commerce builds typically take five to twelve weeks, so start well before the autumn gifting rush. You can see our ongoing marketing retainers on the pricing page.

Marketing tours, tastings and gin schools

Visitor experiences often become a major income stream for a distillery. They also build loyal fans who go on to buy bottles for years. Treat each experience as a product with its own page, clear dates, simple pricing and online booking.

Local search for distilleries and visitor centres

People planning a day out often search for a distillery tour or gin experience plus a town or region. Your Google Business Profile is usually the first thing they see, so choose accurate categories, add opening hours for the shop and tours, upload photos of the stills, bar and tasting room and keep holiday hours up to date. Our Google Business Profile guide covers the setup step by step.

Reviews influence visitor bookings heavily. Ask happy guests for a review after their visit and reply to each one politely. If you are near popular attractions, walking routes or holiday areas, mention them naturally on your experience pages so visitors planning a trip can find you.

Paid campaigns for spirits brands

Alcohol advertising is restricted on most platforms. Google and Meta both allow alcohol ads in the UK under specific policies, including targeting adults only and avoiding certain themes. The CAP Code also sets rules for alcohol marketing in non broadcast media, and the Portman Group code covers naming, packaging and promotion. Read the current versions of these rules before you launch, and remember that compliance approval rests with you as the producer.

Within those limits, paid search can work for high intent searches such as gin school gift voucher, distillery tour plus a region or a specific bottle name. Shopping ads can help product sales, and short paid social campaigns can support new releases and Christmas gifting. Start small, track sales and bookings as conversions and increase spend only when the numbers justify it. Ad spend is always paid separately from any management fee.

Social media and content

Distilleries have plenty of visual material: copper stills, botanicals, cask filling, bottling days and the people behind the spirit. Short videos of production, behind the scenes moments and simple cocktail serves suit social platforms well. Keep content aimed at adults, avoid showing excessive drinking and do not link alcohol with driving, sport performance or daring behaviour.

Useful written content can rank too. Guides to simple serves, food pairings, how your spirit is made and what different styles taste like help new drinkers choose with confidence. Similar ideas work for other drinks producers, and our guides to brewery marketing and vineyard and winery marketing cover approaches that translate well to spirits.

Wholesale, stockists and trade

Most distilleries sell through a mix of direct, trade and retail channels. A clear trade page with your range, a simple enquiry form and downloadable product information makes life easier for bar managers and shop buyers.

Local partnerships often work well: cocktail evenings with nearby bars, bundles with local food producers or gift hampers with farm shops and delis.

Seasonal planning for distillers

Spirits sales tend to peak in the weeks before Christmas, with smaller peaks around Father's Day, Burns Night for whisky, summer gin season and bank holiday weekends for tours. Plan content, emails and campaigns around these dates and make sure gift sets and vouchers are live early. Set clear last order dates for Christmas delivery and display them prominently.

Email and loyal fans

A craft distillery's best customers often buy every new release. An email list lets you tell them first about limited editions, new experiences and seasonal sets. Collect consent at checkout, on tours and at events, and keep messages short and useful. Our guide to email list building covers practical ways to grow your list. Keep all marketing within UK GDPR and PECR rules and check current ICO guidance on consent before you start.

Track which channels bring in orders, bookings and trade enquiries, and give SEO time to work, since it typically takes three to six months to show clear results. We help drinks producers with brand, websites, online shops, local SEO and paid campaigns.

FAQs

What is the best approach to distillery marketing in the UK?

Combine a clear brand story with a well built online shop, bookable tours and tastings, a complete Google Business Profile, seasonal gifting campaigns and regular emails to fans who have given consent.

Can a distillery sell spirits online in the UK?

Yes, with the right licences, an age confirmation step and a delivery service that checks age where needed. Check current official guidance on licensing and duty before you launch.

Can I run Google or Meta ads for gin or whisky?

Both platforms allow alcohol ads in the UK under their alcohol policies, usually with adult only targeting. Read the current policies and the CAP Code first, as approval rests with you.

How do I market distillery tours?

Give each experience its own page with dates, inclusions, age rules and online booking. Keep your Google Business Profile accurate, collect reviews and sell gift vouchers ahead of Christmas.

How much does a distillery website cost?

A small website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000. An online shop with age confirmation, gift sets and experience booking usually costs £1,500 to £3,500 depending on features.

Should a small distillery focus on wholesale or direct sales?

Most use both. Direct sales and experiences usually earn better margins and build loyalty, while stockists and bars bring discovery. A clear where to buy page helps each support the other.
