Children's Entertainer Marketing in the UK: How to Fill Your Weekend Diary

Children's entertainer marketing in the UK works best with a clear website, local SEO, plenty of reviews, easy booking for parents and careful use of photos.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Parents book entertainers they trust, so reviews, clear safety information and an obvious price guide matter more than clever slogans.
  • Separate pages for each party package, age group and area you cover help you appear in local searches and answer questions before parents ask.
  • Photos and videos of children need parental consent and careful handling, so build privacy into your marketing from the start.

Children's entertainer marketing in the UK is about making it easy for busy parents to trust you, check you are available and book you for a party. The entertainers with full diaries usually have a simple website with clear packages and prices, a strong Google Business Profile, plenty of reviews from other parents, quick replies to enquiries and good relationships with venues, schools and nurseries.

This guide is for party entertainers, magicians, clowns, puppet shows, character performers, party DJs for children, science and craft party providers, and small entertainment companies with several performers. We cover how parents search, your website, local SEO, reviews, social media, partnerships, paid ads and the extra care needed when marketing anything involving children.

How parents find a children's entertainer

Most bookings start with a search a few weeks or months before a birthday. Parents type phrases such as children's entertainer near me, kids party entertainer plus their town, magician for a fifth birthday, or princess party entertainer. Many also ask in local parent groups and school chats, then look up the names that are recommended.

Whichever route they take, parents check the same things: what the party includes, what it costs, whether you suit their child's age, whether you are available on the date and whether other parents rate you. Your marketing should answer those questions quickly and clearly.

Children's entertainer marketing UK: a website parents trust

Your website does a lot of the selling while you are performing. It should feel friendly and fun but also organised and professional, because parents are trusting you with a room full of children. Keep it fast on mobile, since most parents will browse on their phones.

Make enquiries quick and simple

Parents often contact several entertainers at once and book whoever replies first with clear information. A short enquiry form asking for the date, time, location, the child's age, number of children and the package they like is usually enough. Our guide to website contact forms covers how to keep forms short and effective.

Reply as quickly as you can, ideally the same day, with availability, the price and what happens next. A simple deposit and confirmation process reduces last minute cancellations. Put your cancellation and rescheduling policy in writing, in plain English, so there are no surprises.

Local SEO for party entertainers

Most bookings are local, so showing up in local search results is essential. Set up a Google Business Profile as a service area business if you travel to parties rather than working from premises. Choose an accurate category, list the areas you cover, add photos of your props and set up, and post regularly about packages and seasonal themes. Our Google Business Profile guide explains each step.

On your website, create genuine area pages for the main towns you serve, mentioning venues you regularly perform at and the types of party you do there. Avoid copying the same page and only swapping the town name. Consistent listings across reputable directories also help, as explained in our local citations guide.

Reviews from parents

Reviews are often the deciding factor. After each party, send a short thank you message with a direct link to leave a Google review. Reply to every review, thanking the family and mentioning the type of party where it feels natural. Our guide on how to get more Google reviews covers simple systems for asking.

Do not offer incentives in exchange for positive reviews, and never post reviews written by yourself or friends. Genuine feedback, including the occasional mixed review handled politely, builds more trust than a page of perfect scores.

Photos, videos and children's privacy

Party photos and videos are powerful marketing, but anything involving children needs extra care. Only share images where you have clear consent from parents or guardians, and many entertainers avoid showing children's faces altogether by focusing on props, decorations, the performer and wide shots. Never share names, schools or locations that could identify a child.

Content ideaLower risk approach
Party highlightsWide shots, backs of heads, props and set up
Show trailersFilmed at a rehearsal or with consenting family members

Store any photos and personal details securely and delete what you no longer need. Keep your approach in line with UK GDPR and check current ICO guidance, including its guidance on children's data. If in doubt, take advice.

Social media for entertainers

Facebook and Instagram suit party entertainers well, because parents use them to find local services and share recommendations. Short videos of a magic trick, a balloon model or a seasonal theme can be engaging without showing children. Post your availability for popular weekends, new themes and school holiday events. Always link back to your packages and booking page.

Local parent groups can be helpful, but respect each group's rules on promotion. Being genuinely useful, such as sharing party planning tips, tends to work better than repeated adverts.

Plan content around the calendar. Halloween, Christmas, Easter and the summer holidays all bring themed parties and events, and parents start booking popular dates early. A simple posting plan that promotes each season a couple of months ahead helps you fill weekends before they are taken by someone else. Batch your filming and photos on a quiet weekday so you are not scrambling for content during the busy season.

Partnerships with venues, schools and nurseries

Community halls, soft play centres, cafes with party rooms, family pubs and hotels often host children's parties and can recommend entertainers. Schools and nurseries may book entertainers for summer fairs, end of term events and Christmas parties. A short page for venues and organisations explaining your shows for larger groups, your requirements for space and power, and the checks and insurance you hold makes it easy for them to recommend or book you.

Our guide to soft play centre marketing shows how family venues think about parties, which can help you pitch a partnership that benefits both sides.

Paid ads for children's entertainers

Paid search can bring bookings for searches such as children's entertainer plus your town, especially in busy seasons. Keep targeting tight to the areas you travel to, focus on high intent phrases and track enquiries rather than clicks. Our guide to Google Ads for small businesses explains a lean setup.

Paid social can promote seasonal themes and school holiday shows to parents in your area. Platforms have rules about ads aimed at or featuring children, and the advertising codes include specific protections for children, so target parents rather than children and keep messages responsible. Ad spend is always paid separately from any management fee.

Your website and next steps

A small website for a children's entertainer typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks to build. SEO and paid results typically take three to six months to build, so start before your busiest season if you can. FAQs

What is the best approach to children's entertainer marketing in the UK?

Build a clear, mobile friendly website with packages and prices, keep a strong Google Business Profile, collect genuine reviews from parents, reply to enquiries quickly and build relationships with local venues, schools and nurseries.

Should a children's entertainer show prices online?

Usually yes. Parents compare several entertainers at once, and clear package prices or starting from prices help them decide quickly and reduce enquiries that will not go ahead.

Can I post photos from children's parties on social media?

Only with clear consent from parents or guardians. Many entertainers avoid showing children's faces at all and focus on props, set up and the performer instead.

How do children's entertainers get more reviews?

Send a short thank you message after every party with a direct review link, reply to every review and make asking part of your routine. Never offer incentives for positive reviews.

Do Google Ads work for party entertainers?

They can, particularly for local searches in busy seasons. Keep targeting tight to the areas you cover and track enquiries and bookings to judge results.

How much does a website for a children's entertainer cost?

A small website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks to build, depending on the number of pages and features.
