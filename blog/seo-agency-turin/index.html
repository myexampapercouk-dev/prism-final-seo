Search in Turin and Piedmont

Turin is the capital of Piedmont and one of Italy's main industrial and cultural centres. Its economy includes automotive and engineering firms and their suppliers, aerospace, design, food and wine producers, a large university and polytechnic, tourism and a broad base of professional and trade services. The wider region adds wine country in the Langhe and Roero, mountain resorts and many family-owned manufacturers.

This gives Turin search a particular shape. Many valuable searches are specific and technical, such as a component, a material or a specialist service, rather than broad consumer terms. Local businesses, from lawyers to restaurants, compete mainly within the city and nearby towns. And food, wine and tourism brands often target buyers across Italy and abroad. Good SEO starts by identifying which of these groups you belong to.

SEO agency Turin: what the work should include

SEO covers several connected jobs. An agency should be clear about which it handles and in what order. For most Turin businesses, the work falls into five areas.

  • Technical SEO. Crawling, indexing, site speed, mobile usability, redirects, duplicate content and structured data. Our technical SEO audit checklist shows what a thorough audit covers.
  • Keyword research in Italian. Finding the phrases your customers actually use, including regional terms and the way business buyers describe technical products.
  • On-page optimisation. Titles, headings, page structure and internal links that match each search.
  • Local SEO. Google Business Profile, consistent contact details and reviews for businesses that serve Turin customers in person.
  • Content and links. Helpful pages and guides, plus links from relevant Italian trade associations, suppliers, partners and publications. Links should be earned through genuine relationships and useful content, never bought in bulk.

An agency that only sends a ranking report each month, or only publishes blog posts, is covering part of the job. Ask for a written plan that names which of these areas will get attention first and why, and how you will know whether each piece of work has helped.

Common SEO problems on Italian business websites

When we audit sites for Italian businesses, a handful of issues come up again and again. None of them is unique to Turin, but they are worth checking before investing in new content.

  • Everything on one page. Many company sites list every product line or service on a single page, which makes it hard to rank for any of them. Separate pages for each main offer usually work better.
  • Machine translated English versions. An English section that has been translated automatically can read poorly and may create duplicate or thin pages. It is better to have a smaller set of well written English pages than a full copy of weak ones.
  • Missing language tags. Sites with Italian and English versions often lack hreflang tags, so Google may show the wrong language to searchers.
  • PDF catalogues instead of pages. Manufacturers often put product details in downloadable catalogues. Search engines can read some PDFs, but proper product pages are far easier to rank and to link to.
  • Slow, heavy pages. Large uncompressed images and old page builders can make sites slow on mobile, which hurts both rankings and enquiries.
  • Old addresses without redirects. After a redesign, old pages that had links and rankings sometimes simply disappear. Redirecting them properly can recover lost value.

Working in Italian: how language fits in

Most Turin customers search in Italian, so your main pages need to be in Italian too. Prism writes in English. Italian copy should come from your own team or a professional translator who knows your sector, because technical and legal terms need to be precise.

Here is how we usually divide the work. We do the keyword research and SEO planning, produce a page brief in English that sets out the target search, structure, headings and questions to answer, and handle all technical setup. Your team or translator writes or adapts the Italian text from that brief. We then review the finished page for structure, internal links and on-page signals before it goes live. For businesses selling abroad, we can also set up English versions and hreflang tags so each searcher sees the right language.
