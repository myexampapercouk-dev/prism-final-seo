Manufacturer Website Design: Capabilities, Specifications and Quote Requests

Website design for UK manufacturers: capability pages, product specifications, downloadable data sheets, accreditations and RFQ forms.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Industrial buyers and engineers often arrive with a specific requirement, so capability, material and tolerance information matters more than broad marketing copy.
  • Clear quote request routes, with a way to attach drawings, can make it easier for buyers to start a conversation.
  • Accreditations, certifications and compliance statements should be accurate and current, so only show what you actually hold.

Manufacturing websites are often an afterthought. Many firms win work through long-standing relationships, trade shows and word of mouth, and the website was built years ago as a digital brochure. Yet when a new buyer, engineer or procurement team is looking for a supplier, the website is frequently one of the first places they check. They want to know quickly whether you can make what they need, to the standard they need, at the volume they need. A site built around those questions can open doors that a brochure site cannot.

This guide covers website design for UK manufacturers, fabricators, machine shops and contract manufacturers. It focuses on the website itself. For promotion, such as B2B search, LinkedIn and account-based marketing, see our guides to B2B marketing and B2B SEO.

Who visits a manufacturer's website

A manufacturing site may need to serve several quite different visitors:

  • Design and production engineers checking technical capability.
  • Buyers and procurement teams comparing suppliers and checking approvals.
  • Business owners or founders looking for someone to make a new product.
  • Existing customers looking for documents, contacts or reorders.
  • Prospective employees, which matters for firms struggling to recruit.

Each group wants different information. Engineers want specifics, buyers want reassurance and process, founders often want guidance. Clear navigation that lets each find their path quickly is the foundation. Our guide to website navigation covers structuring menus.

Capability pages, not just a list of machines

Many manufacturing sites list their machinery. That is useful for some engineers, but most visitors are really asking what you can make. Capability pages usually work better when organised by process or outcome, for example:

  • Processes, such as CNC machining, sheet metal fabrication, injection moulding or assembly.
  • Materials you work with.
  • Typical part sizes, tolerances and finishes, stated accurately.
  • Batch sizes, from prototypes to production runs, where relevant.
  • Secondary services, such as finishing, inspection or kitting.

An equipment list can then sit underneath as supporting detail. Be careful with technical claims: state tolerances and capacities you can genuinely achieve, and where they depend on the part or material, say so rather than quoting a best case as standard.

Industry and application pages

Buyers in different sectors often have different expectations around quality, documentation and traceability. If you serve particular industries, dedicated pages can explain how you work with each one. Only create pages for sectors you actually supply, and avoid naming customers or showing their parts unless you have permission to do so. Many manufacturing relationships come with confidentiality obligations, so generic descriptions of the type of work are often the safer route.

Products and specifications

If you make your own product range rather than, or as well as, contract work, the site needs a well-structured product catalogue. Useful elements include:

  • Clear product categories and filters based on how buyers search, such as size, material or rating.
  • Specification tables in HTML rather than only in images, so they can be read and searched.
  • Downloadable data sheets, drawings or CAD files where appropriate.
  • Installation or maintenance documents for existing customers.

Keep document versions under control, so that the website always offers the current version. Out-of-date data sheets can cause real problems for customers. Our guide to product page SEO covers how product pages can be found in search.

Request for quote forms

For contract manufacturers, the quote request is often the key conversion. A good RFQ form balances getting enough information to quote with not putting people off. Consider asking for:

  • Contact and company details.
  • A short description of the part or project.
  • Quantities and timescales, if known.
  • Material and finish requirements.
  • File uploads for drawings or models.

Make clear which file types you accept, any size limits, and how drawings will be handled. Many buyers will want reassurance about confidentiality. If you are happy to sign a non-disclosure agreement before seeing drawings, say so. Make sure uploaded files are stored securely and only kept as long as needed, and that your privacy notice covers how you handle the data. Also explain what happens next and roughly when they can expect a response, but only promise turnaround times you can meet. Our guide to website contact forms covers form design.

Quality, accreditations and compliance

Quality and compliance information is often central to winning industrial work. Depending on your business, this might include quality management certifications, sector-specific approvals, or information about product marking, such as UKCA or CE marking, and other regulatory requirements. A few rules:

  • Only show certifications you currently hold, and keep certificates up to date.
  • Use certification body logos only in line with their rules.
  • Describe the scope of a certification accurately, as it may not cover every site or activity.
  • Product compliance requirements are complex and change, so check current official guidance or take advice before making statements about them.

A downloadable copy of key certificates can save buyers time during supplier approval. Our guide to website trust signals covers presenting credentials.

Showing your facility

Photographs and video of your actual facility, machinery and people can help buyers judge whether you are a credible supplier. Real images usually do more than stock photos of generic factories. Check that nothing confidential, such as customer parts or drawings, is visible in shots, and get consent from staff who appear. A short factory tour video can work well; see our guide to website video.

Case studies, with care

Case studies can show buyers how you solve problems, but manufacturing work is often covered by confidentiality. If you cannot name a customer, an anonymised description of the challenge, approach and outcome can still be useful, provided it is accurate and the customer would be comfortable with it. Don't overstate results. Our guide to testimonials pages covers using customer feedback responsibly.

Recruitment pages

Many manufacturers find recruitment as important as winning customers. A careers section with real information about roles, apprenticeships, training and working environment can help. Keep vacancies up to date and remove filled roles promptly.

Ecommerce and trade ordering

Some manufacturers sell standard products or spares online, either to the public or to trade customers. Platforms such as Shopify or WooCommerce can handle this, and B2B features like trade pricing, account login and ordering on account may be needed. Consider how online orders link to your existing systems before committing. Our guide to ecommerce website design covers the wider considerations.

Exporting and international buyers

If you sell abroad, consider whether international buyers can understand your site. Clear units, export information and contact routes help. Translated pages can work, but only where you can maintain them properly and respond to enquiries in those languages.

Speed, structure and search

Industrial buyers often search for specific processes and materials combined with a location or requirement. A clear structure, with a page for each process and material, gives search engines more to work with than a single long capabilities page. Large image and document libraries can slow a site down, so optimise images and host documents sensibly. Our guides to technical SEO and website speed cover the essentials. If you are redesigning an older site that already ranks for useful terms, plan redirects carefully; see redesigning without losing rankings.

Choosing a platform

WordPress is common for manufacturing sites because it handles structured capability pages, document libraries and integrations flexibly. Shopify or WooCommerce suit firms selling standard products online. Very simple sites can run on builders such as Squarespace or Wix, but these may become limiting for large catalogues or technical documentation. See our guide to website builders vs custom websites.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • A homepage full of general statements and no clear statement of what you make.
  • Equipment lists without explaining capability.
  • Specifications only in images or out-of-date PDFs.
  • Out-of-date or overstated accreditations.
  • RFQ forms that cannot accept drawings, or that ask for too much.
  • Showing customer parts or names without permission.

FAQs

What should a manufacturer's website include?

Clear capability pages, materials and process information, accurate accreditations, a straightforward quote request route and real images of your facility.

Should a manufacturer list its machinery?

An equipment list can help engineers, but it usually works best as supporting detail beneath capability pages that explain what you can make.

Can buyers upload drawings through the website?

Many RFQ forms allow file uploads. Make sure files are handled securely, explain accepted formats and say how confidentiality is handled.

Should we show customer case studies?

Only with permission. Anonymised, accurate descriptions of the work can be a useful alternative where confidentiality applies.

Can a manufacturer sell online?

Yes, for standard products or spares. Platforms such as Shopify or WooCommerce can work, but consider trade pricing and how orders link to your systems.
