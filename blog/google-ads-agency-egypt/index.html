Why Google Ads matters for Egyptian businesses

Egypt has a large, young and mobile-first online population, and Google remains the main place people go when they want to compare options, check prices or find a supplier. Searches happen in Arabic, in English and in a mix of both, often typed in Franco-Arabic or with English brand names. That makes careful keyword planning more important than in single-language markets.

Search ads give you control. You choose the searches you appear for, the areas you target, the hours you run and the budget you spend each day. When campaigns are built properly, you can see which keywords lead to calls, forms and WhatsApp messages, and move budget towards what works.

We work with businesses such as:

  • Service firms in Cairo, New Cairo, Sheikh Zayed and the 6th of October area that want more qualified enquiries.
  • Clinics and healthcare providers promoting specific treatments, within the platform and local rules.
  • Online stores that want Shopping and Performance Max campaigns tied to a clean product feed.
  • Education and training providers recruiting students for courses and programmes.
  • Exporters and B2B suppliers targeting buyers in the Gulf, the UK or Europe from an Egyptian base.

Search campaigns in Arabic and English

Search campaigns are usually the best starting point because they capture people who are already looking. In Egypt we often build separate ad groups for Arabic and English searches, because the intent, wording and even the landing page can differ. A searcher typing in English may be an expat, a company buyer or someone comparing international brands, while Arabic searches may come from a wider local audience.

We pay close attention to:

  • Negative keywords, so you do not pay for job searches, free downloads or unrelated meanings.
  • Location settings, so ads reach people in the governorates you actually serve, not everyone with an interest in Egypt.
  • Ad schedules, so budget is focused on the hours when your team can answer calls and messages.
  • Assets such as call, location, sitelink and price assets that make ads more useful.

If your landing pages are slow or unclear, we fix them first. Our Cairo web design guide explains how we build fast pages for Egyptian businesses.

Shopping and Performance Max for online stores

Egyptian e-commerce has grown quickly, and many stores now want Shopping ads that show product images and prices directly in search results. These campaigns depend on a clean Merchant Center feed with accurate titles, prices, availability and shipping details. We review the feed, fix common errors and set up product groups so best sellers get the right share of budget.

Performance Max can work well once conversion tracking is reliable, but it needs good inputs: strong product data, clear audience signals and decent images. We usually start with Standard Shopping or a tightly controlled Performance Max campaign, watch search terms and placements, and expand only when the numbers support it.

Cash on delivery is still common in Egypt, so we talk with you about how to count and track orders that are placed online but paid at the door, and how returns and cancelled orders affect the figures you report back to Google.

Tracking calls, forms and WhatsApp

Many Egyptian customers prefer to call or message on WhatsApp rather than fill in a form. If those actions are not tracked, Google has no idea which ads work. We set up tracking for:

  • Calls from ads and from your website, using Google forwarding numbers where available.
  • Clicks on WhatsApp buttons and links on your landing pages.
  • Form submissions and thank-you pages.
  • Online purchases, with values sent from your store where the platform allows it.

We also recommend a simple way for your team to note which enquiries became real customers, so we can judge campaigns on business value, not just clicks.

SEO and Google Ads together

Paid search brings fast visibility, but every click costs money. Organic search builds slowly and keeps working. Many Egyptian businesses do best with both: ads to capture demand now, and SEO to reduce reliance on paid clicks over time. Search term reports from Google Ads also show which phrases convert, which helps decide which pages to build for organic search. Our SEO agency guide for Egypt covers the organic side.

Some businesses also add social ads for awareness and retargeting. Our guide to Meta ads in Amman shows how we run Meta campaigns in a nearby Arabic-speaking market.

Targeting the Gulf and other markets from Egypt

Some Egyptian businesses sell to customers outside Egypt, such as software firms, outsourcing providers, education platforms and exporters. In those cases we set up separate campaigns for each target country, with their own budgets, languages and landing pages. Costs per click in the Gulf are usually very different from Egypt, so mixing them in one campaign makes results hard to read.

Our guides to Google Ads in Dubai and Google Ads in Jordan cover two of the markets Egyptian firms often target.

Rules and policies to keep in mind

Google has its own advertising policies, and some sectors, such as healthcare, finance and education, face extra checks or restrictions. Egyptian law also covers consumer protection, data and certain regulated professions. We keep campaigns within Google's policies and flag anything that looks risky, but legal and compliance approval rests with you. Please check official Egyptian guidance or your own adviser before promoting regulated services.
