Shopify Navigation Menus: How to Set Up and Structure Them

How to build Shopify navigation menus: main and footer menus, dropdowns, linking collections, mobile menus and keeping navigation tidy as you grow.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify menus are built in Content, then Menus, and your theme decides where each menu appears.
  • Base the main menu on how customers shop, usually by collection, and keep labels short and familiar.
  • Check menus on mobile and with a keyboard, and review them whenever you add or retire collections.

Navigation is how shoppers find their way around your Shopify store. A clear menu helps people reach the right products quickly. A confusing one sends them to search, or away. Shopify keeps menu building fairly simple, but the decisions about what goes in the menu, and in what order, are where most of the work lies.

This guide covers how menus work in Shopify, how to set up main and footer menus, how to handle dropdowns and mega menus, and how to keep navigation tidy as your range changes. For general principles that apply to any site, see our website navigation structure guide.

How Shopify menus work

In the Shopify admin, menus are managed under Content, then Menus. Older admin layouts placed them under Online Store, then Navigation, so you may see either depending on when you last looked.

A new store usually starts with two menus: a main menu and a footer menu. You can create more. Each menu is a list of items, and each item has a label and a link. Links can point to collections, products, pages, blog posts, policies, the search page or a custom web address.

The menus themselves do not decide where they appear. Your theme does. In the theme editor, header and footer sections have settings where you choose which menu to show. If you create a new menu and it does not appear, check the theme editor and select it there.

Building the main menu

The main menu, usually shown in the header, is the most important navigation on the store. Before adding items, step back and think about how customers shop.

  • By product type. Dresses, tops, knitwear. Common for fashion and homeware.
  • By use or room. Kitchen, bathroom, garden.
  • By customer. Women, men, kids.
  • By range or collection. Useful when collections have strong names customers recognise.

Pick the approach that matches how your customers describe what they want, not how products are organised in your warehouse. Your collections should support this. Our Shopify collections structure guide covers how to plan them.

Some practical tips:

  • Keep labels short. One or two words where possible, using everyday language.
  • Put the most important items first. People scan from left to right on desktop and top to bottom on mobile.
  • Do not overload the top level. If the header wraps onto two lines or feels crowded, group items into dropdowns.
  • Include a route to everything. A "Shop all" link can help people who just want to browse.

Dropdowns and nested menus

To create a dropdown, add menu items and then drag them underneath a parent item so they are indented. Shopify supports nesting up to a few levels, but how many levels display depends on your theme.

Use dropdowns to group related collections. For example, a parent item called Clothing might hold Dresses, Tops and Trousers. Think about whether the parent itself should link somewhere. On some themes, clicking the parent opens the dropdown rather than following its link, so the parent's own page may be hard to reach. A "Shop all clothing" item inside the dropdown solves this.

Deep nesting makes menus harder to use, particularly on phones. If you find yourself needing four levels, it may be a sign the collection structure needs simplifying.

Mega menus

Many themes offer a mega menu, which displays a large panel with columns of links and sometimes images. These can work well for stores with wide ranges, because shoppers see more options at once.

Mega menus are usually built from the same nested menu in the admin, with the theme deciding how to lay it out. Some themes add image or promotional blocks in the theme editor. Keep them focused on helping people find products. A panel full of promotions can make actual categories harder to spot.

The footer menu

The footer is where people look for practical information. Typical footer items include:

  • Contact and help pages.
  • Delivery and returns information.
  • Store policies such as privacy, terms and refunds.
  • About the business.
  • Your blog or journal, if you have one.

Many themes let you show several footer menus in columns. Group items logically, such as Help, About and Legal. For more on footer content, see our website footer design guide.

Mobile navigation

On phones, the main menu usually collapses into a menu icon that opens a drawer. Dropdowns become expandable sections. This is where cluttered menus cause the most trouble, because long lists are hard to scroll and tap.

Test the menu on a real phone. Check that every item is reachable, that labels are not cut off, that the drawer closes properly and that tap targets are not too small. Some themes let you show a separate mobile menu or add extra links to the drawer, which can help if your desktop menu does not translate well.

Our mobile website design guide covers wider points about designing for small screens.

Accessibility

Menus should work for people using a keyboard or a screen reader. Most well-built themes handle this, but customisations can break it. Check that:

  • You can reach every menu item using the Tab key.
  • Dropdowns open and close with the keyboard.
  • There is a visible focus outline showing where you are.
  • The menu icon on mobile has a text label for screen readers.

Navigation and SEO

Menu links are internal links, and they tell search engines which pages matter most on your store. Linking your main collections from the header helps those pages get found and understood. Linking to dozens of minor pages from every page dilutes that signal and makes the menu harder for people to use.

When you rename or remove a collection, update the menu and set up a redirect from the old address, so neither customers nor search engines hit a dead end. Shopify can create a redirect automatically when you change a handle, but always check. Our Shopify SEO guide covers this and more.

Keeping menus tidy over time

Menus tend to grow as you add seasonal collections, promotions and new ranges. Without a review they become long and inconsistent. A few habits help:

  • Review after each season or launch. Remove items for collections that have ended.
  • Look for empty collections. A menu item leading to an empty page is a poor experience.
  • Use your analytics. Look at which menu destinations people use and which they ignore, and adjust.
  • Keep a note of the structure. A simple list of menus and their purpose helps your team make consistent changes.

Menus for different markets or languages

If your store sells in several languages, menu labels can be translated so each audience sees their own language. If different markets have different ranges, think about whether every menu item makes sense in every market. Our guide to selling internationally from a UK online store covers the wider planning.

Navigation beyond the menu

Menus are only one way people move around a store. Search, filters, breadcrumbs and links within collection and product pages all help shoppers find what they need. If your menu cannot hold everything, these other routes take some of the load.

Make sure search is easy to find, especially on mobile, and that it returns sensible results for the words customers actually use. Filters on collection pages let people narrow a large range without needing more menu items. Breadcrumbs show where a shopper is and offer a quick route back up a level. Our guides to Shopify product filters and breadcrumb navigation cover both in more detail.

FAQs

Where do I edit menus in Shopify?

In the admin, go to Content, then Menus. On older admin layouts this was under Online Store, then Navigation.

Why is my new menu not showing on the store?

Menus only appear where the theme displays them. Open the theme editor, select the header or footer section and choose your menu in its settings.

How do I create a dropdown menu in Shopify?

Add the items to the menu, then drag the child items underneath and slightly to the right of the parent item so they are nested.

How many items should my main menu have?

There is no fixed number. Keep the top level short enough to fit comfortably on one line on desktop and to scan easily on mobile, and group the rest into dropdowns.

Do menu links help SEO?

They act as internal links, so linking your key collections from the menu helps search engines understand which pages matter. Keep menus focused rather than linking everything.

What happens to menu links when I delete a collection?

The menu item may break or disappear depending on the link type. Update the menu and set up a redirect from the old address.
