Real Estate Marketing Toronto: Websites, SEO and Ads for Agents, Brokerages and Developers

Real estate marketing in Toronto: how agents, brokerages and developers use websites, local SEO, Google and Meta ads and email to win leads, and rules to check.

Real estate marketing in Toronto means reaching buyers, sellers, renters and investors in one of Canada's most competitive property markets, then turning that attention into enquiries you can follow up. For agents, brokerages, developers and property managers, the most reliable approach combines a fast website you own, neighbourhood-level SEO, targeted paid ads and consistent email and content, rather than depending only on listing portals and referrals.

Why real estate marketing in Toronto needs its own plan

The Greater Toronto Area is huge and varied. A condo specialist downtown, a family-home agent in Etobicoke or Scarborough, a brokerage covering Mississauga and Brampton, a pre-construction developer marketing a new tower and a property manager handling rentals all face different buyers and different searches. Toronto also has a high share of newcomers and investors, many of whom research online for months before contacting anyone.

That creates three challenges. First, competition for broad searches is intense, so visibility usually comes from specific neighbourhoods, property types and questions. Second, buying cycles are long, so follow-up matters as much as the first click. Third, housing advertising carries rules that general marketing does not, so campaigns need careful wording and targeting.

A real estate website you own

Listing portals bring exposure, but you do not control them and they do not build your brand. Your own website should be the centre of your marketing. A strong Toronto real estate website usually includes:

  • Neighbourhood pages: genuinely useful guides to the areas you cover, such as housing types, transit, schools to research and what buyers typically ask.
  • Listings or a listings feed: where your brokerage and data agreements allow it, kept accurate and up to date.
  • Clear calls to action: book a valuation, arrange a viewing, join a pre-construction list or request a rental appointment.
  • Trust signals: licensing and brokerage details shown as your regulator and brokerage require, plus clear contact information.
  • Speed and mobile use: most property browsing happens on phones, often with large image galleries.

Local SEO for Toronto agents and brokerages

Search is where many buyers and sellers start. Rather than chasing broad terms dominated by large portals, Toronto real estate businesses usually win by targeting specific, high-intent searches:

  • Neighbourhood and property type, such as condos in a particular downtown area or detached homes in a suburb.
  • Seller questions, such as how to prepare a home for sale or what affects a valuation.
  • Buyer and newcomer questions, such as the steps to buying in Ontario or how pre-construction purchases work.
  • Google Business Profile visibility for searches near your office or service area.

Content should be accurate and general, and anything touching mortgages, taxes or legal steps should point readers to qualified professionals and official sources. Email, content and long buying cycles

Most people who visit a real estate website are not ready to act that week. Email and content keep you in front of them until they are. Useful options include new listing alerts, market update newsletters written in general terms, guides for first-time buyers or newcomers, and follow-up sequences after a valuation request or viewing. Email marketing in Canada must follow Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL), which sets rules on consent, sender identification and unsubscribe options, so build sign-up forms with that in mind.

Video is also valuable. Walk-throughs, neighbourhood tours and short explainers work on YouTube, Instagram and TikTok, and they can be reused on your website. We use AI-powered tools to speed up tasks such as drafting ad variations and outlining content, but every piece is checked by a person for accuracy and compliance before it is published.

Rules to check before you advertise

Real estate is a regulated activity in Ontario, and advertising carries specific expectations. As general information only:

  • Provincial rules: Ontario's real estate regulator sets requirements for how registrants advertise, including how brokerage names and registration details appear. Check current official guidance and your brokerage's policies.
  • Industry trademarks: certain industry terms and listing service names have trademark rules on how they can be used.
  • Privacy: PIPEDA generally covers personal information collected in commercial activity, including enquiries and tracking. If you collect data from Quebec residents, Law 25 may also apply.
  • Email: CASL applies to commercial electronic messages.
  • Platform rules: housing ad categories on Meta and Google can limit targeting.

We are not legal advisers. Approval of advertising wording rests with you and your brokerage, and your own adviser should confirm what applies.

Marketing pre-construction and rental projects

Developers and property managers have different goals from individual agents. A pre-construction launch in the GTA usually needs a dedicated project website or landing page well before sales open, with floor plan and amenity information, a registration form and a clear explanation of the buying process in general terms. Paid search and Meta ads then build a registration list, and email keeps those registrants informed until launch. Purpose-built rental buildings work in a similar way, but the goal is booking tours and filling units within a lease-up period.

In both cases, tracking each registration or tour request back to its source shows which channels are worth more budget. Claims about features, completion dates, pricing or incentives should be accurate and approved by your team, and anything describing deposits, financing or legal steps should point buyers to their own professional advisers.

French and multilingual audiences

Toronto is home to many languages, and some agents serve clients in languages other than English. If you want French or other language content, it should come from your own team or a professional translator. FAQs

What does real estate marketing in Toronto include?

It usually includes a website you own, neighbourhood-level SEO, Google Business Profile, paid ads on Google and Meta, email follow-up and video content, all tracked so you can see which channels produce enquiries.
