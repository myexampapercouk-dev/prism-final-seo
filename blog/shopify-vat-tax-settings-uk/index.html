Shopify VAT Settings: How to Set Up Tax on a UK Store

How to set up Shopify VAT settings for a UK store: registration details, tax-inclusive prices, non-standard rates, shipping, reports and what to check.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 6 November 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify calculates tax from the settings you give it, but deciding whether and how VAT applies to your business remains your responsibility.
  • UK shoppers generally expect to see prices including VAT, so decide this setting before you enter or import your catalogue.
  • Check non-standard products, shipping, discounts and invoices with a test order, then confirm the set-up with your accountant.

Shopify VAT settings tell your store whether to charge VAT, at what rates, and whether the prices you enter already include it. For a UK store, you set this up in the Taxes and duties area of your admin settings by choosing the United Kingdom, adding your VAT registration number if you have one, and choosing how prices should be displayed. You then review any products that do not follow the standard rate. Shopify carries out the calculation, but it relies on you to give it the right instructions.

This guide is for UK store owners setting up a new shop or checking an existing one. It explains where the settings sit, what each one does, how to deal with reduced-rate and zero-rated products, and how to test the result. It is general guidance on how the settings work, not tax advice. VAT rules depend on your products, customers and circumstances, so confirm your set-up with an accountant or against HMRC's current guidance.

First, decide what your store needs to do

Before you open the settings, get answers to four questions. They determine almost everything that follows.

  • Are you VAT registered? Whether you must register depends on your taxable turnover against HMRC's threshold, and you can sometimes register voluntarily. If you are not registered, you generally should not charge VAT at all.
  • Which rates apply to your products? Goods in the UK can fall under the standard, reduced or zero rate, or be exempt. Some of the distinctions can be surprisingly fine, particularly in food, children's goods and health-related products.
  • Where do you sell? Selling to customers in Northern Ireland, the EU or elsewhere can raise separate rules, particularly on goods moving across borders.
  • Do you sell digital products? Digital goods sold to consumers can follow different rules, including for overseas customers.

If you are unsure on any of these, ask your accountant first. Correcting a store's settings is easy, but correcting tax charged on past orders is not.

Where to find the settings

  1. In your Shopify admin, go to Settings and then Taxes and duties.
  2. Select the United Kingdom from your list of regions.
  3. Enter your VAT number if you are registered, or choose the option to collect VAT if that is presented.
  4. Review the tax rates and any exceptions shown for the region.
  5. Check the general calculation options, including how tax applies to prices and shipping.

Shopify does revise its admin, so labels and locations can change. If yours looks a little different, use the search bar in the admin or the current Shopify help documentation. Your region also needs to be in your shipping or market settings for customers there to be able to buy, so if the region is not available, check those areas too. Our guide to Shopify shipping profiles walks through the shipping side.

Show prices with VAT included

UK consumers generally expect the price they see to be the price they pay, and businesses selling to consumers are generally expected to show prices inclusive of VAT. Shopify has a setting for including tax in your prices. With this on, a price you enter is treated as VAT inclusive and the tax is worked out from within it. With it off, tax is added on top at checkout.

This choice affects how the whole catalogue is read, so settle it before entering products. Consider this simple example using a 20% rate:

SettingPrice you enterCustomer paysVAT within the total
Prices include tax£24.00£24.00£4.00
Prices exclude tax£24.00£28.80£4.80

The example is illustrative only, but it shows why switching this setting on an established store needs care. Your entered prices stay as numbers, while the amount customers pay or the margin you keep changes. If you switch, plan how you will update prices rather than assuming the store will adjust them for you, and check your margins at the same time.

If you sell largely to trade customers, you may want prices displayed differently. That is a commercial design decision as well as a tax one, and it can involve separate price lists or customer groups, depending on your plan. Whatever you choose, make it clear on the page whether VAT is included, as uncertainty at this stage can put buyers off. Our guide to pricing page design looks at how to present prices clearly.

Handle products that are not standard rate

If everything you sell is standard rated, this step is short. If not, it needs some thought, because a store-wide rate will apply to everything unless you say otherwise.

Turn tax off on individual products

Each product variant has a setting for charging tax, in the pricing section of the product page. Unticking it means no tax is charged on that item. This is one way of handling zero-rated goods, but it can also misstate your reporting if used without thought, because the sale may not be recorded in the way your accountant expects. Ask how they want zero-rated and exempt sales to appear.

Use tax overrides for different rates

For reduced-rate or zero-rate products, you can generally set up a tax override, which applies a different rate to a chosen group of products. Overrides are usually based on a collection, so the set-up looks like this:

  1. Create a collection containing only the products that share the rate. Our guide to Shopify collections structure explains the options.
  2. Consider keeping it hidden from your navigation, since it exists for tax rather than browsing.
  3. In the region's tax settings, add an override for that collection and enter the rate.
  4. Test a product from the collection in the checkout.

Take care with automated collections, as a change to a tag or product type can quietly add or remove an item from the override. Product tags used for merchandising should not double as tax logic unless someone owns that process. Our guide to Shopify product tags explains how to keep tags tidy. Also plan who checks the tax treatment whenever a new product is added, since a new line in the wrong collection would be charged at the wrong rate.

Mixed baskets

Baskets with a mixture of rates need particular checking. Test an order containing both standard-rated and reduced or zero-rated items, then check the tax line against your own calculation. Add a discount and shipping too, because those are where differences tend to appear.

Shipping, discounts and gift cards

The product is rarely the whole of the transaction. There are other parts of an order for which you need a decision.

  • Shipping. There is a setting for charging tax on shipping. VAT on delivery charges generally follows the treatment of the goods being delivered, which can get complicated with mixed baskets, so ask your accountant how your store should handle it. Our guide to online shop shipping rates covers how to set the charges themselves.
  • Discounts. Percentage and fixed discounts reduce the amount on which tax is calculated. Check that the figures look correct on a test order. Our discount code strategy guide explains how to plan offers.
  • Gift cards. Selling a gift card and redeeming one can be treated differently for VAT, and the rules depend on the type of voucher. Shopify gift cards are generally not taxed at the time of sale by default, so check whether that fits your circumstances. Our guide to gift cards and vouchers covers the wider decisions.
  • Refunds. Refunds affect the tax position on an order, so check they are reported the way your bookkeeping expects. The Shopify order editing and refunds guide explains how to make them.

Selling outside the UK

Once you ship beyond Great Britain, the settings can be the smaller part of the job. Depending on where you sell, you may need to think about:

  • Northern Ireland. Goods have their own arrangements because of its position with the EU.
  • EU customers. You may have obligations to register or use an EU scheme, and an agreed approach to who pays import charges.
  • Imports into the UK. If you sell goods from overseas stock to UK customers, special rules can apply depending on the value of the consignment.
  • Duties. Shopify offers options to help estimate and collect duties in some situations, and availability depends on your plan and shipping set-up.

Get professional advice before opening a new market, since the settings in Shopify should follow your decision, not lead it. Our guide to selling internationally from a UK online store covers the commercial side.

Invoices and customer communications

Customers buying for a business may ask for a VAT invoice. HMRC sets out what a valid VAT invoice needs to contain, and a standard order confirmation may not include everything required. Check what your store sends and compare it to HMRC's current guidance.

You can edit notification templates so that your order confirmation shows your VAT number and a tax breakdown, and you can produce invoices through other routes if you have many trade customers. Show your VAT number somewhere clear on your website too if you are registered, and keep your legal pages consistent. Our guides to Shopify store policies and website terms and privacy policies cover those pages.

Test your VAT settings with real scenarios

Do not rely on a settings page looking right. Run real orders through the store, using a test payment method where you can, and compare the results with what you and your accountant expect. A helpful set of scenarios might be:

  • A single standard-rated product delivered in the UK.
  • A single reduced-rate or zero-rated product, if you sell them.
  • A mixed basket with both rate types.
  • An order with a discount code and paid shipping.
  • An order delivered to Northern Ireland, and an order to each overseas region you serve.
  • A partial refund on one of those test orders.

Look at the product page, the cart, the checkout, the confirmation email and the order in your admin. They should all tell the same story. Our Shopify checkout settings guide covers the other options worth reviewing while you are there.

Reporting and your VAT return

Shopify has finance and tax reports that show the amounts charged, and these can be exported for your accountant or bookkeeper. They are helpful for reconciling, but they are not your VAT return in themselves. You still need to make sure the figures are complete, including sales from other channels, refunds and any costs on which you reclaim VAT.

Agree with your accountant early what they would like to receive and how often. If you use accounting software that connects to your store, check how that link treats tax, shipping and fees, and how it posts refunds. Making Tax Digital rules may affect how you keep your records and submit returns, so check how they apply to your business.

FAQs

Does Shopify file my VAT return?

No. Shopify calculates tax on orders and provides reports, but you remain responsible for registering, filing and paying. Your accountant or bookkeeper can tell you what records they need.

Should I add my VAT number if I am not sure I need it?

Only enter a number that HMRC has issued to your business. If you are not sure whether you should register, ask an accountant or check HMRC's guidance before you start charging VAT.

What happens to my prices when I become VAT registered?

Your entered prices do not automatically change. You will need to decide whether to raise prices or absorb the VAT from your margin, then update the store settings and pricing at the date your registration takes effect.

Can I show prices with and without VAT?

There are ways to do so, often through theme changes, and this can suit stores with trade and consumer customers. Make sure the page is clear about which price will be charged at checkout.

Why is the tax in my checkout not what I expected?

Check the customer's delivery address, the included-tax setting, whether the product is set to charge tax, any override collections, and the shipping tax setting. Then compare against a simple single-item order.

Do I need to charge VAT on delivery?

Often it follows the treatment of the goods, but mixed orders can complicate this. Ask your accountant to confirm how your store should treat delivery charges.
