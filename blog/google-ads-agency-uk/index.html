How to Choose a Google Ads Agency in the UK: In-House vs Agency and Questions to Ask

Choosing a Google Ads agency in the UK: when an agency beats in-house, what good management looks like, and the questions to ask before you hire anyone.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good Google Ads agency starts with your margins, tracking and landing pages, not with keywords and bids.
  • In-house suits large, steady accounts; an agency suits businesses that need specialist skills without a permanent hire.
  • Ask who works on your account, who owns it, how results are measured and how you can leave.

To choose a Google Ads agency in the UK, look for one that asks about your margins and goals before talking about keywords, sets up reliable conversion tracking first, and lets you own your ad account. Then compare agencies on scope, transparency and fit, not on headline price or bold promises.

This guide covers when an agency makes more sense than managing ads in-house, what good Google Ads management actually involves, the questions to ask on a sales call and the warning signs to avoid. For costs specifically, our sectors page shows how we approach different markets.

Google Ads agency UK: in-house vs agency

The first decision is whether you need an agency at all. Some businesses do well running their own account; others waste months learning lessons a specialist would avoid. The table below summarises the trade-offs.

FactorIn-houseAgencyFreelancer
Cost structureSalary, training, tools and management timeMonthly fee or retainer, ad spend separateDay rate or monthly fee
Business knowledgeDeep, from day oneBuilt up during onboardingBuilt up during onboarding
Platform breadthDepends on one person's skillsUsually covers search, Shopping, YouTube and MetaOften specialised in one or two areas
Cover for holidays and illnessGaps unless you hire more than one personUsually built inLimited
Best forLarge, steady budgets with ongoing complexityGrowing businesses wanting specialist help across channelsSmaller, well-defined accounts

A hybrid also works well: someone in-house who owns the commercial goals and an agency that handles the build, testing and reporting. Whatever you choose, keep ownership of the accounts with your business.

What good Google Ads management looks like

Before you compare agencies, it helps to know what competent work involves. Strong Google Ads management in the UK usually includes:

  • Tracking first. Conversions set up in Google Ads and GA4, tested, and configured with consent mode so they respect UK GDPR and PECR. Without this, automated bidding optimises towards the wrong thing.
  • Clear targets. A target cost per lead or return on ad spend based on what a customer is worth to you, not a vague aim of more clicks.
  • Sensible structure. Campaigns split by intent, product or location so budget goes where it earns most. Brand and non-brand terms kept separate.
  • Search term hygiene. Regular reviews of the real searches triggering your ads, with negative keywords added.
  • Testing. Ads, assets, bidding approaches and landing pages tested in a planned way.
  • Shopping and Performance Max for retailers. Product feeds that are accurate and well optimised. Our Google Shopping ads guide explains this in more detail.
  • Honest reporting. Spend, conversions and cost per conversion, with commentary on what changed and why.

Questions to ask a Google Ads agency

A short call should tell you a lot. Use these questions and listen for specific, practical answers.

About the account

  • Who will own the Google Ads and GA4 accounts? (The answer should be you.)
  • Is ad spend billed directly to us by Google?
  • Will you audit our existing account before proposing changes?

About the people

  • Who will actually work on our account, and how experienced are they?
  • How often will someone review search terms, bids and budgets?
  • Who do we contact if something breaks?

About measurement

  • Which conversions will you track, and how will you test them?
  • What target will you optimise towards, and how did you work it out?
  • What does a monthly report include?

About the commercial terms

  • What is the fee model, and are there set-up fees or minimum terms?
  • What is the notice period?
  • What happens to our campaigns, data and assets if we leave?

If an agency cannot answer these clearly, that is useful information. Our general guide on how to choose a digital marketing agency in the UK adds broader questions about culture and communication.

Red flags to watch for

  • Guaranteed rankings or results. Google Ads is an auction; nobody controls competitors or prices.
  • Agency-owned accounts that you cannot take with you.
  • Media mark-ups hidden inside a single invoice.
  • Reporting without conversions, or reports full of impressions and click-through rates with no link to revenue or leads.
  • No questions about your business. If they never ask about margins, capacity or seasonality, their targets will be generic.
  • Everything on autopilot. Automated bidding is useful, but someone still needs to set the right goals and inputs.

Sector fit matters

An agency that understands your market will ramp up faster. B2B lead generation, for example, involves longer sales cycles and offline conversions that need importing back into Google Ads; see our B2B marketing page and our guide to LinkedIn ads for B2B. Regulated sectors such as financial advice or healthcare have advertising restrictions to respect, and any final approval of ad copy rests with your own compliance team.

How to compare proposals side by side

Once you have two or three proposals, put them into a simple comparison rather than relying on which sales call felt best. Give every agency the same brief, including your monthly budget, target cost per lead or sale, the products or services that matter most and any seasonality. Then compare the answers on the same lines.

  • Diagnosis. Did they spot specific issues in your current account or website, or is the proposal generic?
  • Priorities. What will they do in the first 30 days, and why in that order?
  • Scope. Which platforms, campaign types and tasks are included, and which cost extra?
  • Measurement. Which conversions count, and how will offline sales or phone calls be captured?
  • Terms. Fee, set-up costs, minimum term, notice period and account ownership.

A proposal that is honest about uncertainty, explains trade-offs and recommends starting smaller where the data is thin is usually a better sign than one that promises a dramatic uplift. It is also reasonable to ask for a short trial period or an audit-only first stage, so you can judge how the agency communicates before committing to a longer arrangement.

Finally, think about how paid search fits with organic search. Ads data shows which keywords and messages convert, and that insight can feed straight into content. If you are planning both, our guide to how much SEO costs in the UK helps you budget the two together.

Checklist: choosing a Google Ads agency

  • Decide whether you need in-house, agency, freelancer or a hybrid.
  • Write down your goal, target cost per lead or sale, and monthly budget.
  • Shortlist two or three agencies and give each the same brief.
  • Confirm you will own every account and that spend is billed directly to you.
  • Ask who works on the account and how often it is reviewed.
  • Check that tracking is part of onboarding.
  • Read the contract for minimum terms, notice and data handover.
  • Agree a review point, typically after 3 to 6 months, to judge progress.

What to expect in the first months

A sensible onboarding usually starts with an audit, tracking fixes and a restructure where needed. Early weeks are about learning which searches and messages convert. Meaningful, stable results typically take 3 to 6 months, though some quick wins such as stopping wasted spend can appear sooner. Agree what success looks like at each stage so progress is easy to judge.

FAQs

How do I choose a Google Ads agency in the UK?

Look for an agency that asks about your margins and goals, sets up tracking first, lets you own your accounts, explains its fee model clearly and reports on conversions rather than just clicks.

Is it better to run Google Ads in-house or use an agency?

In-house suits large, steady accounts with enough work for a dedicated person. An agency suits businesses wanting specialist skills and cover without a permanent hire. A hybrid of the two is common.

Should a Google Ads agency own my ad account?

No. You should own the account and grant the agency access, so you keep your data and history if you change provider.

How long should I give a new Google Ads agency?

Allow around 3 to 6 months for testing and optimisation before judging long-term performance, while expecting clear progress updates along the way.

What should a Google Ads report include?

Spend, conversions, cost per conversion or return on ad spend, key changes made, what was learned and the plan for the next period.

Can a Google Ads agency guarantee results?

Not credibly. Ad auctions depend on competitors and market conditions. A good agency can explain its process and targets, but should not promise fixed outcomes.
