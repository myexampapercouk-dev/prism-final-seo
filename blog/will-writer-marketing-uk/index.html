Will Writer Marketing in the UK: How Will Writing Services Attract the Right Clients

Will writer marketing in the UK works best with clear fixed fees, plain English guides, local SEO, genuine reviews and careful, sensitive advertising.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • People putting off a will need reassurance, so plain English guides, clear fixed fees and a simple process do more than hard selling.
  • Local SEO, reviews and referrals from accountants, financial advisers and funeral directors bring a steady flow of well matched clients.
  • Marketing must be accurate about your status, qualifications and protections, and should never pressure people who may be vulnerable.

Will writer marketing in the UK is about helping people take a step they often put off, by making the process feel simple, affordable and trustworthy. The will writing services that grow steadily usually have a clear website with fixed fees, helpful plain English guides, a strong local search presence, genuine reviews and good referral relationships with other professionals.

This guide is for independent will writers, estate planning firms, home visit will writing services and small practices that also offer lasting powers of attorney and related services. We cover how clients search, your website, content, local SEO, referrals, paid campaigns and the care needed when marketing to people at sensitive moments. This is general marketing information, not legal advice. Your marketing and compliance decisions rest with your business and its own advisers.

How people look for a will writer

Most people start thinking about a will after a life event: buying a home, getting married, having children, a bereavement in the family or retirement. They search for phrases such as will writer near me, how much does a will cost, do I need a solicitor to make a will, or mirror wills for couples. Many also ask friends, family or their accountant or financial adviser for a recommendation.

They often feel uncertain. Questions about cost, what information they need, how long it takes and whether a home visit is possible are common. Marketing that answers those questions calmly and clearly builds the trust needed to book a first appointment.

Be precise about what you are. Do not describe yourself as a solicitor or law firm unless you are one, and only mention qualifications, memberships, codes of practice or insurance you genuinely hold. The rules around will writing and related legal services differ between England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, so make clear which parts of the UK you serve and check official guidance on what your business can offer.

Be clear about fees

Price is one of the first things people search for, and unclear pricing is a common reason people give up. Publishing fixed fees, or clear starting prices with an explanation of what changes the cost, builds trust and saves time on calls that will not go ahead. Our guide to pricing page design explains how to present packages clearly.

Spell out what is included, such as the initial consultation, drafting, a review meeting, signing guidance and storage, and anything charged separately. Avoid hidden extras or language that pressures people into add ons they do not need.

Content that answers real questions

Helpful articles are one of the best ways to reach people early in their thinking. They also show your expertise, which matters for a service where trust is everything. Topics that often work well include:

  • What happens if you die without a will
  • Single wills versus mirror wills for couples
  • How to choose an executor and a guardian for children
  • When to update your will after marriage, divorce or a new home
  • What a lasting power of attorney is and why people make one
  • What to bring to your first will appointment

Keep content general, accurate and up to date, and point readers to official government guidance for detailed rules. Our content strategy guide explains how to plan topics around the questions people search for. A clear FAQ page also helps visitors and can support visibility in AI answers and search features.

Local SEO for will writers

Many clients want someone local, especially if they prefer a home visit. A complete Google Business Profile with an accurate category, service areas, opening hours and regular posts helps you appear in local searches. If you work from home and visit clients, set the profile up as a service area business rather than listing a private address. Our Google Business Profile guide explains the setup.

Reviews matter enormously for trust. Ask satisfied clients for a review once their will is signed, and reply to every one without sharing any details of their affairs. Our guide on how to get more Google reviews has practical tips. Never write reviews yourself or offer incentives for positive ones.

Referral partnerships

Other professionals regularly meet people who need a will. Accountants, financial advisers, mortgage brokers, estate agents and funeral directors can all be valuable referral partners. Build relationships by being reliable, communicating well and making the referral easy, for example with a short guide they can pass on.

Paid campaigns and sensitive marketing

Paid search can work well for high intent searches such as will writer plus your town or mirror wills cost. Keep campaigns focused on your service area, send people to a page that matches their search and track calls and bookings. Our guide to Google Ads for small businesses explains a lean setup. Ad spend is always paid separately from any management fee.

Some people searching for wills are older, unwell or recently bereaved. Marketing should be calm, accurate and free of pressure. Avoid scare tactics, fake deadlines or exaggerated claims about what will happen without a will. Consumer protection law applies to how you advertise and sell, especially for home visits, so check current official guidance and take advice where needed. Email marketing should follow UK GDPR and PECR, and personal information should be handled with particular care. Check current ICO guidance.

Following up and staying in touch

Many people enquire, then hesitate. A short, friendly follow up a few days after an enquiry, answering any outstanding questions and offering a convenient appointment time, often helps them move forward. Avoid repeated chasing, which can feel like pressure on a sensitive subject.

After a will is signed, clients may need to update it as their circumstances change. With their agreement, a gentle reminder every few years, or after common life events, keeps you in mind and helps them keep their affairs current. Only send marketing messages where you have consent or a soft opt in genuinely applies, and make opting out easy. Track where each new client came from, whether search, a review, a partner or a past client, so you know which activities are worth more of your time.

FAQs

What is the best approach to will writer marketing in the UK?

Combine a clear website with fixed fees and plain English guides, a strong Google Business Profile, genuine client reviews, referral relationships with other professionals and calm, focused paid search.

Should a will writer publish prices online?

Usually yes. Clear fixed fees or starting prices build trust, help people decide and reduce time spent on enquiries that will not go ahead.

How can a will writer get more clients without pressure selling?

Publish helpful guides, answer common questions, make booking simple and build referrals. People respond well to calm, clear information when making a sensitive decision.

Can a will writer advertise on Google?

Generally yes, as long as ads are accurate, comply with platform policies and consumer protection rules, and do not misdescribe your status or qualifications.

What content should a will writing website have?

Service pages, a fees page, a how it works page, an about page with genuine credentials, and plain English guides answering common questions about wills and powers of attorney.

How much does a website for a will writer cost?

A small professional services website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks to build, depending on pages and features.
