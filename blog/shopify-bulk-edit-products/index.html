How to Bulk Edit Products in Shopify Without Making Costly Mistakes

Bulk edit products in Shopify with the bulk editor, bulk actions or a CSV. Learn which method suits each job, how to back up first and how to check changes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify offers three main ways to change many products at once: bulk actions on the product list, the spreadsheet-style bulk editor, and CSV export and import.
  • Always export a copy of your product data before a large change, because there is no simple undo button for most bulk edits.
  • Filter carefully, change a small batch first, and spot-check the storefront before rolling the same change out across the whole catalogue.

To bulk edit products in Shopify, select the products from your product list and either use a bulk action, such as adding tags or changing status, or open the bulk editor to change fields like prices, stock and product types in a spreadsheet-style grid. For very large or complex changes, exporting a CSV, editing it and importing it back is often the better route.

Bulk editing saves hours, but it also lets you make the same mistake on hundreds of products in seconds. This guide explains each method, when to use it, and the checks we recommend before and after any large change. Shopify updates its admin regularly, so check Shopify's current help pages for the exact buttons and limits.

The three ways to bulk edit in Shopify

Each method suits a different kind of job. Picking the right one is the first step to a safe edit.

MethodBest forWatch out for
Bulk actions on the product listAdding or removing tags, adding to collections, changing status, archivingApplies the same change to every selected product, so check your selection
Bulk editorChanging prices, compare-at prices, stock, SKUs, weights, product types or vendors across a set of productsLarge grids are easy to misread; edits save to the live store
CSV export and importBig catalogue changes, rewriting descriptions, moving data between storesFormatting errors and overwriting fields you did not mean to change

How to bulk edit products in Shopify with the bulk editor

The bulk editor is the tool most store owners mean when they talk about bulk editing. It shows your selected products as rows in a grid, with fields as columns, so you can change values much as you would in a spreadsheet.

  1. Filter the product list. Use search and filters, such as product type, vendor, tag, status or collection, to narrow the list to the products you want to change.
  2. Select the products. Tick the products on the current page or select all matching products if your filter is accurate.
  3. Open the bulk editor. Choose the bulk edit option. The grid opens with a default set of columns.
  4. Add the columns you need. Add fields such as price, compare-at price, SKU, inventory, weight or product type. Remove columns you do not need so the grid is easier to read.
  5. Make your changes. Edit individual cells, or select a column of cells and apply the same value where the editor allows it.
  6. Save. Changes apply to your live store once saved, so check the grid carefully first.

Products with variants appear as several rows, one per variant, for variant-level fields such as price and SKU. Make sure you are editing the right variant. Our guide to Shopify product variants explains how options and variants relate to each other.

Bulk actions: quick changes from the product list

Some changes do not need the bulk editor at all. From the product list you can select products and apply actions such as adding tags, removing tags, adding products to a manual collection, setting products as active or draft, or archiving them.

These are ideal for organisation tasks. For example, you might tag a range for a seasonal sale, move discontinued lines to draft, or add a group of products to a new collection. If you rely on tags for automated collections or filters, check what a new tag will trigger before you add it. Our guides to Shopify product tags and Shopify collections structure explain how tags and collections interact.

Back up before you bulk edit

For most bulk changes there is no simple undo. If you change the wrong prices, the quickest fix is usually to restore them from a copy you made beforehand.

  • Export a product CSV covering the products you are about to change. Save it with the date in the file name.
  • Note what you are changing and why in a short log. This helps if someone asks later why a price or tag changed.
  • Consider timing. Make large changes at a quieter time of day, not in the middle of a promotion or a busy sales period.

Product data is only one part of your store. For a wider view of backups and recovery, our website backup and recovery plan guide sets out what to keep copies of and how often.

Common bulk editing jobs and how to approach them

Changing prices for a sale. For a temporary sale, many stores set a compare-at price and lower the selling price. Before you do, record the original prices so you can restore them afterwards. UK pricing rules expect sale prices and reference prices to be genuine, so check current official guidance on price marking and pricing practices before running promotions. An automatic discount or discount code may be simpler than editing prices directly, and our discount code strategy guide covers when each approach makes sense.

Updating stock levels. Stock is held per location, so make sure you are editing the right location if you have more than one. For regular stock updates, the inventory screens or a stock-sync app may be more appropriate than the bulk editor. Our Shopify inventory management guide covers locations, tracking and stock counts.

Tidying product types and vendors. Inconsistent product types, such as T-shirt, Tshirt and Tee, make filters and reports less useful. The bulk editor is a quick way to standardise them. Filter by each variation, select the products and change them to the agreed value.

Fixing weights for shipping. Shipping rates based on weight rely on accurate product weights. If a range was imported without weights, the bulk editor lets you add them variant by variant. Check that shipping rates calculate correctly at checkout afterwards.

Checks to make after a bulk edit

Saving the change is not the end of the job. A few quick checks catch most problems before customers do.

  • Open several edited products in the admin and confirm the values are correct, including variants.
  • View a sample of products and collections on the storefront, on both desktop and mobile.
  • Add an edited product to the basket and go as far as the checkout to confirm prices and delivery charges look right.
  • Check that automated collections and filters still show the products you expect.
  • If you use sales channels such as Google or social shops, check they pick up the new data after their next sync.

Mistakes that cause the most trouble

  • Selecting all products by accident. Selecting all matching products rather than only the current page can change far more than intended. Read the selection count before applying anything.
  • Editing the wrong column. Price and compare-at price sit side by side in many layouts. Remove columns you do not need to reduce the chance of a slip.
  • Overwriting with a CSV. Importing a CSV with blank cells can clear fields depending on how the import treats them. Test with a small file first.
  • Forgetting connected apps. Some apps sync product data from another system, such as an inventory or wholesale tool. Changes made in Shopify may be overwritten at the next sync, so make edits at the source.

When to get help

Bulk editing is well within reach of most store owners for everyday jobs. For catalogue-wide restructures, migrations or changes that touch prices, stock and data feeds at once, it can be worth having someone plan and test the change with you. FAQs

How to bulk edit products in Shopify?

Filter and select products on the product list, then either apply a bulk action such as adding tags or changing status, or open the bulk editor to change fields such as price, stock and SKU in a grid. For very large changes, export a CSV, edit it and import it back.

Can I undo a bulk edit in Shopify?

There is generally no single undo button for bulk edits. The safest approach is to export a product CSV before you make changes, so you can restore the original values if something goes wrong.

Can I bulk edit variants in Shopify?

Yes. In the bulk editor, variant-level fields such as price, SKU, weight and stock appear as separate rows for each variant, so you can change them together. Check you are editing the right variant before saving.

Is the bulk editor or CSV import better?

The bulk editor is quicker for changing a few fields across a moderate number of products. CSV import is better for very large catalogues, long text such as descriptions, or copying data between stores, but it needs more care with formatting.

Can I bulk edit product descriptions?

Long descriptions are usually easier to edit through a CSV export and import than in the grid. Rewrite them in a spreadsheet, test a small import first, then import the rest.

Will bulk editing affect my Google Shopping feed?

Changes to prices, titles or availability will flow through to connected sales channels after they next sync. Check your channel or feed tool afterwards to make sure the new data has been accepted.
