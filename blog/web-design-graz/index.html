The Graz market and what it means for your website

Graz is Austria's second-largest city and the capital of Styria. It has a large student population across several universities, a strong automotive and engineering cluster in the wider region, a healthy services sector, and a historic old town that draws visitors throughout the year. Many local firms also sell into Slovenia, Germany and wider Europe.

That mix produces three common types of website project:

  • Local service sites for trades, clinics, salons, cafes, restaurants and professional firms that serve people in Graz and nearby towns.
  • Business-to-business sites for engineering suppliers, technology firms and consultancies selling across Austria and beyond.
  • Online shops for Styrian producers, food and drink brands, fashion and design businesses selling to Austrian and German buyers.

Each has different needs, but all share the same foundations: speed, clarity, accessibility and a structure that supports search.

Web design Graz: what a good site needs

When we plan a site for a Graz business, we start with what visitors need to do, then work back to pages and design. A strong site usually has:

  • A clear home page that says what you do and who you serve in the first screen.
  • One page per main service, rather than a single page listing everything.
  • A contact page and visible contact details, with a map and opening hours where relevant.
  • Fast loading on mobile, with compressed images and lean code.
  • Accessible design: readable text, good contrast, keyboard-friendly menus and labelled forms. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act and websites explains why this matters more now in the EU.
  • Clean URLs, sensible headings and structured data so search engines understand the site.
  • Privacy and cookie notices that reflect how you actually use data. Legal wording should come from your own adviser or official guidance.

German and English content

Most Graz customers expect a German-language site, and some sectors, such as tourism, higher education, engineering and technology, also benefit from English pages. Austrian German has its own vocabulary and tone, so it is worth having a native writer or translator who knows local usage.

Because Prism writes in English, we keep roles clear. We plan the structure, write English page briefs or English copy where you want it, design the templates and build the site. Your team or translator provides the German text. If you run both languages, we set up the language versions properly so search engines can show the right version to each visitor.

Building for search from the start

A new site is the cheapest moment to get SEO right. We set up page titles, headings, internal links, image text, structured data, an XML sitemap and redirects from any old pages, so you do not lose rankings you already have. If you are moving from an older site, our SEO migration checklist shows the steps we follow.

Local search matters for most Graz firms. A site with clear location details, a well-kept Google Business Profile and genuine pages for the districts or nearby towns you serve, such as Gleisdorf, Leibnitz or Weiz, gives you a much better chance in the map results. If you want ongoing SEO after launch, our SEO and GEO services pick up where the build leaves off.

Choosing a web designer in Graz

A local designer is a good fit if you want regular face-to-face meetings. A remote studio can be a good fit if you are comfortable with video calls and written feedback. Either way, ask:

  • Will I own the domain, hosting account and site files?
  • Who writes the German content, and who checks it?
  • How will the site be tested for speed, mobile and accessibility?
  • What happens to my existing rankings when the new site goes live?
  • How do I update the site myself after launch?

Clear answers to these questions tell you more about a designer than a portfolio alone.

Keeping the site working after launch

A website is not finished on launch day. Plugins, themes and apps need updates, contact forms need testing, and content needs to stay accurate as your services, prices and opening hours change. We hand over a short guide to editing your pages, and we recommend a simple monthly routine: check that forms still deliver to the right inbox, review your Google Business Profile, update any out-of-date details, and look at which pages bring visitors and enquiries.

Security matters too, especially on WordPress. Regular updates, strong passwords, backups and limited admin accounts prevent most common problems. If you prefer, we can keep an eye on the technical side as part of an ongoing retainer, so your team can focus on running the business.

It also helps to review the site properly once a year. Look at which pages visitors use most, which services bring enquiries, and which pages are out of date or no longer needed. Small, regular improvements, such as clearer calls to action, better photos, new answers to common questions and faster images, usually do more for a Graz business than a full redesign every few years.
