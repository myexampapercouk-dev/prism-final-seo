How clients look for a lawyer in Calgary

Calgary's legal market reflects its economy. Energy, construction, real estate, agriculture, technology and finance generate corporate, commercial, employment and regulatory work, while a growing population brings steady demand for family law, wills and estates, real estate conveyancing, personal injury, immigration and criminal defence. Clients come from the Beltline, downtown, the suburbs and surrounding communities such as Airdrie, Cochrane, Okotoks and Chestermere.

Private clients usually start with a search: a practice area plus Calgary, or a specific problem described in their own words. Business clients often search too, but they also check LinkedIn, ask for referrals and review a firm's website carefully before contacting anyone. In both cases, your website is where the decision is made. Our guide to law firm marketing covers these principles in a UK context, and they transfer well to Alberta.

A credible law firm website

Your website should explain clearly who you help, with what, and how to start. Visitors want practice-area pages written in plain language, a sense of your approach, information on how fees and first consultations work, and an easy way to make contact. Business clients want to see depth on the issues they face.

We build law firm websites with:

  • Separate, well-structured pages for each practice area and key sub-topic.
  • Lawyer profile pages your firm writes or approves.
  • Clear contact routes on every page, with forms that state what not to send before a retainer is agreed.
  • Fast, accessible, mobile-friendly layouts and secure hosting.
  • Content management your team can update without a developer.

A small firm website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks. Many firms prefer WordPress for flexibility; see our guide to WordPress in Calgary and our article on web design in Calgary.

SEO for Calgary law firms

Search engine optimisation is often the most valuable long-term channel for law firms, because people with legal problems search for answers before they search for lawyers. We focus on:

  • Practice-area pages that target the terms clients actually use, alongside Calgary and nearby communities you genuinely serve.
  • Helpful guides answering common questions in general terms, reviewed and approved by your lawyers.
  • A complete Google Business Profile with the right categories, hours and contact details.
  • Technical health: speed, crawlability, structured data and internal links.
  • Content that AI search tools can understand and quote accurately.

Results typically take three to six months to build in competitive practice areas. Our guides to SEO in Calgary and local SEO in Canada explain more, and our SEO and GEO service sets out what a monthly programme includes.

Paid advertising for law firms

Google Ads can bring enquiries quickly for urgent practice areas, but legal keywords are among the most expensive in search. Campaigns need careful keyword selection, strong negative keyword lists, precise location targeting and landing pages focused on one practice area. Our guide to Google Ads in Calgary covers our approach. Some legal services may also require advertiser verification under platform policies.

Meta Ads can work for practice areas where clients research over time, such as wills, estates and immigration, using broad local audiences and clear educational messages. See our guide to Meta Ads in Calgary. For corporate and commercial work, LinkedIn Ads let you reach people by job function, industry and company size; our article on LinkedIn Ads in Canada explains how. Ad spend is always paid separately to the platforms.

Professional rules and compliance

Lawyers in Alberta are regulated, and there are professional conduct rules about marketing legal services. These can cover how you describe expertise, specialisation, fees, comparisons with other firms, testimonials and the use of past matters. Platform rules from Google, Meta and LinkedIn add further requirements.

We provide general marketing information only and are not legal advisers. We will not write claims about outcomes or guarantees, and every page and advert should be reviewed by a lawyer at your firm before it is published. Approval rests with your firm, and the regulator's official guidance is the place to check what is allowed. Privacy matters too: website forms and tracking should follow your privacy obligations, and confidential client information should never be used in marketing tools.

Handling enquiries well

Marketing only works if enquiries are handled quickly and carefully. Many firms lose potential clients not because nobody found them, but because calls went to voicemail, web forms sat unanswered for days or intake questions felt cold. We help firms review the path from first click to first consultation and make it smoother.

  • Response times: agree who responds to web enquiries and how fast, especially for urgent practice areas.
  • Intake forms: ask only what you need to check conflicts and fit, and state clearly that no lawyer-client relationship exists until a retainer is signed.
  • Call tracking: see which pages and campaigns produce calls, without recording confidential details in marketing tools.
  • Follow-up: simple, approved email templates for people who enquired but did not book.

We also help firms understand which enquiries are a good fit. A campaign that produces many calls about practice areas you do not handle wastes staff time, so we refine keywords, page wording and targeting until most enquiries match the work you want. Over time, this lets you shift budget toward the practice areas with the strongest demand and capacity.

Building authority over time

For commercial and corporate work in particular, credibility builds slowly. Clear lawyer profiles, regular updates on legal changes affecting Alberta businesses, and practical guides for in-house teams all show expertise without making claims your rules would not allow. We plan a realistic content calendar, draft articles for lawyer review and promote approved pieces through LinkedIn and email, so your expertise reaches the people who refer and instruct firms like yours.
