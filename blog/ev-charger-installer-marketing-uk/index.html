EV Charger Installer Marketing in the UK: How to Get More Home and Business Installs

EV charger installer marketing in the UK works when local SEO, clear install pages, accurate grant wording and focused ads turn new EV drivers into booked jobs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Many EV charger enquiries are triggered by a car purchase, so being visible for local install searches at that moment matters more than broad awareness.
  • Separate pages for home chargers, workplace charging and installs for landlords or flats match different buyers and their very different questions.
  • A short survey form that collects photos of the fuse board and parking spot qualifies leads and speeds up accurate quotes.

EV charger installer marketing in the UK is about being the obvious local choice when someone has just ordered an electric car and needs a charger fitted at home, or when a business decides to add charging for staff and visitors. The installers who grow usually combine strong local search visibility, clear pages for each type of install, careful wording around grants and a quote process that takes minutes rather than days.

This guide is for electricians adding EV charging to their services, specialist charge point installers and renewables firms that fit chargers alongside solar panels and batteries. We cover how buyers search, what your website should include, how paid search fits in and how to win more commercial work.

How customers look for an EV charger installer

A typical home charger enquiry follows a car purchase. The driver has a delivery date and wants to charge at home from day one. They search for things like "EV charger installation near me", "home charger fitted [town]", "how much to install an EV charger" or the name of a charger brand plus "installer". Some come via a car dealer or manufacturer recommendation, then check reviews before choosing.

Business buyers behave differently. A facilities manager, landlord or small business owner may search "workplace EV charging installer" or "commercial EV charger installation", and expects to see evidence of larger projects, load management, back-office software options and maintenance. Your marketing needs to speak to both groups without confusing either.

Structure your website around install types

One page titled "EV charging" will not rank well or convert well. Buyers want to see that you handle their specific situation. A useful structure looks like this:

  • Home EV charger installation: what the survey checks, typical install steps, cable routes, smart charging features and how long the job takes.
  • Chargers you fit: the brands and models you install, with honest notes on who each suits.
  • Workplace and commercial charging: multiple charge points, load management, payment and access control options, and maintenance.
  • Landlords, flats and shared parking: general information on the extra considerations, such as permissions and shared supplies.
  • Solar and battery integration: if you offer it, explain how chargers can work alongside home generation.
  • Grants and funding: a general overview, clearly dated, telling readers to check current official guidance.

Keep grant and regulation wording general

Funding schemes for chargers have changed several times, with different support for different groups such as renters, flat owners, landlords and businesses. Charger technical rules and electrical standards also apply. These details matter to customers, but they are easy to get wrong on a website that is not updated often.

Describe schemes in general terms, explain how you help customers check eligibility and apply, and tell readers to confirm the current position with official government guidance. For technical and safety requirements, say that installs are carried out to the relevant current standards by suitably qualified electricians, and list only the registrations and approvals you genuinely hold, ideally with a way for customers to verify them.

Make quoting fast

Many home installs are straightforward, and customers expect a fast price. A remote survey form can qualify most jobs without a visit and helps you quote accurately. Ask for:

  1. Postcode and property type.
  2. Where the car is parked and roughly how far that is from the fuse board.
  3. Photos of the fuse board, the meter and the proposed charger location.
  4. The car model, if known, and preferred charger.
  5. Delivery date of the car, so you can prioritise urgent jobs.

Our guide to website contact forms covers how to keep forms short while still collecting what you need. Respond quickly with a clear quote that explains what is included and any likely extras.

Local SEO for charger installers

Home charger work is local. Set up your Google Business Profile with accurate categories and services, add photos of neat installs and cable runs, and ask every customer for a review that mentions the charger and town. Build area pages only for places you genuinely serve, with real job photos and useful notes rather than repeated text. Our UK local SEO guide explains the approach in more detail.

Helpful content also earns links and visibility in AI search answers: guides to choosing a home charger, what happens during an install, and how smart charging works. Our SEO and GEO service covers this content alongside the technical work that helps pages rank.

Google Ads for EV charging

Paid search can bring in home install jobs quickly, but EV keywords are full of irrelevant traffic: people looking for public charge points, charging apps, car reviews and jobs. Focus on searches that include "install", "installer" or "fitted", plus brand-plus-installer phrases, and maintain a strong negative keyword list. Separate home and commercial campaigns so you can set different budgets and landing pages.

Track form submissions and calls as conversions so you can see which searches lead to booked installs. Our guide to Google Ads negative keywords is a good starting point. Our paid media service can manage campaigns for you, with ad spend paid separately.

Winning commercial work

Commercial projects are larger and less frequent, and they depend on trust and evidence. Create a commercial section with project photos (with permission), a clear explanation of your process from site survey to commissioning and maintenance, and answers to common questions about load management and software. Reach out directly to local businesses, managing agents and landlords with a short, specific message and a link to that section.

Maintenance and support contracts deserve their own page, because many sites need ongoing servicing and fault response after installation.

Build referral partnerships

Car dealers, used EV specialists, solar installers and local electricians who do not fit chargers all meet people who need one. A simple referral arrangement can bring a steady flow of warm leads. Give each partner a dedicated landing page or form link so you can track which relationships produce work, and make sure referred customers get the same fast response and clear quote as everyone else. If you offer partners anything in return, put the arrangement in writing and keep it transparent to customers.

Measure booked installs

Clicks and enquiries are only part of the picture. Record the source of each lead, whether it became a quote and whether the install went ahead. Review the numbers monthly by channel, area and job type. You will quickly see whether home installs, commercial work or partner referrals give the best return, and you can shift budget and effort accordingly.

FAQs

What is the best EV charger installer marketing in the UK?

For most installers, local SEO with clear install pages, a strong Google Business Profile, tightly targeted Google Ads and a fast remote quote process deliver the most booked jobs.

Should I mention EV charger grants on my website?

Yes, but keep it general. Explain how you help, date the page and tell readers to check current official guidance for eligibility.

How do I stop Google Ads showing for public charging searches?

Use phrase and exact match keywords that include "install" or "installer", and add negative keywords such as "public", "map", "app" and "near me free".

Do I need separate pages for home and commercial charging?

Yes. The buyers, questions and decision process are very different, so separate pages rank and convert better.

Can I quote for EV charger installs without a site visit?

Many installers quote straightforward home jobs from a remote survey form with photos, then confirm on the day. Complex jobs still need a visit.

How long until marketing brings in more installs?

Google Ads can produce enquiries within weeks. SEO typically takes three to six months to show consistent results.
