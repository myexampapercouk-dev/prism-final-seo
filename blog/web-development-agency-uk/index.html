Web Development Agency for UK Businesses: A Practical Guide

Hiring a web development agency in the UK? Learn what agencies build, how WordPress and Shopify compare, typical costs and timelines and what to ask first.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 26 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A web development agency turns designs and requirements into a fast, secure, working website, and should plan for speed, search, tracking and maintenance.
  • Prism Works Studio develops business websites from £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites from £1,500 to £3,500, mainly on WordPress and Shopify.
  • Agree scope, platform, ownership, hosting and aftercare in writing before development starts to avoid surprise costs.

A web development agency builds the technical side of a website: the code, platform setup, integrations, performance and security that turn a design into something customers can actually use. In the UK, a good web development agency should explain its platform choice clearly, build for speed and search, and leave you with a site you own and can maintain.

We build business websites from £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites from £1,500 to £3,500, mostly on WordPress and Shopify. This guide explains what development involves, how to compare agencies and where costs come from.

Web design or web development: what is the difference?

Web design is about how a site looks and how people move through it. Web development is about how it works: building templates, setting up the content management system, connecting payment providers and other tools, and making sure pages load quickly and securely. Many agencies, including us, do both. Larger agencies may split them between separate teams.

For most small and medium UK businesses, it helps to have one team responsible for both, so design choices are realistic to build and nothing gets lost between the designer and the developer.

What good development looks like

Two websites can look identical and behave very differently. These are the qualities we prioritise in every build.

Speed

Fast sites keep visitors on the page and are favoured by search engines. We keep code lean, compress images, limit third-party scripts and test pages against Core Web Vitals before launch. Our website speed optimisation guide covers what slows sites down and how to fix it.

Security

We use reputable hosting, keep plugins and themes to a minimum, apply updates and set sensible user permissions. For WordPress sites, our WordPress security guide explains the steps that matter most.

Search-friendly structure

Clean URLs, a logical heading structure, an XML sitemap, structured data and correct redirects are part of development, not an afterthought. Getting them right at build stage is far cheaper than fixing them later.

Easy editing

Your team should be able to add pages, update text and publish posts without calling a developer. We build editable templates and show you how to use them at handover.

Our development process

  1. Requirements: we list every page, feature and integration, and agree what is in and out of scope.
  2. Platform and hosting: we recommend a platform and hosting setup, with ongoing costs explained.
  3. Design sign-off: key templates are approved before development begins.
  4. Build on staging: we develop on a private staging site you can review throughout.
  5. Content and migration: we add or migrate content and set up redirects from any old site.
  6. Testing: devices, browsers, forms, payments, speed and tracking.
  7. Launch and handover: go-live, Search Console and analytics checks, and a training session.

Prism Works Studio is UK-based and works remotely, so each stage runs through video calls, shared documents and the staging site.

Integrations and automation

Many projects connect the website to other tools: email marketing platforms, CRMs, booking systems, accounting software, reviews or stock management. We use established integrations where they exist and keep custom code to what is genuinely needed. Where you want AI-powered features or workflow automation, such as routing enquiries or drafting product descriptions for your team to review, we scope and price that by quote.

Replacing or rebuilding an existing site

If you already have a website, the rebuild needs extra care. We audit current pages, traffic and rankings, decide what to keep, map old URLs to new ones and check indexing after launch. Questions to ask a web development agency

  • Which platform do you recommend for us, and what will it cost to run each year?
  • Will we own the code, hosting account, domain and content?
  • How do you test speed, security and accessibility before launch?
  • How do you handle redirects and search setup?
  • What support do you offer after launch, and how are changes charged?
  • Who will actually build the site?

Clear, written answers are a good sign. Vague answers about ownership or ongoing costs are a reason to keep looking.

After launch

Websites need regular care. Software updates, backups, security checks and small content changes keep the site healthy. Our website maintenance guide explains what to plan for. Many clients also pair a new build with SEO or paid ads, which our website and ecommerce development services page explains.

Common development problems we are asked to fix

A lot of our development work starts with sites that were built quickly and have become hard to manage. The same problems come up again and again.

  • Plugin overload: dozens of WordPress plugins, some unused or out of date, slowing the site and creating security risks.
  • Page builder bloat: heavy visual builders that add large amounts of code to every page. Our guide to WordPress page builders explains the trade-offs.
  • Hard-coded content: text and images built into templates, so even small changes need a developer.
  • No staging site: changes made directly on the live site, with no safe place to test them first.
  • Missing backups: no reliable way to restore the site if an update goes wrong.
  • Broken tracking: analytics or ad tags removed or duplicated during earlier changes, so results cannot be trusted.

Sometimes these can be fixed in place. Sometimes a clean rebuild is cheaper over the long run. We will explain which applies before recommending either.

Choosing the right size of agency

Larger agencies suit complex projects with many stakeholders, custom applications or strict procurement processes. Small studios suit most small and medium businesses that want direct contact with the people building their site, a fixed price and a quick timeline. Freelancers can be a good fit for simple projects, though cover during holidays or illness can be limited. Whichever you choose, the most important things are a clear written scope, honest advice on platform and running costs, and confidence that you will own everything once the site is live. It also helps to meet, even by video, the person who will actually build your site, so you can judge how clearly they explain technical choices.

FAQs

What does a web development agency do?

It builds the working parts of a website, including templates, platform setup, integrations, performance and security. Many agencies also handle design, search setup and support after launch.

How much does a web development agency in the UK charge?

Our business websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites £1,500 to £3,500. Hosting, apps and platform subscriptions are paid separately to those providers.

Which platform is best for a UK small business website?

WordPress suits most service and content-led businesses, and Shopify suits most online shops. We recommend based on what you sell and who will manage the site.

How long does website development take?

A standard business website usually takes three to five weeks and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks, depending on scope and content readiness.

Will I own the website code and content?

Yes. With us, your domain, hosting, site files, content and analytics stay in your name. Confirm ownership in writing with any agency you hire.

Prism Works Studio is UK-based and works remotely. We run projects through video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can review at any time.
