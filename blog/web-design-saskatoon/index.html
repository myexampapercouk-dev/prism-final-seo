Web Design for Saskatoon Businesses: How Remote Website Projects Work

Looking for web design in Saskatoon? How remote website projects work for Saskatchewan businesses, from planning and platforms to local SEO, timings and costs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Web design for a Saskatoon business should produce a fast, clear website that explains what you do, works well on phones and makes it easy for customers across Saskatchewan to get in touch.
  • Many Saskatoon firms serve customers across a wide rural region as well as the city, so service area pages and local search need careful, honest planning.
  • Our small websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000 and take three to five weeks, with Canadian dollar quotes available on request.

Web design for a Saskatoon business should give you a fast, clear website that explains what you do, works well on phones, is easy for search engines to understand and makes it simple for customers in Saskatoon and across Saskatchewan to call, book or enquire. Because planning, design, building and launch all happen online, a website project can be delivered remotely. Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs websites for Canadian businesses online; we have no office in Saskatoon or anywhere else in Canada.

This guide explains what a good website needs for a Saskatoon business, how to choose a platform, how to plan for customers spread across a large region, what our projects cost and what to ask before choosing a web designer.

What good web design in Saskatoon should include

Saskatoon's economy includes agriculture and agri-food, mining and resources, agricultural technology and research, construction, healthcare, education, transport and logistics, and a wide range of trades, clinics, restaurants, shops and professional firms. Many businesses also serve farms, rural communities and towns well beyond the city. Whatever your sector, a good website should include:

  • A clear message on every key page: what you do, who you serve and how to get in touch.
  • Mobile-friendly design, because many visitors arrive on phones, sometimes with patchy rural connections.
  • Fast loading, with optimised images and lean code.
  • Service pages that explain each offer in enough detail for customers and search engines.
  • Simple contact options, including click-to-call, short forms and clear opening hours.
  • Accessibility, so people with disabilities can use the site. Our website accessibility guide covers the principles.
  • Search foundations such as page titles, headings, structured data and a sensible site structure.

Our website and ecommerce development service covers all of these, and our small business website checklist is a useful way to review what your current site has or lacks.

Serving Saskatoon and the wider region

Saskatoon is the largest city in Saskatchewan, and many businesses based there serve customers across a large area, including Warman, Martensville, Prince Albert, North Battleford, Humboldt and rural municipalities in between. An equipment dealer, agronomy consultancy or trades firm may travel long distances for work, while a clinic or restaurant will mostly serve people within the city.

That makes it important to describe your service area honestly. A strong page for each core service, supported by a small number of area pages where you genuinely work and have something specific to say, usually performs better than dozens of near-identical pages that only change the town name. Your Google Business Profile should reflect the same service area. Our guide to local SEO in Canada explains how these pieces fit together.

Choosing a platform: WordPress, Shopify or custom

The right platform depends on what your website needs to do and who will update it. WordPress suits most service businesses because it is flexible, widely supported and easy to edit once set up well. Our guide to WordPress for Canadian businesses explains the options. If you sell products online, Shopify is usually the better choice, handling payments, inventory, shipping and taxes in one place; our guide to Shopify in Canada covers that route.

A lean custom build can make sense for very simple sites that rarely change, or where speed is a priority. Whatever the platform, you should own the domain, hosting account and all content, and be able to move to another provider if you wish.

Pages that turn visitors into enquiries

A good-looking website is only useful if it brings in work. That means every important page should make the next step obvious. For a trades business, that might be a quote form and a phone number at the top of every service page. For an agricultural supplier, it might be a clear way to request pricing or find the nearest dealer. For a clinic, it is usually a booking link.

If you plan to run Google or Meta advertising, dedicated landing pages built around a single offer usually convert better than sending people to the homepage. Our guide to landing page design explains what to include. We also set up analytics and conversion tracking at launch, so you can see which pages and channels bring enquiries.

Trust signals matter too, especially for businesses that customers may not have met in person. Genuine reviews, photos of real work, details of the areas and industries you serve, and clear information about how a job or order works all help visitors feel confident enough to get in touch. We plan space for these during the design stage so they are not squeezed in later, and we never invent reviews or examples on your behalf.

Accessibility, privacy and French content

Saskatchewan has its own accessibility law, The Accessible Saskatchewan Act, which sets out a framework for improving accessibility across the province. What it requires of your organisation can depend on your sector and type, and the details may develop through regulations, so check the official Government of Saskatchewan guidance. Building an accessible website is good practice for every visitor regardless.

If your website collects personal information through contact or booking forms, the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) generally governs how private-sector businesses handle it, and Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) applies to marketing email such as newsletters. If you serve customers in Quebec, that province's privacy law, often called Law 25, may add requirements. These notes are general information rather than legal advice, so refer to official guidance from the relevant regulators.

If you want to reach French-speaking customers, separate French pages work better than automatic translation. We write in English; French content is written with your team or a professional translator, and we set up the site so each language version is indexed correctly.

How a remote web design project works

Saskatchewan does not change its clocks, so the UK is usually six or seven hours ahead of Saskatoon depending on the time of year. We agree a regular call slot in the UK afternoon, which falls in the Saskatoon morning, and share designs and progress online so you can review them whenever suits you.

  1. Discovery. We agree goals, pages, features, platform and the customers you want to reach.
  2. Structure and content plan. We map the pages and agree who provides text and images.
  3. Design. We design key pages for your feedback before building the rest.
  4. Build. We develop the site on a staging version you can review at any time.
  5. Testing and launch. We test on phones and desktops, check forms and tracking, and launch.

Questions to ask any web designer

  • Which platform do you recommend for us, and why?
  • Will we own the domain, hosting and all content?
  • How will the site handle our service area across Saskatchewan?
  • Who writes the content, and what do you need from us?

FAQs

How do I choose a web design company in Saskatoon?

Choose a designer who asks about your customers and goals, recommends a platform for clear reasons, builds with speed, accessibility and search in mind, and leaves you owning everything. Ask how it would present your service area across the region.

Can a UK studio design a website for a Saskatoon business?

Yes. Website projects are planned, designed and built online, so location is not a barrier. Prism has no Canadian office; we work remotely and schedule calls in the Saskatoon morning.

How much does web design cost with Prism?

Small websites typically cost £1,000 to £2,000, and ecommerce websites £1,500 to £3,500. We can quote in Canadian dollars on request.

How long does a new website take?

A small website typically takes three to five weeks, and an ecommerce website five to twelve weeks, depending on the number of pages, products and features.

Should my Saskatoon website include pages for nearby towns?

Only where you genuinely work and have something specific to say. A few useful area pages usually work better than many thin pages that only change the place name.

Do I need a bilingual website in Saskatchewan?

Most Saskatoon businesses do not, but if you serve French-speaking customers, separate French pages written with your team or a translator are the best approach.
