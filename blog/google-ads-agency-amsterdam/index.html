Google Ads Agency for Amsterdam Businesses: Remote PPC Management

Looking for a Google Ads agency in Amsterdam? Dutch and English campaigns, tracking and consent, what a remote UK studio handles and how our fees work.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in the Netherlands; we manage Google Ads remotely, write English ads and work with your team or a translator on Dutch.
  • Management retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month, quoted in pounds with other currencies on request; ad spend is paid separately to Google.

A Google Ads agency for an Amsterdam business should turn your ad budget into qualified leads or sales, with campaigns built around how Dutch and international buyers search. Good management means clear account structure, ads in the right language, reliable conversion tracking and straightforward reporting on what the spend is producing.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that manages Google Ads remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Amsterdam or anywhere in the Netherlands. Our paid media service runs by video call and shared reports, one hour behind Amsterdam time. We write ads in English and work with your team or a translator on Dutch versions.

Why Amsterdam Google Ads campaigns need careful setup

Amsterdam has a large international business community alongside a strong local market. Technology, ecommerce, creative agencies, professional services, hospitality and logistics all compete for attention online. A few features of the market shape how campaigns should be built:

  • Dutch and English side by side. Many people in the city are comfortable in English, but plenty still search in Dutch, especially for local services and consumer purchases.
  • International reach. Many Amsterdam companies sell across Europe from day one, which affects targeting, budgets and currencies.
  • Busy visitor traffic. Tourists and business visitors search for Amsterdam terms, which matters if you only want residents or local businesses.
  • Strong ecommerce competition. Online retail is mature in the Netherlands, so Shopping and product campaigns need clean feeds and sharp pricing data.

If you run campaigns in several countries, our guide to international PPC management explains how to structure accounts across languages and markets.

Dutch and English campaigns

Google Ads language targeting works on the languages people use, not the language of your ads. An English campaign targeting the Netherlands can reach Dutch speakers whose browsers are in English, and the reverse. Mixing languages inside one ad group usually leads to weak relevance and wasted spend, so we normally build one campaign per language, each with its own keywords, ads and matching landing pages.

Prism writes ads in English in-house. For Dutch ads, we work with your team or a native-speaking writer or translator, and we handle the structure, bidding, testing and tracking. We also check that each Dutch ad leads to a Dutch page, because sending people from a Dutch ad to an English page tends to hurt results.

Location settings matter as much as language. Our Google Ads location targeting guide explains the difference between people in a location and people interested in it, which is important in a city with so many visitors.

Shopping and Performance Max for Dutch online shops

For ecommerce brands, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are often where most of the budget goes. They rely heavily on the quality of your product feed: titles, descriptions, images, prices, stock and shipping information all affect where and how often products appear.

  • Feed quality. Clear product titles in the right language, with the attributes people search for.
  • Country setup. Separate feeds or settings for each country you ship to, with correct currency and delivery details.
  • Campaign control. Splitting products by margin or performance so budget goes to what sells profitably.

Our Google Shopping feed and campaign management guide covers this in detail, and our Performance Max guide explains when it helps and when it hides problems.

Conversion tracking and consent in the Netherlands

Google Ads needs accurate conversion data to bid well. We set up conversion actions for the results that matter, such as purchases, form submissions, calls and bookings, and remove actions that inflate numbers, such as page views counted as leads. Our guide to Google Ads conversion tracking setup explains the steps.

The Netherlands follows the GDPR and EU rules on cookies, and tracking for advertising generally needs consent. Consent choices affect how much data reaches Google Ads, so tracking should be designed with consent in mind. We keep this general: check current official guidance and confirm your approach with your legal adviser, who has final approval.

Common problems in Amsterdam accounts

When we audit existing accounts, similar issues come up:

  • Dutch and English keywords mixed in the same ad group.
  • Location set to people interested in Amsterdam, so ads reach tourists who cannot buy the service.
  • Broad match keywords without enough negatives, paying for irrelevant searches.
  • Duplicate or inflated conversions, leading the bidding in the wrong direction.
  • Product feeds with missing attributes, wrong prices or poor titles.
  • Budgets spread across too many small campaigns to gather useful data.

Fixing these basics is often the fastest way to improve performance before increasing spend. Our negative keywords guide shows how to cut wasted clicks.

Landing pages that match the ad

Even a well-built campaign struggles if the landing page is slow, generic or in the wrong language. Each ad should lead to a page that reflects the search: a specific service or product, a clear headline, a single main call to action and accurate information about delivery, pricing or next steps. Pages should load quickly on mobile, where many searches happen. We review landing pages as part of every audit and suggest practical changes, and we can design new pages where needed, with Dutch copy written or reviewed by your team or a translator.
