Ecommerce Marketing in Riyadh: A Guide for Online Stores and Brands

Ecommerce marketing in Riyadh: store builds, SEO, Google Shopping, Meta and TikTok Ads, and Noon and Amazon listings to grow sales, run remotely by a UK studio.

Ecommerce marketing in Riyadh means attracting Saudi shoppers to your online store or marketplace listings and turning their visits into orders, using search, social ads, marketplaces and a store built for mobile. The capital has a large, young, mobile-first population, so brands that combine a fast store with well-tracked campaigns are usually best placed to grow.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely. We have no office in Riyadh or anywhere in Saudi Arabia. We work through video calls, shared documents and written reports. We write in English, so Arabic product copy and ad text come from your team or a professional translator. We do not shoot photos or film video. Your team or a provider supplies the visuals, and we handle the store, strategy, campaigns and tracking.

Why Riyadh matters for ecommerce

Riyadh is Saudi Arabia's largest city and a major centre for retail, fashion, beauty, electronics and food delivery. Shoppers are comfortable buying online, often on their phones, and many discover products through Instagram and TikTok before searching on Google or a marketplace. Delivery speed, clear returns and familiar payment methods all affect whether they buy.

Demand follows the calendar. Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Saudi National Day, Founding Day, back-to-school and the end-of-year sale season all bring peaks. Many campaigns succeed or fail on preparation done weeks earlier: stock, offers, landing pages and creative.

Arabic is central in Saudi ecommerce. Most successful stores offer a full Arabic experience, often alongside English. We build the structure and right-to-left layout, and your team or a translator supplies the Arabic text. If you sell across the kingdom, see our guide to Shopify in Saudi Arabia.

Build a store that converts

Before spending more on ads, the store itself needs to work well. We look at:

  • Mobile speed. Slow pages lose shoppers before they see a product.
  • Arabic and English versions. Correct right-to-left layout, with text supplied by your team or a translator.
  • Product pages. Clear photos, sizes, specifications, delivery times and prices in Saudi riyals.
  • Checkout. Few steps and the payment methods your customers expect, which may include local cards, wallets, buy now pay later and cash on delivery.
  • Trust. Easy-to-find delivery, returns and contact information.

Our guide to web design in Riyadh covers wider planning, and our guide to Shopify in Riyadh covers the platform most growing brands use. An e-commerce site costs £1,500 to £3,500 and usually takes five to twelve weeks.

Search: SEO and Google Ads

Search brings shoppers with clear intent. SEO builds lasting visibility for categories and products, while Google Shopping and search ads deliver sales quickly. We focus on category structure, product titles, helpful descriptions, internal links and technical checks. Arabic keyword research should be done with your team or translator so pages match how people actually search. See our guide to SEO in Riyadh.

For Google Ads, the product feed is everything. Titles, prices, stock and images must match the store. Our guide to Google Ads in Riyadh explains setup and bidding.

Social ads: Meta, TikTok and YouTube

Social platforms are where many Riyadh shoppers first meet a brand. Meta catalogue ads show people the products they viewed, and prospecting campaigns find new buyers. Our guide to Meta Ads in Saudi Arabia covers the details.

TikTok is strong for fashion, beauty, gadgets and gifts aimed at younger audiences. Ads that feel like normal posts usually beat polished commercials. See our guide to TikTok Ads in Saudi Arabia. YouTube is useful for products that need a demonstration and for retargeting.

We write creative briefs and manage targeting, testing and budgets. Your team or a creator supplies photos and footage, and Arabic captions come from your team or translator.

Marketplaces: Noon and Amazon

Many Saudi shoppers search Noon or Amazon before anywhere else. Listing there can add reach, though fees and competition are higher. We help with marketplace SEO: clear titles, bullet points, backend search terms, images briefed for your photographer, and pricing checks. See our guide to Noon SEO in Saudi Arabia, which also covers how the same listing principles apply on Amazon.

Your own store should remain the home of your brand. Marketplaces bring sales, but your store gives you the customer relationship and better margins.

Tracking, reporting and compliance

We set up Google Analytics 4, platform conversion tracking and server-side events where possible, then compare reported sales with your store's orders. Monthly reports show spend, revenue, cost per order and next steps.

Saudi Arabia has rules covering ecommerce, consumer protection and personal data. Keep regulatory notes general in your own planning and check official guidance or your adviser, since final compliance approval rests with your business.

Planning around peak seasons

Peak periods in Saudi ecommerce can bring a large share of a year's sales into a few weeks, so preparation matters. We usually start planning six to eight weeks ahead. That means agreeing which products to push, checking stock levels with your team, preparing landing pages and briefing your photographer or creator on the visuals needed.

During Ramadan, shopping habits shift. Many people browse later in the evening and after iftar, and gifting, food, fashion and home categories often see more interest in the run-up to Eid. Ad schedules, delivery cut-off messages and customer service hours should all reflect this. After the peak, retargeting and email help bring back shoppers who browsed but did not buy.

For sale events such as the end-of-year season, the store needs to cope with extra traffic. We check page speed, test checkout and make sure discount codes and sale prices match across the store, the product feed and marketplace listings. Mismatched prices are one of the most common reasons products get disapproved in Google Shopping.

Email, retention and repeat customers

Winning a first order is expensive, so keeping customers matters. Email and, where customers have opted in, messaging tools help bring them back. Useful flows include welcome messages, abandoned cart reminders, post-purchase follow-ups and reorder prompts for products that run out. Consent must be collected properly, and customers must be able to unsubscribe easily.

We set up and write these flows in English, and your team or translator supplies the Arabic versions. Over time, repeat customers lower your average cost per order and make paid campaigns easier to scale.

Common mistakes Riyadh stores make

  • Treating Arabic as an afterthought. A half-translated store feels untrustworthy to many Saudi shoppers.
  • Scaling ads on a slow store. Fix speed and checkout before raising budgets.
  • Unclear delivery information. Shoppers want to know when an order will arrive in their city.
  • Relying on one channel. A mix of search, social and marketplaces spreads risk.
  • Skipping tracking checks. Platform numbers should be compared with real orders.
