SEO Agency for Dublin Businesses: How Remote SEO Support Works

Looking for an SEO agency in Dublin? What good SEO covers for Irish businesses, how a remote UK studio works with you, what to ask and how pricing works.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • SEO for a Dublin business means competing for Irish searches, so keyword research, local signals and content should reflect how people in Ireland actually search, not how UK or US buyers do.
  • A remote, UK-based studio can run technical SEO, content and reporting for Irish companies, because both countries share a time zone and largely the same working week.
  • SEO results typically take three to six months to build, and retainers are priced in pounds, with other currencies such as euro quoted on request.

An SEO agency for a Dublin business should help you rank for the searches your Irish customers make and turn that visibility into enquiries, bookings or sales. Good SEO support covers technical health, keyword research for the Irish market, strong service and product pages, local visibility in maps results and honest monthly reporting.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely with businesses in Ireland and elsewhere in Europe. We do not have an office in Dublin and we will not pretend otherwise. What we offer is structured SEO and generative engine optimisation delivered online, in the same time zone as Dublin, with regular video calls and shared documents. This guide explains what to expect from any agency, what to ask before you sign and how the work is usually priced.

Why SEO for Irish businesses needs its own plan

The fundamentals of SEO are the same in every country: search engines need to crawl your site, understand it and trust it enough to show it. What changes in Ireland is the market around those fundamentals.

  • A smaller but competitive market. Ireland has fewer searches per topic than the UK, so each keyword matters more. Losing a handful of high-intent terms to a competitor can make a real difference to your pipeline.
  • UK and international sites in the same results. Irish searchers often see British retailers, global brands and directories alongside local firms. Pages that are clearly written for Ireland, with Irish pricing, delivery and contact details, help search engines and buyers see you as the relevant choice.
  • Local language and place names. People search by county, by Dublin postal district and by neighbourhood. Some services also attract searches in Irish, and certain public sector or education organisations publish in both languages.
  • Country targeting signals. A .ie domain, euro pricing, an Irish address where you genuinely have one and links from Irish sites all help reinforce that you serve Ireland.
  • AI answers sit above many listings. Search features that summarise answers reward pages that answer questions clearly and specifically. The same principles that help with AI Overviews in the UK apply equally in Ireland.

Keyword research for the Irish market

A common mistake is to copy a UK keyword list and assume it will work in Ireland. The intent may be similar, but the wording, place names and volumes are often different. Irish searchers may use different terms for some trades, services and products, and they frequently add the county or town to a search.

Good research starts with your own data where it exists: Search Console queries, enquiry forms, sales calls and the questions customers ask in person. From there we group terms by intent so each page has a clear job. A practical walkthrough is in our keyword research guide; the method is the same, but the data source should be set to Ireland.

Smaller search volumes are not a reason to skip a topic. In a market the size of Ireland, a term with modest monthly searches can still bring a steady flow of qualified enquiries, particularly for higher-value services.

Local SEO in Dublin

For any business that serves customers in person, local visibility is usually the fastest route to new enquiries. Map results appear above standard listings for many searches, and they are driven by a combination of relevance, distance and prominence.

  • Business profile. Choose accurate primary and secondary categories, add services, opening hours and photos, and keep the details consistent with your website. Our guide to Google Business Profile categories covers how to choose them.
  • Reviews. A steady flow of genuine reviews, with replies, helps both rankings and conversion.
  • Area pages. If you serve several parts of Dublin or several counties, a clear page for each genuine service area is more useful than one long list of place names.
  • Consistent details. Your name, address and phone number should match across directories and listings.

If you run several branches across Ireland, the planning gets more involved. Our guide to multi-location SEO explains how to structure pages and profiles without creating thin duplicates.

Serving Ireland and the UK from one website

Plenty of Irish companies also sell into Northern Ireland or Great Britain, and plenty of UK companies sell into Ireland. One website can serve both, but it needs to be set up so search engines understand which page is meant for which country.

That might mean separate country folders, euro and sterling versions of product pages, or hreflang tags that tell search engines an Irish page and a UK page are alternatives for different audiences. It also means avoiding near-identical pages that compete with each other. Our international SEO guide sets out the main options and their trade-offs.

Privacy, cookies and compliance

SEO work touches tracking and analytics, so it is worth knowing the broad rules. Ireland follows the EU General Data Protection Regulation and the ePrivacy rules on cookies, and the Irish Data Protection Commission publishes guidance on consent for analytics and marketing cookies. In practice, most sites need a proper consent banner before non-essential tracking runs.

We set up analytics and consent tools in line with common practice, but we are not lawyers. Rules and guidance change, so check the current official guidance or take legal advice for your specific situation. The same applies to accessibility duties under the European Accessibility Act, which affect some online businesses selling to consumers.

How a remote SEO agency works with Dublin clients

Working with a studio that is not in the same city is now normal for SEO, because almost all of the work happens on your website, in analytics tools and in shared documents. What makes it work is structure.

  1. Kick-off call. We agree goals, priority services or products, target areas and how you measure success.
  2. Access. You grant access to Search Console, analytics, your business profile and the website, using your own accounts so you keep ownership.
  3. Audit and plan. We run a technical and content audit and turn it into a prioritised plan for the first three months.
  4. Monthly delivery. Fixes, page improvements, new content and local work are delivered against the plan, with drafts shared for your approval.
  5. Reporting. A monthly report and call cover what changed, what moved and what comes next. Our guide to SEO reporting shows what a useful report includes.

Ireland and the UK share a time zone for most of the year, which makes calls and quick questions easy to schedule. Invoicing is in pounds by default, and other currencies such as euro can be quoted on request. VAT treatment on cross-border services depends on your circumstances, so confirm it with your accountant.

Questions to ask any SEO agency in Dublin

Whether you choose a local Dublin agency or a remote studio, the same questions help you separate solid offers from vague ones.

  • What will you do in the first three months, in order, and why?
  • Will you research Irish search data specifically, or reuse UK or US keyword lists?
  • Who owns the accounts, content and any links built during the engagement?
  • How do you report, and will the report connect rankings to enquiries or sales?
  • What notice period applies if we want to stop?
  • How do you approach AI search features and answer engines?

Be wary of anyone promising a guaranteed first position or a fixed number of links per month regardless of quality. Neither is in your interest.

FAQs

Do you have an office in Dublin?

No. Prism Works Studio is based in the UK and works remotely with clients in Ireland and across Europe. Work is delivered through video calls, shared documents and direct access to your website and analytics accounts.

How do I choose an SEO agency in Dublin?

Ask for a clear three-month plan, confirmation that keyword research uses Irish data, transparent reporting and fair contract terms. Avoid guaranteed rankings and link packages sold by volume. Whether the agency is local or remote matters less than how clearly it explains the work.

Can you work on a .ie website?

Yes. The domain ending does not change how we work. A .ie domain can be a helpful signal that you serve Ireland, and we can also advise on setting up country sections on a .com site if you serve several markets.

Do you invoice in euro?

Our published prices are in pounds. Other currencies, including euro, can be quoted on request. VAT treatment for cross-border services depends on your business, so please check it with your accountant.

How long does SEO take to work in Ireland?

Results typically take three to six months to build. Technical fixes and improvements to existing pages can show earlier, while new content and local visibility take longer to mature, especially in competitive Dublin sectors.

Do you help with Google Business Profile for Irish locations?

Yes. Local visibility work covers your business profile, categories, services, photos, review responses and consistent details across directories, alongside the on-site pages that support each location you genuinely serve.
