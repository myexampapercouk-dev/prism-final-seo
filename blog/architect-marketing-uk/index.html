Architect Marketing UK: How Practices Win Better Projects Online

Architect marketing UK guide for small and mid-sized practices: SEO, portfolio websites, project case studies, LinkedIn, PR and winning the right clients.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clients judge architects on their work, so a fast, well-organised portfolio website is the foundation.
  • SEO for project types and locations helps homeowners and commercial clients find you when they start planning.
  • Case studies, PR and LinkedIn build the credibility that turns interest into appointments.

Architect marketing in the UK is about getting the right projects, not just more enquiries. A small or mid-sized practice usually has a clear idea of the work it wants: residential extensions, new-build homes, heritage projects, commercial fit-outs, education buildings or something more specialist. Good marketing makes that focus obvious, shows the work clearly and helps the right clients find you when they start planning.

This guide is for principals and marketing leads at architecture practices. It covers positioning, websites, SEO, case studies, PR, LinkedIn and paid channels, with notes on measuring what matters.

Why architect marketing is different

Architecture is a considered, high-value service with a long decision cycle. Homeowners may research for months before contacting a practice, often comparing portfolios and fee approaches. Commercial and public sector clients may involve committees, frameworks and formal tenders. In both cases, trust and evidence of relevant work matter more than slogans.

Practices also face a common trap: a website that looks beautiful but says very little. Large images without context, project pages with no description, and vague statements about design philosophy make it hard for clients and search engines to understand what you actually do. The best architect marketing combines strong visuals with clear, specific words.

Professional bodies may have guidance on how architects describe their services and qualifications. The title architect is protected in the UK and may only be used by those on the statutory register, so check current requirements before describing team members or services, and take advice if unsure.

Positioning: be clear about the work you want

Before changing channels, decide what you want to be known for. A practice that says it does everything struggles to stand out. Consider:

  • Project types. Which sectors and scales do you want more of?
  • Locations. Where do you want to work, and where can you realistically deliver?
  • Client types. Homeowners, developers, schools, businesses or public bodies?
  • Strengths. Specialisms such as sustainability, conservation, planning-sensitive sites or complex briefs.

Your homepage, service pages and case studies should reflect those choices. Our brand strategy guide explains how to sharpen positioning, and our brand strategy service can help.

A portfolio website that explains, not just shows

Your website is your main sales tool. Clients want to see work similar to their project and understand how you would approach it. A strong architecture website usually includes:

  • Clear service pages for each project type, such as house extensions, new homes or commercial projects.
  • Project pages with written context: the brief, the constraints, the approach and the outcome, alongside photography.
  • Filtering by project type or sector so visitors find relevant work quickly.
  • An explanation of how you work, from first conversation through design stages to completion, described in your own terms.
  • A clear contact route with a short form asking about project type, location and timescale.
  • Fast loading despite large images, with photography compressed and sized properly.

Our website speed guide explains how to keep image-heavy sites fast, and we build sites through our website and ecommerce development service.

SEO for architects

People search for architects in predictable ways: a project type plus a place, such as an extension architect in a town, or broader questions about planning, costs and design. SEO helps your practice appear for those searches.

  • Service and location pages. Target the project types and areas you want, with genuine local detail where you have worked.
  • Project pages with words. Descriptive text on each project helps them rank for relevant searches.
  • Helpful guides. Content explaining planning, permitted development or how design stages work can attract early-stage clients. Keep any regulatory explanations general and hedged, and point readers to official sources or professional advice.
  • Google Business Profile. A complete, accurate profile helps local searches and reviews.

Case studies and credibility

Case studies are where interest becomes confidence. A good case study explains the client's situation, the challenges, the decisions you made and the result. Include the people involved, with their permission, and be accurate about your role, especially where other consultants or contractors contributed.

Testimonials should be genuine and approved by the client. Avoid claiming awards, accreditations or results you cannot evidence. If a project has received recognition, describe it accurately.

Digital PR and LinkedIn

Completed projects, new approaches and expert commentary can all make good stories for design, property and local publications. Coverage builds credibility and can produce valuable links. For commercial work, LinkedIn is often where relationships form. Principals and senior team members sharing project insights, lessons and work in progress can keep the practice visible with developers, clients and collaborators. Paid search for architects

Google Ads can work for residential services with clear demand, such as extensions or loft conversions in a defined area. Keep campaigns focused on the project types you want, use negative keywords to filter out jobs and courses, and send traffic to relevant service pages. Common mistakes to avoid

Many practices hold themselves back with a few recurring issues. Watch out for these:

  • Image-only project pages. Beautiful photography with no words gives search engines and clients little to work with.
  • Too many priorities. Trying to appeal to every sector at once dilutes the message and the budget.
  • Outdated work. A portfolio that has not been updated for years can suggest the practice is less active than it is.
  • No follow-up process. Enquiries that wait days for a reply often go elsewhere, especially for residential clients comparing several practices.
  • Vague calls to action. Tell visitors exactly what to do next, such as booking an initial conversation, and what information to have ready.
  • Ignoring mobile. Many homeowners browse portfolios on a phone, so test galleries and forms on small screens.

Fixing these basics often delivers more than adding a new channel, and it makes every other marketing activity work harder.

Measuring what works

FAQs

What is the best marketing for an architecture practice?

For most practices, a clear positioning, a well-organised portfolio website with written project context, and SEO for the project types and locations you want form the foundation. PR and LinkedIn build credibility, especially for commercial work.

Do architects need SEO?

If you want enquiries from people researching projects online, SEO helps. Homeowners often search for project types plus a location, and descriptive service and project pages give you a chance to appear.

How should architects write case studies?

Explain the brief, the constraints, your approach and the outcome, alongside photography. Be accurate about your role and get client approval before publishing.

Is Instagram useful for architects?

It can be, particularly for residential work and visual discovery. Link posts back to detailed project pages on your website so interest can turn into enquiries.

Can architects use Google Ads?

Yes, especially for residential services with clear local demand. Keep targeting tight and judge results on enquiry quality as well as volume.

How long does architect marketing take to work?

SEO and PR usually take several months to build. Because architecture projects have long decision cycles, review results over a quarter or longer.
