Mortgage Broker Marketing in the UK: Lead Generation, SEO and Compliance Sign-Off

Mortgage broker marketing in the UK rests on local SEO, useful content and focused paid search, with every promotion signed off by the firm before use.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Own your leads through local SEO, helpful content and referrals rather than relying only on bought-in lead lists.
  • Paid search works best on specific, high-intent terms such as first-time buyer, remortgage and buy-to-let in your area.
  • Websites, ads and social posts are financial promotions, so the firm or its network must approve them before publishing.

Mortgage broker marketing in the UK works best when you build your own lead sources: local SEO, helpful content for buyers and remortgagers, and focused paid search, supported by referrals from estate agents and past clients. Because your website, ads and social posts count as financial promotions, every piece should be approved by your firm or network before it goes live.

This guide covers where mortgage leads come from, how to win them through search, how to handle paid campaigns, and how to build compliance sign-off into your process. It is general information, not regulatory advice. For how we work with regulated firms, see our financial adviser and mortgage broker marketing.

Where mortgage broker leads come from

Most brokers rely on a mix of sources. Some are cheap but slow to build; others are fast but expensive or of mixed quality.

Lead sourceSpeedCost patternQuality and control
Referrals from clientsSlow to buildLowHigh; depends on service and follow-up
Estate agent and introducer partnershipsMediumVaries by arrangementGood, but you depend on the partner
Local SEO and contentSlow, typically 3 to 6 monthsOngoing time or retainerHigh; leads come to you directly
Paid searchFastAd spend per click, paid separatelyGood on specific terms, weaker on broad ones
Bought-in leadsFastPer leadMixed; often shared with other brokers

A healthy mix reduces dependence on any one source. Many brokers start with referrals and bought leads, then gradually shift towards channels they own, such as search.

Local SEO for mortgage brokers

People often search with a place in mind: "mortgage broker Reading", "mortgage adviser near me", "first-time buyer mortgage advice Glasgow". Ranking for these depends on local signals and a site that clearly explains what you do.

  • Complete your Google Business Profile with the right category, service area, opening hours and a link to an enquiry page. Our Google Business Profile guide covers the settings.
  • Create service pages for each main need: first-time buyers, remortgages, buy-to-let, home movers and self-employed applicants.
  • Mention the areas you cover naturally, and add location pages only where you have genuine local detail to share.
  • Collect reviews from clients, and respond professionally without discussing their circumstances.
  • Keep regulatory disclosures and your firm details clear and consistent across the site.

Our local SEO guide and financial adviser SEO guide go into more depth on ranking in regulated sectors.

Content that earns enquiries

Buyers and homeowners have lots of questions before they speak to a broker. Content that answers them well can rank, build trust and bring in enquiries over time.

  • How the mortgage process works, step by step, for first-time buyers.
  • What to do when a fixed rate is ending and the options for remortgaging.
  • Mortgages for self-employed people and contractors, and what lenders usually ask for.
  • Buy-to-let basics and what landlords should consider.
  • What a broker does, how you are paid and what clients can expect.

Keep content factual and balanced. Avoid promising approval, the "best rates" or savings you cannot evidence, and include any risk warnings your firm requires. Content of this kind is still a financial promotion, so the same sign-off process applies.

Paid search for mortgage leads

Google Ads can generate enquiries quickly, but mortgage keywords are competitive. Focus on specific, high-intent terms in your area rather than broad national phrases, and send each ad to a matching landing page rather than your homepage.

Financial services ads are subject to Google's policies, which may require advertiser verification and additional disclosures. Check the current requirements before you launch. Ad spend is paid separately from any management fee. Our paid media service covers Google and social campaigns, and our landing page guide explains how to turn clicks into enquiries.

Compliance sign-off: build it into the process

Websites, ads, social posts, emails and even some videos from regulated firms are financial promotions. They must be fair, clear and not misleading, and they usually need approval under your firm's or network's procedures before publishing. Appointed representatives typically need their principal firm to approve promotions.

The simplest way to avoid delays is to make sign-off part of your workflow rather than an afterthought.

  • Agree who approves what, and how long approval normally takes.
  • Prepare content in batches so it can be reviewed together.
  • Keep a record of approved versions and when they were approved.
  • Reuse approved wording for disclosures and risk warnings.
  • Review evergreen pages periodically, especially when products or rules change.

We draft and edit with these rules in mind, but we are not a regulated firm and cannot approve promotions. Final sign-off always rests with the broker firm or its network. Our guide to marketing for financial advisers within the rules covers this in more detail.

Working with estate agents and introducers

Introducer relationships remain one of the most valuable sources of mortgage business, and digital marketing can strengthen them rather than replace them. A clear website that explains how you work with buyers gives agents confidence to refer, and a simple referral page or form makes the handover easy. Some brokers create short guides for buyers that agents can share, such as a checklist of documents to gather before applying.

Keep any referral arrangements transparent and in line with your firm's rules on disclosure, and make sure shared materials have been approved like any other promotion. Track referrals by source so you can see which partnerships bring cases that complete.

Social media for mortgage brokers

Social media works best as a trust builder rather than a direct lead source. Short explanations of the buying process, reminders for clients approaching the end of a fixed rate and local property market context can keep you front of mind with past clients and their friends. LinkedIn suits brokers who work with professionals, landlords or business owners, while Facebook and Instagram suit first-time buyer audiences.

Every post is a potential financial promotion, so batch posts for approval, avoid rate claims that date quickly and include required warnings where needed. Consistency matters more than volume: a few approved, useful posts each month usually beat daily posting that is hard to keep compliant.

Tracking leads and conversions

Track enquiry forms, phone calls and booked appointments as conversions, and record the source of each case. Over time, compare cost per completed case by channel, not just cost per lead, because cheap leads that never complete are expensive in practice. Mortgage broker marketing checklist

  • List your current lead sources and what each costs per completed case.
  • Complete your Google Business Profile and keep firm details consistent.
  • Create service pages for first-time buyers, remortgages, buy-to-let and other key needs.
  • Plan helpful content around real client questions.
  • Agree a sign-off process with your firm or network and keep approval records.
  • Check advertising platform requirements for financial services.
  • Run paid search on specific local terms with matching landing pages.
  • Track enquiries, calls and appointments as conversions.
  • Ask satisfied clients for reviews and referrals.
  • Review results quarterly and shift budget towards channels you own.

FAQs

What works best for mortgage broker marketing in the UK?

A mix of referrals, local SEO, helpful content and focused paid search usually works best. Building channels you own reduces reliance on bought-in leads, and every promotion should be signed off by your firm or network.

Are mortgage broker websites financial promotions?

Generally, yes. Websites, ads, social posts and emails from regulated firms are usually treated as financial promotions and need to be fair, clear and not misleading. Check your firm's procedures for approval.

Who approves our marketing?

Your firm, or your principal firm or network if you are an appointed representative. An agency can draft and edit content with the rules in mind, but cannot approve financial promotions.

Should mortgage brokers buy leads?

Bought leads can fill gaps, but quality varies and leads may be shared with other brokers. Most brokers benefit from building their own sources alongside them.

Can mortgage brokers run Google Ads?

Yes, within Google's financial services policies, which may include verification and disclosure requirements. Focus on specific local terms and track cost per completed case.

How long does SEO take for a mortgage broker?

SEO typically takes three to six months to show meaningful results, and longer for competitive city terms. Local and niche terms often move sooner.
