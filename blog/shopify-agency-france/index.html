What a Shopify agency in France should do

French e-commerce covers everything from fashion, beauty and homeware brands to food and drink producers, sports equipment and specialist B2B suppliers. Whatever you sell, a good Shopify partner should handle:

  • Store design. A clean, fast theme adapted to your brand, with clear collections and product pages.
  • French-language setup. Proper translations, French URLs where sensible and correct language settings in Shopify Markets.
  • Payments and delivery. Card payments, digital wallets and the delivery options French customers recognise, such as home delivery and pick-up points.
  • Migration. Moving products, customers and order history from another platform without losing search visibility.
  • Growth. SEO, Google Ads, Meta Ads and conversion improvements once the store is live.

Selling to French shoppers on Shopify

French shoppers expect a store that feels local. That means natural French copy rather than literal translation, prices shown in euros with tax included, clear delivery times and costs, and an easy returns process. Many shoppers like pick-up point delivery through relay networks, so check which carriers and apps support this for your store.

Cartes Bancaires is widely used for card payments in France. Shopify Payments and other providers can support it, but confirm availability for your account and business setup. Digital wallets such as Apple Pay and Google Pay help on mobile.

If you sell in France and other countries, Shopify Markets lets you manage languages, currencies and pricing per market from one store. Our selling internationally guide covers the decisions involved, and our multilingual website design article explains language structure and hreflang.

Migrating a French store to Shopify

Many French brands start on PrestaShop, WooCommerce or another platform and move to Shopify for simpler hosting and maintenance. A careful migration protects your search rankings and customer data.

  1. Audit. We review your current catalogue, URLs, apps and integrations.
  2. Data migration. Products, variants, customers and order history move across in a planned way.
  3. Redirects. Every important old URL is redirected to its new equivalent so search engines and bookmarks still work.
  4. Testing. We test checkout, tax, delivery rules and translations before launch.
  5. Monitoring. After launch we watch Search Console for errors and fix them quickly.

Our WooCommerce to Shopify migration guide covers the process in more detail. For larger brands, our Shopify Plus migration article explains when Plus is worth considering.

Compliance notes for French Shopify stores

This is general information, not legal advice. Check official guidance or speak to your own adviser.

  • GDPR. French businesses are subject to the EU General Data Protection Regulation. Your store needs a clear privacy notice and a lawful basis for collecting customer data, including for email marketing.
  • Cookie consent. Under EU ePrivacy rules, non-essential cookies such as analytics and advertising pixels generally need consent first, and refusing should be as easy as accepting. Our cookie banner design article covers practical options.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing services, including e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt. EN 301 549, which refers to WCAG at level AA, is the usual technical reference.
  • Consumer information. EU consumer rules cover pricing, withdrawal rights and returns information. Your legal pages should be reviewed by your own adviser.

For regulated products such as supplements, cosmetics claims or alcohol, approval of product content rests with your compliance team.

Choosing a Shopify partner for France

Whichever agency you choose, ask:

  • Will you own the store, theme changes, domain and data?
  • How will French copy and translations be handled?
  • Which payment and delivery options will be set up, and have they been confirmed for your account?
  • How will redirects and SEO be protected during a migration?
  • What support is available after launch?

A local French agency may suit you if you want French-language meetings. A remote UK studio can suit brands comfortable working in English, especially those selling in both the UK and France.

Common Shopify mistakes for French stores

Several problems come up again and again when we review stores selling into France. The first is translation quality. Automatic translation apps can be a useful starting point, but product descriptions, size guides and policy pages that read awkwardly in French reduce trust and conversion. A professional translator or a French-speaking team member should review every key page.

The second is incomplete delivery information. French shoppers want to know delivery times, costs and pick-up point options before they reach checkout. Hiding this until the final step leads to abandoned baskets.

The third is app overload. Stores often install many apps for reviews, upsells, pop-ups and translations, and each one adds scripts that slow the store down. A regular Shopify apps audit keeps the store fast.

The fourth is consent. Advertising pixels and analytics often fire before visitors have agreed, which creates compliance risk. A properly configured consent banner, connected to Shopify's customer privacy settings, solves this.

Finally, many brands launch without a plan for collections and filters. Clear collection structure helps shoppers browse and helps search engines understand your range. Good structure at launch is far easier than reorganising hundreds of products later, so we plan collections, tags and filters during discovery rather than after the store is live.

None of these problems is hard to fix, but each one quietly costs sales. Tackling them before launch, or in the first months after a migration, gives your French store a stronger base for every campaign that follows, from search and Shopping ads to social media and email.
