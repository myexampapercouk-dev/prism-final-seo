How search works for Sunderland businesses

Sunderland has a distinctive economy. Advanced manufacturing and automotive supply chains sit alongside a university, healthcare, logistics, a growing digital sector, retail and a large number of trades and independent service firms. Many businesses here serve customers across Washington, Houghton-le-Spring, Seaham, South Shields and into Durham and Newcastle.

That shapes how people search. A homeowner may look for a "boiler repair Sunderland" or an "electrician Washington". A business buyer might search for a component supplier, a commercial cleaner or an accountant "in the North East". Your SEO plan should reflect those real searches rather than chasing broad national terms that bring the wrong visitors.

Local SEO across Wearside

For many Sunderland businesses, the Google map results bring more calls than standard listings. Showing there depends on how close you are to the searcher, how relevant your profile is and how prominent your business appears online.

  • Complete your profile. Accurate categories, services, opening hours, photos and a clear description. Our guide to the Google Business Profile in the UK covers each section.
  • Keep details consistent. Your business name, address and phone number should match across your website and the directories you are listed on.
  • Ask for reviews fairly. Request honest reviews from real customers and reply to them politely, including the critical ones.
  • Write useful area content. Explain how you serve the places you genuinely cover, rather than creating thin pages that only swap the town name.

Content that answers real questions

Content is how you earn rankings for the searches that lead to enquiries. We research the terms and questions your customers use, then plan pages that answer them clearly. That might mean a stronger page for each core service, a guide to costs, or answers to the questions your team hears every week.

We write in plain UK English, answer the question near the top of the page and use headings, tables and lists so readers can scan. This style also helps your pages appear in AI-generated answers. Common SEO mistakes we see on Wearside websites

When we audit websites for North East businesses, the same problems come up again and again. None of them are unusual, and most can be fixed without starting again from scratch.

  • One page trying to rank for everything. A single services page listing every job you do rarely ranks well for any of them. Separate pages for your main services give Google a clear match for each search.
  • Thin or copied location pages. Pages for Washington, Seaham and Houghton-le-Spring that only swap the town name add little value and can weaken the whole site.
  • Missing page titles and descriptions. Many sites still use the business name alone as every page title, which wastes one of the strongest on-page signals.
  • Slow mobile pages. Large images, heavy page builders and too many plugins slow sites down, especially on mobile data.
  • No tracking of enquiries. Without form and call tracking, it is impossible to tell whether SEO is producing real business or just traffic.
  • Old redirects left broken. After a previous redesign, old URLs often point nowhere, losing the value of links built up over years.

Fixing these basics is often the first month of our work. Once the foundations are sound, content and local authority have a much better chance of moving rankings.
