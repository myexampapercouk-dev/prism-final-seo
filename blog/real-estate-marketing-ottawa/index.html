Who this guide is for

Ottawa's property market is shaped by the federal government, a strong technology sector, universities and hospitals, plus suburban growth across communities such as Kanata, Orléans, Barrhaven and Nepean. Buyers include public servants, relocating professionals, families, students and investors, and many transactions involve people moving from elsewhere in Canada. This guide is for:

  • Independent agents and teams building their own brand and lead flow.
  • Brokerages supporting agents with websites and campaigns.
  • Developers and builders marketing new homes and condos.
  • Property managers attracting landlords and tenants.
  • Mortgage and related services, where compliance rules apply.

Websites that win trust and leads

Real estate buyers and sellers research heavily before contacting anyone. A good website shows who you are, which areas you know, how you work and what clients can expect. Key pages include neighbourhood guides, buying and selling guides, a home valuation request, an about page and clear contact options.

Listing data is usually provided through board or brokerage feeds, which come with their own rules on display. We build sites that can work with your feed provider, but you should confirm what your board and brokerage permit. Our guide to web design in Ottawa covers our wider approach to local sites.

Local SEO for neighbourhoods and communities

People search for homes by area, so neighbourhood pages are central to real estate SEO. Useful pages explain housing types, transport links, schools in general terms, parks, amenities and what kind of buyer each area tends to suit. Thin pages that swap one place name for another rarely rank and do little for visitors.

Your Google Business Profile also matters. Accurate categories, regular photos, honest reviews and posts about open houses or market updates all help. Our guides to an SEO agency in Ottawa and local SEO in Canada go into more detail, and our SEO and GEO services page explains how we also prepare content for AI-powered search tools.

Google and Meta ads for real estate

Paid campaigns bring faster results while SEO builds. Google Ads works well for high-intent searches such as home valuations or agents in a specific community. Meta Ads works well for open houses, new developments and guides that attract people early in their search.

Housing ads on Meta and Google fall under special policies designed to prevent discrimination, which limit targeting by age, gender and postal code in many cases. We plan campaigns within these rules. Our guides to a Google Ads agency in Ottawa and a Meta ads agency in Ottawa explain how campaigns are run in the city.

English and French content

Ottawa sits on the border with Gatineau, Quebec, and many residents speak both English and French. If you serve French-speaking clients or market across the river, bilingual content can help. Prism writes in English. French pages, ads and emails should be written or checked by your own team or a professional translator. We then build the structure, language tags and campaigns so each version is found by the right people. If you market in Quebec, Quebec's language rules may also apply, so check official guidance.

Privacy, consent and industry rules

Real estate marketing involves valuation forms, email lists and CRM data. In general terms, PIPEDA is the federal privacy law that covers many private-sector businesses, CASL sets rules for commercial electronic messages such as marketing emails and texts, and some provinces have their own privacy laws. Quebec's Law 25 adds specific requirements where you deal with people in Quebec. Real estate is also regulated provincially, with rules on advertising and how agents present themselves, and boards set rules for listing data. These notes are general only. Please check official guidance and speak to your own adviser, brokerage or regulator. Compliance approval for your marketing rests with you.

Lead follow-up and nurturing

Many real estate leads are not ready to act for months. A buyer browsing neighbourhood guides in spring may not book a viewing until autumn, and a homeowner requesting a valuation may be planning a move next year. The agents who win these clients are usually the ones who follow up quickly and then stay in touch with helpful information rather than pressure.

We help set up simple systems for this: form notifications that reach the right person quickly, tags in your CRM to track where each lead came from, and email sequences with market updates, neighbourhood news and practical guides for people who have agreed to hear from you. The aim is a steady, respectful process that means fewer leads go cold.

Content ideas for Ottawa agents

Useful content builds trust and search visibility at the same time. Ideas that suit the Ottawa market include guides for people relocating to the city for government or technology roles, comparisons of suburban communities, explanations of the buying and selling process in Ontario in general terms, first-time buyer checklists, condo buying considerations and seasonal market commentary written in your own words. Each piece should answer real questions your clients ask, and link to your service and contact pages.

Video works well too. Short neighbourhood tours, open house walk-throughs and answers to common questions can be used on your website, on social media and in ads. You or your team film locally, and we help plan, edit and distribute the content.

Before publishing any market commentary, check the facts against official or board sources and keep claims general. Readers value honesty, and accurate content protects your reputation as well as your rankings over the long term.
