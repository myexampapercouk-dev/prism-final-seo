Choosing a B2B Marketing Agency in the UK: Channels, Lead Generation and Long Sales Cycles

A B2B marketing agency in the UK builds qualified pipeline through SEO, LinkedIn, Google Ads and content. What it does, what it costs and how to choose.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good B2B marketing agency measures success in qualified leads and pipeline, not clicks or impressions, and agrees what counts as a qualified lead before spending anything.
  • Most UK B2B firms do best with a small mix of channels: search for buyers already looking, LinkedIn for reaching named job titles, and content that helps a buying committee decide.
  • Long sales cycles mean results compound slowly, so expect organic work to take 3 to 6 months and judge paid channels on lead quality over several months.

A B2B marketing agency in the UK helps businesses that sell to other businesses generate qualified enquiries and sales pipeline, usually through a mix of SEO, LinkedIn and Google Ads, useful content and a website built to convert. The right agency plans around your sales cycle and your buying committee, and reports on leads and pipeline rather than vanity metrics.

This guide explains what a B2B marketing agency actually does, which channels tend to work for UK firms, how long sales cycles change the plan, what it typically costs and how to choose a partner you will not regret hiring.

What does a B2B marketing agency UK firms hire actually do?

B2B marketing is different from consumer marketing in a few important ways. Fewer people buy, but each deal is worth more. Decisions are made by groups, not individuals. And the gap between a first visit and a signed contract can be weeks or many months. A specialist agency builds its work around those facts.

In practice, the work usually covers:

  • Positioning and messaging: making it obvious who you serve, what problem you solve and why you are a safer choice than the alternatives. Our brand strategy service covers this groundwork.
  • Search visibility: ranking for the terms buyers use when they research suppliers, and increasingly being cited in AI answers. See our SEO and GEO service.
  • Paid acquisition: Google Ads for high-intent searches and LinkedIn Ads for reaching specific job titles, industries and company sizes, run through our paid media service.
  • Content and digital PR: guides, comparison pages and thought leadership that answer the questions a buying committee asks.
  • Website and conversion: clear service pages, credible proof, simple enquiry forms and tracking that connects leads to revenue.

Why search usually comes first

Many B2B buyers start by searching for a problem or a type of supplier. If your site does not show up for those searches, you are invisible at the moment a buyer is actively looking. Search also tends to produce leads that are further along in their thinking than leads from social channels. That is why many B2B plans start with search, then add LinkedIn to reach accounts that are not searching yet.

Where LinkedIn fits

LinkedIn is useful when you can describe your buyer precisely by job title, seniority, industry or company size. It is less useful when your audience is broad or when the deal value is too small to justify higher click costs. Lead gen forms make conversion easy, but that ease can also attract low-intent submissions, so qualification matters.

How long sales cycles change the marketing plan

If deals take three, six or twelve months to close, judging marketing on last month's sales will mislead you. A sensible B2B plan does three things differently.

  1. Agree a lead definition. Marketing and sales should agree what a marketing qualified lead and a sales qualified lead look like before any campaign goes live.
  2. Track the whole journey. Use CRM fields and UTM tags so a lead from a LinkedIn ad in March can still be credited when the deal closes in September.
  3. Feed the middle of the funnel. Case-style explainers, pricing guidance, comparison pages and FAQs help buyers who are already interested make the case internally.

Leading indicators such as qualified enquiries, meetings booked and pipeline value help you judge progress long before revenue lands. An agency that only reports clicks and impressions is not giving you what you need.

Checklist: what to ask before hiring a B2B agency

  • Do they ask about your average deal value, sales cycle and buying committee before proposing channels?
  • Will they agree a written definition of a qualified lead with your sales team?
  • Can they connect leads to your CRM so pipeline and revenue can be tracked?
  • Do they explain why each channel is in the plan, and what they would cut if budget were tight?
  • Is ad spend kept separate from the management fee and visible in your own ad accounts?
  • Will you own the ad accounts, analytics and website content if you part ways?
  • Do monthly reports cover leads, lead quality and pipeline, not only traffic?
  • Are they clear that organic results typically take 3 to 6 months?

Common mistakes UK B2B firms make with agencies

The same problems come up again and again. Spreading a small budget across five channels means none gets enough to learn from. Sending paid traffic to a generic home page wastes clicks that should land on a focused service page. Gated content with long forms can collect emails but put off senior buyers. And without CRM tracking, nobody can prove which activity created the deals that closed.

Another common issue is copy written for the wrong reader. Technical teams want specifics, finance teams want risk and cost clarity, and decision-makers want confidence that you have done this before. Good B2B content speaks to each of them, often on different pages.

Finally, many firms underinvest in the website. Strong campaigns cannot rescue a slow, unclear site with no obvious next step. If your site needs work, our website development service covers lead-focused builds.

Is a specialist B2B agency worth it?

A generalist agency can run competent campaigns, but B2B marketing rewards understanding of buying committees, long cycles and lead qualification. If your deals are high value and your buyers are specialists, an agency that thinks in pipeline rather than clicks is usually the better fit. If your offer is simple and transactional, a generalist may be enough.

FAQs

What does a B2B marketing agency in the UK do?

It helps businesses that sell to other businesses generate qualified leads and pipeline, usually through SEO, Google Ads, LinkedIn Ads, content and a conversion-focused website, with reporting tied to leads and sales rather than traffic alone.

How much does a B2B marketing agency cost?

It depends on scope. Our retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month, with ad spend paid separately to the platforms. Wider market prices vary considerably between agencies.

Is LinkedIn or Google Ads better for B2B?

Google Ads suits buyers already searching for a solution, while LinkedIn suits reaching specific job titles and sectors who may not be searching yet. Many firms use both, starting with whichever matches how their buyers behave.

How long does B2B marketing take to work?

Paid campaigns can produce leads within weeks, while SEO and content typically take 3 to 6 months to build momentum. Long sales cycles mean revenue often lags behind lead generation.

What is a qualified B2B lead?

A qualified lead fits your target profile and shows genuine buying intent, for example the right company size, role and need. The exact definition should be agreed between marketing and sales before campaigns start.

Do I need a new website before hiring a B2B agency?

Not always. Many sites need clearer service pages, better forms and proper tracking rather than a full rebuild. An honest agency will tell you which fixes matter most before suggesting new work.
