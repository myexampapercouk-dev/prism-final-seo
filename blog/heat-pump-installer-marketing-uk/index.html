Heat Pump Installer Marketing in the UK: How to Win More Surveys and Installs

Heat pump installer marketing in the UK works when local SEO, honest cost pages, clear grant explainers and easy survey booking win over cautious homeowners.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Heat pump buyers research for weeks, so pages that explain suitability, running costs, grants and the install process do more selling than any single advert.
  • Local SEO and a well-kept Google Business Profile win the high-intent searches, while tightly controlled Google Ads fill gaps in quieter months.
  • Trust is the deciding factor: show your accreditations accurately, explain the survey honestly and make booking a home assessment simple.

Heat pump installer marketing in the UK is about earning trust with homeowners who are curious but cautious, then making it easy for them to book a home survey. The installers who grow steadily usually pair strong local search visibility with plain-English pages on suitability, costs, grants and the installation process, so that by the time someone calls they already understand what happens next.

This guide is for independent heating engineers moving into renewables, established plumbing and heating firms adding air source and ground source heat pumps, and specialist low-carbon installers who want more qualified enquiries. We cover how people search, what your website needs, where paid ads fit and how to stand out in a market full of national brands and lead-generation sites.

How homeowners look for a heat pump installer

Very few people wake up and buy a heat pump on the same day. A typical journey starts months earlier: an old boiler is on its last legs, energy bills have climbed, a neighbour has had one fitted, or a news story mentions a grant. The homeowner starts with research searches such as "are heat pumps worth it", "heat pump running costs" or "will a heat pump work in an old house". Later the searches turn local and commercial: "heat pump installer near me", "air source heat pump [town]" and "heat pump installation cost".

That longer journey changes how you market. You need content that helps people during the research phase, a reason for them to remember your name, and a strong local presence when they finally search for an installer. Our wider guide to marketing for tradespeople covers the basics that apply to every trade; this post focuses on what is specific to heat pumps.

Build a website that answers the hard questions

Heat pump buyers arrive with doubts. Will my house be warm enough? Do I need new radiators? Where does the outdoor unit go? Will it be noisy? How long does the install take? What happens to my hot water cylinder? Your website should answer these directly, in your own words, rather than leaving people to piece it together from forums.

A practical structure for most installers looks like this:

  • Air source heat pumps: what they are, which homes suit them, what the survey checks and what a typical install involves.
  • Ground source heat pumps: if you offer them, explain boreholes versus trenches and why projects take longer.
  • Grants and funding: a general explainer of the schemes that may apply, with a clear note to check current official guidance.
  • Costs: the factors that move the price, such as property size, radiator upgrades, cylinder replacement and pipework.
  • Our process: survey, heat loss calculation, design, quote, install, commissioning and aftercare.
  • Area pages: one page for each main town you cover, with local job photos and relevant notes.

If your current site is a single page with a phone number, a rebuild is usually the biggest single improvement. Explain grants carefully and accurately

Grant schemes are one of the biggest reasons people start researching, and they are also one of the easiest places to make mistakes. Schemes change, eligibility rules shift and amounts get revised. A page that confidently promises a figure that is no longer right damages trust quickly and could mislead customers.

Write your grant content in general terms. Explain what the main scheme is for, who it is broadly aimed at, and how you help customers through the process. Then tell readers to check the current official government guidance for eligibility and amounts, and date-stamp the page so you remember to review it. If you are not certain whether you can claim a grant on a customer's behalf, say so plainly and explain what you will check during the survey.

Show accreditations honestly

Installer accreditation and membership of consumer protection schemes matter a great deal to heat pump buyers, partly because some funding routes depend on them. Display the logos and registration details you genuinely hold, link the reader to where they can verify them, and avoid implying you hold anything you do not. If you work with a partner who holds certification on your behalf, explain that arrangement clearly rather than hiding it.

The same applies to warranties. Separate the manufacturer's warranty from your own workmanship guarantee and say what each covers. Clear, specific wording reassures people far more than a row of badges.

Local SEO for heat pump installers

When research turns into buying, most searches are local. That makes your Google Business Profile and town-level pages your most valuable organic assets. Choose accurate primary and secondary categories, set a realistic service area, upload photos of real installs (outdoor units, cylinders, neat pipework), and ask every happy customer for a review that mentions the type of job. Our Google Business Profile guide and advice on getting more Google reviews walk through the details.

On your site, each area page should be genuinely useful: housing stock you commonly see in that town, typical project types, local photos and a short note on how quickly you can survey. Thin pages that swap one town name for another rarely rank and can drag the rest of the site down. Using Google Ads without wasting money

Paid search can bring in survey requests quickly, but heat pump keywords attract plenty of people who will never buy from you: engineers looking for jobs, students, DIY enthusiasts, people looking only for free schemes and people outside your area. Start with a tight set of high-intent phrases such as "heat pump installer [town]" and "air source heat pump installation", use phrase and exact match, and build a strong negative keyword list from week one.

Send each ad group to a focused landing page with a short survey request form rather than your home page. Track form submissions and calls properly so you can see which searches lead to booked surveys, not just clicks. Nurture the long decision

Because heat pumps are a considered purchase, many people request a quote and then go quiet. That is normal. A simple follow-up plan keeps you in the frame without being pushy:

  1. Send the survey summary and quote promptly, with a plain explanation of each line.
  2. Follow up a week later to answer questions, rather than to chase a signature.
  3. Share a short case-style explainer of a similar property you have worked on, using your own photos and no invented figures.
  4. Offer a second visit or video call for other household members.
  5. Check in again before the colder months, when heating decisions feel more urgent.

Remarketing ads on Meta and YouTube can support this by showing installation videos and process explainers to people who have visited your cost or grant pages.

Measure what actually matters

Clicks and website visits are easy to measure, but they are not the goal. Track survey requests, surveys completed, quotes sent and installs won, and tie them back to the channel and page where the enquiry started. Even a simple spreadsheet updated weekly will show you which areas, keywords and pages bring in profitable work. Review it monthly and move budget towards what produces installs, not just enquiries.

FAQs

What is the best heat pump installer marketing in the UK?

For most installers, the best mix is local SEO with strong service and area pages, a well-maintained Google Business Profile, helpful content on costs and grants, and tightly managed Google Ads. Together they capture both early research and later buying searches.

Should I mention grants on my website?

Yes, because many buyers start by researching funding. Keep the wording general, explain how you help with the process, date-stamp the page and tell readers to check current official guidance for eligibility and amounts.

Can I publish prices for heat pump installation?

You can explain the factors that affect price and give honest ranges if you are confident in them. Many installers prefer to explain cost drivers and offer a survey, because every property differs.

How long does it take to get more enquiries from SEO?

SEO usually takes three to six months to show consistent results, depending on competition in your area and the state of your current website. Paid search can produce enquiries sooner while SEO builds.

Are lead-generation websites worth using?

They can fill a diary, but you often compete with several other installers for the same lead and build no lasting asset. Many firms use them as a top-up while building their own website and search presence.

Do heat pump installers need social media?

It helps as a supporting channel. Short videos of installs, outdoor unit placement and finished cylinder cupboards build familiarity, but social media works best alongside search rather than instead of it.
