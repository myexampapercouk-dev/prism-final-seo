A restaurant website that answers the basics fast

Most people visit a restaurant website for four things: the menu, the hours, the address and a way to book or order. If any of those are slow or hidden, they move on. We design restaurant websites that:

  • Show the menu as real text, not a PDF, so it loads quickly and can be read by search engines.
  • Put hours, address and a map link near the top of every page.
  • Link clearly to your booking system and online ordering.
  • Include pages for private dining, events and catering where relevant.
  • Work well on phones, where most restaurant searches happen.

For bilingual sites, we set up English and French versions with proper language switching, so diners can read the menu in the language they prefer. Our restaurant website design guide covers the design side in more depth, and the hospitality sector page shows how we work with venues.

Local SEO and Google Maps for Ottawa restaurants

When someone searches for a restaurant nearby, Google Maps results usually appear first. Being visible there depends heavily on your Google Business Profile and on what your website says. We help by:

  • Choosing accurate primary and secondary categories.
  • Adding current hours, including holiday hours, menus and booking links.
  • Uploading fresh photos of food, interiors and patios.
  • Posting regular updates about events, specials and seasonal menus.
  • Setting up a simple, honest way to ask happy guests for reviews.

We also write location-focused pages, such as a page for each venue in a group, so you show up for searches in different parts of the city. Our guides to SEO agencies in Ottawa and Google Business Profile explain more.

Google Ads for restaurants

Google Ads work well for searches with clear intent, such as catering for an office, a venue for a party or a specific cuisine nearby. We use:

  • Search campaigns for high-intent phrases like private dining, group bookings and catering.
  • Location targeting around your venue and the neighbourhoods your guests come from.
  • Ad scheduling so budget is spent at the times people decide where to eat.
  • Conversion tracking for bookings, calls and direction requests.

Our guide to Google Ads agencies in Ottawa explains our approach for local businesses in the city.

Meta, Instagram and TikTok ads

Food is visual, so Meta and Instagram are a natural fit for restaurants. We use them for:

  • Promoting new menus, seasonal dishes and patio openings.
  • Selling tickets or bookings for events such as tasting nights.
  • Reaching people who live or work near your venue.
  • Retargeting people who visited your menu page but did not book.

TikTok ads can work for venues with a strong personality and a team happy to film short clips. Prism does not film or edit video; your team supplies the clips and we turn them into campaigns. See our guide to Meta ads in Ottawa for more detail.

Email, privacy and consent

A guest list is one of the most valuable assets a restaurant has. Collecting emails through bookings, Wi-Fi sign-ups or a newsletter form lets you announce events and offers directly. In Canada, a few rules apply at a general level:

  • CASL, Canada's anti-spam legislation, sets rules on consent, identification and unsubscribe options for commercial emails and texts.
  • PIPEDA is the federal privacy law that applies to many private-sector businesses handling personal information.
  • If you serve guests across the river in Quebec, Law 25 may also be relevant to how you handle their information.

We build clear opt-in forms and privacy page templates, but how your business meets these rules is your decision. We recommend guidance from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and the CRTC, or your own adviser.

Common restaurant marketing mistakes

  • Menus only available as PDFs or images that are slow and hard to read on a phone.
  • Out-of-date hours on Google, especially around holidays.
  • No direct booking link, sending guests to third-party apps by default.
  • Ads running all day at the same budget instead of at peak decision times.
  • Collecting emails without clear consent.

Fixing these basics is often the fastest way to more covers. Our wider restaurant marketing guide covers more ideas that apply to Ottawa venues too.

Planning around Ottawa's seasons

Ottawa restaurants feel the seasons more than most. Patio weather, winter festivals, tourist peaks in summer and quieter stretches after the holidays all change who is searching and when. We plan marketing around that rhythm:

  • Spring. Update photos, announce patio openings and refresh Google Business Profile hours.
  • Summer. Reach visitors and tourists near major attractions with location-based ads and clear opening hours.
  • Autumn. Promote private dining, group bookings and holiday party packages early.
  • Winter. Focus on regulars, takeout, gift cards and cosy indoor dining, and keep holiday hours accurate.

Shifting budget between seasons, rather than spending the same amount every month, usually gives a better return. It also means your website and profile always reflect what you are actually offering right now, which builds trust with diners who check before they visit.
