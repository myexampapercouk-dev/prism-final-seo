The Bergen market and paid search

Bergen is Norway's second-largest city and the gateway to the western fjords. Its economy includes maritime and offshore industries, seafood, research and education, and a large tourism sector built around cruise visitors, hotels and fjord trips. Many firms in the region also serve customers across Norway and abroad.

Those sectors use Google Ads in different ways:

  • Local service businesses use search ads to capture urgent, high-intent searches in the city.
  • Tourism and hospitality businesses target visitors planning trips, often in English and German as well as Norwegian.
  • Business-to-business firms in maritime, energy and technology target narrow, specialist search terms with high value per lead.
  • Online shops use Shopping and Performance Max campaigns to reach buyers across Norway.

Language and targeting in Norway

Most Norwegian searches are made in Norwegian, though many people in Norway also search in English, especially in technical and business sectors. The right approach depends on your customers:

  • For local consumers, Norwegian keywords and ads are usually essential.
  • For tourism, separate campaigns by language often work better than one mixed campaign.
  • For specialist business-to-business services, English terms can carry real volume alongside Norwegian ones.

We set up location targeting carefully, so ads reach people in or interested in your area rather than the whole country by default. Your team or translator should review all Norwegian copy before launch, as natural phrasing matters for both quality and trust.

Setting a budget

Ad spend is set in Norwegian kroner within your own Google Ads account and paid directly to Google. Our management fee is separate. Rather than picking a figure at random, we look at:

  1. Estimated search volume for your core keywords in your target area.
  2. Likely cost per click, based on competition in your sector.
  3. The value of a lead or sale to your business.
  4. How quickly you want enough data to make good decisions.

Our guides to Google Ads costs and PPC management fees explain the thinking in more detail. The figures there are UK-based, but the logic applies anywhere.

Tracking that you can trust

Without accurate tracking, Google's automated bidding cannot work well, and you cannot tell which campaigns are profitable. We set up conversion actions for the things that matter to your business, such as form submissions, calls, bookings or purchases. We also connect Google Ads with GA4 and check that the numbers line up. Our GA4 set-up guide covers the basics.

Consent rules in Norway follow the European Economic Area framework, so your cookie banner and consent settings affect what can be tracked. We set up consent mode in line with good practice. The legal wording and decisions on consent rest with you and your adviser, guided by official sources.

Landing pages that convert

Sending all ad traffic to your home page is one of the most common causes of wasted spend. Each main service or product group should have a page that matches the ad's message, loads quickly on mobile and makes the next step obvious. Our guide to landing page design sets out what works. If your site needs rebuilding, a small website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and typically takes three to five weeks.

AI-powered campaigns, with human oversight

Google Ads now relies heavily on automation, from Smart Bidding to Performance Max. Used well, these AI-powered tools can find conversions that manual bidding would miss. Used carelessly, they can spend money on low-quality placements or searches you would never choose. We use automation where the conversion data supports it and keep close control of search terms, placements and brand safety. We also review results every week.

Google Ads alongside SEO

Paid search brings traffic quickly, while SEO builds over three to six months. Many Bergen businesses use both: ads to win enquiries now and test which keywords convert, and SEO to reduce reliance on paid clicks over time. Our SEO service can share keyword data with your paid campaigns, so both channels learn from each other.

Common Google Ads mistakes we see

When we review accounts for businesses in Norway and elsewhere, the same problems come up again and again. Most are simple to fix once spotted:

  • Broad match without negatives. Broad match can work with good conversion data, but without regular negative keyword reviews it often pulls in irrelevant searches.
  • Tracking the wrong thing. Counting page views or button clicks as conversions teaches automated bidding to chase the wrong behaviour.
  • Location settings left on default. The default setting can show ads to people merely interested in a location, which may not suit a local service.
  • One campaign for everything. Mixing brand, service and product searches in one campaign makes it hard to control budgets.
  • Ads that never change. Testing new headlines and assets every few months keeps performance from going stale.
  • No link between ads and sales. If enquiries are closed offline, importing those results back into Google Ads helps bidding focus on quality leads.

Fixing these basics often makes more difference than raising the budget, so we always start there before recommending extra spend.
