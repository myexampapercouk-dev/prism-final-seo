Golf Club Marketing in the UK: How to Win Members, Visitors and Societies

Golf club marketing in the UK works best when membership, green fee, society and clubhouse audiences each get clear pages, easy booking and targeted campaigns.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • A golf club sells to several audiences at once, so members, visiting golfers, societies and clubhouse guests each need their own pages and messages.
  • Online tee time booking, clear membership categories and honest course information turn browsing golfers into bookings and enquiries.
  • Local search, reviews and targeted paid campaigns help fill quiet tee times and promote the clubhouse, functions and junior or beginner programmes.

Golf club marketing in the UK works when you treat membership, visiting golfers, golf societies and clubhouse guests as separate audiences, each with its own page, offer and booking route. Clubs that grow tend to combine a clear website, easy online tee time booking, strong local search and targeted campaigns aimed at the tee times and membership categories that are hardest to fill.

This guide is for proprietary and members' golf clubs, pay-and-play courses, nine-hole courses, driving ranges and golf centres across the UK. We are a studio that builds websites and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so we focus on the digital side: how golfers find you, what your website needs to do, and how to promote membership, green fees, societies and the clubhouse without wasting budget.

Who you are really marketing to

Most golf clubs are several businesses under one roof. The same website has to speak to a lifelong member checking competition results, a visitor looking for a round on holiday, a society organiser planning a day for twenty players and a couple looking for a venue for a family celebration. If every audience lands on the same crowded homepage, most of them leave without acting.

  • Prospective members comparing fees, categories, course quality, social life and how easy it is to get a tee time.
  • Visiting golfers searching for green fees, availability and course reviews, often from another part of the country.
  • Society and group organisers wanting a package with golf, food and prizes at a set price per head.
  • Beginners and returners looking for lessons, academy schemes or a relaxed way into the game.
  • Juniors and their parents wanting coaching, junior sections and safeguarding information.
  • Clubhouse and function guests booking dinners, wakes, birthday parties, weddings or business meetings.

Write down which of these matter most to your club this year. A club with a waiting list does not need membership campaigns, but may want to grow society and visitor income. A club with falling membership needs the opposite.

Golf club marketing UK: start with your website

Your website is the hub that every campaign points to, so it needs to answer each audience quickly. A good structure usually includes a clear homepage, then separate sections for membership, visitors and green fees, societies, lessons and academy, juniors, the clubhouse and functions, and the course itself. Our guide to website navigation structure explains how to group these so people find the right page in a click or two.

The pages that most often need work are:

  • Membership: every category explained in plain English, with what is included, any joining fee, payment options and how to apply or book a trial round. If you prefer not to publish fees, say how to request them and reply quickly.
  • Visitors and green fees: rates by day and season, any dress code, handicap requirements, buggy and trolley hire, and a clear book button.
  • The course: a hole-by-hole guide with photos or video, course length, par, and honest notes on drainage and winter conditions.
  • Societies: packages, minimum numbers, what the day includes and an enquiry form that captures date, numbers and budget.
  • Clubhouse and functions: rooms, capacities, menus and photos, so non-golfers can book too.

Golfers often check tee times on their phones, so mobile design matters. Our article on mobile website design covers the basics.

Online tee time booking

For visitors and many members, the most important feature is booking. If a golfer has to phone the pro shop during opening hours to check availability, many will book elsewhere. Most clubs use a dedicated golf booking system for tee times and link to it from the website, ideally with a live view of availability.

When you choose or review a system, think about how it presents prices, whether visitors can pay online, how it handles cancellations and weather, and whether it records where bookings came from so you can measure your marketing. Our guide to online booking systems covers what to look for and how to integrate one without breaking the look of your site.

Local SEO and Google Business Profile

Many golfers search for "golf course near me", "golf club in Surrey" or "green fees Norfolk". To appear for these, your Google Business Profile needs the right categories, opening hours for the course, pro shop and clubhouse where relevant, up-to-date photos and a link to your booking page. Our Google Business Profile guide walks through the setup.

Reviews matter for visitors choosing between courses they have never played. Ask happy visitors and society organisers for a review after their round, and reply to every review, including critical ones about course condition or pace of play. Our guide on how to respond to Google reviews has practical examples.

On the website, SEO comes from useful, specific pages: a strong course guide, separate pages for societies and functions, and content that answers real questions about winter golf, junior coaching or visiting the area. Our SEO and GEO service covers this work, including making your pages easier for AI search tools to understand and cite.

Paid campaigns for golf clubs

Paid advertising works best when it targets a specific gap. Rather than a general "visit our club" campaign, run focused campaigns for the things you most need to sell, such as weekday green fees, twilight golf, society days for next season or a beginners' course.

  • Google search ads catch golfers actively looking for green fees, society venues or lessons in your area.
  • Meta ads on Facebook and Instagram suit beginner programmes, open days, clubhouse events and membership offers, using local targeting and short video of the course.
  • Remarketing reminds people who viewed your membership or society pages but did not enquire.

Set a sensible radius for visitor campaigns, which may be wider for a destination course near holiday areas. Our guide to Google Ads location targeting explains the options. Our paid media service can plan and run these campaigns, with ad spend paid separately.

Membership campaigns that respect your members

Membership marketing has to balance growth with the experience of existing members. If new members cannot get a tee time, both groups become unhappy. Before promoting heavily, check tee sheet capacity at the times new members are likely to want.

Ideas that tend to work include flexible or points-based categories for busy people, intermediate categories for younger adults, and trial periods or open days that let people experience the course and the clubhouse. Explain each category clearly on the website and make the application or enquiry process simple. Email remains useful for staying in touch with people who enquired but did not join yet; our guide to building an email list covers consent and sign-up forms under UK GDPR and PECR, though you should check current ICO guidance for your own situation.

Societies, functions and the clubhouse

Society days and functions can add useful income outside the membership. Give societies a dedicated page with packages, sample menus, what is included and a quick enquiry form. Reply fast, as organisers often contact several clubs at once.

For the clubhouse, show the rooms, views and food clearly, and list the occasions you host. Many people booking a function have never played golf, so avoid jargon and make it obvious that non-members are welcome where that is true. Seasonal campaigns for Christmas parties, Mother's Day lunches or summer events can bring new people through the door, some of whom may later take up the game.

Juniors, beginners and growing the game

Beginner and junior programmes are often the long-term pipeline for membership. Make them easy to find and easy to join, with clear information about dates, equipment, coaching and costs. Where children are involved, set out your safeguarding approach and point parents to the club's policies; follow the current guidance from your national golf body rather than relying on general marketing advice.

Photos and short videos of real lessons, with appropriate permissions, help nervous beginners picture themselves at the club. Keep the tone welcoming and explain any dress code or etiquette simply.

Measuring what works

Track where tee time bookings, membership enquiries and society enquiries come from. Set up analytics with conversion events for form submissions and booking clicks, and add tracking links to emails and social posts. Review the numbers each month alongside the tee sheet so you can see whether campaigns are filling the right slots. FAQs

What is the best way to approach golf club marketing UK clubs can sustain?

Split your audiences into members, visitors, societies, beginners and function guests, give each a clear page and booking route, and focus campaigns on the tee times or categories you most need to fill. A steady mix of local SEO, reviews, email and targeted paid ads is usually more sustainable than one-off promotions.

Should a golf club publish membership fees online?

Many clubs do, because prospective members compare clubs before enquiring and clear fees save time on both sides. If you prefer not to, explain each category clearly and make it easy to request the fees, then reply promptly.

How can we fill quiet weekday tee times?

Target the people who can play then, such as retired golfers, shift workers and visitors on holiday, with specific weekday or twilight offers. Promote them through search ads, your Google Business Profile, email and booking platforms, and track which slots fill.

Do golf clubs need social media?

Social media helps show the course, the clubhouse and the community, and it suits beginner programmes, events and open days. It works best alongside a good website and booking system rather than as a replacement for them.

How long does it take for golf club SEO to work?

SEO results typically build over three to six months, depending on competition and the state of your website. Clear course, visitor and society pages, an accurate Google Business Profile and steady reviews are the foundations.

Can you build a new website for our golf club?

Yes. We design and build websites with clear sections for each audience and links to your chosen tee time booking system. A small website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks, depending on the content and features you need.
