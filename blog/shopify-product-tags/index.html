Shopify Product Tags: A Practical Guide to Naming, Using and Tidying Them

How to use Shopify product tags well: what they do, naming conventions, automated collections, search and filtering, and keeping your tag list tidy.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify product tags are simple labels, and their value comes from using them consistently across the whole catalogue.
  • Tags work well for internal organisation and automated collection rules, while metafields are often better for structured product information.
  • A short written tagging convention and an occasional tidy-up stop the tag list from becoming a source of errors.

Product tags are one of the simplest features in Shopify. You type a word or short phrase into a box on the product page, press enter, and the tag is saved. Because they are so easy to add, tags often grow without a plan. A few years in, a store can have several versions of the same tag, half of which do nothing, and nobody is sure which ones are safe to remove.

This guide explains what Shopify product tags do, where they are useful, where another feature may be a better fit, and how to keep them under control. It is aimed at store owners and the people who manage products day to day.

What Shopify product tags are

A product tag is a free-text label attached to a product. A product can have many tags, and the same tag can be used on many products. Tags are not visible to customers by default, although some themes display them or use them behind the scenes.

Shopify also allows tags on other things, such as orders, customers and draft orders. This guide focuses on product tags, but the same principles of naming and consistency apply elsewhere. Our guide to Shopify draft orders shows one place where order tags can be useful.

What tags are useful for

Automated collections

The most important use of product tags is in automated collections. An automated collection includes every product that meets a set of conditions, and product tag is one of the conditions you can use. For example, a collection of gifts could include every product tagged gift. When you tag a new product, it appears in the collection automatically.

This saves time and reduces the risk of forgetting to add products to collections by hand. Our guide to Shopify collections structure explains how to plan collections so tags and conditions work together.

Internal organisation

Tags help staff find and manage products in the admin. You can filter the product list by tag, which makes bulk edits easier. Tags such as a season name, a supplier code or a review flag can help the team work through groups of products.

Search

Product tags can be used by Shopify's storefront search, which means a customer searching for a term may find products tagged with it. This varies with your theme and search settings, so test it rather than assuming.

Theme features

Some themes use tags to trigger display features, such as a badge or a size chart. These conventions are theme-specific, so check your theme documentation before relying on them.

When tags are not the best tool

Tags are flexible, but that flexibility has limits. They are plain text, with no structure, so they are not ideal for information that has a defined type or needs to be shown neatly on the product page.

  • Product details such as material, care or dimensions are usually better as metafields, which can hold structured values and be displayed in specific places. Our Shopify metafields guide explains how they work.
  • Size and colour choices belong in variants and options, not tags.
  • Storefront filters in Shopify can use product type, vendor, availability, price, variant options and metafields. Our guide to Shopify product filters covers the set-up.
  • Broad product classification may be better handled by product type or the product category field.

A common situation is a store that has used tags for everything, including details that would be clearer as metafields. Moving that information is often worthwhile, but plan it carefully, because collections and theme features may depend on the existing tags.

Creating a tagging convention

The single biggest improvement you can make is to agree how tags are written. Shopify treats tags as text, so small differences create separate tags. A convention might cover:

  • Case. Decide whether tags are all lowercase or follow another pattern, and stick to it.
  • Spaces and separators. Choose between spaces and hyphens and use one approach.
  • Singular or plural. Gift and gifts look similar but behave as different tags.
  • Prefixes for groups. A prefix such as a short group name followed by a colon can make tags easier to scan, for example a season prefix or a supplier prefix.
  • Internal versus customer-facing. If your theme displays tags, consider a prefix for internal-only tags so they can be hidden.

Write the convention down in a short document, list the tags currently in use and what each one does, and share it with everyone who edits products. Our guide to Shopify staff accounts and permissions may help if you also want to limit who can change products.

Adding and editing tags

You can add tags on the individual product page, in the tags field in the product organisation section. Shopify suggests existing tags as you type, which helps avoid duplicates if people choose from the list.

For many products at once, you have options:

  • Bulk actions. Select products in the product list and use the bulk action to add or remove tags.
  • The bulk editor. Edit tags in a spreadsheet-like view across many products.
  • Product CSV import and export. Export products, edit tags in a spreadsheet and re-import. This is powerful but easy to get wrong, so test on a few products first and keep a copy of the original export.

Before any bulk change, check which automated collections use the tags involved. Removing a tag can quietly remove products from a collection.

Tidying up an untidy tag list

If your tag list has grown messy, a structured tidy-up helps.

Export and review

Export your products and list every tag in use. Group duplicates and near-duplicates together, such as different spellings or cases.

Find what each tag does

For each tag, check whether it is used in an automated collection condition, by your theme, by a connected app or in any internal process. Tags that are used nowhere may be safe to remove, but confirm first.

Decide on the keepers

For each group of duplicates, choose one version that matches your convention. Note which collections need their conditions updated.

Make changes in a careful order

Add the correct tag to the relevant products first, update any collection conditions, check the collections still contain the right products, and only then remove the old tags. Doing it in this order avoids products dropping out of collections in between.

Document the result

Update your tag list document. A tidy list is much easier to keep tidy than a messy one is to fix.

If tags were added by connected apps over time, an apps audit can help you understand which ones are still in use.

Tags when importing or migrating products

Tags often arrive in bulk when products are imported from a spreadsheet or moved from another platform. Old category names, supplier codes and import labels can all come across as tags, and they may not match your convention. Before a large import, decide which source fields should become tags, which should become metafields or product types, and which can be left behind. After the import, filter the product list by a few new tags to check they landed as expected. If you are moving from another platform, our guide to moving from WooCommerce to Shopify covers how product data is usually mapped during a move.

Tags and SEO

Some themes create tag-filtered collection pages, which can result in many thin pages with similar content. Whether this matters depends on how your theme handles them, so it is worth checking how these URLs are treated. Our Shopify SEO guide covers the wider picture of how collection and product pages are indexed.

In most cases, tags themselves have little direct effect on search visibility. Their value is in organising products into well-structured collections with good descriptions.

FAQs

Can customers see Shopify product tags?

Not by default. Some themes display tags or use them for features such as badges, so check your theme to see how tags are used.

Are Shopify tags case sensitive?

Small differences in how tags are written can lead to separate-looking tags, which makes the list harder to manage. Agreeing a single convention, such as all lowercase, avoids the problem.

Should I use tags or metafields in Shopify?

Tags suit simple labels and collection rules. Metafields are usually better for structured product details that need to be displayed or used in filters.

How do I remove a tag from many products?

Select the products in the admin and use the bulk action to remove tags, or use the bulk editor. Check which automated collections use the tag first.

Do product tags help Shopify SEO?

Tags rarely have much direct effect. Their main value is organising products into clear collections, which can then be well described and linked.
