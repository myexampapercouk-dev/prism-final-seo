Who this guide is for

Portland has a distinctive business mix. Independent retail, food and drink, outdoor and active brands, creative services, software firms and a large base of trades and professional services all compete for attention, often against national chains with bigger budgets. SEO tends to suit:

  • Local service businesses such as contractors, clinics, salons and repair shops covering Portland, Beaverton, Gresham, Lake Oswego and Vancouver across the river.
  • Ecommerce and direct-to-consumer brands that sell nationally and need category and product pages to rank.
  • Software and B2B companies that win customers through problem-led content and comparison pages.
  • Hospitality and food businesses that rely on map results, menus and reviews.
  • Professional firms such as accountants, architects and consultants that want steady, qualified enquiries.

What an SEO agency in Portland should actually do

Good SEO is not a single task. It is a set of connected jobs that reinforce each other. When we take on a Portland client, the work usually falls into five areas.

Technical SEO. We crawl the site to find broken links, redirect chains, duplicate pages, slow templates, missing canonical tags and indexing problems. Our technical SEO audit checklist shows the full list we work through. Fixing these issues first means every later piece of content has a fair chance to rank.

Keyword and intent research. We map the searches your buyers actually make, grouped by intent: people researching a problem, people comparing options and people ready to buy or book. Each group needs a different type of page.

On-page optimisation. Titles, headings, internal links, structured data and page copy are rewritten so that each page clearly answers one main query. Our schema markup guide explains how structured data helps search engines understand your business, products and FAQs.

Content. We plan and write guides, service pages and location pages in English that answer questions in plain language. If you need pages in Spanish or another language, that copy comes from your own team or a translator, and we handle the structure and optimisation around it.

Local and off-page signals. We tidy your Google Business Profile, check that your name, address and phone number match across directories, and build links through useful content and genuine relationships rather than paid link schemes.

Local SEO for Portland neighbourhoods and the metro area

Many Portland searches include a neighbourhood or nearby city: the Pearl District, Alberta Arts, Hawthorne, Sellwood, Beaverton, Hillsboro or Tigard. If you serve these areas, your website and profile should make that clear without creating thin, copy-and-paste location pages.

What works better is a small number of genuinely useful area pages, each with details that only apply there: the services most requested in that part of town, parking or access notes, typical project types and answers to local questions. Pair these with a well-maintained Google Business Profile. Our Google Business Profile guide covers categories, services, photos, posts and review replies, and the same principles apply in Oregon.

If you operate across the Columbia River in Washington as well as in Oregon, we make sure your pages and profiles describe that service area accurately, because customers on each side search differently and expect to see their own city named.

AI search and generative engine optimisation

More people now ask AI assistants and AI Overviews for recommendations before they click a search result. These tools tend to cite pages that give direct, well-structured answers and that are consistent with what other sources say about you.

As part of our AI-powered SEO work, we write answer-first introductions, clear comparison tables and question-led FAQs, keep your business details consistent across the web and track which queries mention you. This does not replace traditional SEO. It builds on the same foundations: a fast site, clear content and a trustworthy reputation.

Common SEO problems we see on Portland websites

These issues come up again and again when we review small and mid-sized business websites:

  • One page trying to rank for everything. A single services page lists ten offers, so none of them ranks well.
  • Slow mobile pages. Heavy images, sliders and too many scripts hurt both rankings and conversions. Our Core Web Vitals guide explains what to fix first.
  • Thin location pages. Dozens of near-identical city pages that add nothing for the reader.
  • Inconsistent business details. Old addresses or phone numbers on directories confuse both customers and search engines.
  • No internal linking. Blog posts sit in isolation and never pass value to the pages that bring in revenue.
  • Tracking gaps. Calls and form submissions are not recorded, so nobody knows which pages actually produce leads.

Accessibility, privacy and tax notes

SEO work often touches areas with legal implications in the United States. We keep these points general and recommend you check official guidance or speak to your own adviser.

Accessibility. Accessible websites are easier to use and often easier to crawl. We build to recognised accessibility practices such as clear headings, alt text and good contrast, but questions about ADA obligations should go to a qualified adviser.

Privacy. Analytics and advertising tags collect data. Several US states have their own privacy laws, and requirements vary. We can set up consent tools and tag management, but the decisions about your privacy policy and compliance rest with you and your adviser.

Sales tax. If you sell online, how sales tax applies depends on where you and your customers are. We do not give tax advice; your accountant should confirm your obligations.

Choosing an SEO agency: Portland checklist

Whether you work with us or someone else, these questions help separate solid agencies from risky ones:

  • Will they explain exactly what they will do each month, in plain language?
  • Do they fix technical issues before writing lots of new content?
  • How do they build links, and will they avoid paid link networks?
  • Will you own your content, accounts and data if you leave?
  • Do they report on enquiries and revenue, not just rankings?
  • Are they honest about timelines rather than promising first-page results?
