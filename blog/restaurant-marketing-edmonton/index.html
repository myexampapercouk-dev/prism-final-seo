A restaurant website that answers the basics fast

Most people visit a restaurant website for four things: the menu, the hours, the address and a way to book or order. If any of those are slow or hidden, they leave. We design restaurant websites that:

  • Show the menu as real text, not a PDF, so it loads quickly on a phone and can be read by search engines.
  • Put hours, address, parking notes and a map link near the top of every page.
  • Link clearly to your booking system and online ordering.
  • Include pages for private dining, events, catering and gift cards where relevant.
  • Work well on phones, where most restaurant searches happen.

Our restaurant website design guide goes deeper into the design side, and the hospitality sector page shows how we work with venues.

Local SEO and Google Maps for Edmonton restaurants

When someone searches for a restaurant nearby, Google Maps results usually appear first. Being visible there depends heavily on your Google Business Profile and on what your website says about you. We help by:

  • Choosing accurate primary and secondary categories for your cuisine and service style.
  • Keeping hours current, including holiday and festival hours.
  • Adding menus, booking links and fresh photos of food, interiors and patios.
  • Posting updates about events, specials and seasonal menus.
  • Setting up a simple, honest way to ask happy guests for reviews.

For groups, we write a page for each venue so you show up in searches across different neighbourhoods, from Old Strathcona to the west end. Our guides to Google Business Profile and SEO agencies in Edmonton explain more.

Google Ads for restaurants

Google Ads work best for searches with clear intent, such as catering for an office, a venue for a birthday or a specific cuisine nearby. We use:

  • Search campaigns for high-intent phrases like private dining, group bookings and catering.
  • Location targeting around your venue and the areas your guests travel from, including St. Albert and Sherwood Park.
  • Ad scheduling so budget is spent when people decide where to eat.
  • Conversion tracking for bookings, calls, orders and direction requests.

Our guide to Google Ads agencies in Edmonton explains how we set up and manage campaigns for local businesses, and our paid media service covers the full offer.

Meta, Instagram and TikTok ads

Food is visual, so Meta and Instagram are a natural fit for restaurants. We use them to:

  • Promote new menus, seasonal dishes and patio openings.
  • Sell tickets or bookings for events such as tasting nights and live music.
  • Reach people who live or work near your venue.
  • Retarget people who looked at your menu page but did not book.

TikTok ads can suit venues with a strong personality and a team happy to film short clips. Prism does not film or edit video; your team supplies the clips and we turn them into campaigns. Our guide to Meta ads in Edmonton has more detail.

Planning around Edmonton's seasons and events

Edmonton restaurants feel the seasons sharply. Long summer evenings and patio weather, the city's busy festival calendar, hockey nights and cold winter stretches all change who is searching and when. We plan marketing around that rhythm:

  • Spring. Refresh photos, announce patio openings and update Google Business Profile hours.
  • Summer. Reach festival-goers and visitors with location-based ads and clear opening hours.
  • Autumn. Promote private dining, group bookings and holiday party packages early.
  • Winter. Focus on regulars, takeout, gift cards and warm indoor dining, and keep holiday hours accurate.

Shifting budget between seasons, rather than spending the same each month, usually gives a better return and keeps your profile honest about what you offer right now.

Email, privacy and consent

A guest list is one of a restaurant's most valuable assets. Collecting emails through bookings, Wi-Fi sign-ups or a newsletter form lets you announce events and offers directly. At a general level:

  • CASL, Canada's anti-spam legislation, sets rules on consent, identification and unsubscribe options for commercial emails and texts.
  • PIPEDA is the federal privacy law for many private-sector businesses, and Alberta's PIPA may apply to your business instead.

We build clear opt-in forms and privacy page templates, but how your business meets these rules is your decision. We recommend official guidance from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner of Alberta, the CRTC, or your own adviser.

Common restaurant marketing mistakes

  • Menus only available as PDFs or images that are slow and hard to read on a phone.
  • Out-of-date hours on Google, especially around holidays and big events.
  • No direct booking link, sending every guest to third-party apps by default.
  • Ads running all day at the same budget instead of at peak decision times.
  • Collecting emails without clear consent.
