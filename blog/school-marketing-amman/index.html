School Marketing in Amman: Websites, Search and Ads for Schools in Jordan

School marketing in Amman, explained: admissions-focused websites, local SEO and Google and Meta Ads for private schools, from a UK studio working remotely.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 28 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • School marketing in Amman works best when a clear, fast website, strong local search visibility and well-timed paid ads all guide parents towards an open day, tour or admissions enquiry.
  • Parents compare curriculum, fees, location, transport and admissions steps, so a school website should answer those questions plainly in English and Arabic.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based, works remotely and has no office in Amman; retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 per month, a small website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and dinar pricing is quoted on request.

School marketing in Amman means helping parents find your school, understand what makes it different and take the next step, such as booking a tour, attending an open day or starting an application. For most private and international schools, that comes from a clear admissions-focused website, good local search visibility and well-timed paid ads.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely. We have no office in Amman or anywhere else in Jordan. We work with schools and nurseries that are comfortable collaborating through video calls, shared documents and written monthly reports. We write in English; Arabic content comes from your own team or a translator.

How parents in Amman choose a school

Amman has a wide range of private schools, from national curriculum schools to those offering British, American and International Baccalaureate programmes. Parents often shortlist several schools and compare them carefully. They look at curriculum, language of instruction, fees, location, transport, facilities, class sizes and how admissions work.

Much of that research now happens online, often on a phone. Parents search for schools by area and curriculum, ask other parents in WhatsApp and Facebook groups, read reviews and browse school websites late in the evening. A school that is easy to find and easy to understand has a clear advantage over one with an outdated site and scattered information.

A website built around admissions

Your website is usually the first detailed look a parent gets at your school. It should make the important information easy to find and give a clear picture of daily life, without vague slogans. A strong school website usually includes:

  • A clear explanation of your curriculum and how it is taught
  • Pages for each phase, such as early years, primary and secondary
  • An admissions section with steps, required documents, key dates and an enquiry or tour booking form
  • Fees information, or a clear route to request it if you prefer not to publish it
  • Location details, including transport and bus routes if you offer them
  • Facilities, activities and pastoral care, described honestly
  • English and Arabic versions that are set up properly for search engines

Our post on school website design explains these features in more detail. It is written for UK schools, but the principles apply in Amman. For a new or rebuilt site, see our page on web design in Jordan. A small website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and usually takes three to five weeks.

We never invent results, rankings, awards or accreditations. Any exam results, inspection outcomes or accreditations on your site should be supplied and checked by your school.

Local SEO for schools in Amman

Parents often search with a curriculum and area in mind, such as British school in a particular district or IB school in Amman. Local SEO helps your school appear in those searches and in map results.

We focus on a complete Google Business Profile with accurate details and photos, separate pages for each curriculum and phase, clear location information, helpful content that answers common parent questions, and technical health so pages load quickly and are easy for search engines to read. Our post on SEO in Jordan covers local search more widely. We also help schools set up a polite process for asking parents for honest reviews and replying to them.

Paid ads around admissions season

Paid ads are most useful during the busiest admissions periods, before open days and when a school has places to fill in specific year groups. They let you reach parents at the right time rather than waiting for SEO to build.

  • Google Ads: reaches parents searching for schools by curriculum and area. Our guide to Google Ads in Jordan explains how we set these up.
  • Meta Ads: promotes open days, tours and admissions deadlines to families in specific areas of Amman. See our page on Meta Ads in Amman.
  • Retargeting: reminds parents who visited your admissions pages to book a tour.

Advertising to parents needs care. We target adults, keep claims factual and follow platform rules on education and audiences. We do not target children. We do not film or edit video; any school video should come from your team or a production company.

Measuring enquiries, not just clicks

For a school, the result that matters is admissions enquiries and tours that lead to enrolments. We set up tracking for enquiry forms, tour bookings and phone calls, and report each month on which channels bring enquiries. Where possible, your admissions team can share which enquiries turned into applications, so we can steer budgets towards the channels that bring the right families.

We use AI-powered tools to group parent search terms, find common questions and draft ad variations for testing. A person checks every output, and your school approves any content before it goes live. Our education marketing page explains how we support schools and colleges, and our guide on choosing a marketing agency for a school lists questions worth asking any agency.

Content that answers parents' questions

Parents in Amman tend to ask the same questions again and again: how the curriculum compares with others, what a typical school day looks like, how children settle in when they join mid-year, how the school supports children learning in a second language and what happens after graduation. Answering those questions on your website saves your admissions team time and builds trust before a parent ever visits.

We help you plan a set of helpful pages and articles around these questions. Your teachers and admissions staff supply the facts and examples, and we shape them into clear, well-structured content that search engines can understand. This kind of content also makes it more likely that your school is described accurately when parents use AI search tools to compare schools.

Good topics often include:

  • A plain guide to your curriculum and how it compares with other options in Amman
  • What to expect on the first day, written for new families
  • How admissions assessments work and how to prepare
  • Transport options and which areas your buses cover
  • Activities, clubs and how the school supports wellbeing

Each page links to the admissions section so that a parent who is ready can enquire straight away. We review these pages once a year, before admissions season, so that dates, fees information, transport routes and contact details stay accurate and parents never act on out-of-date details.

Rules, privacy and safeguarding

Schools handle sensitive information about children and families. Jordan has its own rules on personal data, and the Ministry of Education sets requirements for private schools. We follow good practice on consent, privacy notices and data collected through forms, and we never publish photos of pupils without your school's approval. We are not legal advisers, so please check official guidance or speak to your own adviser, and final approval of content and data handling rests with your school.
