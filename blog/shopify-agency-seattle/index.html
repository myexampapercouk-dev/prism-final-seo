Shopify Agency Seattle: How a Remote Team Builds and Grows Shopify Stores for Seattle Brands

Looking for a Shopify agency in Seattle? A remote team can design, build, migrate and improve your store for buyers across the US. Here is the process and cost.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Shopify agency for a Seattle brand should plan the store around products, buyers and operations first, then design and build around that plan.
  • Most Seattle online stores sell well beyond the city, so shipping, sales tax settings, product data and search matter as much as the look.
  • We are a UK-based studio building Shopify stores remotely, with ecommerce sites from £1,500 to £3,500 and other currencies quoted on request.

If you are searching for a Shopify agency in Seattle, the best fit is the team that can plan, build and improve a store that sells, not necessarily the one nearest your warehouse. A remote agency can design new Shopify stores, migrate from other platforms and improve existing shops for Seattle outdoor brands, coffee roasters, apparel labels, home goods makers and specialty food producers, provided the work is built on your products and your customers.

We are a UK-based studio that works remotely with US brands. We have no Seattle office and no US office. This guide explains what a Shopify project for a Seattle business should include, the decisions that matter most, how a remote project runs and what it costs.

What a Shopify agency in Seattle should deliver

Seattle has a strong direct-to-consumer culture. Outdoor and active gear, coffee and tea, craft food and drink, sustainable fashion, homeware and gadgets are all sold online from the city and the wider Puget Sound area. Whatever you sell, a complete Shopify project usually covers:

  • Discovery: your range, margins, buyers, fulfilment set-up and the apps or systems you already rely on.
  • Store structure: collections, navigation, filters and product templates planned around how people shop your range.
  • Design: a theme set up or customised to fit your brand, with mobile shoppers in mind first.
  • Product data: titles, descriptions, variants, images and metafields organised so they work for shoppers and search.
  • Checkout and operations: payments, shipping profiles, taxes, notifications and policies configured and tested.
  • Apps and integrations: only the apps you need, connected to inventory, email, reviews or fulfilment tools.
  • Launch and handover: redirects, tracking, test orders and training so your team can run the store.

Our broader guide to choosing a Shopify agency for US brands sets out the questions worth asking before you sign anything.

Decisions that matter most for Seattle online stores

Most Seattle brands sell across the US, and some ship to Canada or further. That makes a few early decisions especially important:

  • Shipping: zones, rates and delivery promises that are accurate and easy to understand, including any free shipping threshold.
  • Sales tax: Shopify has tools to collect tax, but where you must register and collect depends on your situation. Check official guidance or speak to your own adviser before launch.
  • Seasonal range: outdoor, rain and winter gear sells in waves, so collections and homepage sections should be easy to rotate.
  • Subscriptions: coffee, tea, snacks and other repeat products often suit subscriptions, which need the right app and clear terms.
  • Product content: technical details such as materials, sizing, fit and care help buyers decide and reduce returns.
  • Wholesale: if you also sell to retailers, decide early whether that runs through the same store or a separate channel.

Performance should be part of the brief from day one. Heavy themes and too many apps slow a store down, and our guide to Shopify speed optimisation explains how to keep pages quick.

Turning visits into orders

A good-looking store is not the same as a store that sells. Once the foundations are in place, the most useful improvements are usually on product pages, collections and the cart:

  • Clear product pages: strong photos, honest descriptions, sizing help and visible delivery and returns information.
  • Helpful filters: let shoppers narrow by size, colour, activity or material, depending on your range.
  • Trust: genuine reviews, clear policies and easy ways to get in touch.
  • A smooth cart: no surprises on shipping or tax, and a quick path to checkout.
  • Tracking: analytics and conversion events set up so you can see where shoppers drop off.

Our guide to Shopify conversion rate optimisation goes into these changes in more depth. Search matters too: product and collection pages need to be found, and our guide to ecommerce SEO for US DTC brands explains how to plan for it.

How a remote Shopify project works for a Seattle brand

Seattle is usually eight hours behind the UK, so your early morning overlaps with our late afternoon. We keep calls to that window and rely on written updates and a preview store for everything else, so work moves forward while you are asleep. A typical project runs like this:

  1. Discovery call: products, margins, operations, current platform and goals.
  2. Plan and quote: a written scope covering pages, features, apps, migration needs, timeline and price.
  3. Design: homepage, collection and product page designs shared for comments.
  4. Build: theme set-up, product import, apps, shipping, taxes and checkout configured on a development store.
  5. Testing: test orders, mobile checks, speed checks and redirect testing.
  6. Launch and handover: go-live, training and notes so your team can manage day to day.

You own the Shopify store, the domain and all accounts. If you are weighing up remote options in general, our guide to Shopify agencies in Los Angeles covers a similar remote set-up for another West Coast city.

Whichever route you choose, ask to see a sample scope and a sample monthly report before you commit. They show far more about how a team works than a portfolio does, and they make it easy to compare agencies on the same terms: what is included, who owns what and how changes are agreed.

How much a Shopify store costs for a Seattle brand

The cost depends on the number of products, how much custom design you need, migration work and the apps or integrations involved. Our prices are in pounds, and we can quote US dollars or other currencies on request:

  • Ecommerce website: typically £1,500 to £3,500.
  • Timeline: usually five to twelve weeks, depending on range size and integrations.
  • Ongoing support, SEO and ads: monthly retainers at Launch £350, Growth £750 or Scale £1,500.
  • Third-party costs: your Shopify plan, paid apps and themes are billed separately by their providers.

Our pricing page explains what each option includes, and our website and ecommerce development service shows how we plan and build stores. If you also need a non-ecommerce site, see our guide to web design in Seattle.

Accessibility and privacy also deserve attention before launch. We build to recognised accessibility guidelines, but we do not give legal advice on the Americans with Disabilities Act or US privacy laws, so check official guidance or speak to your own adviser about your obligations.

FAQs

How do I choose a Shopify agency in Seattle?

Look for a team that asks about your products, margins and operations before talking about design, gives you a written scope and timeline, and makes sure you own the store and accounts. Ask how they handle migrations and support after launch.

Do you have an office in Seattle?

No. We are a UK-based studio and work remotely with US brands. We have no Seattle office or any other US office, and we run projects through video calls, shared documents and a development store you can review.

Can you migrate my store to Shopify without losing rankings?

We plan migrations to protect search visibility, mapping old URLs to new ones with redirects and moving product content carefully. No one can promise rankings, but a careful migration greatly reduces the risk.

Do you set up sales tax in Shopify?

We can configure Shopify's tax settings based on the registrations you tell us you hold. Where you need to register and collect is a question for official guidance or your own adviser.

How much does a Shopify store cost?

Our ecommerce sites typically cost £1,500 to £3,500, with your Shopify plan and paid apps billed separately. Prices are in pounds, and we can quote US dollars or other currencies on request.

How long does a Shopify build take?

Most Shopify projects take five to twelve weeks from agreed plan to launch, depending on the size of your range, migration needs and integrations.
