Google Ads Location Targeting: How to Show Ads Only Where You Work

Google Ads location targeting decides where your ads appear. Learn how to set areas, radius and presence options so a UK local budget is not wasted.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Set locations to match the area you can genuinely serve, using towns, postcode areas or a radius around your premises.
  • Check the presence setting so ads reach people in your area, not just people who show interest in it.
  • Use location reports to adjust bids or exclude areas once real data comes in.

Google Ads location targeting controls which places your ads can appear in, so a local UK business only pays for clicks from people it can actually serve. You choose countries, regions, cities, postcode areas or a radius around an address, then pick whether to reach people physically in those places or also people who simply show interest in them.

Getting this wrong is one of the quickest ways to waste a small budget. A plumber in Leicester does not want to pay for a click from someone in Aberdeen, and a café in Bath gains little from people searching in Bristol. This guide walks through how location targeting works, how to set it up sensibly and how to refine it over time. Google changes the interface and settings fairly often, so check the current Google Ads help pages for exact menu names and options before you make changes.

What location targeting does in Google Ads

Every campaign has location settings. They tell Google which geographic areas are eligible to see your ads. When someone searches, Google estimates their location using signals such as device settings, IP address and the search itself, then decides whether your campaign is allowed to show to them.

Location targeting works alongside your keywords, not instead of them. A keyword such as emergency electrician can be searched anywhere in the country, but your location settings narrow the audience to the places you choose. If you are new to the platform, our Google Ads guide for small businesses covers the wider account structure first.

The main ways to choose a location

There are several ways to define your area, and the right one depends on how your business operates.

OptionHow it worksGood fit for
CountryTargets the whole of the UK or another countryOnline shops that deliver nationwide
Region or countyTargets a wider area such as a county or nation within the UKService firms that cover a broad patch
Town or cityTargets a named place and its boundary as Google defines itBusinesses serving a specific town
Postcode areaTargets postal areas where availableTrades and delivery businesses with tight service zones
RadiusDraws a circle around an address or point on the mapShops, clinics, salons and venues people travel to

You can combine several locations in one campaign, and you can exclude places too. For example, a removals firm might target a county but exclude a city it does not want to work in.

Choosing between named areas and a radius

Named places follow administrative or postal boundaries, which do not always match how customers travel. A radius is simpler to understand but can spill over rivers, estuaries or borders into areas you cannot reach easily. Many local businesses start with a radius, then look at the map preview and swap to named areas if the circle catches places that make no sense.

Presence versus interest: the setting most people miss

Inside the location options there is usually a setting that controls who counts as being in your area. One option reaches people who are in, or regularly in, your targeted locations. Another, broader option also reaches people who are searching for or showing interest in your locations, even if they are somewhere else.

The broader option can make sense for tourism, hotels, wedding venues and anything people research before travelling. A holiday cottage in Cornwall genuinely wants people in London planning a trip. For most local services, though, the narrower presence option is usually the safer choice, because a dentist or gardener can only help people nearby.

The exact wording and defaults have changed over the years, so open the location options in your campaign settings, read the current descriptions and confirm which one is selected. It is worth checking this even on older campaigns, because the default may not match what you want.

How to set up Google Ads location targeting

The steps below describe the general process. Menu names may differ slightly in your account.

  1. Open the campaign and go to its settings.
  2. Find the locations section and choose to enter another location.
  3. Search for a town, county or postcode area, or use the advanced search to set a radius around your address.
  4. Check the map preview to see what is included, and add exclusions for any areas you do not serve.
  5. Open the location options and choose the presence setting that suits your business.
  6. Save, then check the campaign a few days later to confirm impressions are coming from the expected places.

If you run several branches, consider separate campaigns or ad groups for each area so you can control budget, ad copy and landing pages per location. That mirrors the thinking in our guide to multi-location SEO, where each branch gets its own relevant page.

Matching ads and landing pages to the area

Location settings decide where an ad can appear, but the ad and landing page decide whether someone clicks and gets in touch. Mentioning the town or area in headlines can help searchers see that you are local. Our guide to responsive search ads explains how to write headlines that work in different combinations.

The landing page should then confirm the area too: the towns you cover, travel times, opening hours or delivery zones. A generic page that never mentions location can feel less relevant to someone who searched for a local service. See our advice on landing page design for the wider structure of a page that converts.

Using location reports to refine targeting

Once a campaign has been running for a few weeks, look at the location reports. These show where impressions, clicks and conversions came from, both by targeted location and by where users actually were. Typical findings include:

  • A neighbouring town that brings in plenty of clicks but few enquiries, which may be worth excluding or bidding down.
  • An area inside your radius that consistently converts well, which may justify its own campaign or a bid adjustment.
  • Clicks from outside your target area, which can point to the presence setting being broader than you intended.

These reports are only useful if you are tracking conversions properly. If enquiries, calls and purchases are not recorded, you will be judging locations on clicks alone. Our guide to Google Ads conversion tracking explains how to set that up first.

Bid adjustments and automated bidding

With manual bidding, you can usually raise or lower bids for particular locations. With automated bidding strategies, Google may handle much of this itself and some manual adjustments may be ignored or behave differently. Check the current help pages for how location bid adjustments interact with your chosen bid strategy before relying on them.

Location targeting and negative keywords

Location settings and keywords work together. If you only serve Nottingham, a search such as plumber derby could still trigger your ad when the person searching is physically in your targeted area. Adding other town names as negative keywords can help, though it needs care so you do not block valid searches. Our guide to negative keywords explains match types and how to build a list without blocking good traffic.

How location affects cost

Competition varies by area. Busy cities often have more advertisers bidding for the same searches, which can push up cost per click, while smaller towns can be cheaper but have fewer searches. Targeting too narrowly can limit volume so much that a campaign struggles to gather useful data. For a broader view of what drives spend, read our guide to Google Ads costs in the UK. Ad spend is always paid to Google separately from any management fee.

FAQs

How does Google Ads location targeting work?

You choose which places your campaign can show in, such as a country, town, postcode area or radius. Google estimates where each searcher is and only shows your ads if they match your settings and the presence option you selected.

Should I use a radius or named towns?

A radius suits businesses people travel to, such as shops and clinics. Named towns or postcode areas suit businesses with clear service boundaries. Check the map preview either way to see exactly what is covered.

Why are my ads showing to people outside my area?

The most common reason is a presence setting that includes people showing interest in your location, not just people in it. Location estimates are also not perfect. Review the location options and the location reports.

Can I exclude a town from my Google Ads?

Yes. Location settings let you add excluded locations, so ads will not show to people in those places. This is useful when a radius or county includes areas you do not serve.

How big should my targeting radius be?

There is no single right size. Start with the distance customers realistically travel or you are willing to cover, then adjust once you can see which areas produce enquiries.

Does location targeting change my cost per click?

It can. Areas with more competing advertisers tend to cost more per click, and very narrow targeting can limit volume. Location reports help you see where budget is working best.
