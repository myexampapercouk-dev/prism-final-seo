How to Set Up Google Search Console for Your Business Website

Set up Google Search Console by adding your site, verifying ownership and submitting a sitemap. A step by step guide for UK small business owners.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Add a Domain property where you can, verify through your DNS, then submit your sitemap.
  • The Performance, Pages and Experience reports show what Google sees and where to act.
  • Give access to people who need it, and remove access when a supplier relationship ends.

To set up Google Search Console, sign in with a Google account, add your website as a property, verify that you own it (usually through your domain's DNS settings), and submit your XML sitemap. Once verified, Search Console starts showing which searches bring people to your site, which pages Google has indexed and any problems it has found.

It is free, it comes straight from Google and it is one of the first tools we check on any project. This guide walks a UK small business owner through the set-up and the reports worth checking. Google changes the interface from time to time, so check the current Search Console help pages for exact names and steps.

What Search Console is for

Search Console is different from Google Analytics. Analytics tells you what people do once they arrive on your website. Search Console tells you about your presence in Google Search itself: the queries you appear for, how often people click, which pages are indexed and whether Google has spotted technical problems.

Used together, they give a much clearer picture. If you have not yet set up analytics, our GA4 setup guide and our guide to website analytics for small businesses are good companions to this one.

Domain property or URL prefix?

When you add a site, Search Console usually offers two property types.

Property typeWhat it coversHow you verifyBest for
DomainEvery version of your domain: http, https, www, non-www and subdomainsA DNS TXT record at your domain registrarMost businesses that can access their DNS
URL prefixOnly addresses that start with the exact prefix you enterSeveral options, such as an HTML file, meta tag or analyticsWhen you cannot edit DNS, or need a section of a site

We usually recommend a Domain property because it captures everything in one place. If your domain was registered by a previous web designer and you are not sure where it lives, sort that out first. Our guide to website ownership and handover explains why you should hold those logins yourself.

How to set up Google Search Console step by step

  1. Choose the Google account. Use a business account the company controls, not a personal one belonging to a single staff member or supplier.
  2. Add a property. Enter your domain, for example yourbusiness.co.uk, without https or www for a Domain property.
  3. Copy the TXT record that Search Console gives you.
  4. Add the record at your DNS provider. This is often your domain registrar or your host. Look for DNS settings and create a new TXT record with the value provided.
  5. Return and verify. DNS changes can take time to spread, so if verification fails at first, wait and try again later.
  6. Submit your sitemap in the Sitemaps section. Many platforms create one automatically, often at an address such as /sitemap.xml. Check your platform's documentation.
  7. Add other users who need access, with the lowest permission level that lets them do their job.

If you use Shopify, WordPress, Wix or Squarespace, each has its own help pages on sitemaps and verification. Shopify, for example, generates a sitemap automatically, and many WordPress SEO plugins do the same. If you are still connecting your domain, our guide on connecting a custom domain to Shopify or on business domain and email set-up may help.

The reports worth checking

Search Console has a lot of screens. For a small business, these are the ones that usually matter.

Performance

This shows clicks, impressions, average click-through rate and average position for your pages in Google Search. Filter by query to see what people typed, and by page to see which pages earn visits. Look for queries where you appear often but rarely get clicked; a better title or description can help. Our keyword research guide explains how to turn those queries into content ideas.

Pages (indexing)

This report shows which pages Google has indexed and which it has not, with reasons. Not every excluded page is a problem: redirects, duplicates and pages you have deliberately set to noindex will appear here. Focus on important pages that should be indexed but are not.

URL inspection

Paste in any address on your site to see whether it is indexed and when Google last crawled it. After publishing or fixing an important page, you can request indexing here, although that does not guarantee speed.

Experience and Core Web Vitals

These reports group pages by real-user speed and stability data where Google has enough of it. Smaller sites may see limited data. Our Core Web Vitals guide explains what the metrics mean.

Security and manual actions

Rarely used but important. If Google detects hacked content or applies a manual penalty, you will see it here, and Search Console can email you alerts.

Turning the data into actions

Search Console is only useful if it changes what you do. A few practical ways to act on it:

  • Improve titles and descriptions for pages with plenty of impressions but few clicks. Make them clearer and closer to how people actually search.
  • Strengthen pages ranking just off the first page. Adding helpful detail, answering related questions and linking to them from other pages can help.
  • Write new content for queries you appear for but have no dedicated page about.
  • Fix indexing problems on important pages, such as accidental noindex tags or pages blocked by robots rules.
  • Check after every change to your website, especially platform moves and redesigns.

Export the Performance data occasionally and keep a copy, because Search Console only holds a limited window of history. That makes year-on-year comparisons much easier later. Check the help pages for how long data is currently kept.

Common set-up mistakes

  • Verifying with a supplier's personal account, then losing access when you change agency.
  • Adding only the www or only the non-www version as a URL prefix property, then wondering why data looks thin.
  • Removing the verification record during a DNS tidy-up, which can cause you to lose verified status.
  • Ignoring the property after a redesign, when indexing problems are most likely. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings covers the checks to make.
  • Panicking over every excluded page, when many exclusions are normal.

Sharing access safely

Search Console lets you add users with different permission levels. Owners can manage users and settings; full and restricted users have fewer rights. Keep at least two owners within your business, give agencies and freelancers the level they need, and remove anyone who no longer works with you. Check the current help pages for the exact permission types.

FAQs

How do I set up Google Search Console?

Sign in with a business Google account, add your site as a Domain property, add the TXT record Search Console gives you at your DNS provider, verify, then submit your sitemap. Check Google's current help pages for exact steps.

Is Google Search Console free?

Yes. Search Console is a free tool provided by Google for website owners.

How long does Search Console take to show data?

Verification can be quick once DNS updates, but performance data usually builds up over the following days. New sites may see very little until Google has crawled and indexed their pages.

Do I need Search Console if I have Google Analytics?

Yes, they do different jobs. Analytics covers behaviour on your site; Search Console covers how your site appears and performs in Google Search.

Where do I find my sitemap address?

Many platforms generate one automatically, often at /sitemap.xml or a similar address. Check your platform's help pages or your SEO plugin settings.

Should my web designer own my Search Console account?

No. Your business should own the property, and you can add your designer or agency as a user. That way you keep access if you change supplier.
