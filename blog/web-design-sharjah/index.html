What makes web design in Sharjah different

Sharjah has its own mix of customers and businesses. Many companies serve local families, students and residents who also travel into Dubai and Ajman for work, while others trade across the whole of the UAE. A few practical points shape how a website should be planned:

  • Two languages. Many customers are comfortable in English, but plenty prefer Arabic, and some sectors expect both. Deciding early whether you need one language or two affects page structure, navigation and the build budget.
  • Mobile first. Most visitors will arrive on a phone, often from a search result, a map listing or a social post. Our guide to mobile website design covers what that means in practice.
  • Messaging as the main enquiry route. Many UAE customers would rather send a message than fill in a long form. A clear click-to-chat or call button is often worth more than any design flourish.
  • Local search. People search for services with Sharjah area names, so service and location pages need clear, specific content rather than one generic page for the whole country.

Planning Arabic and English pages

If you need a bilingual site, the decision should be made before design starts. Arabic reads right to left, so layouts, menus, icons and forms need to mirror properly rather than simply swapping text. Each language should have its own URLs so search engines can index both versions, and the two versions should be linked correctly so the right one appears for the right searcher.

We plan the structure, build the templates and write the English content. Arabic copy is written by your team or a translator you choose, and we load and check it. Our guide to a bilingual Arabic and English website explains the planning steps in more detail, and our wider notes on multilingual website design cover how to keep two languages in step as the site grows.

How to choose a web designer in Sharjah

You can hire a studio in Sharjah, one elsewhere in the UAE or a remote team abroad. Location matters less than process, communication and ownership. When you speak to designers, ask:

  1. Who writes the content? Many projects stall because nobody owns the words. Agree who writes each page and in which language.
  2. What platform will you use and why? WordPress, Shopify and other platforms all have strengths. The answer should follow from what you need to manage, not the designer's habit.
  3. Who owns the domain, hosting and site files? The answer should be you.
  4. How will enquiries be tracked? Calls, messages and form submissions should all be measured from launch.
  5. What happens after launch? Ask about updates, security, backups and small changes, and what they cost.
  6. How do you handle time zones? A remote team should agree regular call times and response expectations up front.

If you are weighing up a team outside the UAE, our guide on how to hire an overseas web design agency sets out the questions that matter most.

Working with a remote studio from the UK

We work with UAE clients entirely online. The UK is a few hours behind the UAE for most of the year, which leaves a comfortable overlap in the working day for calls. A typical project runs like this:

  • Discovery call. We learn about your business, customers, competitors and goals, and agree the page list and languages.
  • Sitemap and content plan. You approve the structure and who writes what before any design.
  • Design. We design key page templates for mobile and desktop and refine them with your feedback.
  • Build and content loading. We build the site, add content in each language and set up tracking.
  • Testing and launch. We test forms, speed and layouts on real devices, then launch and hand over logins.

Most small business websites take 3-5 weeks from approved content to launch, and ecommerce sites usually take 5-12 weeks. Bilingual sites often sit towards the longer end because the second language needs its own writing and checks.

What to prepare before you contact a designer

A little preparation shortens any project and makes quotes easier to compare. Before your first call, gather:

  • A short list of your main services or product ranges, and the areas of Sharjah or the UAE you serve.
  • Two or three websites you like, and a note of what you like about each.
  • Your logo, brand colours and any photography you already own.
  • Access details for your current domain, hosting and analytics, if you have them.
  • A clear idea of who will write or approve the Arabic content, if you need it.

With these in hand, a designer can give you a firm quote and a realistic timeline rather than a broad estimate.

Getting found after launch

A new website does not bring visitors on its own. To appear when people in Sharjah search for what you offer, pages need clear titles, useful content, fast loading and correct technical set-up. We build these foundations into every site, and if you want ongoing help our SEO and GEO service covers search engines and AI answer tools. Typical SEO results take 3-6 months to show, depending on competition and how much content you can publish.

Regulated businesses, such as clinics or financial firms, may have rules about what they can say online. We provide general guidance only, and you should check current official guidance and have your own compliance adviser approve the content.
