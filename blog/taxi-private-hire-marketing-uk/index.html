Taxi and Private Hire Marketing in the UK: How to Win More Bookings and Accounts

Taxi firm marketing in the UK works when local search, a quick booking route and pages for airport, school and business accounts make you the obvious call.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most taxi customers decide in seconds from the map results, so a complete Google Business Profile with the right number and steady reviews comes first.
  • Separate pages for airport transfers, business accounts, contract work and longer journeys attract higher-value bookings than a single homepage.
  • Any claims about licensing, safety or vehicles must be accurate and up to date, and firms should check current guidance from their licensing authority.

Taxi firm marketing in the UK is about being the number people call or the site they book on when they need a car, whether that is right now or for an airport run next week. Independent taxi and private hire firms that grow usually win the local map results, make booking quick by phone, app or website, and build steady income from airport transfers and business accounts.

This guide is for independent taxi and private hire operators, executive car services and small firms competing with national apps. It covers how customers choose a firm, Google Business Profile, your website and booking, service pages, paid ads, business accounts and staying compliant in your marketing. We are a studio that builds websites and runs SEO and paid campaigns, so we focus on the digital side of winning bookings.

How customers choose a taxi firm

Taxi searches are often urgent. Someone at a station, a pub or a hospital types "taxi near me" or "taxi [town]" and calls the first firm that looks reliable. Others plan ahead, comparing firms for an early airport run, a school contract or a regular business journey.

That gives you two different marketing jobs. For urgent journeys, you need to appear in the map results with a clear phone number and good reviews. For planned journeys, you need pages that answer questions about prices, vehicles, reliability and booking in advance.

Typical customers include:

  • Local residents needing journeys to stations, shops, appointments and nights out.
  • Airport travellers wanting a fixed price and a reliable early pick-up.
  • Businesses needing account travel for staff, clients and visitors.
  • Councils, schools and organisations tendering for contract transport.
  • Visitors and event-goers needing journeys to hotels, venues and weddings.
  • Customers needing accessible vehicles, if you offer wheelchair-accessible cars.

Taxi firm marketing UK: win the map results first

For urgent searches, your Google Business Profile matters more than almost anything else. Our Google Business Profile guide covers the settings in detail. For a taxi firm, the priorities are:

  • The correct business category and a business name that matches your real trading name, without extra keywords.
  • A phone number that is answered quickly, at the hours you say you operate.
  • Accurate hours, including whether you run 24 hours.
  • Photos of your vehicles, signage and office if customers visit.
  • Services listed clearly, such as airport transfers, accounts and accessible vehicles.

Consistent details across directories help your local visibility. Our guide to local citations explains where to list your firm and how to keep your name, address and phone number consistent.

Reviews influence urgent decisions heavily. Ask customers for a review after the journey, through a booking confirmation message or a card in the vehicle with a QR code. Reply to every review, and handle complaints about lateness or pricing calmly and specifically. Our guide on how to respond to Google reviews can help.

Your website and booking options

Your website should load quickly on a phone, show your phone number at the top of every page and offer an easy way to book ahead. Many customers prefer to call, but others want to book online, especially for airport runs and early mornings.

Booking routes worth offering:

  • Click-to-call buttons on every page.
  • Online booking or quote forms for journeys booked in advance.
  • A booking app, if your dispatch system provides one.
  • Messaging options, if you can respond reliably.

Keep forms short. Pick-up, drop-off, date, time, passengers and luggage are usually enough to quote. Our guide to website contact forms explains how to make forms easy to complete on a phone. If your dispatch software has an online booking module, it can usually be embedded on your website.

Paid ads for taxi firms

Paid search can help you appear for urgent and planned searches in your area. Location targeting needs care: a taxi firm usually wants to appear for people searching within its operating area, not far beyond it. Our paid media service covers Google Ads and other channels.

Call ads and call extensions suit urgent journeys, because the customer wants to speak to someone quickly. For airport transfers and longer journeys, ads can point to booking or quote pages. Our guide to Google Ads for small business explains how to start with a sensible budget and avoid common mistakes.

Schedule ads for the hours you can answer the phone. Paying for calls that go to voicemail wastes budget and can harm your reputation.

Business accounts and contract work

Business accounts and contract transport can provide steady income that is less dependent on weekend demand. Local businesses, hotels, colleges and organisations may need regular travel for staff, clients or visitors.

To win account work, make it easy for businesses to find and trust you:

  • A dedicated business accounts page explaining how accounts work, booking options, invoicing and support.
  • A simple enquiry form for account set-up.
  • Clear information on your licensing, insurance and safety policies, kept accurate and up to date.
  • Direct outreach to local businesses, hotels and venues, supported by a professional website they can check.

Contract work for councils or schools usually goes through formal tender processes with their own requirements, so check each tender carefully and make sure your website supports what you say in your submissions.

Licensing and compliant marketing

Taxi and private hire licensing in the UK is handled by local licensing authorities, and the rules on how you advertise, describe your services and take bookings can differ between areas. For example, there may be rules about how private hire vehicles are booked and how firms can describe themselves. Check current guidance from your licensing authority before publishing marketing claims, and keep your website consistent with your licence conditions.

Be careful with pricing claims too. If you advertise fixed airport prices or offers, make sure they are accurate and include any conditions. Under the CAP Code, advertising must not mislead, so check current guidance if you are unsure.

SEO for taxi firms

Local SEO helps you appear for searches such as "taxi [town]", "airport taxi [town]", "[town] to Heathrow taxi" and "wheelchair accessible taxi [area]". Each main service page should be genuinely useful, with specific details about how the service works. If you cover several towns, create location pages only where you genuinely operate and can say something specific about each area.

FAQs

What is the best taxi firm marketing UK operators can start with?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile, a phone number that is always answered during your stated hours, regular reviews and a fast mobile website with clear booking options.

How can a local taxi firm compete with ride-hailing apps?

Focus on what apps may not offer as well: local knowledge, pre-booked airport transfers, business accounts, accessible vehicles and a personal service. Make these clear on your website and in your listings.

Should a taxi firm have online booking?

Usually, yes, especially for airport and pre-booked journeys. Many customers still prefer to call for urgent journeys, so keep click-to-call prominent too.

Are Google Ads worth it for taxi firms?

They can be, especially call ads for urgent journeys and search ads for airport transfers. Tight location targeting and scheduling ads for staffed hours help control costs.

Do taxi firms need separate pages for airport transfers?

It is usually worth it. Airport customers search with specific phrases and want details on pricing, pick-up times and luggage, which a dedicated page can answer well.

Are there rules on how taxi firms can advertise?

Yes. Licensing rules are set locally and advertising must not mislead. Check current guidance from your licensing authority and the relevant advertising codes before publishing claims.
