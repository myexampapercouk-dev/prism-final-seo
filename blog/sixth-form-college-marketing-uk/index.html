Sixth Form College Marketing in the UK: Recruiting Students for Post-Sixteen Study

Sixth form college marketing in the UK: course-led websites, SEO for subject searches, open evening campaigns and social ads that reach students and parents.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Sixth form college marketing has two audiences, students choosing courses and the parents who support them, and both need clear, separate routes through the website.
  • Course pages, open evening campaigns and short-form video on TikTok, YouTube and Instagram do most of the work, timed around the application and enrolment calendar.
  • We offer retainers from £350 to £1,500 a month with ad spend paid separately, and safeguarding, data protection and published information remain the college’s responsibility.

Sixth form college marketing in the UK means helping sixth form colleges, school sixth forms and further education colleges attract students for A levels, T levels, BTECs and other post-sixteen courses. The core of it is simple: young people need to find the right course quickly, see what college life is really like and apply before the deadline, while their parents need enough reassurance to support the choice.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio that works remotely with education providers. This guide explains how we approach post-sixteen recruitment, which channels matter, how to plan around the admissions calendar and what it costs. If you are a school marketing to younger pupils, our guide to school website design covers the wider picture.

Why post-sixteen recruitment is different

Most secondary schools recruit families who live nearby and choose a school when their child is ten or eleven. Post-sixteen providers recruit the student directly, often against several local options: a school sixth form, a standalone sixth form college, a general FE college, an apprenticeship or a job. The decision window is short, usually from the start of Year 11 to the spring, and the student is now the main decision-maker.

That changes the marketing in three ways:

  • The language changes. Content has to speak to sixteen-year-olds without sounding like an advert, and still give parents the facts they want.
  • The channels change. Students spend time on TikTok, YouTube and Instagram, while parents are more likely to use Google and Facebook.
  • The product is the course. Students search for subjects and career routes, not for colleges by name.

Sixth form college marketing UK: course pages do the heavy lifting

The most important pages on any college website are the course pages. They are what students land on from search, what they compare side by side and what they share with friends and parents. In our experience many college sites have thin course pages built from a prospectus template, with little that helps a sixteen-year-old decide.

A strong course page usually includes:

  • What the course is and what students actually do day to day.
  • Entry requirements, written in plain English.
  • How the course is assessed, and how much is exam or coursework.
  • Where it leads, including university courses, apprenticeships and jobs.
  • Subjects that combine well with it.
  • A clear apply button and the next open event.

We build course templates in the content management system so the college team can update them each year without help. Our guide to landing page design explains the principles behind pages that convert, and the same thinking applies to course pages.

SEO for subject and career searches

Students and parents search in specific ways: "A level psychology near me", "T level digital", "sixth form colleges in" a town, "college courses for animal care", or "what can I do after GCSEs". Search engine optimisation for a college means having a well-structured page for each of these intentions and making sure search engines can crawl and understand them.

Our SEO and GEO service covers technical health, course page structure, local search, structured data and content planning. For colleges with several campuses, each site needs its own location page and Google Business Profile. We also write guides that answer common questions, such as the difference between A levels and T levels or how to choose subjects, using information the college approves. These pages tend to attract students earlier in their decision and help AI search tools describe the college accurately. Our guide to AI Overviews and SEO explains why clear, factual content matters more than ever.

Social video and paid campaigns

For student recruitment, short-form video is often the most effective paid channel. Authentic clips of real students, real classrooms and real workshops tend to outperform polished brand films. We plan campaigns on TikTok, YouTube and Instagram for students, and on Google and Facebook for parents, each pointing to the right course or event page.

Our guides to TikTok Ads in the UK and YouTube Ads versus TikTok Ads explain the differences between platforms. Each platform restricts how advertisers can target users under eighteen, so campaigns rely on location, broad age ranges permitted by the platform and strong creative rather than detailed interest targeting. We keep copy factual and avoid pressure tactics.

Planning around the admissions calendar

Post-sixteen recruitment follows a predictable cycle. Campaigns work best when they are planned a year ahead.

PeriodStudent mindsetTypical activity
Early autumnStarting to think about optionsOpen evening campaigns, course guides, social video
Late autumn and winterComparing providers and applyingApplication reminders, remarketing, parent-focused search ads
SpringHolding offers, attending interviews and taster daysTaster day promotion, student life content, keep-warm emails
Summer and results periodConfirming plans or changing them after resultsLate application campaigns, clearing-style course availability pages

The results period is particularly important. Some students change their plans when their GCSE results arrive, and colleges that have clear "places still available" pages ready can capture that demand.

Converting interest into applications and enrolments

A college can generate thousands of website visits and still struggle to fill courses if the application process is confusing. We review the full journey from first visit to enrolment and look for points where students drop out: long forms, unclear entry requirements, broken links to an external application system or a slow mobile experience.

We set up tracking for the actions that matter, such as open event bookings, application starts and completed applications, in the college's own analytics and advertising accounts. Where the application system is run by a third party, we work with the college to track as much of the journey as their system allows, and we report on enquiries and applications rather than clicks.

Data protection, consent and safeguarding

Colleges collect data from young people, some of whom are under eighteen. In general terms, UK GDPR applies to forms and applicant data, and PECR applies to cookies and marketing messages, including texts and emails. Consent should be clear, and tracking tools should only run where the visitor has agreed when consent is required. Images and videos of students should only be used in line with the college's consent records and safeguarding policy.

We are not legal advisers. Check official guidance from the ICO and the Department for Education, along with any funding or inspection requirements, and take advice from your own data protection lead. Decisions about consent, safeguarding and published information rest with the college.

FAQs

What is sixth form college marketing in the UK?

It is the work of attracting students to post-sixteen courses through course-led websites, SEO, social video, paid campaigns and a clear application journey, timed around the recruitment calendar.

Which platforms reach sixteen-year-olds best?

Short-form video platforms such as TikTok, YouTube and Instagram usually reach students best, while Google and Facebook are better for reaching parents.

Can you target ads at under-eighteens?

Platforms restrict targeting for younger users, so we rely on location, permitted age ranges and strong creative. We follow each platform's current policies.

Do you work with school sixth forms as well as colleges?

Yes. We work with school sixth forms, standalone sixth form colleges and FE colleges, adapting the approach to each provider's competition.

How do you measure success?

We track open event bookings, application starts and completed applications in your own accounts, and report on those rather than clicks or impressions.

When should a college start its recruitment marketing?

Ideally before the summer, so course pages, tracking and creative are ready for the autumn open evenings when most students start comparing options.
