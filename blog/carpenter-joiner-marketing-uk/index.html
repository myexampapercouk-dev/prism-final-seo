Carpenter and Joiner Marketing in the UK: How to Win Better Projects

Carpenter and joiner marketing in the UK works when a strong portfolio, clear service pages and local search help you win the bespoke work you want to do.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Carpentry is judged on craftsmanship, so a well-photographed portfolio of your own work is the most persuasive marketing asset you can build.
  • Separate pages for fitted wardrobes, staircases, doors, kitchens and bespoke joinery attract the specific searches that lead to higher-value jobs.
  • Positioning matters: decide whether you want small repair jobs or bespoke projects, then shape your website, ads and photos around that work.

Carpenter and joiner marketing in the UK is about showing the quality of your work clearly enough that the right customers choose you for the projects you actually want. The carpenters and joiners who move up from odd jobs to bespoke commissions usually build a strong photo portfolio, create a page for each type of work, rank well locally and make it easy to start a conversation about a project.

This guide is for site carpenters, bench joiners, small joinery workshops and fitters who make or install fitted furniture, staircases, doors, windows and kitchens. We cover how customers choose a carpenter, what your website needs, how local SEO and ads fit in, and how to steer your marketing towards better-paid work.

How customers choose a carpenter or joiner

Carpentry customers vary widely. Some need a quick fix, such as a door hung or skirting replaced, and search "carpenter near me" wanting someone available this week. Others are planning something bigger: fitted alcove units, a new oak staircase, built-in wardrobes or bespoke sash windows. They search for the specific item, browse photos for inspiration, compare portfolios and often wait weeks for the right maker.

The second group is usually where the better margins are, and they choose almost entirely on visible quality. That means your photos, how you describe your work and how professional you appear online carry a lot of weight. Builders, architects and interior designers also look for reliable joiners, and they judge you the same way. Our post on interior designer website design gives a sense of how design professionals present work, which is useful when you want to attract their referrals.

Carpenter and joiner marketing UK: decide what you want more of

Before changing anything, decide which work you want to grow. A carpenter who wants steady small jobs needs a different approach from a joinery workshop chasing bespoke commissions. The table below shows how the focus shifts.

GoalWebsite focusMarketing focus
Steady local jobsServices list, areas covered, quick contact optionsGoogle Business Profile, reviews, local search ads
Fitted furnitureGallery of wardrobes, alcoves, media walls and storageInstagram, Pinterest-style photos, targeted local ads
Staircases and doorsDetailed project pages with materials and finishesProduct-specific SEO pages, builder and architect referrals
Trade and contract workCapabilities, capacity, insurance, workshop detailsDirect outreach to builders, developers and designers

Most businesses mix these, but leading with the work you want more of changes who gets in touch.

Build a portfolio that sells your craft

Photos do the heavy lifting for carpentry. Plan to photograph every project worth showing, ideally when the room is tidy and the light is good. Take wide shots that show the piece in its setting, close-ups of joints, grain, ironmongery and finishes, and progress photos from your workshop or the site. If the customer agrees, revisit once the room is furnished for a final set.

Group your portfolio by type of work, and add a short caption to each project: the timber or material, the finish, the room and the problem it solved. A sentence about why the client wanted it made to measure tells future customers you understand their needs. Never use other makers' photos or stock images of joinery, and only use client details or quotes with clear permission.

Your homepage should lead with your strongest images and a clear line about what you make and where. Our guide to website homepage design covers how to structure that first screen.

Create pages for each type of work

People search for specific items, so a page for each main service helps you rank and shows depth. Depending on what you do, useful pages might include:

  • Fitted wardrobes and bedroom furniture, including awkward spaces and eaves.
  • Alcove units, shelving and media walls, a popular search for living rooms.
  • Staircases and balustrades, whether replacement, refurbishment or new.
  • Internal and external doors, including hanging, making and fitting.
  • Windows, including sash repair or replacement, if you offer them.
  • Kitchens, whether bespoke or fitting supplied units.
  • General carpentry and repairs, for smaller jobs.

Each page should explain what you make or fit, the materials and finishes you work with, how a project runs from measure to installation, typical lead times in general terms and what affects the price. If you also fit kitchens and bathrooms, our post on kitchen and bathroom fitter website design covers that side in more detail.

Explain your process and pricing approach

Bespoke work can feel daunting to customers who have never commissioned anything before. A simple process page helps: initial chat, site visit and measure, design or drawings, quote, making, fitting and finishing. Explain how deposits work in general terms, how long they might wait for a slot and how you protect their home during installation. You do not need to publish fixed prices, but describing what affects cost, such as timber choice, size, complexity and finish, helps people come to you prepared.

If your work involves listed buildings, fire doors, structural changes or staircases that must meet building regulations, keep any explanation general and tell customers to check current official guidance and, where relevant, their local authority or building control.

Local SEO for carpenters and joiners

Most carpentry work is local, so start with your Google Business Profile. Choose accurate categories, set your service area, add your workshop address if customers visit, and upload project photos regularly. Ask every satisfied customer for a review and reply to each one, using the approach in our guide on responding to Google reviews.

On your website, mention the towns you cover naturally, and consider location pages only where you genuinely do plenty of work and can show local projects. Write helpful articles that answer real questions, such as how to choose timber for a staircase or what to consider before commissioning fitted wardrobes. Our local SEO guide explains the essentials.

Paid ads for carpentry work

Google Ads can help fill gaps in your diary, particularly for higher-value searches like "bespoke fitted wardrobes [town]" or "oak staircase makers". Keep targeting tight to your service area, send each ad to the matching service page and exclude searches about DIY, courses, jobs, tools and flat-pack assembly unless you want that work. Our guide to Google Ads for small businesses covers the basics.

Visual platforms such as Instagram and Facebook suit carpentry well. Short videos from the workshop, a time-lapse of an installation or a before-and-after of a dated staircase can build a local following. Paid social ads showing your best work to homeowners nearby can support this. Our paid media service covers both search and social campaigns if you would rather not run them yourself.

Win referrals from builders and designers

Builders, architects, interior designers and kitchen showrooms often need a reliable joiner. A short trade page on your website explaining your capabilities, workshop capacity, insurance and the kind of projects you take on makes it easy for them to recommend you. Follow up with a polite introduction and a link to your portfolio. Be clear about lead times and stick to them, because reliability is what keeps referrals coming.

FAQs

What is the best approach to carpenter and joiner marketing in the UK?

Decide which work you want more of, then build a portfolio and service pages around it. Combine a strong Google Business Profile and reviews with targeted ads and visual social media, and nurture referrals from builders and designers.

How many projects should I show on my website?

Quality matters more than quantity. A smaller set of well-photographed, well-captioned projects across your main services is more persuasive than a long gallery of rushed phone photos.

Should a joiner put prices on their website?

Fixed prices are difficult for bespoke work, but explaining what affects cost and giving honest guidance on how you quote helps customers come prepared. Some joiners show starting points for common items.

How do I attract more bespoke work instead of small repairs?

Lead your homepage and ads with bespoke projects, create detailed pages for fitted furniture, staircases and doors, and show your best finished work. Your messaging and photos shape the enquiries you receive.

Is Instagram worth it for carpenters?

It can be, because carpentry is highly visual. Regular posts of finished pieces, workshop progress and installations help build local recognition, especially when backed by a website where people can enquire.

Do I need separate pages for each carpentry service?

It is usually worthwhile. Customers search for specific items such as fitted wardrobes or staircases, and a focused page for each helps you rank and answers their questions properly.
