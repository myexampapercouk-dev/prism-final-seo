Choosing a Web Design Company in the UK: What to Look For

Looking for a web design company in the UK? Learn what a good studio delivers, what it should cost, how long it takes and the questions to ask before you sign.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 26 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good web design company in the UK plans, designs, builds and launches a fast, mobile-first site that is set up for search and measured from day one.
  • Prism Works Studio builds small business websites from £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites from £1,500 to £3,500, usually in three to five weeks for a standard site.
  • Ask every company who owns the site, how SEO is handled at launch, what support looks like afterwards and exactly what is included in the price.

A web design company plans, designs and builds a website that helps your business win enquiries or sales, then launches it in a way that search engines and customers can find. In the UK, a good web design company should give you a fixed scope, a clear price, a realistic timeline and a site you fully own once it goes live.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio that works remotely with businesses across the country. This guide explains what a web design company actually does, what it should cost, how to compare options and the questions worth asking before you commit. It is written to help you choose well, whether you choose us or someone else.

What a web design company in the UK actually does

The phrase covers a wide range of businesses, from one-person freelancers to large agencies with separate design, development and marketing teams. Most good companies follow a similar process, even if they describe it differently.

  • Discovery: understanding your business, customers, competitors and what the website must achieve.
  • Planning: agreeing the sitemap, page content, features and integrations before design starts.
  • Design: creating layouts that reflect your brand and guide visitors towards enquiring or buying.
  • Build: developing the site on a suitable platform, such as WordPress or Shopify, so it is fast and easy to edit.
  • Search setup: page titles, meta descriptions, headings, redirects, a sitemap and analytics so the site can be found and measured.
  • Launch and support: testing, going live and helping with updates, fixes and improvements afterwards.

Some companies stop at launch. Others, including us, can carry on with SEO, Google Ads and paid social so the new site has a steady flow of visitors. Whether you need that depends on how you plan to market the business.

What separates a good web design company from an average one

Almost every company will show you attractive designs. The differences that matter tend to show up after launch, when the site has to perform.

Speed and mobile performance

Most visitors to UK small business sites arrive on a phone. A good company designs mobile-first, keeps images light and avoids bloated plugins so pages load quickly. Ask how they test speed and whether they check Core Web Vitals before launch. Our guide to Core Web Vitals explains what those measures mean.

Search built in from the start

A beautiful site that nobody finds will not bring in work. The company should set sensible page titles, a clear heading structure, internal links, a sitemap and, for redesigns, a full redirect plan. Redesigns that ignore this can lose rankings overnight, which is why we wrote a guide to redesigning without losing rankings.

Accessibility and the law

A site that works for people using screen readers, keyboards or zoom is better for everyone. Good companies follow recognised accessibility guidance as standard. Our post on website accessibility in the UK covers the basics. For your own legal obligations, check official guidance or speak to your adviser.

Ownership and access

You should own your domain, hosting account, content and analytics. Some companies keep sites on their own servers or proprietary systems, which makes moving away difficult. Ask directly who owns what before you sign.

Questions to ask any web design company

  1. What exactly is included in the price? Pages, revisions, content help, stock images, forms, integrations and training.
  2. Which platform will you use, and why? The answer should depend on your needs, not on habit.
  3. Who will do the work? Find out whether design and development happen in-house or are passed on.
  4. How do you handle SEO at launch? Titles, redirects, sitemaps and analytics should be standard.
  5. What happens after launch? Ask about support, maintenance, hosting and how small changes are charged.
  6. Will I own everything? Domain, hosting, site files, content and accounts should be in your name.
  7. What do you need from me? Content, images and decisions are the most common causes of delay.

A company that answers these clearly and in writing is usually one that will be easy to work with.

Choosing the right platform

Most of the sites we build for UK businesses use WordPress or Shopify. WordPress suits service businesses, content-led sites and companies that want full control. Shopify suits most online shops because payments, stock and checkout are handled reliably out of the box. Our WordPress small business website guide and our Shopify versus WooCommerce comparison go into more detail.

A good web design company will explain the trade-offs honestly, including ongoing costs such as hosting, apps and plugins, rather than pushing one option for every client.

Why a remote web design company can work well

Many UK businesses now work happily with a remote studio. Prism Works Studio is UK-based and works remotely, so we run projects through video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can review at any time. That means we can work with businesses in any part of the UK without travel costs being built into your price.

If you prefer someone nearby, that is a fair choice too. What matters most is clear communication, a written scope and a partner who responds when you need them. If you are based in a city, our guides to web design in London and web design in Manchester look at local considerations.

What to have ready before you contact a company

You will get more accurate quotes, and a faster project, if you prepare a few things first.

  • A short summary of your business, your customers and what the site must achieve.
  • A list of pages or features you know you need, such as booking, a shop or a quote form.
  • Two or three websites you like, and why you like them.
  • Your logo, brand colours and any existing photography.
  • Access details for your current domain, hosting and analytics, if you have them.
  • A realistic budget range and any important deadlines.

Our small business website checklist covers this preparation step by step.

After launch: keeping the site working

A website is not finished on launch day. Software needs updating, content needs refreshing and search engines reward sites that stay useful. Plan for regular maintenance, security updates and backups. Our guide to website maintenance in the UK explains what that involves.

Many businesses also pair a new website with ongoing SEO or paid ads so it starts bringing in enquiries quickly. Organic search usually takes three to six months to build, while paid campaigns can drive traffic within days. Our website and ecommerce development services page explains how we combine the build with marketing support.

FAQs

How do I choose a web design company in the UK?

Compare a few companies on scope, price, timeline, ownership and post-launch support, not just on design. Ask each one the same questions in writing so you can compare their answers fairly.

How much does a web design company charge?

Our small business websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites £1,500 to £3,500. Other companies charge more or less depending on size, features and how much support is included.

How long does it take to build a website?

A standard business website usually takes three to five weeks and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks. Delays most often come from waiting on content, images or decisions.

Do you work with businesses outside London?

Yes. Prism Works Studio is UK-based and works remotely, so we work with businesses anywhere in the UK through video calls, shared documents and a staging site.

Will I own my website?

Yes. With us, your domain, hosting, site, content and analytics accounts stay in your name. Always confirm ownership in writing with any company you hire.

Does a web design company also do SEO?

Many do. We set up the essentials at launch and offer ongoing SEO and paid ads on monthly retainers from £350, with ad spend paid separately to the platform.
