Local search across Cardiff and South Wales

People searching locally often add an area name: Canton, Roath, Cathays, Pontcanna, Llandaff or Cardiff Bay within the city, or Penarth, Barry, Caerphilly, Pontypridd and Newport nearby. Your website and your Google Business Profile should reflect the places you really cover.

Start with a complete Google Business Profile: correct categories, services, hours, real photos and replies to every review. Our Google Business Profile guide covers each setting. Then build location pages only where you have something genuine to say, such as how you work in that area and the jobs you take on there. Our local SEO guide explains how to avoid thin, repeated pages.

Reviews deserve steady attention too. Ask happy customers at the right moment, make leaving a review simple and respond politely to all feedback. A consistent pattern of honest reviews supports both rankings and trust.

Running a bilingual Welsh and English website

Many Cardiff organisations publish in both Welsh and English, either by choice or because of their own obligations. Public bodies and some organisations that work with them may have duties under Welsh language legislation, so check the official guidance from the Welsh Language Commissioner or your own adviser about what applies to you.

From an SEO point of view, a bilingual site works best when:

  • Each language has its own URL, for example in separate folders, rather than switching text on the same page.
  • Hreflang tags tell search engines which page is the Welsh version and which is the English version.
  • A clear language switcher links each page to its matching translation.
  • Page titles, descriptions and headings are properly translated, not left in one language.
  • Keyword research is done separately for each language, because people search differently.

Prism writes in English. Welsh-language content should come from your own team or a professional translator, and we can build and optimise the site structure around it. If your site needs rebuilding to support two languages properly, our guide to web design in Cardiff covers that work.

Content that earns rankings

  3. Local pages for areas where you have real experience to share.

How to choose an SEO agency in Cardiff

Ask every agency the same questions so you can compare fairly:

  • What exactly happens in the first month?
  • How will you handle Welsh and English pages, and who provides the translations?
  • How do you earn links, and do you avoid paid link schemes?
  • Do reports connect rankings to enquiries or sales?
  • Who owns the content, website and accounts if we part ways?

Be wary of guaranteed first-place rankings or vague packages with no detail.

Data protection and sector rules

Analytics, cookies and enquiry forms are covered by UK GDPR and PECR, which set expectations on consent and the handling of personal data. Regulated sectors such as health, finance and legal services have their own advertising and content rules. We build with these in mind, but please check official guidance from the ICO and your regulator, and remember that compliance approval rests with you or your own adviser.

Common SEO mistakes we see on Cardiff websites

When we review sites for businesses in Cardiff and South Wales, the same problems come up again and again. Fixing them is often the quickest way to make progress.

  • One page trying to rank for everything. A single services page listing every offer rarely ranks well for any of them. Separate pages for each main service give search engines a clear answer for each search.
  • Welsh and English versions that are not linked. When translations exist but are not connected with hreflang and a language switcher, search engines may show the wrong version or treat pages as duplicates.
  • Outdated business details. Old addresses, phone numbers or opening hours on directories and profiles confuse both customers and search engines.
  • Slow mobile pages. Large images and heavy plugins slow sites down for people searching on their phones.
  • No measurement of enquiries. Without tracking calls and form submissions, it is hard to tell which pages and searches actually bring in work.

None of these need a large budget to fix, and they lay the groundwork for everything else in your SEO plan.
