WordPress for Small Business: How to Plan a Website That Brings in Enquiries

WordPress for small business guide: planning pages, content, design, hosting, SEO, forms and ongoing care so your UK business website turns visitors into leads.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A small business website should be planned around what customers need to know and the action you want them to take.
  • WordPress is flexible and widely supported, but it needs hosting, updates and care to stay fast and secure.
  • Clear service pages, trust signals, simple contact routes and basic SEO matter more than complex features.

WordPress is one of the most popular ways for small businesses to build a website, and for good reason. It is flexible, widely supported and can grow with your business, from a simple brochure site to a content-rich resource or an online shop. But the platform alone does not make a good website. What turns a WordPress site into a source of enquiries is clear planning: understanding your customers, structuring the right pages and making it easy for visitors to get in touch.

This guide is for UK small business owners planning a new WordPress website or rethinking an existing one. It covers whether WordPress suits you, how to plan pages and content, what to consider in design, hosting and SEO, and how to keep the site in good shape after launch.

Is WordPress right for a small business?

WordPress suits many small businesses, but it is worth weighing the pros and cons honestly.

On the plus side, it gives you control over design, content and structure. You can choose your own hosting and move it whenever you like. There is a huge ecosystem of themes and plugins, and plenty of developers and agencies who work with it. It handles blogs and resources well, and WooCommerce can add ecommerce if you need it.

On the other side, WordPress needs looking after. Updates, backups and security are your responsibility, or the responsibility of whoever supports your site. If you want a very simple site and no involvement in upkeep, a hosted website builder might suit you better. If you want flexibility and room to grow, WordPress is often a strong choice.

Start with goals, not design

Before thinking about colours and layouts, be clear about what the website needs to achieve. For most small businesses, the answer is to generate enquiries, bookings or sales. Ask:

  • Who are our customers and what problems do they need solved?
  • What questions do they ask before buying?
  • What action do we want visitors to take: call, fill in a form, book, buy?
  • How will we know if the website is working?

The answers shape everything that follows, from the pages you need to the wording on your buttons.

Plan the pages you need

Most small business websites need a core set of pages. Keep it focused and add more as you grow.

  • Homepage. Explain clearly what you do, who you help and where, with an obvious next step.
  • Service pages. Give each main service its own page so you can explain it properly and appear for relevant searches.
  • About page. Share your story, values and the people behind the business, honestly.
  • Contact page. Offer a simple form, phone number, email and, if relevant, address and map.
  • Case studies or examples. Show real work, with permission, if you have it.
  • Blog or resources. Answer common customer questions to build trust and search visibility.
  • Legal pages. Include a privacy notice and, where relevant, cookie information and terms.

If you serve particular towns or regions, location pages can help, but only if each offers genuinely useful local information. Our local SEO guide covers this.

Write content that answers real questions

Content is what persuades visitors and helps search engines understand your site. Write in plain English, from the customer's point of view. Explain what you offer, who it is for, how the process works and what customers can expect. Avoid jargon and vague claims.

Address common concerns directly: how to get a quote, how long things typically take, what areas you cover and how to get started. If you are unsure what to include, your sales calls and customer emails are a great source of questions to answer. Our content strategy guide offers more ideas.

Design for trust and clarity

Good design helps visitors find information quickly and feel confident contacting you. Priorities for small business sites include:

  • A clear layout with obvious navigation.
  • Prominent contact options on every page.
  • Real photos of your team, premises or work where possible.
  • Consistent branding that reflects your business.
  • Readable text and sufficient colour contrast.
  • A mobile-first approach, since many visitors will be on phones.

Trust signals matter too. Genuine reviews, accreditations you actually hold, clear contact details and transparent information all help. Never invent testimonials or claim memberships you do not have.

You will also need to decide how the site is built, whether with a theme, a page builder or a custom design. A good WordPress agency can explain the trade-offs.

Choose sensible hosting

Hosting affects speed, reliability and security. For small businesses, the cheapest plan is not always the best value. Look for WordPress-friendly hosting with free SSL, automatic off-site backups, current PHP versions and helpful support. Make sure the hosting and domain are registered in your business's name.

Keep plugins under control

Plugins add features, but each one adds code to maintain and a potential point of failure. Small business sites often need only a handful: perhaps an SEO plugin, a forms plugin, a backup tool and security protection. Choose well-maintained plugins from reputable developers and remove anything you do not use.

Build in SEO from the start

Search visibility is easier to plan from the start than to retrofit. Basic steps include:

  • Researching the words customers actually use to find services like yours.
  • Giving each important page a clear focus, descriptive title and meta description.
  • Using headings logically and writing helpful content.
  • Keeping URLs short and readable.
  • Making sure the site is fast and works well on mobile.
  • Setting up Google Search Console and an XML sitemap.
  • Creating and maintaining a Google Business Profile if you serve local customers.

Our Google Business Profile guide and SEO and GEO service page explain more.

Make contacting you easy

The whole point of most small business websites is to generate contact. Make it effortless. Put phone numbers and contact buttons where people expect them, keep forms short, explain what happens after someone gets in touch and make sure form submissions actually reach you. Test forms regularly, as broken forms quietly lose leads.

Set up analytics and conversion tracking so you can see which pages and channels lead to enquiries.

Accessibility and legal basics

An accessible website is easier for everyone to use. Use clear headings, descriptive link text, alt text for meaningful images, good colour contrast and forms that work with keyboards and screen readers. If you collect personal data, you will need a privacy notice and must handle data in line with UK data protection law. If you use non-essential cookies, consider how you obtain consent. This guide is not legal advice.

Plan for life after launch

A website is not finished when it goes live. WordPress needs regular updates to core, themes and plugins, along with backups and security monitoring. Content should be kept current, with new services, team changes and fresh articles added over time. Our website maintenance guide explains what ongoing care involves.

Review performance regularly. Look at which pages attract visitors, where enquiries come from and where people drop off. Small improvements, such as a clearer call to action or a better service page, can make a real difference.

Costs depend on the size of the site, design approach, content needs, functionality and ongoing support. Our WordPress website cost guide and small business website design cost guide explain the factors involved. For how we work, see our pricing page.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Starting with design before understanding customers and goals.
  • Cramming all services onto one page.
  • Hiding contact details or using long, complicated forms.
  • Installing lots of plugins without a clear need.
  • Letting someone else own the hosting or domain.
  • Launching and then never updating the site.

FAQs

Is WordPress good for small business websites?

Yes, for many small businesses. It is flexible and widely supported, though it needs hosting, updates and security care.

How many pages does a small business website need?

There is no fixed number. Most need a homepage, a page for each main service, an about page and a contact page, plus legal pages. Add more as your business grows.

Can I update a WordPress website myself?

Usually, yes. Most content changes can be made in the WordPress editor once the site is set up well.

Do I need a blog on my business website?

Not necessarily, but helpful articles that answer customer questions can build trust and attract search traffic over time.

Should I use WordPress or a website builder?

It depends on how much control and flexibility you want and who will look after the site. Builders are simpler to maintain; WordPress offers more freedom.

Can I add a shop to my WordPress site later?

Yes. WooCommerce can add ecommerce to an existing WordPress site. Our WooCommerce agency guide explains more.
