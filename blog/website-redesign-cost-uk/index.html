Website Redesign Cost in the UK: What Drives It, SEO Risk and Phases

A website redesign in the UK often costs £1,000 to £3,500 for small and ecommerce sites. See what drives cost, the phases and how to protect SEO.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A small business website redesign typically costs around £1,000 to £2,000, and an ecommerce redesign around £1,500 to £3,500.
  • Page count, content, integrations, platform changes and migration work drive most of the cost.
  • Budget for SEO migration from day one: redirects and checks cost far less than lost rankings.

A website redesign in the UK typically costs around £1,000 to £2,000 for a small business site and £1,500 to £3,500 for an ecommerce store, depending on page count, content and integrations. The hidden cost is SEO risk: a redesign without proper redirects and checks can lose rankings that took years to build.

This guide breaks down what drives website redesign cost, how a redesign is usually phased, where the SEO risk sits, and how to set a realistic budget. If your main worry is protecting search traffic, read it alongside our guide to a website redesign without losing rankings, which covers the migration steps in detail.

What drives the cost of a redesign

A redesign is not always cheaper than a new build. Sometimes it is more complex, because you have existing content, URLs, integrations and rankings to protect. The main cost drivers are:

  • Number of page templates: a site with a home page, service page, blog and contact template costs less than one with ten distinct layouts.
  • Content: rewriting, restructuring or moving hundreds of pages takes time. Reusing good content is cheaper.
  • Platform change: moving from one CMS to another, for example WordPress to Shopify, means data migration and new templates.
  • Integrations: booking systems, CRMs, payment gateways, stock feeds and email tools all add build and testing time.
  • Ecommerce data: products, variants, customers and order history need careful mapping.
  • SEO migration: redirect mapping, metadata transfer and post-launch monitoring.
  • Feedback rounds: clear decisions keep cost and timeline under control.

Redesign or refresh?

Not every site needs a full redesign. If the structure works and rankings are healthy, a refresh of design, speed and key pages may deliver most of the benefit for less money and less risk.

OptionWhen it fitsSEO risk
Refresh (same platform, same URLs)Dated look, slow pages, weak calls to actionLow, if URLs and content stay in place
Redesign (same platform, new structure)Confusing navigation, outgrown templatesMedium, redirects needed for changed URLs
Replatform and redesignPlatform limits growth, costly to maintainHigher, full migration plan essential

The phases of a website redesign

A well-run redesign usually follows these phases. Knowing them helps you understand where the budget goes.

  1. Audit and discovery: review analytics, rankings, top pages, backlinks and conversion paths. Decide what to keep, merge or remove.
  2. Structure and planning: new sitemap, URL plan and redirect map.
  3. Design: key page layouts and components, reviewed on mobile first.
  4. Build: templates, content entry, integrations and forms.
  5. Pre-launch checks: redirects tested, metadata in place, analytics and consent working, speed checked.
  6. Launch: go live at a quiet time, submit the sitemap and monitor.
  7. Post-launch monitoring: watch Search Console for errors, 404s and ranking changes over the following weeks.

Discovery is the step people most want to skip, and the one that most often saves money. Our technical SEO audit checklist shows what to review before the build starts.

Where the SEO risk sits

Most lost rankings after a redesign come from a small set of avoidable mistakes:

  • Changed URLs with no 301 redirects, so old links and rankings point to 404 pages.
  • Removed content that was ranking or earning links.
  • Lost titles, meta descriptions and headings.
  • Staging sites left blocked from indexing after launch.
  • Slower templates that hurt Core Web Vitals.
  • Broken internal links and a missing sitemap.

Each of these is cheap to prevent and expensive to fix after the fact. Hidden costs to plan for

Quotes often focus on design and build, but several costs sit around the edges of a redesign. Planning for them up front avoids awkward conversations later.

  • Content creation: new copy, photography or product descriptions may need a writer or photographer if your team cannot supply them.
  • Licences: premium themes, apps, plugins, fonts and stock images may carry one-off or annual fees.
  • Hosting and domains: a better hosting plan may be part of the reason for redesigning, and it is an ongoing cost.
  • Internal time: someone in your business needs to gather content, give feedback and approve pages. Delays here are the most common reason projects overrun.
  • Training: your team needs to know how to edit the new site safely.
  • Post-launch fixes: small issues always surface once real visitors arrive, so allow time for a round of tweaks.
  • Legal and privacy: cookie banners, privacy policies and accessibility checks may need updating, especially if you add new tracking or forms.

How to compare redesign quotes

Two quotes for the same site can differ widely because they cover different work. When comparing, check whether each includes discovery, the number of page templates, content entry, redirect mapping, analytics and consent setup, speed testing, training and a period of post-launch support. A cheaper quote that leaves out migration may cost more in the long run if rankings drop. Ask each agency how they will measure success after launch and what happens if search traffic falls. Our guide on how to choose a digital marketing agency in the UK covers wider questions about contracts and reporting.

Platform choice also affects long-term cost. Hosted platforms bundle hosting and security but charge for apps, while self-hosted sites give more control but need maintenance. For online shops, the choice between a hosted platform and a self-hosted one is worth making carefully before the redesign begins, because switching again later means another migration. Whichever you choose, plan who will handle updates, backups and security once the new site is live, and include that ongoing care in your annual budget rather than treating launch as the finish line.

How to set a realistic redesign budget

Start with what the site must do: generate enquiries, take bookings or sell products. Then list the templates, integrations and content work needed. Add a line for SEO migration and a small contingency for content or feedback delays. Keep ongoing costs such as hosting, maintenance and marketing in a separate budget so the launch is not the end of investment.

Website redesign checklist

  • Benchmark traffic, rankings, conversions and top landing pages before you start.
  • Export every current URL and map each one to its new home.
  • Keep or improve content on pages that rank or earn links.
  • Transfer titles, meta descriptions and headings.
  • Test 301 redirects on staging before launch.
  • Check mobile layout, page speed and forms.
  • Set up analytics, key events and consent before go-live.
  • Remove staging noindex rules at launch and submit the sitemap.
  • Monitor Search Console and fix 404s in the first few weeks.

FAQs

What is the typical website redesign cost in the UK?

A small business website redesign typically costs around £1,000 to £2,000, and an ecommerce redesign around £1,500 to £3,500. Larger sites, platform changes and complex integrations can cost more.

How long does a website redesign take?

A small website usually takes 3 to 5 weeks and an ecommerce site 5 to 12 weeks, depending on content, integrations and how quickly feedback is given.

Will a redesign hurt my Google rankings?

It can if URLs change without redirects or ranking content is removed. With a redirect map, metadata transfer and post-launch monitoring, most of that risk can be managed.

Is a redesign cheaper than a new website?

Not always. Existing content, URLs and integrations need to be migrated and protected, which can make a redesign as involved as a new build.

Should I redesign or just refresh my website?

If the structure works and rankings are healthy, a refresh of design, speed and key pages may be enough. A full redesign suits sites with outgrown navigation, templates or platforms.

What costs are not included in a redesign quote?

Hosting, domains, premium themes, apps and plugin licences are often separate, as is ongoing marketing. Ask for a clear list of what is and is not included.
