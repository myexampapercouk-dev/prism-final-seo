Google Ads Agency for Copenhagen Businesses: Remote PPC Management from a UK Studio

Looking for a Google Ads agency in Copenhagen? How Danish and English campaigns are planned, tracked with consent and managed remotely, plus our fees in pounds.

A Google Ads agency for a Copenhagen business should show your ads to people searching for what you sell, at a cost that leaves you a profit. That means researching the Danish and English terms buyers really use, building a tight campaign structure, writing ads that match each search, tracking conversions with consent in place and adjusting bids and budgets every week.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that manages Google Ads remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Copenhagen or anywhere in Denmark. Our paid media service runs by video call, shared documents and monthly reports, with Copenhagen one hour ahead of UK time. We write ads in English and work with your team or a translator on Danish versions.

What Copenhagen businesses need from Google Ads

Copenhagen is the Danish capital and the commercial centre of the country, with a strong mix of design and furniture brands, technology and software firms, life sciences, shipping and logistics, professional services, hospitality and a busy online retail scene. Many of these businesses sell well beyond Denmark, so search campaigns often need to reach several audiences at once.

  • Danish-speaking buyers in Copenhagen and Zealand. Local customers who search in Danish and expect Danish landing pages.
  • Buyers across Denmark. National customers for online shops and service firms working remotely.
  • Nordic and wider European buyers. Customers in Sweden, Norway, Germany and beyond, each with their own language.
  • English-speaking searchers. International companies, expats, students and visitors who search in English.

English is widely spoken in Denmark, but that does not mean Danish buyers search in English. Many search in Danish for everyday products and services, and in English for some technical or business terms. Good campaigns reflect how people really search rather than assuming one language covers everyone.

Danish and English campaigns

Translating English keywords into Danish rarely works well. Danish compound words, different spellings and the mix of Danish and English terms in some sectors mean each language needs its own research. We build separate campaigns per language so a Danish search sees a Danish ad leading to a Danish landing page, and an English search sees English throughout.

Prism writes English ads in-house. For Danish, we supply the structure, keyword themes and English drafts, then a native speaker on your team or a translator writes the final copy, which we check against character limits and the campaign plan. If your site does not yet have proper Danish landing pages, we will say so, because sending Danish ads to English pages usually wastes budget.

Location targeting around Copenhagen

Google Ads can target by city, region, postcode area or radius. A Copenhagen clinic, restaurant or trade business might target the city and nearby municipalities, while a national online shop targets the whole of Denmark. Firms selling into Sweden often add the Malmö area or all of Sweden as a separate campaign with Swedish ads.

The default presence settings can show ads to people who are merely interested in a location rather than physically there, which can waste money for local services. Our guide to Google Ads location targeting explains the settings we use, and our post on international PPC covers accounts that run across several countries.

Campaign types we use

  1. Search campaigns. Text ads on searches with clear buying intent. The core of most accounts.
  2. Shopping and Performance Max. For online shops with a product feed in Google Merchant Center, set up for the Danish market and currency.
  3. Remarketing. Ads shown to people who visited your site, where consent allows.
  4. YouTube and Demand Gen. For brands that need awareness alongside search demand.

Bidding strategy depends on how much conversion data the account has. Newer accounts often start with tighter control, then move to smart bidding once enough conversions are recorded. Our guide to Google Ads bidding strategies explains the options.

Tracking and cookie consent in Denmark

Google Ads only optimises well when it knows which clicks lead to enquiries or sales. We set up conversion tracking for forms, calls, bookings and purchases. Our guide to Google Ads conversion tracking setup covers the process step by step.

Danish websites fall under the GDPR for personal data, and the EU ePrivacy rules, as applied in Danish law, mean analytics and advertising cookies normally need consent before they load. The Danish data protection authority and other official bodies publish guidance worth reading. We connect tracking to your consent banner and set up Google's consent mode, so the account still receives modelled data when visitors decline. Legal wording and compliance decisions should come from your own legal adviser, and official guidance should be checked before relying on any summary.

Landing pages and accessibility

A strong landing page can lift results more than any bid change. It should load quickly, match the ad's promise, state the offer clearly and make the next step easy. Our posts on web design for Copenhagen businesses and WordPress for Danish firms cover the website side in more detail.

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing products and services, including a wide range of online shops and booking services. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but building to WCAG AA, the level referenced by the harmonised standard EN 301 549, is good practice for any page receiving paid traffic. Whether the Act applies to your business is a question for your adviser.

Cutting wasted spend

Click costs in competitive Copenhagen sectors can be high, so waste adds up quickly. The usual causes are broad keywords matching irrelevant searches, ads showing in the wrong language or location, weak landing pages and budgets spread across too many small campaigns. Each week we review the search terms report and add negative keywords. Our negative keywords guide explains how this works.

We prefer steady weekly changes to dramatic rebuilds. Small, regular adjustments to bids, ads, schedules and budgets let the account keep what it has learned while improving over time.

FAQs

Do you have a Google Ads agency office in Copenhagen?

No. Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Denmark. We work remotely by video call, shared documents and monthly reports, one hour behind Copenhagen time.

Can you run Google Ads in Danish and English?

Yes. We build separate campaigns for each language. English ads are written in-house, and Danish ads come from your team or a native-speaking translator, which we check against the plan.

How much does a Google Ads agency in Copenhagen cost with your studio?

Our management retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month, quoted in pounds with kroner or euro quotes on request. Ad spend and translation are paid separately.

Does cookie consent affect Google Ads tracking in Denmark?

Yes. Advertising cookies normally need consent before they load, so some conversions are not observed directly. We set up consent mode so the account still receives modelled data, and your legal adviser approves the wording.

Can you advertise in Sweden and Norway as well as Denmark?

Yes. We set up separate campaigns per country and language, with ad copy in each language supplied by your team or a translator.

How long does Google Ads take to work?

Results typically build over three to six months. Early weeks focus on data, tracking and cutting waste, and performance improves as the account learns.
