Website Maintenance UK: What's Included, a Checklist and Costs to Compare

What does website maintenance in the UK include? See the core tasks, a monthly checklist, the costs to compare and how to choose a support provider wisely.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Website maintenance keeps your site secure, fast, backed up and accurate, so it keeps winning enquiries and sales rather than quietly breaking.
  • A good support plan lists exact tasks, response times and who owns hosting, domain and licences.
  • Compare maintenance quotes on what is included and how quickly problems are fixed, not just the monthly figure.

Website maintenance in the UK covers the regular work that keeps a business website secure, fast, backed up and up to date, including software updates, security checks, backups, fixes and content changes. Without it, sites slowly break, slow down and slip in search, often without anyone noticing until enquiries dry up.

Many businesses treat a website as a one-off purchase. In reality it is more like a vehicle: it needs regular servicing to stay reliable. This guide explains what website maintenance includes, gives you a practical checklist, sets out the costs to compare and helps you choose the right kind of support.

Website maintenance UK: what's included?

Maintenance plans vary, but a sensible plan for a UK business site covers these areas.

  • Software updates: the content management system, themes and plugins kept current, tested before going live where possible.
  • Security: monitoring for malware, login protection, SSL certificate renewal and fixing vulnerabilities.
  • Backups: regular automatic backups stored off the server, with tested restores.
  • Uptime monitoring: alerts when the site goes down, and someone to respond.
  • Speed checks: keeping pages quick on mobile, compressing images and reviewing heavy scripts.
  • Forms and tracking: checking contact forms deliver and GA4 still records enquiries.
  • Content updates: small changes to text, prices, images and team or opening details.
  • SEO health: watching for broken links, crawl errors and indexing problems in Google Search Console.
  • Legal basics: privacy policy, cookie consent and terms kept in line with UK GDPR and PECR.

Why maintenance matters for UK businesses

An unmaintained site can cost far more than a maintenance plan. Common problems include hacked sites that show spam, contact forms that silently stop sending, expired SSL certificates that trigger browser warnings, and slow pages that lose visitors and rankings. For online shops, a broken checkout means lost sales every hour it goes unnoticed. Our technical SEO audit checklist shows how technical issues affect search visibility.

Monthly website maintenance checklist

Whether you maintain the site yourself or use a provider, this checklist covers the essentials.

  • Apply core, theme and plugin updates, after taking a backup.
  • Confirm backups ran and that at least one recent backup can be restored.
  • Check the SSL certificate is valid and not close to expiry.
  • Submit a test enquiry through every form and confirm it arrives.
  • For shops, place a test order and check payment and confirmation emails.
  • Review Google Search Console for errors, broken pages and indexing issues.
  • Check GA4 is recording visits and key events such as enquiries or purchases.
  • Run a speed test on the homepage and a key service or product page.
  • Scan for broken links and fix or redirect them.
  • Update outdated content: prices, opening hours, offers and contact details.
  • Review user accounts and remove anyone who no longer needs access.
  • Check the domain renewal date and auto-renew settings.

DIY or a maintenance provider?

Doing it yourself can work for simple sites if you have the time and confidence, and you follow the checklist every month. A provider makes sense when the site generates important leads or sales, when you lack the time, or when nobody in the business is comfortable restoring a backup. When comparing providers, ask:

  1. Exactly which tasks are included each month?
  2. How quickly will you respond if the site goes down?
  3. Are updates tested before they reach the live site?
  4. Where are backups stored, and how often are restores tested?
  5. How many hours of content changes are included?
  6. Will I keep ownership of the domain, hosting and licences?
  7. What reports will I receive, and how often?

Maintenance and SEO

Search engines favour sites that are fast, secure and error free. Regular maintenance protects the rankings you have earned, and pairs naturally with ongoing SEO work. Signs your website needs better maintenance

Many maintenance problems give warning signs before they become serious. If you notice any of the following, it is worth reviewing who looks after the site and how.

  • The admin area shows a long list of pending updates.
  • Pages take noticeably longer to load than they used to, especially on mobile.
  • Enquiries have dropped, but traffic has not.
  • Browsers show security warnings, or visitors mention odd redirects.
  • Nobody is sure where backups are kept or when the domain renews.
  • Google Search Console shows a growing number of errors or pages not indexed.
  • Small text changes take days because nobody knows how to make them safely.

What a good maintenance report includes

A maintenance provider should show you what has been done, not just send an invoice. A useful monthly report is short and plain, covering which updates were applied and whether any were held back for testing, confirmation that backups ran and where they are stored, uptime over the month and any incidents with how they were fixed, the results of form and checkout tests, speed test results for key pages, and any Search Console issues found and resolved. It should also flag anything that needs a decision from you, such as an ageing plugin that should be replaced or a licence that is about to expire. If a provider cannot produce something like this, ask how they would show you the work is being done.

Whoever looks after your site, keep a short record of key details in one safe place: where the site is hosted, who holds the domain, which licences need renewing and when, and how to restore a backup. If a provider changes or a problem happens out of hours, this record saves time, money and a great deal of stress for everyone involved.

FAQs

What does website maintenance UK businesses need include?

Software updates, security monitoring, backups, uptime checks, form and tracking tests, speed checks, content updates and basic SEO health checks.

How often should a website be maintained?

Updates and backups should be checked at least monthly, with security and uptime monitored continuously. Content should be reviewed every few months.

How much does website maintenance cost?

It depends on the site and level of support. Compare hosting, licences, included tasks and response times. Maintenance is usually quoted to the site; our separate marketing retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month.

Do Shopify sites need maintenance?

Less technical upkeep than self-hosted sites, but apps, themes, product data, speed and tracking still need regular checks.

What happens if I do not maintain my website?

It becomes more likely to be hacked, slow down, lose rankings or stop sending enquiries, often without you noticing.

Should I rebuild or keep maintaining an old website?

If the site is outdated, slow or hard to update, a rebuild may cost less over time. Our small websites are £1,000 to £2,000 and take 3 to 5 weeks.
