Website Maintenance and Support in the UK: What's Included, a Checklist and Costs to Compare

What website maintenance and support involve, a monthly checklist, the costs to compare and how to decide between doing it yourself and using a provider.

By the Prism Works Studio team · Updated 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Website maintenance is routine care that keeps a site secure, fast, backed up and accurate; support is the help you get when something breaks or needs changing.
  • A monthly checklist of updates, backups, form tests, search checks and renewals covers the essentials, whoever does the work.
  • Compare providers on included tasks, response times and who owns the domain, hosting and licences, not just the monthly figure.

Website maintenance is the regular work that keeps a website secure, fast, backed up and accurate after launch: software updates, security checks, backups, fixes and content changes. Website support is the help you get when something goes wrong or needs changing. Without either, a site can slowly break, slow down and slip in search, sometimes without anyone noticing until enquiries dry up.

A website is less like a one-off purchase and more like a vehicle: it needs regular servicing to stay reliable. This overview explains what web maintenance involves, gives you a monthly checklist, sets out the costs to compare and helps you decide who should do it. If you are already reviewing a contract, our guide to what a website support plan should include goes through the terms in detail.

Website maintenance and support: what is the difference?

The two words are often used together, but they describe different jobs.

MaintenanceSupport
Nature of the workPlanned and routineReactive, in response to a request or fault
ExamplesUpdates, backups, monitoring, speed and link checksFixing a broken form, restoring a page, changing some text
Main aimPrevent problemsSolve problems and make changes

Most business sites need some of both. Maintenance reduces the number of faults, and support means someone is responsible when one still happens.

What website maintenance includes

Scope varies by site, but sensible website maintenance for a UK business usually covers these areas.

  • Software updates: the content management system, themes and plugins kept current, tested before going live where possible.
  • Security: monitoring for malware, login protection, SSL certificate renewal and fixing vulnerabilities.
  • Backups: regular automatic backups stored off the server, with tested restores.
  • Uptime monitoring: alerts when the site goes down, and someone to respond.
  • Speed checks: keeping pages quick on mobile, compressing images and reviewing heavy scripts.
  • Forms and tracking: checking contact forms deliver and GA4 still records enquiries.
  • Content updates: small changes to text, prices, images and team or opening details.
  • SEO health: watching for broken links, crawl errors and indexing problems in Google Search Console.
  • Legal basics: privacy information and cookie consent reviewed when the site changes, with UK GDPR and PECR in mind. Take advice if you are unsure what applies to you.

Why maintenance matters for UK businesses

Neglect tends to cost more when something finally fails. Problems can include hacked sites that show spam, contact forms that silently stop sending, expired SSL certificates that trigger browser warnings, and slow pages that frustrate visitors. For online shops, a broken checkout can mean lost sales for as long as it goes unnoticed. Our guides to WordPress security and technical SEO audits go deeper on those risks.

Monthly website maintenance checklist

Whether you maintain the site yourself or use a provider, this checklist covers the essentials.

  • Apply core, theme and plugin updates, after taking a backup.
  • Confirm backups ran and that at least one recent backup can be restored. Our backup and recovery guide explains how to plan this.
  • Check the SSL certificate is valid and not close to expiry.
  • Submit a test enquiry through every form and confirm it arrives.
  • For shops, place a test order and check payment and confirmation emails.
  • Review Google Search Console for errors, broken pages and indexing issues.
  • Check GA4 is recording visits and key events such as enquiries or purchases.
  • Run a speed test on the homepage and a key service or product page.
  • Scan for broken links and fix or redirect them.
  • Update outdated content: prices, opening hours, offers and contact details.
  • Review user accounts and remove anyone who no longer needs access.
  • On WordPress, review plugins from time to time using a plugin audit.
  • Check the domain renewal date and auto-renew settings.

DIY or a maintenance provider?

Doing it yourself can work for simple sites if you have the time and confidence, and you follow the checklist every month. A provider makes sense when the site generates important leads or sales, when you lack the time, or when nobody in the business is comfortable restoring a backup.

When comparing website support and maintenance providers, start with three questions: exactly which tasks are included, how quickly they will respond if the site goes down, and whether you keep ownership of the domain, hosting and licences. Our support plan guide has the fuller list of questions and terms, and our website ownership guide explains what you should hold in your own name.

Does your web host already do this?

Possibly in part. A host may look after the server and offer backups, but that does not necessarily mean anyone is testing your updates, checking your forms or fixing a fault in your theme. Read what your hosting plan includes rather than assuming, and if the host itself is the problem, see our guide to changing hosting without downtime.

Maintenance and SEO

Maintenance will not win rankings on its own, but broken pages, slow loading and indexing errors can hold a site back. Routine checks catch those problems early and pair naturally with ongoing SEO work. If you are planning a redesign, read how to redesign a website without losing rankings, and see our SEO and GEO service for ongoing search support.

Signs your website needs better maintenance

Many maintenance problems give warning signs before they become serious. If you notice any of the following, it is worth reviewing who looks after the site and how.

  • The admin area shows a long list of pending updates.
  • Pages take noticeably longer to load than they used to, especially on mobile.
  • Enquiries have dropped, but traffic has not.
  • Browsers show security warnings, or visitors mention odd redirects.
  • Nobody is sure where backups are kept or when the domain renews.
  • Google Search Console shows a growing number of errors or pages not indexed.
  • Small text changes take days because nobody knows how to make them safely.

How to know the work is being done

A provider should show you what has been done, not just send an invoice. Ask for a short, plain report covering updates, backups, any incidents and anything that needs a decision from you. Our support plan guide covers reporting in more detail.

Whoever looks after your site, keep a short record of key details in one safe place: where the site is hosted, who holds the domain, which licences need renewing and when, and how to restore a backup. If a provider changes or a problem happens out of hours, this record can save a lot of time and stress.

FAQs

What is website maintenance?

It is the routine care a site needs after launch: software updates, security monitoring, backups, uptime checks, form and tracking tests, speed checks, content updates and basic SEO health checks.

What is the difference between website maintenance and support?

Maintenance is planned work that helps prevent problems. Support is help when something breaks or needs changing. Many plans combine the two, so check what each one includes.

How often should a website be maintained?

Updates and backups should be checked at least monthly, with security and uptime monitored continuously. Content should be reviewed every few months.

How much does website maintenance cost?

It depends on the site and level of support. Compare hosting, licences, included tasks and response times. Our maintenance is not a listed package, so ask for a quote; our separate marketing retainers start from £350, £750 or £1,500 a month.

Do Shopify sites need maintenance?

Less technical upkeep than self-hosted sites, but apps, themes, product data, speed and tracking still need regular checks.

What happens if I do not maintain my website?

It becomes more likely to be hacked, slow down, lose rankings or stop sending enquiries, often without you noticing.

Should I rebuild or keep maintaining an old website?

If the site is outdated, slow or hard to update, a rebuild may cost less over time. Our small websites start from £1,000 to £2,000 and typically take 3 to 5 weeks.
