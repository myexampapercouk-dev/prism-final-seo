What is Shopify Plus, and who needs it?

Shopify Plus is Shopify's plan for larger and faster-growing merchants. It adds features that standard plans do not offer or limit, such as more control over the checkout, automation tools, expansion stores for other markets, wholesale features and more staff accounts. It also comes with a higher monthly platform fee set by Shopify, so it only makes sense when those features solve real problems.

Brands usually look at Plus when they are outgrowing a standard Shopify plan, running several storefronts, selling both direct-to-consumer and wholesale, or moving from an older platform that has become costly to maintain. Check Shopify's own website for current plan pricing and features, because they change over time.

Shopify Plus migrations for US brands

Most Shopify Plus projects start as migrations. A brand may be moving from Magento (Adobe Commerce), Salesforce Commerce Cloud, WooCommerce, BigCommerce or a custom platform. Each has its own data structure, and the move is never as simple as an export and import.

The key questions in any migration are what data moves, what gets cleaned up and what gets left behind. Product data often needs restructuring into Shopify's product, variant and metafield model. Customer accounts need a clear plan for passwords and logins. Order history may need to move for customer service and reporting. Our guide to Shopify metafields explains how extra product data is stored on Shopify.

If you are moving from WooCommerce, our post on WooCommerce to Shopify migration covers the specific steps involved.

Protecting SEO during a Shopify Plus move

For many US brands, organic search brings a large share of revenue, and a migration is the moment it is most at risk. Shopify uses fixed URL patterns for products, collections and pages, so almost every migration changes URLs. Without a full redirect map, Google and returning customers hit error pages and rankings fall.

Our SEO checklist for a Shopify Plus migration includes:

  • Crawling the old site to capture every live URL, including old products still earning links.
  • Mapping each URL to its closest match on the new store and setting up redirects. Our guide to Shopify URL redirects shows how this works.
  • Carrying over page titles, meta descriptions and headings that already rank.
  • Checking structured data for products, reviews and breadcrumbs.
  • Submitting the new sitemap and monitoring Search Console daily after launch.

Our broader SEO migration checklist covers the same principles for any platform change.

Checkout, speed and conversion on Shopify Plus

One of the main reasons brands move to Plus is greater control over the checkout. Plus merchants can customise the checkout further than standard plans allow, using Shopify's current checkout extensibility tools. This can support things like custom fields, upsells, delivery messages and B2B payment terms. Changes should be tested carefully, because the checkout is where small mistakes cost the most. Our article on Shopify checkout settings covers the basics that apply to every plan.

Speed is the other big factor. A heavy theme or too many apps can slow a Plus store as easily as any other. We keep themes lean, audit apps and test on mobile connections. Our Shopify speed guide explains the main fixes.

Marketing a Shopify Plus store after launch

A new store is only the start. Once the Plus build is live, traffic and sales depend on how well the store is found and how well it converts. Many US brands pair a migration with ongoing work in three areas.

  • SEO: improving collection and product pages, adding helpful buying guides and building links so the store ranks for the searches that bring buyers.
  • Paid media: Google Ads, including Shopping campaigns, and Meta, TikTok and YouTube Ads to reach new customers while organic search grows. Ad spend is paid separately.
  • Marketplace SEO: many Plus brands also sell on Amazon or Walmart, and listing optimisation there supports the main store rather than competing with it.

We also use AI-powered tools to speed up tasks such as product description drafts, keyword grouping and reporting, with every output checked by a person before it goes live. Brand tone, product claims and any regulated wording are always approved by your own team.

Planning this work before launch helps. Tracking, conversion events and product feeds should be set up as part of the build, so that marketing can start the day the new store goes live rather than weeks later.

How to choose a Shopify Plus agency

Whichever agency you choose, ask these questions before you sign:

  1. How will you handle redirects and SEO during the migration?
  2. Which data will move, and how will customer logins work after launch?
  3. Which apps will you replace with native Shopify Plus features?
  4. How will you test the checkout before go-live?
  5. Who owns the theme code, store and accounts when the project ends?
  6. What support is available in the weeks after launch?

The answers should be specific. Vague promises about "seamless migrations" are not a plan.
