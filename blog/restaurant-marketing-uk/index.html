Restaurant Marketing in the UK: Google, Bookings, Menus and Reviews

Restaurant marketing in the UK starts with Google Business Profile, easy booking and a readable menu, then reviews, local SEO and social to fill tables.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Google Business Profile is often the first thing diners see, so keep hours, menu, photos and booking link accurate.
  • Make booking direct and easy, and publish the menu as readable text, not just a PDF or photo.
  • Reviews, local SEO and a steady social presence keep the restaurant visible between busy periods.

Restaurant marketing in the UK starts with being easy to find and easy to book: an accurate Google Business Profile, a fast mobile website with a readable menu, and a direct booking button. Once that is working, reviews, local SEO, email and social media keep tables full through quieter weeks.

Diners decide quickly, often on a phone, sometimes from the pavement. This guide covers the channels that matter for independent restaurants, cafés and small groups, with a checklist at the end. Google Business Profile for restaurants

For many diners, your Google Business Profile is your shop window. It appears in map results, on searches for your name and in "near me" searches. Keep it accurate and active:

  • Choose the most specific primary category, such as "Italian restaurant" rather than just "restaurant".
  • Keep opening hours current, including bank holidays and seasonal changes.
  • Add a booking link that goes to your own booking page or system.
  • Link to your menu and keep it up to date.
  • Upload fresh photos of dishes, the dining room and the exterior so people recognise you.
  • Fill in attributes such as outdoor seating, vegan options, accessibility and dog-friendly.
  • Use posts to promote events, new menus and offers.

Making bookings easy

Every extra step loses diners. Put a clear "Book a table" button at the top of every page on mobile, and make sure it works in one or two taps. Whether you use a booking platform or take reservations by phone, the route should be obvious.

Third-party platforms can bring discovery, but direct bookings give you the customer relationship and, with consent, the chance to market to them again. Where possible, link Google and social profiles to your own booking page. Track bookings by source so you know which channels are actually filling tables.

Private dining, group bookings, events and gift vouchers deserve their own pages. These are often higher-value enquiries and people search for them specifically, for example "private dining room" plus your area.

Menus that help, not hinder

A menu uploaded only as a PDF or a photo is hard to read on a phone, cannot be read well by search engines and is often out of date. Publish the menu as text on a web page, with prices, and keep a downloadable version if you like. Benefits include:

  • Search visibility for dishes and cuisines people actually search for.
  • Accessibility for diners using screen readers or zooming in.
  • Clear allergen and dietary information, signposting diners to speak to staff about allergies, in line with UK food information rules.
  • Easier updates when dishes or prices change.

Reviews and reputation

Reviews influence where people choose to eat and help your local ranking. Ask for reviews in a natural way, for example on the bill, a table card or a follow-up email for booked diners, and never offer incentives in exchange for positive reviews. UK consumer law and platform policies take fake or incentivised reviews seriously.

Reply to reviews, good and bad. A short, specific thank you to positive reviews and a calm, helpful response to criticism shows future diners that you care. Take detailed complaints offline rather than debating them in public.

Local SEO and content for restaurants

Beyond your Google profile, your website can rank for searches like "best Sunday roast in" your town, "vegan restaurant near" a landmark, or "private dining" in your area. Useful pages include:

  • A page for each location if you have more than one.
  • Pages for private dining, events and set menus.
  • Seasonal pages, such as Christmas parties, Mother's Day or summer terrace menus, published well in advance.
  • An about page that tells the story of the food, the suppliers and the team's approach, without exaggerated claims.

Mentions in local press and food guides also help. Our guide to digital PR and link building explains how that works. Our SEO and GEO service covers both search engines and AI search answers.

Social media, email and paid ads

Social media works best for restaurants when it shows real food and real atmosphere: new dishes, behind-the-scenes prep, events and seasonal changes. Consistency matters more than polish.

Email is underused by many restaurants. With proper consent under UK GDPR and PECR, a short monthly email about new menus, events and bookable dates can bring regulars back reliably.

Paid social is useful for launches, events and filling quieter nights in a defined radius. Keep targeting local and the offer simple. Our guide to Meta ads for UK businesses explains the basics. Ad spend is always paid separately, directly to the platform.

Planning around the restaurant calendar

Restaurant demand follows a predictable rhythm, and marketing works best when it is planned around it. Christmas party bookings often start in late summer, Mother's Day and Valentine's Day fill weeks ahead, and summer terrace trade depends on the weather and the school holidays. Publish seasonal pages and Google posts early enough for people to find them while they are still deciding.

Quiet periods are just as important. January, early-week evenings and the weeks after big holidays are when a targeted email, a set menu or a local paid social campaign can make the most difference. Keep a simple calendar of key dates, what you will promote and when it needs to go live, so marketing is not left until the week before.

Measuring restaurant marketing

Track the numbers that tie to covers: bookings by source, calls and direction requests from Google, email sign-ups and redemptions of any offer codes. Even a basic weekly note of bookings against what was promoted that week shows patterns over a few months, and helps you decide where to spend time and money next.

Restaurant marketing checklist

  • Google Business Profile complete, with correct category, hours, menu and booking link.
  • Bank holiday and seasonal hours updated in advance.
  • "Book a table" button visible on every mobile page.
  • Menu published as text with prices and allergen signposting.
  • Pages for private dining, events, gift vouchers and seasonal menus.
  • Reviews requested consistently, without incentives, and answered.
  • Bookings tracked by source.
  • Email sign-up with recorded consent.
  • Fresh photos added regularly to Google and social.

FAQs

What is the most effective restaurant marketing in the UK?

For most independents, an accurate Google Business Profile, easy direct booking, a readable online menu and steady reviews do the most work. Social media and email then keep regulars coming back.

Should my restaurant menu be a PDF?

Ideally publish it as text on a web page, with a PDF as an optional extra. Text menus are easier to read on phones, more accessible and visible to search engines.

How do I get more Google reviews for my restaurant?

Ask consistently, for example on the bill, a table card or a follow-up email. Never offer incentives for positive reviews, and reply to the reviews you receive.

Are booking platforms or direct bookings better?

Platforms can help with discovery, while direct bookings give you the customer relationship. Many restaurants use both but link Google and social profiles to their own booking page.

Is paid social worth it for a restaurant?

It can be for launches, events and quieter nights, targeted to a local radius. Keep the offer simple and track bookings. Ad spend is paid separately to the platform.

How long does restaurant SEO take?

Google Business Profile changes can help within weeks, while wider SEO typically takes 3 to 6 months to show meaningful results.
