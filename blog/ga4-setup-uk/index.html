GA4 Setup for UK Businesses: Key Events, Consent Mode and Privacy

A sound GA4 setup in the UK tracks the key events that matter, respects consent under UK GDPR and PECR, and links to Ads and Search Console.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A useful GA4 setup tracks a short list of key events that reflect real business outcomes, such as enquiries, calls and purchases.
  • UK sites generally need a consent banner for analytics and advertising cookies under PECR, and Google consent mode helps tags respect those choices.
  • Link GA4 to Google Ads and Search Console, test every event, and review data monthly.

A good GA4 setup for a UK business tracks a short list of key events that reflect real outcomes, such as form submissions, calls and purchases, and respects visitor consent under UK GDPR and PECR. Getting the basics right at the start saves months of unreliable data and wasted ad spend.

Google Analytics 4 is free and powerful, but its default setup does not know what matters to your business. This guide covers account structure, key events, consent mode, the UK privacy context, linking to other Google tools and a checklist you can work through. It is practical guidance rather than legal advice, so check privacy decisions with your own adviser.

GA4 setup in the UK: the basics

A GA4 property collects data through a data stream, usually one web stream for your site. You add the Google tag directly or through Google Tag Manager. Tag Manager is more flexible if you plan to track several events or run ads on multiple platforms.

  1. Create the account and property: use a business-owned Google account, not one belonging to a freelancer or former employee.
  2. Set the time zone and currency: United Kingdom time and pounds sterling, so reports match your books.
  3. Add a web data stream: keep enhanced measurement on for scrolls, outbound clicks and file downloads, then review what you need.
  4. Install the tag: via Tag Manager or your platform's built-in integration, such as Shopify or a WordPress plugin.
  5. Set data retention: the default is short, so extend it to the longest standard option if it suits your privacy policy.
  6. Filter internal traffic: define your office IP addresses so staff visits do not distort data.
  7. Grant access carefully: give agencies their own user access rather than sharing logins.

Key events: track what matters

GA4 calls the actions you care most about key events. Keep the list short and tied to business value. Too many key events make reports noisy and confuse ad bidding.

Track form submissions on a genuine success signal, such as a thank-you page or a confirmed submission event, rather than a button click that fires even when validation fails. Consent, UK GDPR and PECR

In the UK, the use of cookies and similar technologies is governed by the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR), alongside UK GDPR for personal data. The ICO's guidance generally treats analytics and advertising cookies as non-essential, which means you usually need consent before setting them. Rules and guidance do change, so check the latest ICO position and take advice for your own circumstances.

In practice that typically means:

  • A consent banner that lets visitors accept or reject non-essential cookies as easily as each other.
  • No analytics or advertising cookies set before consent is given.
  • A clear cookie policy and privacy policy explaining what you use and why.
  • A way for visitors to change their mind later.

Google consent mode

Consent mode lets Google tags adjust their behaviour based on the visitor's choice. In basic mode, tags do not load until consent is given. In advanced mode, tags load but send limited signals without cookies when consent is refused, which Google can use for modelling. Which mode is appropriate depends on your legal position and risk appetite, so treat it as a decision for you and your adviser. Many consent management platforms support consent mode, so check your banner tool integrates properly and test that tags respect each choice.

Linking GA4 to Google Ads and Search Console

GA4 is most useful when connected to the tools around it.

  • Google Ads: link the accounts so you can import key events and audiences. Make sure you are not counting the same conversion twice from both a GA4 import and a separate Ads tag.
  • Search Console: link it to see organic queries and landing pages alongside behaviour data.
  • Merchant Center: for ecommerce, connected data helps when running Shopping and Performance Max campaigns.

If you run paid campaigns, accurate key events are what the bidding algorithms optimise towards. Our PPC audit checklist includes a tracking review, and our guides to Google Shopping ads and Performance Max explain why clean data matters for automated campaigns.

Tracking calls and offline leads

Many UK service businesses win most of their work by phone or face to face, which GA4 cannot see on its own. Track taps on click-to-call links as an event, so you at least know which pages and channels prompt calls. If calls matter a lot, call tracking with dynamic numbers can attribute calls to channels, though it adds cost and needs to be set up with privacy in mind.

For leads that turn into sales offline, such as quotes accepted weeks later, consider recording the lead source in your CRM and comparing it with GA4 data. Larger advertisers can import offline conversions into Google Ads so bidding learns which leads actually become customers. Even a simple spreadsheet that records where each sale came from helps you judge whether a channel is worth the spend.

UTM parameters for campaigns

Tag links in emails, social posts and non-Google ads with UTM parameters so GA4 can attribute visits correctly. Agree a simple naming convention, for example lowercase source, medium and campaign names, and stick to it. Inconsistent tagging splits the same campaign across several rows and makes reports much harder to read and compare over time.

Reporting that helps decisions

Default GA4 reports can feel overwhelming. Focus on a handful of questions: which channels bring key events, which landing pages convert, how mobile compares with desktop, and how organic search is trending. A simple monthly review, with notes on what changed and why, is more useful than a complex dashboard nobody opens. GA4 setup checklist

  • Business-owned Google account and GA4 property.
  • UK time zone and GBP currency set.
  • Single tag installation, via Tag Manager or platform integration.
  • Internal traffic filtered.
  • Data retention extended.
  • Key events defined for real outcomes only.
  • Ecommerce events with values, if you sell online.
  • Consent banner live and consent mode configured and tested.
  • Cookie and privacy policies up to date.
  • Google Ads and Search Console linked, with no duplicate conversions.
  • Test every event in DebugView before relying on reports.

FAQs

What does a GA4 setup in the UK involve?

It usually covers creating a business-owned property, installing the tag once, defining key events, configuring consent with a banner and consent mode, filtering internal traffic and linking Google Ads and Search Console.

Do I need a cookie banner for Google Analytics in the UK?

Under PECR and ICO guidance, analytics cookies are generally treated as non-essential, so consent is usually needed before they are set. Check current ICO guidance and take advice for your situation.

What is the difference between basic and advanced consent mode?

Basic mode blocks Google tags until consent is given. Advanced mode loads tags but sends limited cookieless signals when consent is refused. Which suits you is a decision for you and your adviser.

What are key events in GA4?

Key events are the actions you mark as most important, such as purchases, enquiries or bookings. They replaced the older conversions label in GA4 reporting.

Should I use Google Tag Manager with GA4?

Tag Manager is helpful if you track several events or run ads on multiple platforms, because changes can be made without editing site code. Simple sites can use a platform integration instead.

Why does GA4 show different numbers from Google Ads?

The tools use different attribution, timing and consent handling, so figures rarely match exactly. Large gaps usually point to duplicate tags, missing consent configuration or different conversion definitions.
