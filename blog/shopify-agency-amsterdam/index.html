Why Amsterdam brands choose Shopify

Amsterdam has a strong ecommerce culture, with fashion labels, cycling and outdoor brands, design and homeware companies, coffee and food brands and many international businesses using the city as a base for European sales. These brands tend to want the same things from a platform: speed to launch, low maintenance and room to expand.

  • Quick to launch: a focused build can go live in weeks.
  • Easy to run: products, orders, stock and discounts are managed in one admin.
  • Local payment methods: Dutch shoppers commonly expect iDEAL, which Shopify supports, alongside cards and other options.
  • Selling across Europe: Shopify Markets handles multiple currencies and languages from one store.
  • Apps for growth: reviews, subscriptions, loyalty and shipping tools without custom code.

Selling to Dutch shoppers: what matters

Dutch shoppers are experienced online buyers. They tend to expect fast delivery, clear returns and familiar payment methods, and they notice when a store feels unfinished. These are the areas we focus on during an Amsterdam build.

Payments: make sure iDEAL and other trusted options are available and tested at checkout.

Delivery and returns: show delivery costs, timescales and the returns process clearly before checkout. Our guide to ecommerce delivery and returns pages covers what to include.

Language: many Dutch shoppers read English, but a Dutch-language store usually feels more trustworthy for a local audience. We build the language structure, and the Dutch copy comes from you or a translator.

Privacy and accessibility: GDPR applies, so tracking should run through a consent tool. The European Accessibility Act also affects many online shops selling to EU consumers. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act for websites covers the basics. Keep your planning general and check official guidance or your own adviser for specifics.

What to prepare before your Shopify project

Shopify projects move fastest when the core materials are ready early. You do not need everything on day one, but anything that arrives late can push back your launch.

  • Product data: names, prices, variants, weights, barcodes and stock levels, ideally in a spreadsheet.
  • Images: consistent product photos plus a few lifestyle images for banners and collections.
  • Brand assets: logo files, colours, fonts and any brand guidelines.
  • Dutch and English copy: Dutch text from your team or translator; we can write the English copy from a brief.
  • Policies: privacy, terms, delivery and returns wording, reviewed by your own adviser.
  • Market and shipping plan: which countries you will sell to, carriers, rates and the tax approach agreed with your accountant.

We share a checklist at the start of every project and track each item in the shared timeline, so you always know what is outstanding and what comes next. If some items are not ready, we can usually build around placeholders and swap in final content before launch.

Common Shopify mistakes to avoid

Many stores we review share the same avoidable issues. Catching them early protects both sales and search rankings.

  • Too many apps: each one can add scripts that slow the store and monthly fees that add up.
  • Machine-translated copy: Dutch shoppers notice awkward wording quickly, so use a proper translator.
  • Missing hreflang or language setup: without it, search engines can show the wrong language version.
  • Hidden delivery costs: surprise charges at checkout are a common reason for abandoned carts.
  • Broken URLs after migration: without redirects, rankings built over years can disappear.
  • Tracking without consent: analytics set up outside a consent tool can cause GDPR problems and patchy data.

If your store is already live, we can start by reviewing these areas before recommending a rebuild, which is not always needed. Our guide to Shopify speed optimisation covers one of the most common fixes.
