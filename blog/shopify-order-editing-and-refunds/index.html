Shopify Order Editing and Refunds: How to Change Orders Cleanly

How to edit Shopify orders after checkout, issue full and partial refunds, handle restocking and keep customers informed without creating messy records.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Order editing lets you change items and quantities after checkout while keeping one clean order record.
  • Refunds should be issued against the original order so stock, reporting and customer emails stay accurate.
  • A short written process for edits, refunds and restocking prevents staff from handling the same situation in different ways.

Customers change their minds. They pick the wrong size, forget an item, spot a typo in their address or decide they no longer want something that has not shipped yet. Sometimes the mistake is on the shop side: an item turns out to be out of stock, or arrives damaged. Every one of these situations ends with someone in your team opening an order in Shopify admin and deciding what to do.

Handled well, these changes are quick and invisible to the customer. Handled badly, they create duplicate orders, stock counts that do not match the shelves, confusing emails and reports that nobody trusts. This guide explains how order editing and refunds work in Shopify, when to use each and how to build a simple process around them.

What order editing means in Shopify

Order editing lets you change an order after the customer has checked out. Depending on the state of the order, you can typically add products, remove items, adjust quantities and apply discounts to individual lines. Shopify then works out whether the customer owes more money or is due a refund and records the change on the same order.

The key benefit is that the order history stays in one place. Instead of cancelling an order and creating a new one, the original order number remains, the timeline shows what changed and when, and your reports reflect what was actually sold.

There are limits. Edits generally apply to items that have not yet been fulfilled, and some order types or payment methods may restrict what can be changed. Shopify updates its features regularly, so check the current help documentation for the precise rules on your plan before relying on a particular behaviour.

Common situations that call for an edit

The customer wants a different variant

A customer emails to say they ordered the wrong size or colour. If the order has not been fulfilled, you can remove the original line and add the correct variant. If the prices match, no money changes hands. If they differ, Shopify shows the balance owed or the amount to refund.

The customer wants to add something

Adding an item to an existing order can save on separate shipping and keep everything in one parcel. When the edit creates an outstanding balance, you can send the customer an invoice to pay the difference rather than taking card details yourself.

An item is unavailable

If you cannot supply part of an order, remove that line and refund it, then let the customer know what happened. It is usually better to contact the customer first and offer an alternative, as some will prefer a substitute to a refund.

A goodwill discount

Where an item is slightly delayed or you want to apologise for a problem, a line item discount applied through an edit keeps the gesture tied to the order rather than handled as a separate, untracked payment.

How to edit an order step by step

The exact screens may change over time, but the flow is broadly the same:

  1. Open the order in Shopify admin and choose the option to edit it.
  2. Add, remove or change the quantity of unfulfilled items, and apply any discounts.
  3. Decide whether removed items should be restocked to your inventory.
  4. Review the summary showing the new total and any balance owed or refundable.
  5. Add a reason or note so colleagues can see why the change was made.
  6. Choose whether to notify the customer, then save the changes.

The note in step five is easy to skip and valuable later. When a customer contacts you weeks afterwards, a single line explaining the edit saves a lot of detective work.

How refunds work

A refund returns money to the customer against an existing order. In Shopify you can refund an entire order or individual items, refund shipping separately, and in some cases refund a custom amount. Refunds are normally returned to the original payment method.

Full refunds

A full refund is appropriate when an order is cancelled before dispatch or when everything comes back. If the order has not been fulfilled, cancelling it with a refund usually makes the most sense, as it marks the order clearly and can restock the items in one action.

Partial refunds

Partial refunds cover situations such as one item from a larger order being returned, a damaged product the customer keeps at a reduced price, or a delivery charge being waived after a late parcel. Select the specific items or enter the amount, and add a reason.

Shipping refunds

Decide in advance how you treat delivery charges on returns, and state it on your returns page. Applying the same rule every time avoids staff guessing and keeps customers from receiving different answers to the same question.

Restocking: getting inventory right

When you remove or refund an item, Shopify asks whether to restock it. This choice matters more than it looks. If you restock an item that has actually been thrown away or is still in transit back to you, your online stock will show units that do not exist, and you may sell something you cannot send.

A sensible rule is to restock only when the item is physically back and in a sellable condition. For returns, that may mean refunding first and adjusting stock later once the parcel has been checked. If you hold stock at more than one location, make sure the item is restocked to the right one.

Edit, refund or cancel: choosing the right action

  • Edit when the order is still going ahead but its contents or price need to change.
  • Refund when money needs to go back for items that were paid for, whether or not they have shipped.
  • Cancel when the whole order will not go ahead, typically before anything has been dispatched.

Avoid the temptation to cancel an order and create a fresh one for small changes. It doubles the number of records, can trigger extra emails and makes sales reports harder to read. Draft orders have their place for creating orders on a customer's behalf, but they are not a substitute for editing an existing one.

Keeping customers informed

Shopify can send notification emails when an order is edited or refunded. Review these templates in your notification settings so they match your tone of voice and explain clearly what has changed. A refund email should say how much is being returned and that it may take some time to appear, as processing times depend on the customer's bank or card provider.

For anything unusual, a short personal message alongside the automated email goes a long way. Customers are generally more understanding when they know what is happening and why.

Consumer rights and your policies

In the UK, consumers buying online generally have rights to cancel and to return faulty goods, and the rules differ depending on the type of product and circumstances. Your refund and returns policy needs to sit alongside those rights, not contradict them. This is an area where the detail matters, so take appropriate advice if you are unsure how the rules apply to your products, and keep your policy pages in line with the way you actually process refunds.

Building a simple internal process

Even a small team benefits from writing down how order changes are handled. A one-page process might cover:

  • Who is allowed to edit orders and issue refunds, using staff permissions to limit access where needed.
  • When to contact the customer before making a change.
  • Your rules on shipping refunds, restocking and goodwill discounts.
  • What to write in order notes and how to tag orders that needed attention.
  • How to handle edits to orders that are already being packed.

Linking this process to your fulfilment workflow is important. If an order is edited after a picking list has been printed, the warehouse or packing bench needs to know. Some shops use an order tag or a simple check before dispatch to catch late changes.

Reporting and reconciliation

Because edits and refunds are recorded on the original order, Shopify's sales reports can show gross sales, discounts, returns and net sales separately. Review these regularly to spot patterns: a product with an unusual number of returns may have a sizing issue or a misleading description. Your accountant or bookkeeper will also want refunds recorded consistently, so agree with them how refunds appear in your accounts and how payment provider fees on refunded orders are handled.

FAQs

Can I edit an order that has already shipped?

Generally, editing applies to unfulfilled items. Once an item has shipped, you would usually handle changes through a return and refund rather than an edit. Check Shopify's current documentation for the exact rules.

Does editing an order charge the customer automatically?

If an edit increases the total, the customer typically needs to pay the balance, for example through an invoice sent from the order. If it reduces the total, you can refund the difference.

Should I restock refunded items straight away?

Only restock items that are physically back and sellable. Restocking too early can lead to overselling stock you do not have.

Can I refund to a different payment method?

Refunds normally go back to the original payment method. If you need to compensate a customer another way, such as with a gift card or store credit, make sure this is consistent with your policy and the customer's rights.

Will the customer be notified of a refund?

Shopify can send a refund notification, and you can choose whether to send it. It is good practice to notify the customer so they know to expect the money.

How do I stop staff issuing refunds without approval?

Use staff account permissions to control who can issue refunds and edit orders, and agree a written process for when approval is needed. Our guide to Shopify staff accounts and permissions covers this in more detail.
