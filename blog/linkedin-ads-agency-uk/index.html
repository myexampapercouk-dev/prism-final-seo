LinkedIn Ads Agency UK: What They Do, When It Makes Sense and How to Choose

What a LinkedIn Ads agency does for UK B2B businesses, when the channel is worth the cost, how to compare providers and the questions to ask before you sign.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A LinkedIn Ads agency plans targeting, creative, lead capture and measurement so your campaigns reach the right decision-makers and produce useful sales conversations.
  • The channel tends to suit B2B businesses with a clearly defined audience and customers valuable enough to justify the cost of reaching them.
  • Choose a provider that reports on qualified leads and pipeline, and that ties campaign work to your sales process rather than clicks and impressions.

A LinkedIn Ads agency plans and manages paid campaigns aimed at professional audiences, using details such as job function, seniority, industry and company. For UK businesses it tends to suit B2B companies with a well-defined buyer and a customer value high enough to justify the cost of reaching that person. The value of an agency lies in joining up targeting, creative, lead capture and sales follow-up, because good targeting alone does not create a qualified lead.

This guide focuses on deciding whether to hire help and how to compare providers. For more on campaign planning, see our guide to LinkedIn Ads for B2B lead generation.

What a LinkedIn Ads agency does

  • Audience planning: turning your ideal customer into targeting that is specific without being too narrow.
  • Offer development: working out what is worth a busy buyer's attention, whether that is a guide, a demo or a useful insight.
  • Creative and copy: writing and briefing ads for people who were not looking for you at that moment.
  • Lead capture: deciding between LinkedIn's lead forms and your own landing pages, then connecting them to your systems.
  • Tracking: setting up conversion measurement and links to your sales data.
  • Optimisation: testing audiences and messages, reviewing lead quality and adjusting budgets.
  • Reporting: showing what happened to leads after the form was submitted.

When LinkedIn Ads makes sense

The platform is often more expensive per click than many other channels, so the commercial case matters. It tends to be a stronger fit when:

  • You can describe your buyer by role, seniority, sector or company type.
  • One new customer is valuable enough to absorb the cost of reaching and nurturing several prospects.
  • Your buyers are hard to reach through search, because few of them are actively looking for your category yet.
  • You have a sales process that can follow up quickly and properly.
  • You want to reach a defined list of target accounts.

When another channel may come first

If you sell low-cost products, serve mainly consumers or have only a small budget, LinkedIn may be difficult to justify. Where people already search for what you offer, search ads may capture that demand more directly. Your budget also needs to be able to produce enough data, as a test with very few clicks tells you little.

What good targeting looks like

New advertisers often make audiences either too broad or too narrow. Ticking every possible job title and sector can pull in the wrong people, while layering filters until almost nobody is left makes results expensive and hard to read.

A strong agency will usually ask:

  • Who signs off the purchase, who influences it and who feels the problem day to day?
  • Which sectors and company sizes have been your best customers?
  • Do you have a target account list that can be uploaded lawfully?
  • Who should be excluded, such as competitors, existing customers or job seekers?

Job titles vary, so a mix of function and seniority may reach people that a short list of titles would miss. Check the audience settings regularly, as the platform's expansion options can widen who sees your ads.

Why the offer matters as much as the audience

LinkedIn can reach the right person when they are not thinking about buying. Asking that person to book a sales call straight away is often a big step. Depending on how well they know you, one of these may work better:

  • A short, useful guide or checklist based on your experience.
  • A clear explanation of a problem and how to think about solving it.
  • An event or webinar on something your audience needs to understand.
  • A demo or consultation, which is usually better for people already familiar with you.

An agency should question an offer that is unlikely to interest your audience. Gated content can also bring in contact details from people who are not ready to buy, so plan what happens next.

Lead forms or landing pages?

LinkedIn lead forms can make it easy to respond, because details may be filled in from the person's profile. That convenience can bring more leads but sometimes weaker intent. Your own landing page asks for more effort and gives you more room to explain the offer and qualify people. Testing both is reasonable where volume allows. Either way, leads should reach the right person quickly, so check the connection to your CRM or inbox before launch. Make sure your privacy information and consent wording are suitable, and take advice if you are unsure.

Measuring what matters

Clicks and engagement show that an ad got attention. They do not show that the campaign is helping the business. Ask for reporting that follows leads further:

  • How many leads were contacted and how many were qualified?
  • Which audiences and offers produced sales conversations?
  • What has entered the pipeline, and what has closed?
  • What feedback did the sales team give?

B2B sales cycles can be long, so early reports may rely on lead quality rather than revenue. Agree those early measures in advance, and make sure someone can feed sales outcomes back into the campaign.

Questions to ask a provider

  • Is LinkedIn right for us, and what would you try first if it is not?
  • How would you define our audience, and how will you check its quality?
  • Who writes the ads and produces the creative?
  • Which offer would you test first, and why?
  • How will leads reach our team?
  • What will you report beyond cost per lead?
  • Who owns the account, audiences and creative if we part ways?
  • How is your fee structured, and what is excluded?

Cost per lead on its own can reward weak leads, so treat any provider focused only on that number with care. Compare fees using our guide to paid media management costs, and check that media spend is shown separately from management fees.

What to prepare before you start

A provider can give you better advice if you bring a few facts to the first conversation. Gather what you know about recent customers, including their roles, sectors and company sizes. Note your typical sales cycle, how leads are followed up today and any content you already have that could support an offer. Being clear about these points also makes it easier to compare proposals fairly.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed lead numbers before anyone has seen your market or offer.
  • Control of the ad account being kept by the agency.
  • Reports that stop at impressions, clicks and engagement.
  • No interest in your sales process or lead quality.
  • The same offer and audience approach proposed for every client.

FAQs

Is LinkedIn advertising worth it for small businesses?

It can be when a small business sells high-value services to a clearly defined audience. If customer value is low or budgets are tight, other channels may be a better place to start.

Why does LinkedIn often cost more than other platforms?

You are paying for access to professional targeting, and auction competition can be strong for valuable audiences. Judge the cost against qualified leads and customer value rather than clicks.

Should I use LinkedIn Ads or Google Ads?

They do different jobs. Google Ads reaches people actively searching, while LinkedIn reaches people who match a professional profile. Many B2B businesses begin where there is clearer demand, then add the other.

How long should I test LinkedIn Ads?

Long enough to gather meaningful lead-quality data, which depends on your budget, audience and sales cycle. Agree the test measures upfront so a decision can be made fairly.

Can an agency use my personal LinkedIn profile for ads?

Some formats can involve an individual's profile or content, depending on current platform options and permissions. Agree in advance what may be published in anyone's name.
