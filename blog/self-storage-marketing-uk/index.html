Self Storage Marketing in the UK: How to Fill More Units

Self storage marketing in the UK works best when local search, a clear unit size guide and an easy online quote or booking sit at the centre of everything.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most storage enquiries start with a local search, so Google Business Profile and location pages matter more than anything else.
  • A clear unit size guide and simple online quote or reservation remove the biggest reasons people phone a competitor instead.
  • Paid search works for storage when it is tightly local, tied to life events and measured on reservations rather than clicks.

Self storage marketing in the UK is mostly a local search game: people look for storage near them when something in their life changes, and they choose the site that looks trustworthy, shows clear sizes and lets them reserve quickly. If your website, Google Business Profile and local ads answer those three needs better than the site down the road, you will fill more units.

This guide is for independent and small multi-site self storage operators, container storage yards and business storage providers across the UK. It covers who your customers are, how they search, what your website must do, and where paid media and SEO fit. We are a UK studio that builds websites and runs search and paid campaigns, so we will be honest about what tends to work and what usually wastes budget.

Who actually looks for self storage

Storage demand is driven by moments rather than hobbies. Very few people wake up wanting a storage unit; they need one because something has happened. Understanding those moments shapes every page and every ad you write.

  • Home movers caught between completion dates, or downsizing into a smaller property.
  • Students needing somewhere for belongings over the summer between tenancies.
  • Renovators clearing rooms while a kitchen, loft or extension is built.
  • Families dealing with a bereavement who need time to sort a house before it is sold.
  • Small businesses such as online sellers, tradespeople and events companies storing stock, tools or equipment.
  • People moving abroad or working away for a fixed period.

Each group searches differently and worries about different things. A home mover wants flexibility on dates. A business wants access hours, loading bays and perhaps a delivery acceptance service. A family clearing a relative's home needs patience and a calm, plain tone. Good storage marketing speaks to each of these in turn rather than repeating "secure, clean, affordable" on every page.

What a self storage website must do

A storage website has one main job: turn a worried visitor into a reservation or a quote request. Everything else is support. In our experience, the sites that convert well share a few traits.

Make unit sizes easy to understand

Most people have no idea what 35 square feet looks like. A size guide that translates units into real life helps enormously: "roughly the contents of a one-bedroom flat", "about the size of a large garden shed", "fits a sofa, bed, boxes and white goods". Pair each size with a simple illustration or photo and a short list of what typically fits. This one page often does more for conversion than any amount of advertising.

Show prices or a fast way to get one

Storage buyers compare. If a competitor shows a weekly or monthly starting price and you show "call for a quote", many visitors will leave. You do not have to publish every rate, but a clear starting price per size or an instant quote form that responds quickly will keep people on your site. Our guide to pricing page design explains ways to present prices without boxing yourself in on promotions.

Offer online reservation where you can

Many storage management systems offer an online booking or reservation widget. Where your system supports it, putting that on the website lets people secure a unit in the evening or at weekends, when much of the planning around a move happens. If your system does not support it, a short enquiry form with move-in date, size and contact details is the next best option. Our article on website online booking systems covers the trade-offs.

Answer the access and security questions up front

Visitors want to know opening hours, access hours, whether they need to bring their own padlock, whether there is CCTV, whether there are trolleys and a loading area, and whether vans can park close to the door. Put these near the top of the page and repeat them on every location page. Describe what is genuinely in place at each site rather than generic claims.

Build trust without overpromising

Real photos of the actual site, the entrance, the corridors and the units beat stock imagery every time. Show your address clearly, your company details, and your reviews if customers have left them. Our guide to website trust signals has a fuller checklist.

Location pages for single and multi-site operators

If you run one site, your homepage and a well built location page will carry most of your local search work. Mention the town, the nearby areas you serve, the roads people use to reach you, and practical landmarks. Do not invent coverage you cannot serve conveniently; someone forty minutes away is unlikely to choose you.

If you run several sites, each one needs its own page with unique content: different photos, its own opening and access hours, local directions, parking notes and the sizes available there. A single template with the town name swapped in rarely performs well and can look thin to search engines. Our guide to multi-location SEO in the UK explains how to structure this so each branch can rank on its own.

Content that earns searches between moves

Beyond your core pages, a small amount of useful content can bring in people earlier in their planning. Topics that tend to suit storage operators include:

  • How to pack a storage unit so you can reach what you need.
  • What you cannot keep in self storage, explained in plain English.
  • Storage options for students over the summer.
  • Business storage for online sellers and tradespeople.
  • Decluttering before a house sale or a move.

Keep these practical and specific to your sites. The point is to answer real questions that customers already ask on the phone, not to publish for the sake of it. Good SEO planning covers this, so content supports enquiries rather than just traffic.

Paid media for storage operators

Paid search can work very well for storage because intent is strong: someone searching "storage units in [town]" usually needs one soon. The risk is waste. Storage keywords overlap with removals, skip hire, shipping containers for sale and national brand names, so careful keyword selection and negative keywords matter.

Practical points we would usually recommend:

  1. Target a tight radius around each site rather than a whole county.
  2. Write ads that mention the town, sizes available and a clear next step such as reserving online.
  3. Send traffic to the relevant location page or a focused landing page, not the homepage.
  4. Track reservations, quote requests and calls as conversions, not just clicks.
  5. Review search terms weekly at first to block irrelevant queries.

Social ads on Meta platforms can help with awareness among local movers, students and renovators, and with reminding people who visited your site but did not book. Our Google Ads for small business guide explains campaign basics, and our paid media service page shows how we manage campaigns. Ad spend is always paid separately from any management fee.

Reviews, partnerships and repeat business

Storage customers often stay for months, which gives you time to earn a review. Ask at a natural moment, such as when someone extends their stay or moves out happily, and make it easy with a direct link. Reply to every review, including critical ones, calmly and factually.

Local partnerships can bring a steady flow of enquiries. Estate agents, letting agents, removals firms, builders and universities all meet people who need storage. A simple partner page, a referral code or a dedicated landing page lets you see which partners actually send customers, so you can focus on the relationships that work.

Measuring what matters

Storage marketing should be judged on occupancy and enquiries, not vanity metrics. At minimum, track online reservations, quote form submissions, phone calls from the website and calls from your Google Business Profile. Group them by site if you have more than one. Over time this tells you which locations need more marketing support and which channels genuinely bring tenants.

SEO improvements typically take three to six months to show clearly in rankings and enquiries. Paid search can bring enquiries much faster but stops as soon as the budget stops, so most operators benefit from both working together.

FAQs

What is the best way to approach self storage marketing in the UK?

Start with local search. Make sure your Google Business Profile is complete and active, build a strong page for each site, and make sizes, prices or quotes and reservations easy to find. Then add carefully targeted paid search to capture urgent demand.

Should a storage website show prices?

Usually yes, at least as a starting price per size or a fast instant quote. Storage buyers compare options quickly, and a site that hides all pricing often loses visitors to one that is more open.

Do storage operators need a separate page for each site?

Yes, if you have more than one location. Each page should have its own photos, hours, directions, parking notes and sizes, so it can rank for its own area and answer local questions properly.

Is Google Ads worth it for a self storage business?

It can be, because people searching for storage in a town often need it soon. It works best with a tight radius, strong negative keywords to filter out removals and container sales, and tracking that counts reservations and calls.

How long does SEO take for a storage business?

Typically three to six months before clear improvements in rankings and enquiries, depending on competition in your area and the state of your website and profile when you start.

Can you connect our storage software's booking widget to a new website?

Where your storage management system provides an embeddable widget or booking link, we can usually build it into the site. We will check what your system supports during the discovery stage before confirming the approach.
