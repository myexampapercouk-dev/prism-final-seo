Squarespace vs WordPress for UK Businesses: Which Should You Build On?

Squarespace vs WordPress for UK businesses: ease of use, design, SEO, flexibility, ownership, maintenance and running costs compared, with guidance on choosing.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Squarespace is an all-in-one hosted builder that trades flexibility for simplicity, while WordPress gives far more control in exchange for more responsibility.
  • Both can rank well in Google; content, structure and speed matter more than the platform name.
  • Choose based on how your site needs to grow, who will maintain it and how much control over code, data and hosting you want.

Squarespace and WordPress are two of the most common ways UK businesses build a website, but they work in very different ways. Squarespace is a hosted website builder: you pay a subscription, and the hosting, security, templates and editor all come in one package. WordPress, in the self-hosted form most businesses use, is open-source software you install on hosting of your choice, then extend with themes and plugins. Neither is better in every situation. The right choice depends on what your website needs to do now, how it needs to grow and who will look after it.

This guide is for UK owners and marketing leads weighing up the two for a new site or a rebuild. It compares them on the points that tend to decide the matter in practice, and finishes with some simple scenarios to help you choose.

How the two platforms differ

Squarespace: the all-in-one builder

With Squarespace, you choose a template, edit pages visually and publish. Hosting, SSL, software updates and much of the security work are handled by the platform. Extra features such as commerce, scheduling and email campaigns are available within the same ecosystem, depending on your plan. The upside is consistency: everything is designed to work together. The trade-off is that you are working inside the limits Squarespace sets. If a feature does not exist, your options for adding it are narrower.

WordPress: the flexible framework

WordPress powers a very large share of the web, from small brochure sites to big publishers. Because it is open source, you can host it anywhere, change any part of the code and add functionality through a huge range of plugins or custom development. That flexibility is its greatest strength and its main cost. Someone has to choose good hosting, keep WordPress, themes and plugins updated, manage backups and keep an eye on security.

Note that this guide is about self-hosted WordPress (often called WordPress.org). The hosted WordPress.com service works differently, and some of the points below depend on the plan you choose there.

Ease of use

Squarespace is generally quicker to learn. The editor is visual, the number of settings is manageable and templates look tidy with little effort. For a business owner who wants to update opening times, add a blog post or swap a photo, it is usually straightforward.

WordPress varies a lot depending on how the site is built. A well-built WordPress site with a sensible editing setup can be just as easy to update day to day. A site assembled from a heavy theme and dozens of plugins can be confusing and fragile. The editing experience on WordPress is largely a result of the decisions made during the build, which is worth bearing in mind if you are comparing quotes. Our guide to WordPress page builders explains some of those choices.

Design and branding

Squarespace templates are well designed, and many businesses can get an attractive site live quickly. The catch is that sites built on the same template can look similar, and deeper layout changes may require custom code, which the platform supports only to a point.

WordPress has no practical limit on design. A theme can be customised, or a site can be designed and built from scratch around your brand. That opens the door to distinctive layouts and bespoke components, but it also means design quality depends entirely on whoever builds it. If brand presentation is central to how you win work, our brand strategy service page explains how brand and website work fit together.

SEO and content

Both platforms can rank well in Google. Search engines care about useful content, clear structure, sensible internal links, page speed and the signals that show a business is trustworthy. The platform matters less than what you put on it.

That said, there are differences in control:

  • Squarespace covers the essentials: editable page titles and meta descriptions, clean URLs, automatic sitemaps, redirects and basic structured data. For a modest site this is often enough.
  • WordPress gives finer control over almost everything: URL structures, schema markup, category and tag archives, canonical tags, internal linking at scale and site architecture. SEO plugins add structured controls, and developers can go further.

If content is going to be a major part of how you grow, for example a large library of guides or many location and service pages, WordPress usually gives more room. Our SEO and GEO service page explains how we approach search for both traditional results and AI answers.

Speed and performance

Squarespace manages hosting and delivery for you, so performance is broadly consistent but largely outside your control. Heavy images, video backgrounds and many third-party scripts can still slow a page down.

WordPress performance depends on hosting, theme, plugins and how carefully the site is built. A lean WordPress site on good hosting can be very fast; a bloated one on cheap hosting can be very slow. Our website speed optimisation guide covers what makes the difference.

Flexibility and integrations

This is often the deciding factor. Squarespace offers a curated set of features and a smaller range of extensions. If your needs fit inside that set, the simplicity is a benefit. If you need something specific, such as a complex quote form, membership area, custom calculator, integration with internal systems or a directory, you may hit a ceiling.

WordPress can be extended in almost any direction, through plugins or custom code. That is why it is used for everything from simple brochure sites to complex membership platforms. The responsibility is to choose extensions carefully, because every plugin adds code that must be maintained.

Ecommerce

Both can sell online. Squarespace Commerce suits smaller catalogues, simple products, services and digital downloads. WordPress sells through WooCommerce, which is more flexible but needs more setup and care. If selling is the main purpose of your site, it is worth comparing dedicated ecommerce options too. See our guides on Shopify vs Squarespace and Shopify vs WooCommerce.

Ownership, portability and lock-in

With Squarespace, your content lives on Squarespace's platform. You can export some content, but the design and many features do not transfer to another platform, so moving later usually means a rebuild. That is not necessarily a problem, but it is worth knowing at the start.

With self-hosted WordPress, you own the files and database. You can move hosts, change developers or redesign while keeping your content. Whichever platform you choose, make sure the account, domain and any hosting are registered to your business, with logins you control.

Maintenance and security

Squarespace handles platform updates and server security, which removes a whole category of work. You still need strong passwords, two-factor authentication and sensible user permissions.

WordPress needs regular attention: core, theme and plugin updates, backups stored away from the server, security monitoring and occasional fixes when an update conflicts with something. This work is not difficult when done routinely, but it does need an owner. Our WordPress security guide sets out the basics.

Running costs

Squarespace costs a predictable monthly or annual subscription, with transaction fees on some commerce plans and payment processing fees on sales. Prices change over time, so check Squarespace's own pricing page for current figures.

WordPress software is free, but a business site still costs money to run: hosting, a domain, any premium themes or plugins, and the time or support needed for maintenance. The initial build is often a bigger investment too, especially for a custom design. Over several years, the comparison depends heavily on how complex the site is and who maintains it. Our WordPress website cost guide explains the main cost drivers, and our pricing page shows how we structure our own work.

Some simple scenarios

A freelance photographer or designer whose site is mainly a portfolio, an about page and a contact form will often find Squarespace a natural fit. The templates suit visual work and upkeep is light.

A professional services firm planning dozens of service pages, sector pages, a regular blog and integration with a CRM is likely to be better served by WordPress, where structure and integrations can be built around how the firm wins work.

A small local business needing five or six pages might go either way. If nobody will maintain WordPress, Squarespace removes a risk. If the business expects to add booking tools, area pages or more complex features within a year or two, WordPress may avoid a rebuild later.

For a wider view including custom builds, read our guide to website builders vs custom websites.

Switching between platforms

Moving from Squarespace to WordPress, or the other way, is possible but usually means rebuilding the design and recreating features. The most important part is protecting search visibility: map every old URL, set up 301 redirects, keep valuable content and check tracking. FAQs

Is Squarespace or WordPress better for SEO?

Both can rank well. WordPress offers more control over structure, schema and large content libraries, while Squarespace covers the essentials for smaller sites. Content quality and site structure matter more than the platform.

Is Squarespace easier than WordPress?

Usually, yes, especially for first-time site owners. A well-built WordPress site can also be easy to edit, but that depends on how it was set up.

Is WordPress cheaper than Squarespace?

The WordPress software is free, but hosting, premium plugins, the build and maintenance all cost money. Over time either can be cheaper depending on complexity and who maintains the site.

Can I move from Squarespace to WordPress later?

Yes, but expect to rebuild the design and features. Plan redirects carefully so you keep your search visibility.

Which is better for a small business website?

For a simple site you will edit yourself, Squarespace is often enough. If you expect to grow into more complex features or a large amount of content, WordPress gives more room.

Do I own my website on Squarespace?

You own your content and domain, but the site runs on Squarespace's platform and its design cannot be moved elsewhere. Self-hosted WordPress gives you the files and database.
