Why Jeddah businesses need a tailored SEO plan

Jeddah is a commercial port city with a strong mix of retail, hospitality, logistics, healthcare and professional services, plus significant seasonal demand linked to visitors travelling through the city. That mix shapes search behaviour. A clinic, a restaurant group and a freight forwarder all compete for very different queries, and many of those queries are typed in Arabic, in English or in a blend of the two.

  • Arabic and English demand. Customers may search in formal Arabic, in local dialect, in English or with transliterated spellings. Each needs researching on its own terms rather than translated from an English list.
  • Seasonal peaks. Some sectors see search interest rise and fall around holidays, school terms and visitor seasons. A good plan publishes and updates content ahead of those peaks, not during them.
  • Local and national competition. A Jeddah business may compete with local rivals in map results and with national or international brands in organic results. The strategy should reflect which battle you are actually in.
  • Mobile first. Most searches happen on phones, so speed and mobile usability are not optional. Our guide to Core Web Vitals explains the measures Google uses.

Arabic SEO is not translated English SEO

The most common mistake we see in Gulf SEO is a site that was written in English and then machine-translated into Arabic. The Arabic pages often read awkwardly, miss the terms people actually search for and sometimes duplicate the English pages in ways that confuse search engines.

A better approach treats each language as its own audience. Keyword research is done separately in Arabic and in English. Page titles, headings and descriptions are written natively in each language. The site uses correct language tags so Google shows Arabic speakers the Arabic page and English speakers the English one. Right-to-left layouts are tested properly, not just mirrored. Our guide to building a bilingual Arabic and English website covers the structure in more detail.

Because Prism writes in English, we plan the Arabic side with your team or a translator: we supply the keyword research, page briefs and technical setup, and the Arabic copy is written or reviewed by a fluent native speaker. That keeps the language natural and keeps you in control of how your brand sounds.

Local SEO for Jeddah: maps and Google Business Profile

If customers visit your premises or you serve particular districts, map results can matter as much as organic rankings. Local SEO for a Jeddah business usually includes:

  • A complete, accurate Google Business Profile with the right primary category, opening hours, photos and services.
  • Consistent business name, address and phone details wherever you are listed.
  • A steady flow of genuine customer reviews, with polite and helpful responses.
  • Location or service-area pages that are genuinely useful rather than thin copies with the district name swapped.

Our local SEO guide was written for UK businesses, but the principles of relevance, distance and prominence apply in Saudi Arabia too.

How to choose an SEO agency in Jeddah

Use these questions to compare agencies, including us. Good answers are specific; vague answers are a warning sign.

  1. What will you do in the first three months? Expect an audit, keyword research in both languages, a prioritised fix list and a content plan, not just a promise to optimise the site.
  2. Who writes the Arabic content? Ask whether it is written by native speakers or machine-translated, and who reviews it.
  3. How do you report? You should receive a clear monthly report showing work completed, rankings and traffic trends, and enquiries or sales where tracking allows. Our guide to SEO reporting shows what a useful report includes.
  4. Do you guarantee rankings? The honest answer is no. Nobody controls Google's results.
  5. Who owns the work? Content, accounts and data should belong to you if you leave.
  6. What is the contract length and notice period? Understand the commitment before you sign.

SEO and AI search in Saudi Arabia

More people now see AI-generated answers alongside or instead of traditional results. Content that is clear, well structured and factually careful is easier for these tools to understand and cite. That means direct answers near the top of pages, honest FAQs and consistent information about your business wherever it appears. A note on rules and data

Saudi Arabia has its own data protection and advertising rules, and they change over time. If your site collects personal data through forms, bookings or analytics, check current official guidance and take local legal advice where needed. We can set up tracking and consent tools technically, but compliance decisions rest with your business and its advisers.

FAQs

How do I choose an SEO agency in Jeddah?

Ask each agency what it will do in the first three months, who writes the Arabic content, how it reports and who owns the work. Avoid anyone who guarantees rankings. Compare clear proposals rather than headline prices alone.

Do you have an office in Jeddah?

No. Prism Works Studio is based in the UK and works remotely with businesses in Saudi Arabia through video calls, shared documents and written monthly reports.

Can you write Arabic SEO content?

We write in English. For Arabic pages we provide keyword research, page briefs and the technical setup, and the copy is written or reviewed by your team or a native-speaking translator, so it reads naturally.

How long does SEO take to work in Saudi Arabia?

Results typically take three to six months to build, depending on how competitive your sector is, the state of your site and how much content you need. Technical fixes can show sooner; authority takes longer.

How much does an SEO agency in Jeddah cost?

Prices vary widely between agencies. Our retainers are Launch £350, Growth £750 and Scale £1,500 a month, and we can quote in other currencies on request.

Should I focus on Arabic or English SEO first?

It depends on who your customers are and how they search. Keyword research in both languages usually shows where demand and competition sit, and many businesses start with the language that brings the most valuable enquiries.
