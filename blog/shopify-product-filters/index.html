Shopify Product Filters: Helping Shoppers Narrow Down Choices

How Shopify product filters work, how to set them up with the Search and Discovery app, and how to plan filter options, metafields and SEO sensibly.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Filters let shoppers narrow a collection by attributes such as size, colour, price, availability or material.
  • Shopify's Search and Discovery app controls which filters appear, and metafields allow filters based on custom product details.
  • Good filters depend on clean, consistent product data, so tidy your variants, tags and metafields first.

Once an online store has more than a handful of products in a collection, shoppers need a way to narrow things down. Someone looking for a navy jumper in a medium does not want to scroll past every jumper in every colour and size. Product filters solve that problem by letting visitors refine a collection or search results page to only the items that match what they want.

Shopify has built-in support for filtering, and setting it up is not difficult. Setting it up well, so that filters are genuinely useful and do not create problems elsewhere, takes a bit more thought. This guide walks through how Shopify filters work, how to configure them and how to plan your product data so filters stay accurate.

What are product filters?

Filters appear on collection pages and search results, usually as a sidebar on desktop and a drawer or panel on mobile. They let shoppers select values such as:

  • price range,
  • availability, such as in stock only,
  • product type,
  • brand or vendor,
  • variant options such as size and colour,
  • tags, and
  • custom details stored in metafields, such as material, fit or suitable age.

Filtering is different from sorting. Sorting changes the order of products, for example by price or newest first. Filtering removes products that do not match. Most stores need both.

How filtering works on Shopify

Shopify's storefront filtering relies on two things: a theme that supports filtering, and the configuration you set in Shopify's Search and Discovery app, which Shopify provides for free.

Theme support

Shopify's current free themes, and most well-built paid themes, support storefront filtering. Older or heavily customised themes may not, or may use a different method such as tag-based filtering. If filters do not appear even after configuration, the theme is the first thing to check. Our guide to Shopify theme customisation covers the wider options for adjusting a theme.

The Search and Discovery app

In the Search and Discovery app you choose which filters to show and in what order. You can add filters based on availability, price, product type, vendor, tags, variant options and metafields. You can also rename filter labels and, for some filter types, group values together, such as combining "Navy" and "Dark blue" under one colour.

The app also handles other discovery features, such as search synonyms and product recommendations, which work alongside filters on your site search.

Choosing the right filters

More filters do not automatically mean a better experience. Too many options can overwhelm shoppers and clutter mobile screens. Start by asking which attributes customers genuinely use to make decisions in each part of your range.

  • Clothing and footwear: size, colour, fit, material and price tend to matter.
  • Homeware: room, material, colour and dimensions may be more relevant.
  • Beauty and skincare: skin type, concern and ingredient preferences might be key.
  • Technical or trade products: specifications, compatibility and brand often come first.

Look at your customer service questions, search terms and product reviews for clues. If shoppers regularly ask whether something is suitable for a particular use, that attribute may deserve a filter.

Shopify shows only filter values that apply to products in the current collection, so a filter for "material" will only list materials actually present. Even so, keep the overall list focused.

Using metafields for custom filters

Some of the most useful filters are based on details that do not fit Shopify's standard product fields. That is where metafields come in. A metafield is a custom field attached to a product or variant, such as "Material", "Heel height" or "Dietary information".

To use a metafield as a filter, you create a metafield definition with a supported type, fill it in on your products, then add it as a filter in the Search and Discovery app. Shopify supports filtering on a range of metafield types, including single-line text, numbers, true or false values and lists of values, as well as its standard category metafields. Check Shopify's current documentation for the exact supported types, as these have expanded over time.

Our Shopify metafields guide explains how to plan and create metafield definitions in more detail.

Clean product data comes first

Filters can only be as accurate as the product data behind them. Common data issues that break filters include:

  • inconsistent option names, such as "Colour" on some products and "Color" on others, which create separate filters,
  • inconsistent values, such as "Grey", "Gray" and "grey" appearing as different choices,
  • products missing a value entirely, so they disappear when a filter is applied,
  • overloaded tags used for many purposes, making tag filters confusing, and
  • variants set up inconsistently across similar products.

Before switching on filters, export your products and tidy option names and values. Agree a naming standard and stick to it when adding new products. If you are still deciding how to set up options, our guide to product variants and bundles covers the fundamentals.

Filters and collections work together

Filters are not a replacement for a sensible collection structure. Collections define the broad groups shoppers browse, such as "Women's knitwear", while filters help them refine within that group. If your collections are too broad, filters have to do too much work; if they are too narrow, filters become pointless.

A practical balance is to create collections for the main ways customers think about your range, then use filters for the attributes that cut across them. Our guide to Shopify collections structure covers how to plan this.

Designing filters for mobile

Many shoppers browse on their phones, where screen space is tight. Good mobile filters usually:

  • sit behind a clear "Filter" button that opens a panel or drawer,
  • show how many filters are active,
  • make it easy to clear one filter or all of them,
  • keep tap targets large enough to use comfortably, and
  • show results quickly without reloading the whole page where the theme allows.

Test your filters on real devices, not just a resized desktop browser. Our mobile website design guide has more general advice.

SEO considerations

When a shopper applies a filter, Shopify typically adds parameters to the collection URL. That means a single collection can produce a very large number of filtered URL combinations. Generally, you do not want search engines spending time crawling every combination.

Shopify themes usually set the canonical tag on filtered pages to point back to the main collection, which helps. Still, it is worth checking how your theme handles filtered URLs and keeping an eye on Google Search Console for signs of wasted crawling. If a particular filtered view represents something people search for, such as "linen shirts", it may be better to create a dedicated collection with its own title, description and clean URL. Our Shopify SEO guide covers collection optimisation more broadly.

Apps and alternatives

Shopify's native filtering covers many stores' needs. Some stores, particularly those with very large catalogues or complex technical products, use third-party search and filter apps for features such as more advanced merchandising, visual swatches or more filter types. Before adding an app, check whether native filtering can do the job, as every app adds another dependency. Our guide to a Shopify apps audit explains how to weigh that up.

Measuring whether filters help

Once filters are live, review how shoppers use them. Analytics can show which filters are used, which collections see heavy filter use and whether filtered sessions lead to product views and purchases. If a filter is rarely used, consider removing it. If shoppers often search for an attribute you do not offer as a filter, consider adding it.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Switching on every possible filter instead of the ones customers use.
  • Inconsistent option names and values creating duplicate filters.
  • Relying on messy tags rather than structured metafields.
  • Forgetting to fill in metafields for new products.
  • Filters that are hard to use or clear on mobile.
  • Letting filtered URLs be crawled without checking canonical tags.

FAQs

Does Shopify have built-in product filters?

Yes. Shopify supports storefront filtering on collection and search pages, configured through the free Search and Discovery app, provided your theme supports it.

Why are my Shopify filters not showing?

The most likely reasons are that your theme does not support storefront filtering, the filters have not been added in the Search and Discovery app, or products lack the relevant data.

Can I filter by custom attributes like material?

Yes. Store the attribute in a product or variant metafield of a supported type, then add that metafield as a filter in the Search and Discovery app.

Do filtered pages cause duplicate content?

They can create many URL variations. Shopify themes usually point canonical tags at the main collection, but it is worth checking your theme's behaviour.

Should I use a filter app instead?

Only if native filtering cannot meet your needs. Apps can add advanced features, but they also add cost, complexity and another dependency.
