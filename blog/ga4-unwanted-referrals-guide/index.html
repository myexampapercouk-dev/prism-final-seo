How to Fix Unwanted Referrals in GA4: Stop PayPal and Stripe Stealing Credit

Fix unwanted referrals in GA4 by listing payment and booking domains in your data stream, so sales stay credited to the real source, not PayPal or Stripe.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Payment providers, booking tools and login services can appear as referral sources and take credit for sales in GA4.
  • List those domains under unwanted referrals in your web data stream tag settings so the original source keeps the credit.
  • Check traffic acquisition reports regularly and update the list whenever you add a new third-party checkout or booking tool.

To fix unwanted referrals in GA4, open your web data stream, go to the tag settings, choose the option to list unwanted referrals, and add the domains of payment providers, booking tools or other services that send visitors back to your site. GA4 then stops treating those returns as new referral visits, so purchases and leads stay credited to the channel that really brought the customer.

This is a common problem for UK online shops and service businesses that use a third-party checkout or booking step. If your reports show paypal.com or a card authentication domain as a top source of revenue, your attribution is almost certainly being distorted. This guide explains why it happens, how to fix it and how to check the result. Google updates GA4 often, so check the current Analytics help pages for exact menu names.

Why unwanted referrals happen

When a customer leaves your site to complete a step elsewhere, such as paying through PayPal, passing a bank security check or confirming a booking on a third-party page, they are then sent back to your thank you page. GA4 can see that return visit as arriving from the external domain. That can result in:

  • The sale or key event being credited to a referral from the payment provider, instead of to Google Ads, organic search or email.
  • Extra sessions being created, inflating session counts.
  • Paid campaigns looking less effective than they really are, which can lead to poor budget decisions.

For anyone running ads, this matters a great deal. If conversions are being credited to a payment provider in GA4, any campaign analysis based on GA4 data will understate paid performance. Common domains to check

The exact domains depend on the tools you use. Look in your own reports rather than copying a list blindly, but these categories are typical:

Type of serviceWhy it appearsWhat to look for in reports
Payment providersCustomer pays on the provider's page then returnsPayPal, Stripe checkout, Klarna, Clearpay and similar domains
Card authenticationBank security checks during card paymentDomains belonging to card schemes or banks that appear only at checkout
Booking and scheduling toolsBookings confirmed on a hosted pageThe booking platform's domain as a referral linked to key events
Login and identity servicesCustomers sign in through a third-party serviceLogin provider domains appearing as referrers
Your own other domainsCustomers move between your main site and a separate shop or booking subdomainYour own domain listed as a referral source

How to fix unwanted referrals in GA4

  1. Find the culprits. Open the traffic acquisition report, set the primary dimension to session source or session source and medium, and look for referral sources linked to purchases or key events that should not be there.
  2. Open your data stream. In Admin, go to data streams, select your web stream and open configure tag settings.
  3. List unwanted referrals. Choose the option to list unwanted referrals. Add a condition for each domain, using a match type such as referral domain contains or ends with.
  4. Save and note the date. Changes are not retroactive, so record when you made them.
  5. Check the results. After a week or two of normal trading, review the traffic acquisition report again. The payment or booking domains should no longer appear as sources of conversions.

If your site uses more than one domain, for example a main website and a separate shop, also check the configure your domains setting in the same area. That handles cross-domain measurement so a visitor moving between your own domains is treated as one journey. Our GA4 setup guide covers data stream settings in more detail.

Unwanted referrals versus cross-domain tracking

These two settings are often confused. Use the right one for the situation:

SettingUse whenExample
List unwanted referralsA third-party domain you do not control sends visitors back to youA customer returns from a payment page to your order confirmation
Configure your domainsYou control both domains and both have your GA4 tag installedYour main site and your separate booking site

If you cannot put your tag on the other domain, it is an unwanted referral case. If you can and the visitor moves between your own sites, use cross-domain configuration.

Checking your ecommerce and lead data afterwards

Once the fix is in, take a broader look at your reports:

  • Revenue by channel. In an online shop, revenue that was previously credited to referrals should now be spread across paid search, organic, email and direct.
  • Key events by source. For lead generation sites, enquiries and bookings should line up more closely with the channels you are investing in.
  • Campaign tags. Make sure email and social links use consistent UTM tracking links, so their traffic is not lumped into direct or referral.
  • Platform comparison. Compare GA4 with your ad platforms. They will never match exactly, because each uses different attribution rules, but large gaps are worth investigating.

Other causes of strange referral traffic

Not every odd referral is from a checkout. Other common sources include:

  • Webmail and messaging apps. Clicks from some email clients appear as referrals rather than email. Tagging links with UTMs fixes most of this.
  • Spam referrals. Occasionally, junk domains show up in referral reports. GA4 handles this differently from older versions of Analytics, but if you see suspicious domains with no real engagement, treat them with caution rather than acting on them.
  • Your own staff. Internal tools and intranets can show up if staff click through to the website from them. Excluding internal traffic by IP helps here.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Adding domains too broadly. A loose rule such as a single common word could match genuine referral partners, such as a directory or a supplier that links to you. Keep each condition specific to the service you are excluding.
  • Fixing GA4 but not the ad platforms. Ad platforms record conversions with their own tags, so this setting does not change what Google Ads or Meta report. It only affects GA4.
  • Forgetting new tools. When you add a new payment method, buy now pay later option or booking system, check referrals again within a few weeks.
  • Reading old reports as if nothing changed. Channel figures before and after the fix are not directly comparable, so flag the change date in any trend report.

A quick monthly check

  1. Open traffic acquisition and filter to the referral channel.
  2. Sort by key events or revenue.
  3. Look for any domain that is a payment, booking, login or authentication service.
  4. Add any new ones to the unwanted referrals list.
  5. Note the date in your reporting so trends make sense later.

It takes a few minutes, and it keeps channel reporting honest. If you build client or management dashboards, our Looker Studio dashboard guide explains how to present channel data clearly with notes on tracking changes.

FAQs

How do I fix unwanted referrals in GA4?

Go to Admin, open your web data stream, select configure tag settings, choose list unwanted referrals and add the payment, booking or login domains that appear as referral sources. Then check your traffic acquisition report after a week or two.

Why does PayPal show as a referral in GA4?

Customers leave your site to pay on PayPal and are sent back to your confirmation page. Without an unwanted referral rule, GA4 can treat that return as a new visit referred by PayPal and give it credit for the sale.

Does fixing unwanted referrals change past data?

No. The setting applies from the time you save it. Earlier data stays as it was, so note the date for future comparisons.

Should I add my own domain as an unwanted referral?

Usually not. If you own multiple domains with your GA4 tag installed on each, use the configure your domains setting for cross-domain measurement instead.

Why does my referral traffic look higher than expected?

Common reasons include payment and booking redirects, untagged email links, staff using internal tools and occasional spam domains. Check which referrers are linked to key events first, as those cause the most distortion.

Do I need a developer to fix unwanted referrals?

No. It is a settings change in GA4 admin. You need edit access to the property, but no code changes are required for the basic fix.
