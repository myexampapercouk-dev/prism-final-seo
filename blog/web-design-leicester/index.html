Web Design in Leicester: Websites for Manufacturers, Textile Firms and Family Businesses

Web design for Leicester makers, textile firms and family businesses: trade enquiries, wholesale ordering, catalogues and sites the next generation can run.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Leicester textile and manufacturing firms often serve trade buyers and consumers at once, so the site structure should separate those journeys clearly.
  • Family businesses benefit from websites that capture the story and know-how of the firm while being simple enough for any family member or staff member to update.
  • Prism Works Studio works with Leicester businesses remotely, without needing site visits.

Web design in Leicester for a manufacturer, textile firm or family business has to balance heritage with practicality. Many of these businesses have built their reputation over decades through trade relationships and word of mouth, and the website has often been an afterthought. A good modern site tells trade buyers exactly what you make and how to order, gives consumers a clear route if you sell direct, and is simple enough that the business can keep it current. Prism Works Studio works with Leicester businesses remotely, so the whole process runs over video calls and shared drafts.

Leicester has a long association with textiles, knitwear, hosiery, footwear and garment making, along with food production, engineering and a wide range of family-owned firms. Many are based in industrial areas around the city and county, such as Belgrave, the Abbey Lane and Frog Island areas, Thurmaston, Wigston and Hinckley. This guide focuses on the specific challenges these businesses face online.

Two audiences, one website

A textile or apparel manufacturer might supply retailers and brands under contract, sell wholesale to independent shops and run its own consumer label. Each group needs different information:

AudienceWhat they need from the site
Brands seeking a manufacturerCapabilities, minimum order quantities, lead times, sampling process, compliance and how to start a conversation
Wholesale and trade buyersProduct ranges, trade terms, how to open an account and how ordering works
ConsumersProducts, sizes, prices, delivery, returns and a smooth checkout
Job seekersRoles, training and what working at the firm is like

Mixing all of these on one homepage tends to confuse everyone. A clearer approach is a homepage that routes people quickly: a section for trade and manufacturing enquiries, a link to the shop if you have one, and a short introduction to the firm. Each journey then has its own pages and calls to action.

Showing manufacturing capability

Brands looking for a UK manufacturer usually want to judge fit before making contact. Pages that explain what you can and cannot do save time on both sides. Useful detail includes:

  • Product types you make, such as knitwear, jersey, outerwear, uniforms or accessories.
  • Processes you handle in-house, from pattern cutting and sampling to finishing and packing.
  • Typical minimum order quantities and lead times, or the factors that affect them.
  • Materials you work with and any sourcing information you can share accurately.
  • Photos of the factory, machinery and finished products that you own.

Only describe certifications, audits or ethical standards you actually hold and can evidence. Buyers increasingly check, and claims about ethical or sustainable production can attract scrutiny from the Competition and Markets Authority and the Advertising Standards Authority if they are vague or unsupported. For broader guidance on industrial sites, see our guide to manufacturer website design.

Wholesale and trade ordering

Trade customers often still order by phone or email, which can work well. A website can make that easier without forcing a full online trade portal on day one. Options range from simple to more involved:

  1. A trade enquiry form that captures business details and the products of interest.
  2. A downloadable or password-protected catalogue for approved accounts.
  3. A trade area in your online store where approved customers see trade prices and can order. Shopify and WooCommerce both support wholesale set-ups through their own features or add-ons.

Which option suits you depends on order volumes, how prices vary between customers and how your stock and accounts systems work. It is usually sensible to start with the simplest version that removes friction, then build more as demand shows.

Selling direct to consumers

Some Leicester manufacturers have launched their own consumer brands, using their production know-how to sell direct. A consumer store needs different thinking from a trade site: strong product photography, accurate size guides, clear delivery and returns information and a checkout that works smoothly on phones.

Product information for clothing and textiles may need to meet labelling rules, such as fibre content, and consumer protection rules on returns for online sales. Check the current requirements from GOV.UK and your trading standards service for your products. Our guides to Shopify for clothing brands and ecommerce website design go into detail on store structure.

Websites for family businesses

Family firms bring particular strengths and challenges to a website project. The strengths are a genuine story, deep product knowledge and long relationships. The challenges are often practical: decisions involve several people, knowledge sits in one or two heads, and the website may have been set up years ago by someone who has since moved on.

Telling the story honestly

A history page can be one of the most engaging parts of a family business site. Old photographs, the founding story and how the business has changed across generations help customers and staff connect with the firm. Keep it accurate and let the people involved review it.

Making the site easy to hand over

Many family businesses are going through succession, with a new generation taking on more responsibility. A website built so that more than one person can update it reduces risk. Practical steps include:

  • Choosing a widely used platform such as WordPress or Shopify, so help is easy to find.
  • Keeping logins in a shared, secure place under the business's control.
  • Making sure the domain and hosting are registered to the business, not an individual's personal account.
  • Writing a short guide on how to update common content.

Our guide to WordPress for small business websites covers ownership and day-to-day management.

Agreeing decisions

When several family members share decision-making, agreeing a clear brief and a single point of contact at the start can keep a project moving. Shared drafts that everyone can comment on help bring different views together before build begins.

Multilingual considerations

Leicester is a diverse city, and some businesses serve customers or work with staff and suppliers who prefer languages other than English. If that applies to you, consider whether key pages, such as contact details or careers information, would benefit from translation. Professional translation and a clear language switcher work better than automatic translation widgets, which can produce errors on important details.

SEO for Leicester manufacturers and family firms

Search priorities depend on who you sell to:

  • Manufacturing services: pages built around product type and process, such as knitwear manufacturing, can attract brands searching across the UK, not just locally.
  • Trade and wholesale: category pages and clear trade information help buyers find you. Our B2B SEO guide covers this.
  • Consumer products: product and category SEO matters most for a direct-to-consumer store.
  • Local presence: a Google Business Profile helps people find a factory shop, trade counter or showroom.

If you are replacing an older site that already ranks for useful terms, plan redirects carefully. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings explains how.

FAQs

Should a manufacturer and its consumer brand share one website?

It depends. A shared site works if the audiences are clearly separated. A separate consumer store can make sense if the brand has a distinct identity or you do not want retail customers mixing with trade information.

Can trade customers order online with their own prices?

Yes. Shopify and WooCommerce both support wholesale set-ups where approved customers see trade pricing. Whether it is worth it depends on order volumes and how your pricing works.

What should a clothing manufacturer's website show brands?

Product types, in-house processes, typical minimums and lead times, sampling, any verified certifications and a clear way to start an enquiry.

How can a family business keep its website from depending on one person?

Register the domain and hosting to the business, store logins securely and centrally, use a widely supported platform and write a short guide for common updates.

Is it worth translating our website?

If a meaningful share of customers, staff or suppliers prefer another language, translating key pages professionally can help. Avoid relying on automatic translation for important details.

No. Prism Works Studio works with Leicester businesses remotely, and the whole project runs over video calls and shared drafts.
