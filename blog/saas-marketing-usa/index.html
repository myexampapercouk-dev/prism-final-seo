SEO for software companies

SEO for SaaS is different from local SEO. Buyers research for weeks or months, compare several tools and read reviews before booking a demo. Your content needs to meet them at each stage.

We usually plan content in layers:

  • Product and feature pages that explain what the software does in plain language.
  • Use case and industry pages showing how different teams use the product.
  • Comparison and alternative pages for buyers weighing options.
  • Integration pages for the tools your product connects with.
  • Guides and templates that answer problems your buyers have before they know your product exists.

We also handle technical SEO, which matters for SaaS sites with documentation, help centers, changelogs and app subdomains. Our technical SEO audit checklist covers what we check. Our SEO and GEO service includes optimising content for AI search tools, which many software buyers now use for research.

Paid search and LinkedIn Ads

Google Ads works well for SaaS when you bid on high-intent searches, such as category terms, problem terms and competitor names where appropriate. Costs can be high in competitive categories, so we focus on tight keyword lists, strong negatives and landing pages built for each campaign. Our guide to a remote PPC agency for US businesses explains how we manage accounts.

LinkedIn Ads let you reach specific roles at specific types of company. They usually cost more per click than other platforms, so they suit products with meaningful contract values. Our guide to LinkedIn Ads for US business-to-business companies covers targeting, formats and budgets.

We do not film or edit video. Where YouTube or video ads are part of the plan, we use product demos and footage your team supplies.

Websites that convert trials and demos

Your marketing website does most of the selling before a buyer ever talks to your team. We design and build SaaS websites with:

  • A homepage that explains the product, who it is for and the main outcome in a few seconds.
  • Clear pricing pages, or a clear route to a quote for sales-led products.
  • Simple trial and demo forms that ask only for what your team needs.
  • Proof points your team can stand behind, such as product screenshots and security information.
  • Fast pages and a structure that supports SEO from launch.

Our landing page design guide sets out the structure we use for campaign pages. We build on WordPress for most SaaS marketing sites, which keeps editing easy for your team. See our website development service for details.

Measurement and attribution

SaaS buying journeys are long, so measurement needs care. We set up analytics events for sign-ups, trial starts, demo requests and key product pages, and connect ad platforms to those conversions. Our GA4 setup guide shows the basics.

Where possible, we also help you pass lead quality back from your CRM, so ad platforms can learn which campaigns bring real opportunities rather than just form fills. Our conversion rate optimisation guide covers how we test and improve pages over time.

Matching marketing to your sales motion

The right plan depends heavily on how your company sells. A product-led company and a sales-led company can sell similar software but need very different marketing.

Product-led companies want as many qualified sign-ups as possible, with the product doing much of the selling. For them, we focus on high-volume search terms, clear pricing pages, short sign-up flows and tracking that follows users from first visit to activation. Content often targets practical problems the product solves, with templates and guides that lead naturally into a trial.

Sales-led companies want fewer, better conversations. For them, we focus on buyer-specific content, comparison pages, LinkedIn targeting by role and company size, and demo forms that collect enough detail for your team to qualify leads. Lead quality matters more than lead volume, so we work with your sales team to define what a good lead looks like.

Many companies sit somewhere in between, offering a free trial but relying on sales for larger accounts. In that case, we build two clear paths on the website and track them separately so neither gets lost.

Common gaps on SaaS marketing websites

When we review software websites, we often find the same gaps:

  • Headlines that describe technology rather than outcomes, leaving buyers unsure what the product does for them.
  • No pages for specific use cases or industries, so the site cannot rank for those searches.
  • Pricing hidden or unclear, which can push buyers to competitors that are more open.
  • Documentation and help pages blocked or poorly linked, wasting content that could rank.
  • Demo forms that are too long, cutting the number of requests your team receives.
  • Tracking that stops at the form, with no link to trial activation or closed deals.

Each of these can be fixed in stages, starting with the pages closest to revenue.

Practical notes for US SaaS companies

Software companies handle customer data, so privacy matters in both your product and your marketing. US privacy rules vary by state, and accessibility expectations apply to many websites. We follow good practice, but we do not give legal advice. Please check official guidance and speak with your own adviser. If your product serves a regulated sector such as healthcare or finance, compliance approval for marketing content rests with your team.
