Why Aarhus businesses need a different kind of website

Aarhus is Denmark's second city, with a large university, a growing technology and creative scene, a major port and a strong base of manufacturing, food and design businesses across East Jutland. Many Aarhus firms sell across Denmark, the Nordics and wider Europe, while others serve customers within a short drive of the city.

Danish buyers tend to expect clean, honest websites. They research carefully, compare options and are quick to leave a site that feels slow, cluttered or vague about prices and terms. A website that works in this market is usually simple, direct and well structured rather than flashy.

English is widely understood in Denmark, but most local customers still search in Danish. That shapes how you plan pages, which we cover below. If you also target the capital, our guide to web design in Copenhagen covers the more competitive market there.

What a strong Aarhus business website includes

The details vary by sector, but most successful small business websites share the same building blocks.

  • A clear homepage. Visitors should understand what you do, who you serve and how to get in touch within a few seconds.
  • One page per service. Each main service gets its own page, which helps both visitors and search engines.
  • Fast loading on mobile. Compressed images, lean code and good hosting. Our guide to website speed optimisation explains why this matters for rankings and conversions.
  • Accessibility built in. Readable text, good contrast, keyboard navigation and descriptive links. The European Accessibility Act has made this more important for businesses selling to consumers in the EU.
  • Clear calls to action. Booking, calling, requesting a quote or buying should be easy from every page.
  • Trust signals. Your company details, CVR number where relevant, terms, privacy information and genuine reviews.
  • Search foundations. Proper page titles, headings, structured data and a sitemap from day one.

Danish, English or both?

Language is one of the first decisions for any Aarhus website. If most of your customers are in Denmark, your main pages should be in Danish, because that is how they search. If you sell internationally, an English version can open new markets, and some technology and research firms in Aarhus run English-first sites because their buyers are global.

Prism writes in English. Danish copy should come from your own team or a professional translator who understands your sector. We can design the page layouts so they work for both languages, set up language switching, and add hreflang tags so Google shows the right version to the right searcher. Danish words are often longer than English ones, so we test headings and buttons with real Danish text before launch rather than relying on placeholders.

How a remote web design project works

Working with a UK studio on an Aarhus website is straightforward. Denmark is one hour ahead of the UK for most of the year, so working days overlap almost completely. A typical small business project follows these stages.

  1. Discovery call. We discuss your goals, customers, services, competitors and any existing site.
  2. Sitemap and content plan. We agree the pages, what each one needs to say and which searches each should target.
  3. Design. We share designs for the homepage and key templates, and you give feedback online.
  4. Build. We build the site on a staging link you can review on your own phone and computer.
  5. Content and testing. Your Danish copy goes in, and we test speed, forms, accessibility and mobile layouts.
  6. Launch and handover. We set up redirects, Search Console and analytics, then show your team how to edit pages.

Small business websites typically take three to five weeks. Ecommerce sites take five to twelve weeks, depending on the number of products, integrations and markets. The main thing that slows projects down is waiting for copy and images, so it helps to start gathering those early.

Redesigning an existing Aarhus website

Many businesses come to us with a site that already ranks for some searches but looks dated or converts poorly. A redesign can fix that, but it carries a risk: if page addresses change without proper redirects, you can lose rankings you have built over years.

Before we change anything, we crawl the current site, record every page that receives search traffic and map each old address to its new one. Our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings explains this process step by step. It is one of the most important parts of any redesign and one of the most often skipped.

Writing pages that convert Danish visitors

Design gets attention, but words do most of the selling. Danish buyers tend to respond to plain, factual language rather than heavy sales claims. Each service page should explain what you do, who it is for, how the process works, what it roughly costs or how pricing is decided, and what happens next.

Even if your final copy is in Danish, it helps to start with a clear structure. Our guide to website copywriting sets out a page structure your team or translator can follow, and the same principles apply in any language.

After launch: getting found on Google.dk

A new website is a starting point. To win steady traffic, most businesses need ongoing SEO: technical checks, new content, local optimisation and links from relevant Danish and European sites. Our guide to SEO for Copenhagen businesses explains how SEO works in the Danish market, and our SEO and GEO services cover search engines and AI search tools.

For local businesses in Aarhus, a well maintained Google Business Profile, consistent contact details and a steady flow of genuine reviews also make a real difference to how often you appear in map results.

Look for a designer or studio that asks about your customers and goals before talking about colours, explains the platform they recommend and why, includes SEO basics in the build and gives you full ownership of your domain, hosting and content.

We write in English. Danish copy should come from your team or a translator, and we design, build and set up the site so it works properly in Danish, English or both.

How long does a new website take?
