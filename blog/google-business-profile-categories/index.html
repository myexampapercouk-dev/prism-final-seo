Google Business Profile Categories: How to Choose the Right Ones

How to choose Google Business Profile categories: pick an accurate primary category, add relevant secondary ones, check competitors and avoid common mistakes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Your primary category is one of the strongest signals for which local searches your profile appears in, so choose the one that best describes the core of your business.
  • Add secondary categories only for services you genuinely offer, and check what similar businesses near you use.
  • Review categories every few months, change them gradually and back them up with matching services and website pages.

To choose Google Business Profile categories, pick one primary category that describes what your business mainly is, then add a small number of secondary categories for other services you genuinely provide. The primary category has the most influence on which local searches you appear for, so it should match the core of your business and the searches you most want to win.

This guide is for UK small businesses managing their own profile, from trades and shops to restaurants, salons and professional services. It explains how categories work, how to research them, how to choose and edit them, and the mistakes that hold profiles back.

What Business Profile categories do

Categories tell Google what type of business you are. Google uses them, along with your business name, description, services, reviews and website, to decide whether your profile is relevant to a search. They also unlock certain features. Depending on the category, a profile may be able to show menus, bookings, product listings, services or attributes that suit that type of business.

There are two kinds:

  • Primary category. One per profile. It is shown publicly beneath your business name and carries the most weight.
  • Secondary categories. Optional extras that describe other parts of your business. Google allows several, but adding more is not automatically better.

You cannot invent your own category. You choose from a fixed list that Google maintains and changes from time to time. If the exact phrase you want does not exist, choose the closest accurate one and use your services list, description and website to fill in the detail. How to choose Google Business Profile categories

Work through these steps before you change anything.

  1. List what you actually do. Write down your main service and every other service that brings in meaningful work.
  2. Decide what you most want to be found for. Usually the service that makes up most of your income or that people search for most in your area. Our keyword research guide helps here.
  3. Search the category list. In your profile editor, start typing into the category box to see the options Google offers.
  4. Check competitors. Look at businesses that appear in the map results for your main searches and note their primary categories.
  5. Pick the primary. Choose the most specific accurate category for your core business.
  6. Add relevant secondaries. Only add categories for services you really offer and want enquiries for.

Choosing your primary category

Be specific. A more precise category usually matches searches better than a broad one. A business that only fits kitchens is better described by a kitchen-specific category than a general builder category, if one exists. A restaurant serving Italian food will usually be better off with an Italian restaurant category than a plain restaurant one.

Describe what you are, not what you sell. Categories should describe the business itself. A cafe that also sells coffee beans is still a cafe. If you have premises that work as two genuinely separate businesses, check Google's current guidelines on whether separate profiles are allowed rather than trying to cover both in one.

Match the searches that matter most. If most of your enquiries come from people searching for emergency plumbing, check whether a category exists that fits that work better than a general one, and whether it accurately describes your business.

How to research competitor categories

Your competitors' categories are a useful guide, because the businesses already appearing in the map results for your target searches show what Google tends to associate with them. The primary category usually shows under the business name on the public profile. Secondary categories are not always shown, though some browser extensions and local SEO tools can reveal them.

Do not simply copy the top competitor. Use their choices to spot categories you may have missed, then decide whether each one honestly fits your business. If you work across several towns or branches, our guide to multi-location SEO explains how to keep categories consistent between profiles.

Common category mistakes

  • Too broad a primary. Choosing a general category when a more specific accurate one exists.
  • Adding every loosely related category. This can dilute relevance and attract enquiries you do not want.
  • Categories for products, not the business. A shop that stocks a brand is not that brand's dealer unless it genuinely is.
  • Frequent changes. Switching the primary category repeatedly can cause ranking swings and may prompt Google to re-check the profile.
  • Mismatch with the website. If your profile says one thing and your linked page says another, the signals are weaker.
  • Keyword stuffing elsewhere to make up for it. Adding services or towns to the business name breaks Google's guidelines and can lead to problems with your profile.

Support your categories with the rest of the profile

Categories work best when everything else agrees with them.

  • Services. Add the services you offer under each category, using plain descriptions.
  • Description. Write a clear summary of what you do and where, without stuffing keywords.
  • Website. Link the profile to a relevant page. A dedicated page for each main service helps Google and visitors. Our guide to service page design explains what to include, and our local SEO guide covers location pages.
  • Reviews and posts. Reviews that mention specific services and regular Business Profile posts reinforce what you do.
  • Citations. Choose matching categories on other directories too. Our local citations guide explains how.

How to change your categories

  1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your profile.
  2. Open your profile from Google Search or Maps and choose to edit the profile.
  3. Find the business category section and edit the primary and additional categories.
  4. Save, then check back after a few days that the change has been published.

Google may review category changes before they go live and occasionally asks for re-verification. Make changes one at a time where you can, and note the date so you can compare performance afterwards in your profile insights and in Search Console. Google adjusts its interface regularly, so if the steps look different, check its current Business Profile help pages.

When to review your categories

Check your categories when you add or drop a service, when local competitors start outranking you for searches you used to win, or every few months as part of routine profile upkeep. Google sometimes adds new categories that fit a business better than the options available when the profile was set up.

FAQs

How do I choose Google Business Profile categories?

Choose the most specific primary category that accurately describes your core business, then add secondary categories only for other services you genuinely offer. Checking the categories used by businesses that rank well near you is a helpful guide.

How many categories should I add to my Google Business Profile?

There is no ideal number. Add as many as accurately describe distinct parts of your business, which for most small businesses means one primary and a handful of secondaries at most.

What if my exact business type is not a category?

Choose the closest accurate category, then describe your exact services in the services section, the description and on your website. Check the list occasionally, as Google adds new categories.

Does changing my primary category affect rankings?

It can. The primary category is a strong relevance signal, so a change may move which searches you appear for. Make changes carefully and monitor results afterwards.

Can I see my competitors' Google Business Profile categories?

The primary category usually shows under the business name on the public profile. Secondary categories are often hidden, but some local SEO tools and browser extensions can display them.

Can the wrong category get my profile suspended?

Choosing a category that misrepresents your business can go against Google's guidelines and may lead to edits being rejected or other action. Pick categories that honestly describe what you do and check Google's current guidelines.
