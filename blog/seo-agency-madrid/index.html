What makes SEO in Madrid different

Madrid is the capital and the largest business centre in Spain, so many searches are competitive. Professional services, technology firms, property businesses, hospitality, education and retailers all compete for the same results pages, often against national brands with large websites. A few features shape how we plan SEO for the city.

  • Spanish first. Most searches from Madrid are in Spanish, and the wording people use can differ from the terms used in Latin American Spanish. Pages need to reflect how people in Spain actually search.
  • International audiences. Madrid attracts international companies, students and visitors, so some businesses need English pages too. These should exist only where there is real demand.
  • Neighbourhood searches. Local businesses are often searched by district, such as Salamanca, Chamberí, Chamartín or the city centre, as well as by service.
  • National reach. Many Madrid companies sell across Spain, so SEO has to balance local visibility with nationwide service and category pages.

Spanish and English pages done properly

If your site has more than one language, each version needs its own URLs, properly translated titles and descriptions, and hreflang tags that tell search engines which page to show to which audience. Machine translation published without review tends to read awkwardly and can damage trust with Spanish buyers.

Prism writes in English. Spanish content is written by your team or a professional translator, or we draft structure and briefs in English for your translator to adapt. A fluent speaker on your side should approve every Spanish page before it goes live. Our international SEO guide covers language and country targeting, and if you also sell in Catalonia, our guide to SEO in Barcelona explains the extra language considerations there.

Local SEO for Madrid

For clinics, restaurants, law firms, shops, gyms and other businesses that serve people in person, map results can bring more enquiries than standard organic listings. Local SEO for Madrid usually includes:

  1. A complete Google Business Profile with accurate categories, opening hours, photos, services and regular updates.
  2. Consistent business details across your website, directories and social profiles.
  3. Location pages only where you genuinely serve an area, with useful local information rather than copied text.
  4. A steady flow of genuine reviews, requested fairly and answered politely, in line with platform rules.

Businesses that work across the whole of Spain without a physical location to visit usually focus on national service pages instead.

Search engines and AI answers

More people now get answers from AI tools and AI summaries in search results before they click on a website. The same foundations that help a page rank also make it easier for these tools to describe and recommend a business: clear facts, consistent details, well-structured pages, helpful FAQs and mentions on trusted sites. We include this AI-powered side of search, often called GEO, in our SEO work rather than selling it as a separate product.

Data protection, cookies and accessibility

Websites in Spain must follow the GDPR and Spanish data protection law, and the ePrivacy rules on cookies mean non-essential cookies, such as most analytics and advertising cookies, generally need consent before they are set. A clear cookie banner with an equal choice to accept or reject, an accurate privacy policy and a legal notice page are normal parts of a Spanish business website.

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many businesses that sell products or services to consumers online, including e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but accessibility still helps every visitor. The usual technical reference is the harmonised standard EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act for websites explains the basics. These notes are general; check official guidance from the Spanish authorities and the European Commission, and ask your own adviser what applies to you.

Content that Spanish buyers trust

Spanish buyers, like buyers anywhere, want clear answers before they get in touch. Pages that perform well usually explain what the service includes, who it suits, how the process works, typical timelines and how to start. Prices or price ranges, where you can share them, often make pages more useful. Guides and FAQs based on the real questions your sales team hears every week tend to outperform generic blog posts written only for keywords.

We plan content around the searches that lead to enquiries and sales, and we prefer fewer, better pages over many thin ones.

Local Madrid agency or remote UK studio?

A local agency offers face-to-face meetings and native Spanish copywriting in-house. A remote UK studio suits businesses that are comfortable working through calls and shared documents, want English-language strategy and reporting, and already have Spanish writers or a translator. It can also suit Madrid businesses that sell into the UK and want one partner for both markets. The right choice depends on how you prefer to work, not on location alone.
