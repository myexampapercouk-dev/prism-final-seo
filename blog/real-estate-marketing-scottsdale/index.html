For most agents and brokerages, that means a fast website with strong neighbourhood pages, local SEO, Google Ads for high-intent searches and Meta Ads for awareness and retargeting.

The Scottsdale property market and how people search

Scottsdale attracts a broad mix of buyers: relocating families, retirees, second-home owners escaping colder winters, golf and lifestyle buyers, and investors. Many of them start their search from out of state, browsing online for months before visiting. Sellers, meanwhile, want proof that an agent understands their specific neighbourhood and price bracket.

Searches tend to be specific. People look by area, such as Old Town, North Scottsdale, McCormick Ranch, Gainey Ranch, DC Ranch or Troon. They look by lifestyle, such as golf communities, gated communities, homes with mountain views or condos near Old Town. They look by need, such as what is my home worth, best time to sell in Scottsdale or moving to Scottsdale from California.

Each of those searches is an opportunity for a page, an ad group or a piece of content that answers the question better than a generic listings portal.

Building a website that wins seller leads

Listings portals dominate buyer searches, so an agent website wins most often on seller and local knowledge searches. A strong Scottsdale real estate website usually includes:

  • Neighbourhood guides for each area you work, with genuine detail on housing types, amenities and lifestyle.
  • A home valuation page with a clear form and honest explanation of how valuations work.
  • Seller guides covering preparation, pricing and the selling process in Arizona, kept general and accurate.
  • Relocation content for buyers moving from other states.
  • IDX or listings integration where your brokerage and MLS rules allow.
  • Fast mobile performance, because most property browsing happens on phones.

Local SEO for Scottsdale agents

Local SEO helps agents appear in map results and organic searches for their name, their areas and common seller questions. The work includes a complete Google Business Profile, genuine client reviews, consistent listings across property directories, neighbourhood pages and helpful content. Results typically take 3 to 6 months to build.

Google Ads and Meta Ads for real estate

Paid ads bring leads faster than SEO, but property advertising has special rules. Meta treats housing ads as a special ad category, which limits targeting by age, gender and postcode. Google applies its own housing ad policies in the US. Ads must also comply with fair housing law and your licensing obligations. We follow platform rules, but compliance approval rests with you and your brokerage, so check with your broker or adviser.

Within those rules, effective campaigns usually include:

  • Google Search campaigns for valuation and agent searches in your areas.
  • Meta campaigns promoting new listings, open houses and seller guides.
  • Retargeting for people who visited your website or neighbourhood pages.
  • Lead forms or landing pages with a clear, honest offer.

How Scottsdale compares with other markets

Marketing to out-of-state and seasonal buyers

A large share of Scottsdale buyers are not local when they start searching. Many come from California, the Midwest, the Pacific Northwest and Canada, and some are looking for a winter home rather than a main residence. They often research for months before a first visit, which changes how marketing should work.

Content for these buyers should answer the questions they ask from a distance:

  • What are the main neighbourhoods, and how do they differ in feel, housing type and price bracket?
  • What is daily life like through the hot summer months compared with the mild winter season?
  • How do homeowners association rules, golf memberships and community amenities typically work?
  • What does the buying process look like in Arizona, in general terms, and who else is involved?
  • How can they view homes and attend appointments remotely before travelling?

Answer these honestly and in general terms, and point readers to their own adviser for legal, tax or lending questions. Guides like this build trust over a long buying cycle and give you a reason to stay in touch by email.

Paid campaigns can support the same audience. Within platform housing rules, you can promote relocation guides and neighbourhood content to broad audiences, then retarget people who engage. Because the cycle is long, we measure guide downloads, email sign-ups and enquiry form submissions rather than expecting instant sales.

Measuring what works

Real estate leads vary hugely in quality, so measurement matters. We set up tracking for calls, valuation requests, viewing requests and email sign-ups, and where possible connect it to your CRM so you can see which channels bring leads that actually progress. Monthly reports show spend, leads, cost per lead and what we changed, in plain English.

Over time, that data shows which neighbourhoods, campaigns and pages bring the strongest seller and buyer enquiries. We then shift budget and content effort towards them, and away from channels that bring volume without quality. You keep ownership of every ad account, analytics property and piece of content, so the knowledge stays with your business whatever happens.
