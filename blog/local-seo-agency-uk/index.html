Local SEO Agency UK: What a Local SEO Agency Does and How to Choose the Right One

A local SEO agency helps UK businesses rank in Google Maps and local search via Google Business Profile, location pages and reviews. Costs and how to choose.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Local SEO agencies focus on your Google Business Profile, location pages, citations, reviews and local links.
  • Multi-location and service-area businesses need a clear plan for location pages that avoids thin, duplicated content.
  • Choose an agency that reports on calls, enquiries and bookings, not just map rankings, and that leaves you owning your profiles.

A local SEO agency helps businesses appear when people nearby search for what they offer, in Google Maps, the local map pack and normal search results. The work centres on your Google Business Profile, location and service pages, consistent business details, reviews and local links.

For UK businesses that serve a town, city or region, local search is often the most valuable channel there is, because searchers are close by and ready to act. This guide explains what a local SEO agency does, how the work differs for single and multi-location businesses, what it costs and how to choose one. For a do-it-yourself overview, see our local SEO guide.

What a local SEO agency UK businesses hire actually does

Local SEO combines several areas of work. A capable agency covers all of them and explains which matter most for your business.

  • Google Business Profile management. Categories, services, products, opening hours, photos, posts, questions and review responses. Our Google Business Profile guide covers this in detail.
  • Location and service pages. Pages that clearly explain what you do and where, with genuinely local information.
  • Citations and consistency. Making sure your name, address and phone number match across directories, industry sites and social profiles.
  • Reviews. A process for asking customers for honest reviews and responding to them, within Google's rules.
  • Local links and mentions. Relevant coverage from local news, partners, associations and community organisations.
  • Technical basics. Mobile speed, LocalBusiness schema, crawlability and click-to-call tracking.

How local rankings work

Google describes local results as being based mainly on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. In practical terms:

FactorWhat it meansWhat an agency can influence
RelevanceHow well your business matches the searchCategories, services, website content
DistanceHow close you are to the searcher or the place searchedLittle, beyond accurate address and service areas
ProminenceHow well known and trusted your business isReviews, links, mentions, overall SEO strength

Because distance is largely fixed, an honest agency will explain that you are unlikely to appear in the map pack for searchers far from your location. Your website can still rank in normal results for wider areas with strong, relevant pages.

Single-location vs multi-location businesses

A single shop, clinic or office needs a fully optimised profile, a strong homepage and service pages, and a steady flow of reviews. The work is focused and often quicker to show progress.

Multi-location businesses, such as restaurant groups, dental practices or estate agents with several branches, need more structure:

  • A separate Google Business Profile for each genuine location.
  • A dedicated page for each branch with its own address, hours, team information where you choose to share it, photos and local details.
  • Clear internal links from a locations page to each branch.
  • Consistent processes for reviews and updates across all branches.

Service-area businesses, such as trades, cleaners or mobile services, often hide their address and list the areas they cover. They need location pages that offer real value for each area, not copies of the same page with the town name swapped. Thin, duplicated location pages can do more harm than good. A useful test: would a local customer learn something from this page that they could not learn from any other page on your site?

Reviews: what an agency should and should not do

Reviews influence both rankings and whether people choose you. A good agency helps you build a simple, consistent way to ask every happy customer for feedback, such as a link in a follow-up email or a QR code at the till.

It should never write fake reviews, buy reviews, offer incentives for positive ones or filter customers so only happy ones are asked. These breach platform policies and UK consumer protection law, and the Competition and Markets Authority takes fake reviews seriously. Responding politely to negative reviews is part of the job too.

What the first three months usually look like

Local SEO work tends to follow a similar pattern at the start, which helps you judge whether an agency is on track.

  • Month one: audit and fixes. A review of your Google Business Profile, website, existing citations and competitors in the map pack. Incorrect categories, missing services, wrong opening hours and inconsistent addresses are corrected first, because they are quick and can matter a lot.
  • Month two: content. Service and location pages are improved or created, with genuinely local detail such as areas covered, parking, nearby landmarks and answers to common questions.
  • Month three onwards: prominence. A steady review process, regular profile updates, local partnerships and relevant links build trust over time.

Throughout, tracking should be in place for calls from your profile, website enquiries, direction requests and bookings. Without it, you can only judge progress by rankings, which move around and vary by where the searcher is standing. Map rankings checked from a single location can be misleading, so ask how the agency measures visibility across your area.

Your involvement matters too. Photos of your premises and team, quick answers to questions about services and prompt approval of new pages all speed things up.

How to choose a local SEO agency

  1. Ownership. You should own your Google Business Profile and website. The agency should be added as a manager, not the owner.
  2. Reporting. Ask how they track calls, direction requests, enquiries and bookings, not just map positions.
  3. Location pages. Ask how they would write pages for your areas without duplicating content.
  4. Reviews. Check their approach complies with Google's policies and UK law.
  5. Links. Ask where local links will come from. Avoid anyone selling bulk directory submissions or paid link packages.
  6. Contract terms. Check notice periods and what happens to work and access if you leave. Our guide to marketing agency contracts explains what to look for.

FAQs

What does a local SEO agency UK businesses use actually do?

It improves how a business appears in Google Maps and local search by managing its Google Business Profile, building location and service pages, keeping business details consistent, supporting reviews and earning local links.

How long does local SEO take?

SEO results typically take 3-6 months. Completing and correcting a Google Business Profile can help sooner, especially if it was previously incomplete.

Can a local SEO agency get me into the map pack?

No agency can guarantee it. Distance to the searcher plays a big part. An agency can improve relevance and prominence, which raises your chances for searches near your location.

Do I need a separate page for every town I serve?

Only where you can make each page genuinely useful. Near-identical pages with the town name swapped add little value and can weaken your site.

Is it allowed to ask customers for Google reviews?

Yes, you can ask customers for honest reviews. You must not offer incentives, ask only happy customers, or post fake reviews.

Should I hire a local SEO agency or do it myself?

Many small businesses can handle the basics themselves using a guide. An agency makes sense when you have several locations, a competitive area or limited time.
