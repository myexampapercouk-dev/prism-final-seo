WordPress Agency for Irish Businesses: Remote Builds from a UK Studio

Looking for a WordPress agency in Ireland? How we plan, build and secure WordPress sites for Irish businesses, with consent, accessibility and fees in pounds.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • WordPress suits Irish businesses that publish regularly, need flexible page editing or want a service site that the team can update without a developer.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Ireland; we build remotely on the same time zone, write in English and work with your team or a translator on any Irish-language pages.
  • Small websites are £1,000 to £2,000 and online shops £1,500 to £3,500, quoted in pounds with euros and other currencies on request; most sites take three to five weeks.

A WordPress agency for an Irish business should build a site that is fast, secure and easy for your own team to edit, with a structure that supports search and enquiries from the first day. WordPress is a strong choice when you publish news or guides, need flexible service pages or want to avoid being locked into a single builder.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that builds WordPress and ecommerce sites remotely for businesses across Europe. We have no office in Dublin, Cork, Galway, Limerick or anywhere else in Ireland. Our website and ecommerce development service runs by video call, shared designs and staging links, and because Ireland and the UK share a time zone, working hours line up exactly. We write in English, and any Irish-language pages come from your team or a translator.

When WordPress suits an Irish business

Ireland has a broad mix of professional services, technology and software companies, hospitality and tourism, retail, food and drink producers, education providers and charities. WordPress tends to fit well when:

  • You publish often. News, guides, events or case pages are easy to add and organise.
  • You need many service or location pages. Templates keep pages consistent while letting each one target its own search terms.
  • Several people edit the site. User roles let staff update content without risking the design.
  • You want full ownership. The site can be moved between hosts and developers without starting again.

If you mainly sell products online, Shopify is often the better fit, and our post on Shopify for Irish businesses covers that route. For a broader view of the platform, see our WordPress agency guide.

Ireland, the UK or both

Many Irish businesses sell into Northern Ireland and Great Britain as well as the Republic. That raises a few practical questions early on: whether to use a .ie domain, a .com or both; whether prices show in euros, pounds or both; and whether delivery, returns or service areas differ between markets. If you target more than one country, we plan the structure so each market has clear pages rather than one confusing mix. Our guide to international SEO explains how domains, folders and hreflang tags work.

Some organisations, particularly in education, public-facing services and culture, also publish pages in Irish. Prism writes English copy in-house. For Irish-language pages we give your team or a translator a brief for each page and check the finished content in the build, with the language switcher sending visitors to the matching page rather than the home page.

Editing the site after launch

One of the main reasons to choose WordPress is that your own team can keep the site up to date. That only works if the editing experience is set up with care. We build pages with the block editor and a set of reusable patterns, such as service sections, call-to-action panels, contact blocks, opening times and frequently asked questions, so staff can create new pages that match the design without touching code.

We also lock the parts of the layout that should not change, such as headers, footers and colour choices, and give each person a user role that fits what they need to do. A short recorded handover covers adding posts, editing pages, replacing images and checking that new pages have a sensible title and meta description. The aim is that everyday changes, from opening hours to new service pages, never need a developer.

Security, hosting and maintenance

WordPress is safe when it is looked after properly. Most problems come from outdated plugins, weak passwords and cheap hosting rather than from WordPress itself. Every build we deliver includes:

  • A small, well-maintained set of plugins chosen for the job.
  • Strong admin accounts with two-factor login and sensible user roles.
  • Automatic backups stored away from the main server.
  • Hosting matched to your traffic, with a server location in Europe where data rules make that sensible.
  • A clear plan for updates, either handled by your team or by us.

Our guides to WordPress security and WordPress hosting go into more detail on both.

Consent, privacy and accessibility

Irish websites fall under the GDPR for personal data, and the EU ePrivacy rules mean analytics and marketing cookies normally need consent before they load. We build a consent banner that is clear and quick to use, and connect it to analytics so tags only fire once consent is given.

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing products and services, including a wide range of online shops and booking services, with an exemption for microenterprises providing services. Building to WCAG AA, the level referenced by the harmonised standard EN 301 549, is good practice either way. Our European Accessibility Act guide explains the basics.

We design and build these pages and features, but legal wording and final approval rest with your legal adviser. Requirements change, so check current official guidance before launch.

Common WordPress problems we see

  • Too many plugins. Each one adds weight and another thing to update.
  • Page builders stacked on top of themes. Pages become slow and hard to edit.
  • No one responsible for updates. Sites drift out of date until something breaks.
  • Hosting chosen on price alone. Slow servers hurt both visitors and search.
  • Old URLs not redirected after a redesign. Rankings and links built over years are lost.

If you are replacing an older site, our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings explains how we protect existing traffic.
