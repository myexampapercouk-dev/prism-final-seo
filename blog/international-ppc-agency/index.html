International PPC Agency: Running Google Ads Across European Markets

What an international PPC agency does for European businesses: campaign structure by country and language, budgets, tracking, consent and how to choose one.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Multi-country Google Ads works best with campaigns split by country or language, so budgets, bids, ad copy and reporting match each market.
  • Keywords and ads should be researched and written in each language, with landing pages in the same language, rather than translated from English.
  • Consent and tracking rules differ in practice across the EU, so measurement needs a consent-aware set-up checked against current official guidance.

An international PPC agency plans and manages paid search and shopping campaigns across several countries and languages, so each market gets its own keywords, ads, budget and reporting. For a business selling into Ireland, the Netherlands, Germany, France, Spain or the Nordics, that structure is what stops one strong market from quietly funding several weak ones.

We are a UK-based studio and manage paid media remotely for clients across Europe. This guide explains how we approach multi-country Google Ads and Microsoft Ads accounts, what it costs to have them managed, and the questions to ask any agency before handing over your account.

What an international PPC agency actually does

The day-to-day work looks like any good PPC management, multiplied by markets and languages. The core tasks include:

  • Market planning. Deciding which countries to start with, based on demand, margins, delivery and your capacity to serve customers.
  • Account structure. Separating campaigns by country or language so budgets and performance can be controlled.
  • Keyword research in each language. Finding the terms people really use, including local spellings and product names.
  • Ad copy and assets. Writing or reviewing ads with fluent speakers, and using local currency, delivery and returns messages.
  • Shopping feeds. Setting up product feeds with the right language, currency and country targeting.
  • Tracking and consent. Making sure conversions are recorded accurately and lawfully in every market.
  • Reporting by market. Showing cost, conversions and return per country, not just account totals.

Our paid media service covers Google Ads, Microsoft Ads, Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok and YouTube, so cross-channel campaigns can be planned together.

Structuring a multi-country Google Ads account

There are a few common ways to structure an account across Europe. The right one depends on how different your markets are and how much budget each can support.

Campaigns per countrySeparate campaigns for each country, each with its own budget and languageMarkets with different margins, prices or deliveryMore campaigns to manage; small markets may lack data
Campaigns per languageOne campaign targets all countries sharing a language, such as German for Germany and AustriaSimilar offers across countries that share a languageHarder to see and control spend by country

For most businesses we start with campaigns per country in the main markets and a grouped campaign for smaller test markets, then split them out as data builds. Location settings matter here: targeting people in a country rather than people interested in it usually prevents wasted spend. Our guide to Google Ads location targeting explains those options.

Language, keywords and landing pages

Google Ads targets by the languages a user's settings suggest they understand, and the ad itself should be written in the language of the landing page. Translating English keywords word for word rarely works. A German searcher may use a compound noun, a French searcher may use a different everyday term, and a Dutch searcher may type the English product name.

A sensible workflow is:

  1. Build keyword lists in each language with a fluent speaker or reviewer.
  2. Write ads natively, including local delivery promises, currency and returns information.
  3. Send traffic to landing pages in the same language, with prices in local currency where possible.
  4. Add negative keywords per language, because irrelevant searches vary by market. Our guide to negative keywords covers the method.

If your website is not yet available in a market's language, it may be better to start with English-speaking audiences, such as Ireland, or to fix the landing pages first. Sending French searchers to an English page usually wastes money.

Shopping and Performance Max in several countries

For ecommerce brands, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns often drive much of the revenue. Each target country needs a feed with the right language, currency, prices, tax and delivery information, and your website must show the same prices the feed does. Google Merchant Center lets you target several countries, but the details, such as which feed languages are accepted where, need checking against Google's current help pages.

Performance Max campaigns can work across markets, but mixing very different countries in one campaign makes it hard to see where the money goes. We usually separate them by country or by country group. Our guides to Merchant Center set-up and Performance Max explain the basics.

Tracking, consent and measurement across the EU

Measurement is often the weakest part of a European account. EU GDPR and ePrivacy rules affect how tracking cookies and similar technologies can be used, and regulators in different countries have issued their own guidance. Google also has requirements for consent signals when advertising to users in the European Economic Area. Check current official guidance and Google's own policies, and take legal advice if you are unsure.

In practice, you will want a consent management platform that works in every language version of your site, consent mode configured correctly, and conversion tracking tested in each market. Our guides to conversion tracking and cookie banner design cover the set-up side.

Budgets and bidding by market

Costs per click and conversion rates vary between countries, and so do your margins after delivery and returns. Setting one target across Europe usually means some markets are starved while others overspend. We set budgets and bidding targets per market, based on what a sale or lead is worth there, and review them as data builds.

Small test markets often need time and budget before automated bidding has enough data. During that phase, simpler bidding and close monitoring tend to work better than aggressive targets.

Microsoft Ads in Europe

Microsoft Ads reaches searchers on Bing and partner sites and is worth testing in many European markets, particularly for B2B and older audiences. It can usually import campaigns from Google Ads, but language, location and feed settings still need checking. See our guide to Microsoft Ads management.

How to choose an international PPC agency

  • Ask how they would structure your account by country and language, and why.
  • Ask who writes and checks ads in each language.
  • Check that you will own the ad accounts, Merchant Center and analytics properties.
  • Ask how consent and tracking will be handled in the EU.
  • Ask to see a sample report broken down by market.
  • Agree notice periods and what happens to your data if you leave.

Be wary of anyone who promises fixed results in a new market before seeing your data. A good agency will explain how it plans to test, what it needs from you, such as translated landing pages and accurate margins, and how decisions about budget will be made as each market develops.

FAQs

What does an international PPC agency do?

It plans, builds and manages paid search and shopping campaigns across several countries and languages. That includes market planning, account structure, keyword research and ads in each language, feeds, tracking, consent-aware measurement and reporting by market.

Should I run one campaign for all of Europe?

Usually not. A single campaign makes it hard to control spend and see which countries perform. Most accounts work better with campaigns per country or per language, with smaller test markets grouped until they have enough data.

Do I need ads in every local language?

If you are targeting people who search in that language, yes, and the landing page should match. In some markets you can test English ads with English-speaking audiences first, but local language campaigns usually need local language pages.

How much does international PPC management cost?

Our retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month, with ad spend paid separately. Multi-country accounts usually need Growth or Scale. Prices are in pounds sterling, and other currencies are quoted on request.

Can a UK agency manage ads for a European business?

Yes. We are a UK-based studio working remotely with clients across Europe, using shared access to your accounts, video calls and regular reports. You keep ownership of your ad accounts and data throughout.

How is tracking affected by EU consent rules?

Consent rules affect which cookies and tags can run and when. You will need a working consent tool and correctly configured consent signals in each market. Check current official and platform guidance, and take legal advice where needed.
