A Remarketing Guide for UK Businesses: Winning Back Website Visitors

Remarketing shows ads to people who have already visited your site or engaged with you. This UK guide covers how it works, platforms, consent and setup.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Remarketing reaches people who already know you, such as site visitors, basket abandoners and past customers, so it usually needs a different message from cold ads.
  • Segment audiences by how close people came to buying, and cap frequency so ads help rather than irritate.
  • In the UK, remarketing relies on cookies and customer data, so consent under PECR and UK GDPR must be handled properly.

Remarketing means showing ads to people who have already interacted with your business, such as website visitors, people who added something to their basket, or past customers, to bring them back to finish what they started. Because these people already know you, remarketing often converts more efficiently than advertising to strangers, as long as it is targeted sensibly and handled within UK privacy rules.

You may also hear it called retargeting. The terms are used interchangeably in practice. This remarketing guide explains how it works, which platforms offer it, how to segment audiences, and what UK businesses need to know about consent.

How remarketing works

When someone visits your website, a tag such as the Google tag or Meta pixel can, with their consent, add them to an audience list. You can then show ads to that list on platforms such as Google, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok. You can also upload customer lists, where you have the right to use the data, which the platforms match against their own users.

Most visitors leave a site without buying or enquiring. Remarketing gives you a second chance to remind them, answer an objection or offer a reason to return.

Search remarketing

On Google, you can apply your audience lists to search campaigns. That lets you bid differently, or show different ads, when a past visitor searches again. It is a quiet but useful technique, especially in sectors where people compare several suppliers before deciding.

Dynamic remarketing

For online shops, dynamic remarketing shows people the specific products they viewed, pulled from your product feed. It is available on Google and Meta and is often one of the more efficient campaign types for ecommerce. Our Google Shopping ads guide explains product feeds, which dynamic remarketing relies on.

Segmenting your audiences

The biggest remarketing mistake is treating every past visitor the same. Someone who glanced at your blog is very different from someone who reached checkout. Segment by how close people came to acting.

Basket or checkout abandonersStarted buying but did not finishReminder, delivery or returns reassurance

Recent convertersJust bought or enquiredOften best excluded for a while

Membership duration matters too. A seven-day list of checkout abandoners is usually far warmer than a ninety-day list of all visitors, and deserves a different budget and message.

Frequency and avoiding the "following me around" problem

Remarketing can annoy people when the same ad appears constantly, or when someone keeps seeing a product they already bought. To keep it helpful:

  • Cap frequency where the platform allows, or watch it closely where it does not.
  • Exclude recent buyers from product reminders.
  • Rotate creative so the message changes over time.
  • Set sensible list durations that match how long your buying cycle really is.
  • Avoid sensitive targeting. Platforms restrict remarketing in sensitive categories such as health, and you should not imply you know something personal about the viewer.

Consent, cookies and UK privacy rules

Remarketing in the UK depends on cookies, pixels and sometimes personal data, so it sits under PECR and UK GDPR. In practical terms:

  • Non-essential cookies, including advertising tags, generally need consent before they fire. Your cookie banner should offer a genuine choice.
  • Your privacy notice should explain that you use advertising tags and remarketing.
  • If you upload customer lists, make sure you have a lawful basis to use the data in this way.
  • Google's Consent Mode can help your tags respect consent choices while still giving the platform modelled data.

This is general information rather than legal advice. Check the ICO's guidance and, where needed, get advice for your specific setup. Our GA4 setup guide touches on consent configuration for analytics.

Setting up your first remarketing campaign

  1. Install tags properly, linked to your consent banner, and check they record visits and key actions.
  2. Build audiences for your most valuable segments first, such as basket abandoners or service page visitors.
  3. Wait for lists to fill. Platforms need a minimum audience size before ads can serve, so smaller sites may need time.
  4. Write tailored ads for each segment, rather than reusing cold-audience creative.
  5. Send people somewhere useful, such as the product they viewed or a focused landing page.
  6. Measure incrementally. Remarketing can claim credit for sales that would have happened anyway, so compare against your overall results rather than trusting platform figures alone.

What remarketing costs

There is no fixed price. Remarketing audiences are usually small, so spend is often modest compared with prospecting campaigns, but costs depend on the platform, auction and how many people are on your lists. Treat any cost figure you see online as variable.

Writing remarketing ads that work

Remarketing ads speak to people who already know something about you, so they should not simply repeat the message they saw first time. Think about why someone left without acting, then address that reason directly.

  • Uncertain about delivery or returns? Explain your delivery options, such as Royal Mail, Evri or DPD, and your returns policy clearly.
  • Worried about price? Show what is included, payment options such as Klarna or Clearpay if you offer them, or the value compared with alternatives.
  • Not sure you are trustworthy? Use genuine reviews, guarantees you actually offer, or details about how you work.
  • Simply got distracted? A short, friendly reminder with a direct link back is often enough.

Avoid relying on discounts as the default answer. If every abandoner receives a code, some customers will learn to leave their basket on purpose. Use offers selectively and test whether they truly add sales or only reduce margin.

Remarketing for service businesses and B2B

Remarketing is not only for online shops. Service businesses and B2B companies often have longer decision cycles, where a prospect visits several times before getting in touch. In those cases, remarketing works well as a way to stay visible and helpful during the research period.

Useful approaches include showing a practical guide to people who viewed a service page, sharing case-study style explanations of how you work (without invented results), or inviting people to book a call once they have visited your pricing page. Keep the tone helpful rather than pushy, and match list durations to how long your buyers usually take to decide.

For businesses with lower traffic, combine website audiences with other sources such as video viewers or people who engaged with your social profiles. This helps lists reach the size platforms need, while still focusing on people who have shown some interest.

FAQs

What is this remarketing guide for UK businesses about?

It explains how to show ads to people who already visited your website or engaged with you, which platforms offer remarketing, how to segment audiences, and how UK consent rules affect setup.

Is remarketing the same as retargeting?

In practice, yes. Google tends to say remarketing and many other platforms say retargeting, but both describe advertising to people who have previously interacted with your business.

Do I need cookie consent for remarketing in the UK?

Generally, yes. Advertising tags are non-essential, so under PECR you usually need consent before they set cookies. Your cookie banner and privacy notice should reflect this. Check ICO guidance for your situation.

How big does my audience need to be?

Each platform sets its own minimum list size before ads will serve, and these can change. Smaller sites may need longer list durations or broader segments to reach the threshold.

How long should people stay on a remarketing list?

Match it to your buying cycle. Quick purchases may suit lists of a week or two, while considered purchases, such as B2B services or furniture, may justify longer durations.

Can remarketing work for a small business?

Yes, if the site has enough traffic to build audiences. Small, well-segmented campaigns with tailored messages and frequency control are often a sensible addition once core search or social campaigns are running.
