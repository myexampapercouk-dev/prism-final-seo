What real estate marketing in Vancouver involves

Vancouver's property market spans downtown condos, family homes across the city and suburbs, new developments in growing areas such as Burnaby, Surrey and Richmond, rental buildings, commercial property and recreational property beyond the city. Buyers and sellers usually research online long before they contact anyone, comparing neighbourhoods, prices, schools, transit and agents.

That means the businesses that win enquiries tend to be the ones with useful information, fast websites, a consistent presence in search and social, and quick, organised responses. A solid real estate marketing plan usually includes:

  • A clear website with listings or project pages, agent profiles, neighbourhood guides and simple contact options.
  • Local and neighbourhood SEO, so you appear for searches about specific areas, property types and questions.
  • Paid search to capture people actively looking to buy, sell or value a property.
  • Paid social on Meta, YouTube or TikTok to build awareness for developments, open houses and personal brands.
  • Lead tracking and follow-up, so every enquiry is recorded, routed to the right agent and answered quickly, whether it arrives by form, phone, email or social message.

Our paid media service covers Google, Meta, TikTok, YouTube and LinkedIn advertising, and we combine it with web design and SEO so every channel points to pages that convert.

Websites for Vancouver agents, brokerages and developers

A real estate website should make it easy for someone to understand what you offer, trust you and get in touch. For agents and brokerages, that usually means agent profiles, neighbourhood pages, a valuation or consultation request, recent activity described in your own words, and listings presented through the feed or tools your brokerage and board permit. For developers, a dedicated project site with floor plans, location details, a registration form and clear next steps tends to work best.

Speed and mobile design matter, because many people browse property on their phones. Our guide to estate agent website design covers the features that matter most, and our article on web design in Vancouver explains how a remote build works. Small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and typically take three to five weeks.

Vancouver is a multilingual city, and many agents serve clients who prefer Chinese, Punjabi, Korean, Farsi, Tagalog or other languages, as well as French. Prism writes in English. We build the site structure and English content, and content in other languages is written with your team or a professional translator so that meaning and tone are right.

Neighbourhood SEO and local search

Property searches are highly local. People search for homes, condos or agents in specific neighbourhoods such as Kitsilano, Mount Pleasant or the North Shore, and they ask detailed questions about strata, schools, transit and prices. A strong SEO plan covers:

  • Neighbourhood pages with genuinely useful information, not thin copies with the area name swapped.
  • A complete Google Business Profile for each office or agent where allowed, with accurate details and steady reviews.
  • Helpful guides answering buyer, seller and investor questions in plain language.
  • Technical foundations, including speed, structured data and clean handling of listing pages that come and go.

Our guide to SEO in Vancouver covers the local search picture, and our guide to local SEO in Canada explains the country-wide basics. SEO results typically build over three to six months, so it pairs well with paid campaigns in the meantime.

Google and Meta ads for property

Paid search captures people who are already searching, such as someone looking for a home valuation or a presale condo in a particular area. Our guide to Google Ads in Vancouver explains how search campaigns work locally. Property advertising has its own platform rules. Google and Meta apply special policies to housing ads in some countries, which can limit targeting options such as age, gender or postcode. We check the current policies for Canada before launch and build campaigns that work within them. We use AI-powered tools to speed up research, ad variations and reporting, but every ad is reviewed by a person before it runs.

Advertising and privacy rules in general terms

Real estate is a regulated profession in British Columbia, and marketing touches several areas of law. In general terms:

  • Real estate advertising rules: BC's real estate regulator sets rules for how licensees advertise, and your brokerage and real estate board may have their own requirements for listings and data use.
  • Privacy: BC's Personal Information Protection Act applies to many private sector organisations in the province, and PIPEDA can apply to commercial activity across provincial borders. Quebec's Law 25 matters if you market to people in Quebec.
  • CASL, Canada's anti-spam legislation, governs commercial emails and texts, including new listing alerts and newsletters.
  • Truthful advertising: claims about properties, prices and returns should be accurate under Canada's Competition Act.

We are not lawyers or real estate compliance advisers. Check the official guidance from the provincial regulator, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, BC's privacy commissioner and the CRTC, and take advice from your own adviser. Compliance approval for property advertising rests with you and your brokerage.
