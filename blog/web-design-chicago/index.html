Web Design Chicago: How Remote Web Design Works for Chicago Businesses

Need web design in Chicago? A remote studio can plan, design and build a fast, accessible site. See how the process works, what it costs and what to ask first.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A strong Chicago website depends on a clear brief, useful content and a fast, accessible build, not on the studio having an office in the Loop.
  • Chicago is usually six hours behind UK time, which gives a comfortable morning overlap for calls while the rest of the project runs in writing.
  • We are a UK-based studio working remotely with US clients; website prices are fixed in pounds and other currencies are quoted on request.

If you are searching for web design in Chicago, the result you get depends on the quality of the planning, design and build rather than where the studio sits. A remote web design studio can deliver a fast, well-structured website for a Chicago business, provided the project runs on a clear written brief, scheduled check-ins and thorough testing before launch.

We are a UK-based studio and we work remotely with US businesses. We do not have a Chicago office or any office in the US. This guide covers what a good website project should include, how a remote project runs day to day, what it costs, how long it takes and what to ask any web designer before you commit.

What web design in Chicago should cover

Chicago has a broad business base: professional and financial services, manufacturing and logistics, healthcare, hospitality, food and drink, education and a growing technology scene. Many Chicago firms sell to other businesses, which means longer buying cycles and visitors who want detail before they get in touch. A solid web design project usually includes:

  • Discovery: agreeing who the site is for, what they need to find and what you want them to do next.
  • Information architecture: a sitemap and page plan built around your services, sectors and the questions buyers ask.
  • Content planning: deciding early who writes which pages and what proof, such as case details you are able to share, will support them.
  • Visual design: layouts that reflect your brand and work on a phone first.
  • Build: a well-configured platform, typically WordPress for service businesses or Shopify for product sales.
  • Search foundations: page titles, headings, internal links, structured data and redirects from any old pages.
  • Accessibility and testing: checks across browsers, devices and keyboard use before launch.
  • Handover: logins, training and documentation so you own and control the site.

Our website and ecommerce development service follows this order. Our small business website checklist is a useful companion if you are preparing for a new build.

How a remote web design project runs

Chicago is usually six hours behind UK time, with a brief period each spring and autumn when the gap changes because the clocks move on different dates. That means the Chicago morning overlaps with our afternoon, which is when we hold calls. Between calls, everything happens in writing, so decisions are recorded and easy to find later.

  1. Brief and discovery call: goals, audiences, pages, features and integrations such as forms, booking or a CRM.
  2. Proposal: a fixed scope, price and timeline, with exclusions written down.
  3. Sitemap and wireframes: the structure is agreed before visual design begins.
  4. Design: key page templates shared for comments, usually in one or two rounds.
  5. Build on a staging site: a private, working version you can click through and comment on.
  6. Testing and launch: forms, speed, redirects, analytics and accessibility checked before go-live.
  7. Aftercare: fixes, training and optional ongoing support.

Recorded walkthrough videos help a lot across time zones: you can watch a five-minute tour of a design at a time that suits you and reply in writing. Our guide to website staging and testing explains why the review stage is where most problems are caught.

How long a Chicago website takes

A typical brochure or service website takes three to five weeks from agreed brief to launch. An ecommerce build usually takes five to twelve weeks, depending on the size of the catalogue, payments and any custom features. Late content is the most common cause of delay, so we agree who is writing what at the very start. Our guide on how long it takes to build a website breaks down each stage.

Design choices for Chicago buyers

Many Chicago businesses sell to other companies or to professionals who research carefully before making contact. That shapes the design:

  • Clear service pages: one page per service, explaining who it is for, how it works and what happens next.
  • Sector pages: if you serve distinct industries, pages that speak to each one help both visitors and search engines.
  • Evidence you can share: accreditations you genuinely hold, process explanations and real photography build trust better than stock images.
  • Simple contact routes: short forms, a visible phone number if you take calls and a clear note of response times.
  • Speed and mobile layout: decision-makers often first visit on a phone, so the small-screen version must work properly.

For service businesses, WordPress is often the most practical platform because it is flexible and easy for your team to update. Our guide to WordPress for small business websites explains when it fits and when another option is better.

Replacing an existing site without losing search traffic

If your current site already brings in enquiries from search, a redesign has to protect that. That means mapping every old URL to its new equivalent, keeping valuable content, setting up redirects and checking Search Console after launch. Our guide to website redirects when relaunching walks through the process, and if you want ongoing search work after launch, our guide to SEO for Chicago businesses explains how that fits alongside a new site.

Accessibility and legal points

Website accessibility is an active legal topic for US businesses, and claims relating to inaccessible websites are brought across the country. We build to recognised accessibility good practice: sufficient colour contrast, keyboard navigation, descriptive alt text, labelled forms and a logical heading structure, all tested before launch. The legal position depends on federal and state law and on court decisions that develop over time, so please check current official guidance and speak to your own lawyer about your obligations. The same applies to privacy notices, consent for tracking and any rules specific to your sector.

Our guide to website accessibility covers the practical design and build steps, which apply equally to Chicago sites.

Questions to ask a Chicago web designer

  • Who will own the domain, hosting, files and analytics?
  • What is included in the fixed price, and how are extra requests handled?
  • How many design rounds are included?
  • Who writes the copy and who sources photography?
  • How will existing search rankings be protected during the switch?
  • How are accessibility, speed and forms tested before launch?
  • What does support cost after launch, and is it optional?

Clear, written answers to these questions are a good sign that a project will run smoothly, wherever the studio is based.
