Web design for a Barcelona business should give local, Spanish and international visitors a fast, clear website that explains what you offer and makes it easy to enquire, book or buy. That means planning languages, structure, content and legal pages together before any visual design begins.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio. We design and build websites remotely for Barcelona businesses through our website and e-commerce development service. This guide covers what a Barcelona website usually needs, how a remote project runs, and what it costs.

Web design Barcelona: what businesses usually need

Barcelona has a varied economy, with hospitality and tourism, design and fashion brands, technology start-ups, professional services, education, health and property all competing for attention online. Most of these businesses share a few needs.

  • Several languages. Many Barcelona sites serve Spanish and Catalan speakers, and often international visitors in English too.
  • Mobile-first pages. Most visitors arrive on phones, often while out in the city, so pages must load quickly and be easy to use with one hand.
  • Clear next steps. Booking, calling, messaging or buying should take as few steps as possible.
  • Search readiness. The site structure should be planned for SEO from the start, not fixed afterwards.
  • Legal basics. A legal notice, privacy policy and cookie consent that work properly.

Planning Spanish, Catalan and English versions

Language is usually the biggest planning decision for a Barcelona website. Some businesses need all three languages, some need Catalan and Spanish only, and some need English as the main language because they serve international customers. The right answer depends on who your customers are and who will write and maintain each version.

Each language should have its own URLs, translated navigation, titles and descriptions, and hreflang tags so search engines show the right version to the right visitor. A simple language switcher that keeps visitors on the equivalent page is better than one that sends them back to the homepage. Our guide to multilingual website design explains the options in more detail.

Prism writes in English. Spanish and Catalan content is written by your team or a professional translator, and a fluent speaker on your side approves it. We build the structure so each language can be updated independently.

How a remote website project runs

  1. Discovery. A call to agree goals, audiences, languages, pages and features, followed by a written scope.
  2. Content and structure. We plan the sitemap and page outlines, and you or your translator prepare the content for each language.
  3. Design. We design key pages and share them for comments in a single feedback document.
  4. Build. We build the site on a staging address so you can test it on your own devices.
  5. Launch. We set up redirects from any old site, connect analytics with consent, submit the sitemap and check everything works.

Small websites typically take 3 to 5 weeks and e-commerce sites 5 to 12 weeks, mostly depending on how quickly content and feedback arrive.

Cookies, privacy and accessibility for Spanish websites

Spanish websites must follow the GDPR and Spanish data protection law. Under the ePrivacy rules on cookies, non-essential cookies such as most analytics and advertising tags generally need consent before they load. That means a cookie banner that genuinely blocks those tags until a visitor chooses, with rejecting as easy as accepting. Our guide to cookie banner design explains how to make consent clear without hurting the visitor experience, and the same principles apply in Spain.

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many businesses that sell to consumers online, including e-commerce and online booking. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but accessible design helps every visitor. The usual reference is the harmonised standard EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA, covering colour contrast, keyboard use, form labels, alt text and clear headings. Our guide to the European Accessibility Act for websites explains the basics. These notes are general; check official Spanish and European guidance and ask your own adviser what applies to your business.

Speed and mobile performance

Many Barcelona visitors are tourists or busy locals on mobile data, so speed matters. We keep pages light, compress and resize images, avoid unnecessary scripts and plugins, and test on real phones. Faster pages are easier to use and also support search visibility. Our website speed optimisation guide covers the main techniques.

Websites for hospitality and tourism

Restaurants, hotels, guest houses, tour operators and experience providers need websites that make booking simple. That usually means clear menus or room details, real photos, opening hours, location information and a booking system that works on mobile. Where you use third-party booking or reservation tools, we connect them cleanly and make sure their cookies are covered by your consent banner. Direct booking pages can reduce reliance on commission-based platforms over time, as long as they are quick and trustworthy.

Online shops for Barcelona brands

Barcelona is home to many fashion, design, food and lifestyle brands that sell online across Spain and Europe. We build online shops on Shopify and WordPress with WooCommerce, depending on your products and how you want to manage them. Common needs include euro pricing, several languages, clear shipping and returns information, and a smooth checkout. Our guide to Shopify in Spain covers store setup in more depth.

Search engine optimisation from day one

A new website is the best moment to get SEO right. We plan one clear page per main service or product category, write descriptive titles and headings, add structured data and make sure every language version can be crawled and indexed. If you are replacing an older site, we map old URLs to new ones so existing rankings are protected. For ongoing search work after launch, our guide to SEO in Barcelona explains our approach, and businesses in the capital can read our guide to web design in Madrid.

Redesigning an existing Barcelona website

Many Barcelona businesses already have a website that looks dated, loads slowly or is hard to update. A redesign is a chance to fix the structure as well as the look. Before we change anything, we review which pages bring visitors and enquiries, which searches you already rank for and which language versions are actually used. We then keep the content that works, improve weak pages and remove pages that no longer serve a purpose, with redirects so visitors and search engines are not sent to dead ends.

We also check the technical basics that older sites often miss, such as missing language tags, cookie banners that load tracking before consent, forms without labels and images without alt text. Fixing these during a redesign is usually simpler and cheaper than fixing them later.

What you will need to provide

  • Content for each language, or access to the person or translator who will write it.
  • Photos and logos in good quality, with permission to use them.
  • Legal text such as your legal notice, privacy policy and terms, reviewed by your own adviser.
  • Access to your domain, hosting and any existing analytics or booking accounts.
  • One decision-maker who can approve designs and content so the project keeps moving.

FAQs

How much does web design in Barcelona cost?

Our small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and e-commerce sites £1,500 to £3,500. Prices are in pounds, and euros are quoted on request.

Do you have an office in Barcelona?

No. Prism Works Studio is based in the UK, has no office in Spain and works remotely through calls, shared documents and staging previews.

Can my website be in Catalan, Spanish and English?

Yes. We build multilingual sites with separate URLs and hreflang. Catalan and Spanish content comes from your team or a professional translator.

Does my Barcelona website need a cookie banner?

Generally yes, if it uses non-essential cookies such as analytics or advertising tags. Check official guidance and your adviser for your exact obligations.

Does the European Accessibility Act apply to my site?

It may if you sell products or services to consumers online. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt; ask your adviser to confirm.

How long does a new website take?

Small websites typically take 3 to 5 weeks and online shops 5 to 12 weeks, depending on content, languages and feedback speed.
