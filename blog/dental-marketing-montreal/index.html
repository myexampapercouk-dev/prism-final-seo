How patients choose a dentist in Montreal

Montreal is a city of neighbourhoods. A patient in the Plateau, Rosemont, Verdun, NDG, Saint-Laurent or the West Island usually wants a clinic close to home, work or a metro station. That makes the map results on Google the most important piece of real estate for most clinics. People search for a general dentist, an emergency appointment, a hygienist cleaning, children's dentistry, implants, aligners or whitening, then compare a handful of clinics on reviews, photos, opening hours and how easy it is to book.

The city is also bilingual. Many patients search in French, many search in English, and plenty switch between the two. Quebec's language rules also set expectations for how businesses present themselves in French. We write in English only. French content for your site and ads should come from your own team or a professional translator, and you should check your language obligations with official Quebec guidance or your own adviser. What we do is structure the site so that both language versions are clean, linked correctly and easy for search engines to understand.

A clinic website that turns visits into bookings

Your website does two jobs. It reassures patients that your clinic is modern, friendly and competent, and it makes booking the obvious next step. Most patients arrive on a phone, often on the move, so speed and clarity matter more than visual effects.

We build dental websites with:

  • A separate page for each core treatment, written in plain language your dentists approve.
  • Click-to-call, online booking links and opening hours visible on every page.
  • Clear information on location, parking, metro access and accessibility.
  • Pages for your dentists and hygienists that your clinic writes or signs off.
  • Clean bilingual structure so English and French pages point to each other correctly.
  • Fast, accessible, mobile-first layouts and secure hosting.

A small clinic website costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks. Local SEO and your Google Business Profile

For most clinics, local SEO brings the steadiest flow of new patients. The aim is to appear in the map pack when someone nearby searches for a dentist, and in the organic results for treatment-specific searches.

  • Google Business Profile: correct primary and secondary categories, accurate hours including holidays, real photos of the clinic and team, services listed, and a booking link.
  • Reviews: a simple, compliant process for asking patients for reviews, and polite, private-information-free replies to every review.
  • Treatment pages: pages for the treatments you want more of, with the neighbourhoods you genuinely serve mentioned naturally rather than stuffed in.
  • Consistent listings: the same name, address and phone number across directories and health listing sites.
  • Technical health: speed, structured data for a dental clinic, correct language tags and clean internal links.

Google Ads and Meta Ads for dental clinics

Paid search is useful for emergency dentistry, for launching a new clinic and for higher-value treatments such as implants and aligners. We set up tightly themed campaigns, write ads that describe the service honestly, send each campaign to a matching treatment page, and track calls and booking requests so you can see what each pound or dollar of spend produces. Budgets are set with you and the ad spend is paid directly to Google, separately from our fee.

Meta Ads on Facebook and Instagram work well for reaching parents, new residents and people within a set distance of your clinic, usually with offers such as new-patient check-ups or information about aligner consultations. Health-related advertising has platform policies on targeting and claims, so we keep targeting broad and wording factual. See our guides to Google Ads in Montreal and Meta Ads in Montreal for detail on how we run campaigns in the city.

Staying within the rules on dental advertising

Dentists in Quebec are regulated professionals, and advertising by health professionals is subject to rules on claims, testimonials, pricing, before-and-after images and comparisons with other clinics. Privacy law also applies to patient information, including anything a patient shares through a contact form.

We give general marketing information only. We are not legal or regulatory advisers. Before anything goes live, your clinic should check it against guidance from your professional regulator and, where needed, your own adviser. Approval of all content, ads and patient-facing wording rests with the clinic. In practice we help by:

  • Writing factual treatment descriptions without promises of outcomes.
  • Avoiding patient testimonials and before-and-after images unless your clinic confirms they are allowed and consented.
  • Keeping contact forms short and telling patients not to send medical details through them.
  • Keeping a record of what was approved and when.
