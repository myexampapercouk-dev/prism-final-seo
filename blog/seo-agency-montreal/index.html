Choosing an SEO Agency for a Montreal Business: Bilingual Search Done Properly

Hiring an SEO agency in Montreal? How bilingual SEO works for English and French pages, what a sound plan covers, what it costs and what to ask before signing.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • SEO in Montreal usually means two markets in one city: French and English searchers use different queries, so each language needs its own keyword research and properly written pages.
  • Good bilingual SEO depends on technical setup as much as words, with separate URLs per language, hreflang tags and consistent business details in both languages.
  • SEO results typically take three to six months, so agree priorities, reporting and account ownership before any retainer starts.

The right SEO agency for a Montreal business is one that can research and plan for French and English searchers separately, set up a bilingual site so Google indexes both versions correctly, and show how that work turns into enquiries or sales. Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that handles SEO remotely for Canadian clients; we have no office or staff in Montreal or anywhere else in Quebec. We write in English, and French pages are written with your team or a professional translator.

This guide explains what bilingual SEO involves, how remote SEO works on Eastern Time, what a sound plan should include, how pricing is structured and the questions to ask before you hire anyone.

Why SEO in Montreal is different

Most city SEO plans assume one language. Montreal rarely works that way. A clinic on the Plateau, a software firm in Mile End or a retailer in the West Island may serve customers who search in French, in English or in both, and they do not simply translate their queries word for word. A French speaker looking for an accountant may use different terms, and different search volumes apply, from an English speaker looking for the same service.

That has practical consequences:

  • Keyword research per language. French and English keyword lists should be built separately, not by translating one into the other. Our keyword research guide explains the general method.
  • Pages written for each audience. Machine-translated pages tend to read badly and rank poorly. French content should be written or carefully adapted by fluent writers who know your market.
  • Technical setup. Each language needs its own URL, with hreflang tags so Google shows the right version to the right searcher.
  • Local listings in both languages. Google Business Profile details, categories and review responses should reflect how your customers actually speak.

French-language rules: a general note

Quebec has specific laws on the use of French in business, including on websites and commercial content, and these were strengthened in recent years. In broad terms, businesses serving Quebec customers should expect French to be available and given due prominence. The details depend on your business and situation, and they change, so check current official guidance from the Quebec government or take legal advice. As a studio, we plan the site structure and SEO for both languages, while French copy is written or approved by your team or a translator.

Quebec's private-sector privacy rules have also been updated in recent years, with implications for analytics, cookies and consent. Again, check current official guidance or take legal advice; we set tracking up transparently and follow the consent approach your adviser agrees.

How remote SEO works on Eastern Time

The UK is usually five hours ahead of Montreal. That fits SEO well: research, technical fixes and drafting happen during the UK day, and calls are booked for a UK afternoon, which is a Montreal morning.

  • Discovery call. We agree goals, priority services and areas, and how leads are measured in each language.
  • Audit. A technical, content and local review of your current site, including how the two language versions are linked.
  • Plan. A prioritised list of fixes and pages for the first months.
  • Delivery. Technical fixes, English content, briefs for French content, local profile updates and outreach.
  • Reporting. A monthly report and an optional short call.

Accounts such as Google Analytics, Search Console and your Google Business Profile stay in your business's name throughout. If you are weighing up a studio outside Canada, our guide to outsourcing SEO to an overseas agency sets out the questions and risks.

Common bilingual SEO mistakes

  • One page serving both languages. Mixing French and English on the same URL confuses searchers and search engines alike.
  • Missing or broken hreflang. Without correct tags, Google may show the English page to French searchers, or treat the versions as duplicates.
  • Untranslated details. Menus, buttons, forms, error messages and image alt text left in one language undermine an otherwise good page.
  • Uneven content. A full English site paired with a thin French one rarely performs well in French search.
  • Automatic language redirects. Forcing visitors to a language based on their browser can stop people, and search engines, reaching the version they want. A clear language switch is usually safer.

What to prepare before an SEO project starts

Before the discovery call, it helps to gather access to your website, Google Analytics, Search Console and Google Business Profile; a list of the services or products that matter most to revenue; the areas of Montreal and Quebec you genuinely serve; and details of who on your team, or which translator, will handle French content. A note of any past SEO work, including links bought or pages removed, also saves time during the audit.

Questions to ask before hiring

  1. How will you research French and English keywords separately?
  2. Who writes or checks the French content, and how is quality assured?
  3. How will you set up and test hreflang between the two versions?
  4. Will all accounts and content remain in my business's name?
  5. What will the first three months include, and how will I see progress by language?
  6. What happens if I want to change scope or stop?

FAQs

How do I choose an SEO agency in Montreal?

Look for an agency that treats French and English as separate markets, explains its technical approach to bilingual sites, keeps accounts in your name and reports on enquiries as well as rankings.

Does Prism Works Studio have an office in Montreal?

No. We are a UK-based studio working with Canadian clients remotely through video calls, shared documents and dashboards, with calls booked for Montreal mornings.

Do you write French SEO content?

We write in English. French pages are written with your team or a professional translator, working from keyword research and briefs we prepare, and we handle the structure and technical setup.

Is it enough to translate my English pages into French?

Usually not. French searchers often use different terms, so French pages should be based on their own keyword research and written to read naturally, not translated word for word.

How much does an SEO agency in Montreal cost?

Our retainers are Launch at £350 a month, Growth at £750 a month and Scale at £1,500 a month, with other currencies quoted on request. Translation or French copywriting is a separate cost.

How long does SEO take for a bilingual site?

Results typically take three to six months to show. Bilingual sites can take a little longer at the start, because technical setup and content are needed in two languages.
