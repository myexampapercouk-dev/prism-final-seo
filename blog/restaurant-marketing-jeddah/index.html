Restaurant Marketing in Jeddah: Websites, Local SEO and Social Ads

Restaurant marketing in Jeddah: fast menu websites, local SEO, Google Maps and Meta or TikTok ads to fill tables, by a UK-based remote studio, no Saudi office.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 13 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Restaurant marketing in Jeddah works when a quick mobile website, a strong Google Business Profile and well-targeted social ads all lead diners to book, order or visit.
  • Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that markets Jeddah restaurants and cafes remotely and has no office in Jeddah or anywhere in Saudi Arabia.
  • Smaller websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and retainers start at £350 a month; Saudi riyal pricing is quoted on request and Arabic copy comes from your team or a translator.

Restaurant marketing in Jeddah is about being the place people find when they search for somewhere to eat nearby, and the place they remember when they scroll their social feeds. Prism Works Studio builds restaurant websites, improves local search visibility and runs Google, Meta and TikTok ads for Jeddah restaurants, cafes and dining groups, working remotely from the UK.

We are a UK-based web design and digital marketing studio. We have no office in Jeddah or anywhere in Saudi Arabia. We work through video calls, shared previews and written monthly reports. We write in English; Arabic menus, pages and ad copy are written with your team or a translator, and we build everything around that copy.

Who we work with in Jeddah

Jeddah has a lively food scene, from seafront dining and family restaurants to speciality coffee shops and late-night spots. Diners include local families, visitors to the city, pilgrims passing through and business travellers. We support:

  • Independent restaurants that want more bookings and walk-ins.
  • Cafes and speciality coffee shops building a regular local following.
  • Multi-branch groups that need one website with clear pages for each branch.
  • Cloud kitchens and delivery-led brands that want more direct orders.
  • Hotel restaurants that need their own identity beyond the hotel website.

How diners in Jeddah choose where to eat

Most diners make a quick decision on a phone. They search for a type of food or a district, glance at the map results, check photos and reviews, look at the menu and then decide. Others discover a place on social media and save it for later. Your marketing needs to work at each of these moments: when they search, when they scroll and when they check your menu before leaving the house.

That is why we focus on three things first: a strong Google Business Profile, a fast menu website and social ads that show your food at its best. Our general guide to restaurant marketing explains the same thinking in more depth.

Restaurant websites that work on a phone

A restaurant website has a few simple jobs. It should load quickly, show the menu clearly, make it obvious how to book or order and tell people where you are and when you are open. Many restaurant sites fail at these basics because the menu is a slow PDF, the booking link is hidden or the opening hours are out of date.

We build menus as real web pages rather than PDFs, so they load fast and can be found in search. We add clear buttons for your booking tool or ordering system and keep branch details consistent. For bilingual sites, our guide to bilingual Arabic and English websites explains how we handle both languages properly. Our web design in Jeddah post covers the wider service in the city.

Local SEO and Google Maps

For restaurants, the map results often matter more than the standard search results. Diners searching for food nearby see a short list of places with photos, ratings and directions. Getting into that list depends on your Google Business Profile, your website, how consistent your details are across the web and how close you are to the searcher.

Our local SEO work in Jeddah covers:

  • Profile set-up and clean-up: correct categories, accurate hours, menu links, attributes and fresh photos.
  • Branch pages on your website for each location, with address, hours and a short description.
  • Review habits: simple ways to ask happy diners for reviews and a plan to reply to every one.
  • Regular posts about new dishes, events and seasonal offers.

Our guide to Google Business Profile explains each step, and our article on SEO in Jeddah covers search more broadly. SEO usually takes three to six months to show clear movement.

Google Ads for Jeddah restaurants

Google Ads helps when you need bookings quickly, for example after opening a new branch or launching a new menu. We run tightly targeted search campaigns for cuisine and area searches, plus brand campaigns so your own website appears when people search your name. Each campaign links to the right menu or branch page, and we track bookings, calls and direction requests where possible. Our article on Google Ads in Jeddah explains how we set these up. Ad spend is paid directly to Google and is separate from our fees.

Meta and TikTok ads for food and drink

Food sells visually. Social ads let you show a dish, an atmosphere or an offer to people within a set distance of your branches. We plan audiences, write English copy, set up tracking and manage budgets on Meta and TikTok, while Arabic copy comes from your team or a translator.

We work with photos and short videos your team or a content creator already provides. Prism does not film or edit video, so we plan campaigns around the creative you can supply and advise on which formats tend to suit each platform. Our article on TikTok ads in Saudi Arabia covers that platform in more detail.

Seasons, Ramadan and special occasions

Demand in Jeddah shifts through the year. Ramadan brings iftar and suhoor menus, Eid brings family gatherings, and cooler months bring more outdoor dining. We plan campaigns and website updates ahead of these periods so your offers are live before people start searching. Timing, wording and imagery for religious occasions should always be approved by your team, who understand your guests best.

Working with a UK-based studio

We work remotely and do not visit restaurants. That suits most projects because the work is online: website builds, profile updates, ads and reports. We share previews, agree priorities on calls at times that suit Jeddah and send a short written report each month.

Your team handles anything that needs local presence or approval, including Arabic content, food photography, prices, licences and any municipal or food authority requirements. Keep regulatory notes general on your website and check details with official guidance or your own adviser. If you also operate in the capital, our article on restaurant marketing in Riyadh covers that city.

Common mistakes we fix first

When we review a Jeddah restaurant's marketing, the same problems come up again and again. Fixing them is often the quickest win, because they cost you diners every day without anyone noticing.

  • Out-of-date opening hours on Google, especially around Ramadan and public holidays, which send diners to a closed door.
  • Menus only available as images or PDFs, which load slowly and cannot be read by search engines.
  • Several branches sharing one listing or one page, so diners cannot tell which location is closest.
  • No clear booking or ordering button on the mobile version of the site.
  • Ads sending people to the home page rather than the menu or offer they clicked on.

We list these problems in a short written review at the start, agree an order of work with you and fix the most damaging issues first.

Direct orders and delivery apps

Delivery apps are a big part of dining in Jeddah, and they bring customers you might not reach otherwise. They also take a commission on each order. Where your ordering system allows it, we make your own website the easiest place to order, and we use your Google profile and social channels to point regular customers there. We do not manage your delivery app accounts, but we make sure your branding, menu names and opening hours stay consistent with them, so diners see the same information wherever they look.
