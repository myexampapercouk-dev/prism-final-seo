Shopify Order Fulfilment Workflow: From New Order to Delivered Parcel

A practical guide to the Shopify order fulfilment workflow: order statuses, picking and packing, partial fulfilment, tracking, returns and staff routines.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify separates payment status from fulfilment status, and understanding both is the foundation of a reliable dispatch routine.
  • A written, repeatable workflow for checking, picking, packing and marking orders as fulfilled reduces mistakes more than any single setting.
  • Tracking details, customer notifications and a clear returns process all belong to fulfilment, not just getting the parcel out of the door.

Taking an order is only half the job. Once a customer pays, someone has to check the order, find the right items, pack them, hand them to a carrier and tell the customer what is happening. In Shopify, this is called fulfilment, and the admin gives you a fair amount of structure to work with. The difficulty is that the tools are flexible, so two shops can use them in very different ways, and a loose routine can lead to missed orders, duplicate dispatches or confused customers.

This guide walks through a practical Shopify order fulfilment workflow for shops that pack and send their own orders. It explains what the order statuses mean, how to structure the daily routine, how to handle awkward cases such as partial shipments and held orders, and how to keep customers informed. It does not cover choosing a carrier or warehouse partner in detail, and features can vary by plan and region, so check what is available in your own admin.

Understanding Shopify order statuses

Every Shopify order carries more than one status, and mixing them up is a frequent cause of confusion. The two that matter most for fulfilment are:

  • Payment status. Whether the order has been paid, is pending, partially paid, authorised but not yet captured, refunded or partially refunded.
  • Fulfilment status. Whether the items have been sent. An order can be unfulfilled, partially fulfilled, fulfilled, or in some cases on hold or scheduled.

These run independently. An order can be paid but unfulfilled, which is the normal state for a new order waiting to be packed. It can also be fulfilled but later refunded, or authorised but not yet captured if your payment settings capture payment manually. Before anything leaves the building, the person packing should be confident that the payment status is what you expect.

Orders are also either open, archived or cancelled. Shopify can archive orders automatically once they are fulfilled and paid, which keeps the main list tidy. If you prefer to archive manually, you can change that in the order processing settings. Our guide to Shopify checkout settings covers several of the related options.

Setting up your fulfilment foundations

A few settings are worth checking before you design the daily routine.

Locations

Shopify uses locations to know where stock is held and where orders are fulfilled from. If you pack from a single unit or spare room, you may only have one location. If you hold stock in more than one place, such as a shop and a storeroom, each location should be set up properly so orders are assigned sensibly. Location setup is closely tied to stock levels, which our guide to Shopify inventory management covers in more depth.

Shipping profiles and rates

The shipping rates customers see at checkout come from your shipping profiles. These should reflect how you actually send parcels. If a customer pays for a tracked next-day option but your routine only sends standard post, the problem starts at checkout, not in the packing area.

Automatic or manual fulfilment

Shopify lets you choose whether to fulfil items automatically or manually. Automatic fulfilment can suit digital products or services where nothing physical is sent. For physical products, manual fulfilment is usually safer, because it means an order is only marked as fulfilled once someone has actually packed it.

Customer notifications

Shopify sends notification emails at various points, including order confirmation and shipping confirmation. Review these templates so the wording matches your brand and your real process. If you say parcels go out the same day, make sure that is true.

A daily fulfilment routine

The most reliable fulfilment workflows are boring and repeatable. The following outline is a starting point that you can adapt to your own shop.

Review new orders

Start by filtering the orders list to show unfulfilled, paid orders. Saved views make this quicker, so it is worth creating one for the routine. Look over each order for anything unusual:

  • Order notes or gift messages that need action.
  • Shipping addresses that look incomplete or odd.
  • Fraud analysis indicators, where shown, that suggest a closer look.
  • Mixed orders containing items that are out of stock or made to order.

If an order needs checking, add a tag or a note so it does not get packed by accident. Our guide to gift wrapping and order notes explains how to make special instructions easier to spot.

Pick the items

Picking means collecting the right products from your shelves. For small shops, working from the order screen may be enough. As volume grows, a printed or on-screen packing slip that lists items by SKU and location saves time. Shopify includes packing slip templates you can edit, so it is worth adding the information your team actually needs, such as SKU and variant.

Pack and check

Before sealing a parcel, check the contents against the packing slip. This single habit catches many errors. Include anything the customer should receive in the box, such as a returns note, care instructions or a gift message.

Create the shipment and add tracking

Next, mark the items as fulfilled. If you buy labels through Shopify, where available, the tracking number is usually added for you. If you use a carrier's own system, add the tracking number and carrier to the fulfilment manually so the customer receives a useful shipping confirmation.

Hand over and close the day

Once parcels are collected or dropped off, a final glance at the unfulfilled list helps confirm nothing was missed. Any orders still waiting should have a clear reason, such as a tag for awaiting stock.

Partial fulfilment and split shipments

Sometimes you cannot send everything at once. One item may be out of stock, or a made-to-order product may take longer than the rest. Shopify allows you to fulfil some items in an order and leave others unfulfilled. The order then shows as partially fulfilled.

Before splitting a shipment, think about the customer. Two parcels can mean extra postage cost for you and confusion for them. If you do split, say so clearly. Shopify sends a shipping confirmation for each fulfilment, but a short personal note explaining that the rest is on its way can prevent a worried email.

If you regularly sell items that ship later than the rest of your range, look at how you present them at checkout. Our guide to Shopify pre-orders covers how to set expectations before the customer pays.

Holding orders

There are good reasons not to send an order straight away. The address may need confirming, the payment may need reviewing, or the customer may have asked you to wait. Depending on your setup, Shopify can place fulfilment on hold so the order is clearly marked as not ready to pack. Where that is not suitable, a consistent tag such as a hold tag, combined with a saved view that excludes tagged orders, does a similar job.

Whatever method you use, agree who is responsible for releasing held orders and how often they are reviewed. Held orders that nobody owns can sit untouched for longer than anyone intended.

Local delivery and collection

If customers can collect from your premises or receive local delivery, those orders follow a slightly different path. Instead of a carrier label, the key step is letting the customer know the order is ready, then marking it as collected or delivered. Our guide to Shopify local pickup covers the set-up and the notifications involved.

Working with third-party fulfilment

Some shops hand fulfilment to a warehouse or fulfilment partner. In that case, Shopify passes orders to the partner, who picks, packs and sends them, then updates the order with tracking. Your role shifts from packing to monitoring. You still need to watch for orders that are stuck, check that tracking is being added, and make sure stock levels stay in step. The workflow principles in this guide still apply; only the person doing each step changes.

Staff roles and permissions

If more than one person handles orders, decide who can do what. A packer may need to view and fulfil orders but not issue refunds or change settings. Shopify allows you to set permissions for each staff account, so you can give people access to what they need without opening up the whole admin. Our guide to Shopify staff accounts and permissions explains how to approach this.

A short written procedure helps too. It does not need to be long: a page describing the daily steps, the tags you use and what to do when something looks wrong is often enough. It also makes holiday cover far easier.

Returns, exchanges and refunds

Fulfilment does not always end at delivery. Customers return items, ask for exchanges or report damage. Shopify includes tools for creating returns and issuing refunds from the order screen, and these keep the order history accurate.

Consumer law gives online shoppers certain rights to cancel and return goods, and there are rules about how refunds are handled. The details depend on the product and circumstances, so it is sensible to check current guidance or take advice rather than relying on a general summary. Make sure your returns policy is easy to find, and that your process matches what the policy says. Our guide to website terms and privacy policies touches on where these policies should sit on your site.

When a return arrives, check the item, decide whether it can go back into stock, and restock it in Shopify if so. Forgetting to restock is an easy way for inventory counts to drift.

Keeping customers informed

Most order-related customer emails are some form of where is my parcel. Clear communication reduces them. Useful steps include:

  • Stating realistic dispatch times on product pages and in the cart.
  • Making sure shipping confirmation emails include working tracking links.
  • Giving the order status page clear wording about what happens next.
  • Explaining delays proactively rather than waiting to be asked.

The order status page that customers reach from their confirmation email is part of the experience. It is worth checking how it looks on a phone and whether it answers the obvious questions.

Reviewing and improving the workflow

Every so often, step back and look at where things go wrong. Are certain products often packed incorrectly? Are notes being missed? Does the packing slip leave out something the packer needs? Small changes, such as a clearer SKU, a better saved view or an extra line on the packing slip, often make the biggest difference.

If your shop has grown and the admin feels cluttered, it can also be worth reviewing the apps connected to orders. Our Shopify apps audit guide covers how to check what each connected tool is doing.

FAQs

What does fulfilled mean in Shopify?

An order is marked as fulfilled when all its physical items have been recorded as sent. It is separate from payment status, so an order can be paid and unfulfilled, or fulfilled and later refunded.

Should I use automatic fulfilment in Shopify?

Automatic fulfilment can suit digital products. For physical goods, manual fulfilment is usually safer because orders are only marked as sent once someone has packed them.

Can I send part of a Shopify order?

Yes. You can fulfil some items and leave the rest unfulfilled, and the order will show as partially fulfilled. Tell the customer clearly so they know more is coming.

How do I stop an order being packed by mistake?

Use the hold option where your setup supports it, or add a consistent tag and exclude tagged orders from the saved view your packers work from.

Do I need to add tracking numbers in Shopify?

Adding tracking is strongly recommended. It lets customers follow their parcel from the shipping confirmation email and tends to reduce delivery questions.
