Who this guide is for

Raleigh sits at the heart of the Research Triangle alongside Durham and Chapel Hill, with nearby Cary, Apex, Wake Forest and Morrisville growing quickly. The local economy includes technology, life sciences, education, healthcare, professional services, construction and a busy consumer scene. We most often help:

  • Service businesses such as contractors, cleaners, landscapers and clinics that need steady local enquiries.
  • B2B and technology firms that need credible, well-structured sites for buyers who research thoroughly before calling.
  • Professional practices such as accountants, consultants and architects.
  • Retail and DTC brands selling online across North Carolina and nationally.
  • Start-ups preparing for a launch or funding round that need a polished site quickly.

What good web design in Raleigh looks like

A good business website is not about decoration. It is about clarity, speed and trust. Every site we build is planned around a few principles.

Clear message above the fold. Visitors should understand what you do, who you do it for and where you work within a few seconds, with one obvious next step such as calling, booking or requesting a quote.

Mobile first. Most local searches happen on phones. We design for small screens first, with large tap targets, readable type and click-to-call buttons.

Fast loading. We keep pages light, compress images and avoid unnecessary scripts. Our Core Web Vitals guide explains the speed measures Google looks at.

Search-ready structure. Each key service gets its own page with a clear heading, useful copy, internal links and structured data, so it can rank for the searches that matter.

Trust signals. Real photos of your work and team, clear contact details, reviews you have collected yourself and honest service information all help people feel confident before they get in touch.

Our web design process

We run projects remotely using video calls, shared documents and a staging site you can review at any time. A typical project follows these steps.

  1. Discovery. We learn about your services, customers, competitors and goals, and agree the pages you need.
  2. Sitemap and wireframes. We plan the structure and layout of key pages before any visual design begins.
  3. Design. We create the look and feel based on your brand, then apply it across templates.
  4. Build. We develop the site on WordPress or Shopify, add content, set up forms and connect analytics.
  5. SEO setup. Titles, descriptions, headings, redirects from your old site, structured data and a sitemap are all in place before launch.
  6. Review and launch. You review the staging site, we make changes, then we go live and monitor for problems.

Calls are usually held in the morning Eastern time, which is the afternoon for us in the UK. That overlap leaves plenty of room for regular check-ins.

Making your Raleigh website work for local search

A new website is a good moment to get local SEO right. We create service pages that mention the areas you actually cover, such as North Raleigh, Cary, Apex, Garner, Wake Forest or Durham, without filling the site with duplicate city pages. We add your business details in a consistent format, connect the site to your Google Business Profile and set up tracking so you can see which pages produce calls and form submissions.

After launch, many clients add an SEO retainer to keep building content and links. Our local SEO guide covers the fundamentals, and our landing page design guide explains how to build pages for paid campaigns.

What to prepare before your project starts

Projects move faster when a few things are ready before the first design call. You do not need everything finished, but the more of this you can gather, the sooner we can show you a working staging site.

  • Your logo and brand files, ideally in vector format, plus any colours and fonts you already use.
  • A list of services or products, in order of importance to the business, with a note on which bring in the most profit.
  • Real photographs of your premises, projects, products or team. Stock images can fill gaps, but genuine photos build far more trust.
  • Logins for your domain registrar, current hosting, Google Business Profile, Search Console and analytics, shared securely through your own password manager.
  • Two or three websites you like, along with what you like about each, so we understand your taste early.
  • Common customer questions, which often become the most useful page copy and FAQs on the new site.

If you do not have copy ready, we can write it in English as part of the project. We will interview you about your services, then draft page text that is clear, specific and written to match what people in the Triangle search for. You review and approve every page before launch.

Accessibility, privacy and payment notes

We keep these points general. Please check official guidance or speak to your own adviser for advice on your situation.

Accessibility. We design with accessibility in mind: logical headings, keyboard-friendly navigation, readable contrast, alt text and labelled forms. Whether and how the ADA applies to your website is a question for a qualified adviser.

Privacy. Forms, analytics and ad tags collect personal data. State privacy laws vary, so we can set up consent banners and a tag manager, but your privacy policy and compliance decisions rest with you.

Sales tax. If you sell online, Shopify and WooCommerce can calculate tax, but the settings depend on your obligations. Your accountant should confirm where you need to collect sales tax.

  • Who will own the domain, hosting account and website files?

  • How will you handle redirects from our old URLs so we do not lose rankings?
