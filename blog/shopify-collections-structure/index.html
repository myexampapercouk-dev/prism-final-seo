Shopify Collections Structure: How to Organise Products So Shoppers Find Them

How to plan a Shopify collections structure: manual vs automated collections, naming, menus, filters and SEO, so shoppers and search engines find products.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Plan collections around how shoppers think about your range, not around how your stockroom or supplier list is organised.
  • Use automated collections with clear tag or field rules for anything that changes often, and manual collections for curated edits.
  • Treat each important collection as a landing page with a unique title, intro copy and description, then link to it from menus.

A Shopify collections structure is the way you group products into collections so shoppers can browse, filter and land on the right range quickly. A good structure makes menus simpler, gives search engines clear category pages to rank, and saves you time when new products arrive. A poor one leads to duplicate pages, confusing menus and products that nobody can find.

This guide explains how collections work, how to plan them, and how to keep them tidy as your range grows. If you are still setting up your shop, our Shopify store setup guide covers the wider picture first.

What a Shopify collection actually is

In Shopify, a collection is a group of products shown together on one page with its own URL. A product can sit in as many collections as you like. That flexibility is useful, but it also means structure is your job: Shopify will not stop you creating overlapping or near-identical collections.

There are two kinds:

  • Manual collections. You choose each product by hand. Good for curated edits such as gift guides, staff picks or a seasonal lookbook.
  • Automated (smart) collections. Products join automatically when they match conditions you set, such as product type, vendor, tag, price or a metafield value. Good for core categories that change as stock comes and goes.

Most shops benefit from a mix. Core categories run automatically so you never forget to add a new product, while a handful of manual collections handle merchandising.

Start with how shoppers think

The biggest mistake is copying an internal structure into the shop. Supplier names, warehouse codes or the order products were added mean little to a customer. Instead, ask how someone would describe what they want.

Useful ways to understand this include:

  • Looking at the words people type into your site search, if you have that data.
  • Checking Google Search Console for the queries that already bring people to your shop.
  • Reading customer emails and reviews for the language they use.
  • Looking at how well-organised competitors group similar products, without copying them.

From this you can sketch a simple tree. Usually that means a small number of top-level categories, each with a few subcategories. Keep it shallow: shoppers should reach a product list within a couple of clicks from the homepage. Our guide to website navigation structure goes deeper on menu planning.

Categories, attributes and filters

Not every way of slicing your range deserves its own collection. A helpful rule is to separate categories from attributes.

  • Categories describe what a product is: dresses, candles, dog beds, road bike helmets. These usually become collections.
  • Attributes describe a property: colour, size, material, scent, price band. These usually work better as filters on a collection page.

If you create a separate collection for every colour of every category, you end up with a huge number of thin pages that compete with each other and are hard to maintain. Shopify's built-in Search and Discovery app lets you add filters based on product options, tags, vendors and metafields, so shoppers can narrow a category without you building extra pages.

There are exceptions. If people genuinely search for a specific combination, such as a material plus a product type, a dedicated collection with its own copy can make sense. Decide case by case rather than by default.

Using metafields to keep rules clean

Tags are quick to add but easy to get wrong. A typo in one tag means a product quietly drops out of an automated collection. Product metafields, with defined value lists, give you more structured data that can drive both automated collections and filters. They take a little more setup but are usually easier to keep consistent as more people edit products.

Naming and URLs

Collection names appear in menus, page headings, breadcrumbs and search results, so make them plain and descriptive. "Women's Running Shoes" is clearer than "Run Her". Clever names can work for campaign edits, but core categories should use the words shoppers use.

Shopify builds each collection URL from a handle, for example /collections/womens-running-shoes. A few points help:

  • Set the handle carefully when you create the collection. Changing it later changes the URL.
  • If you must change a handle, add a URL redirect from the old address so links and rankings are not lost. Our redirects guide explains why this matters.
  • Avoid handles with dates or campaign codes for collections you intend to keep.

Make collection pages worth landing on

Collection pages are often the pages that rank for broader searches, because they match category-level intent. Yet many shops leave them as a bare grid of products. A little effort helps both shoppers and search engines.

  • Unique SEO title and meta description. Edit these in the collection's search engine listing section rather than relying on defaults.
  • Short intro copy. A sentence or two above the grid explaining what is in the range and who it suits. Longer helpful text, such as buying advice, can sit below the products.
  • A clear collection image if your theme uses one, with descriptive alt text.
  • Sensible default sort order. Best selling, newest or manual order, depending on what you want shoppers to see first.
  • Internal links to related collections and useful guides.

Our Shopify SEO guide covers the wider technical points, including how Shopify handles product URLs that appear inside collections.

Connecting collections to menus

Creating a collection does not add it to your navigation. You need to place it in a menu under Online Store, Navigation. Plan the menu from your category tree, then link each item to the matching collection.

Some practical tips:

  • Keep top-level menu labels short and clear.
  • Use a "Shop all" link inside each category so people can browse everything in it.
  • Do not put every collection in the main menu. Curated edits can live on the homepage, in banners or in a secondary menu.
  • Check the mobile menu separately. A structure that looks neat on desktop can become a long scroll on a phone.

Breadcrumbs, where your theme supports them, help shoppers see where they are in the structure. Our breadcrumb navigation guide explains how to use them well.

Avoiding duplicate and thin collections

Over time, shops tend to collect collections. Old sales, abandoned campaigns and near-duplicates build up. This can confuse shoppers and dilute signals to search engines.

Review your collections from time to time and ask:

  • Does this collection still have products in it?
  • Is it linked from anywhere, or is it an orphan?
  • Does another collection cover almost exactly the same products?
  • Would this be better as a filter on an existing collection?

When you retire a collection that has links or search visibility, redirect it to the closest relevant collection rather than simply deleting it. For empty seasonal collections you plan to reuse, consider keeping the page live with helpful copy rather than recreating it with a new URL each year.

Planning for growth

A structure that suits a small range can strain as you add products or new lines. Building in some flexibility early saves rework later.

  • Decide on consistent product types and metafield values before you bulk import products.
  • Document the rules for each automated collection so whoever adds products knows which fields to fill in.
  • Leave room in the menu for a new top-level category without a full redesign.
  • If you sell in several markets, check how collections and translated content display for each.

If you are planning a larger restructure, it is worth briefing a developer properly. Our guide on how to brief a Shopify developer explains what to include.

A simple process to follow

  1. List your current products and how shoppers describe them.
  2. Sketch a shallow category tree with clear names.
  3. Decide which properties become filters rather than collections.
  4. Set up product types, tags or metafields consistently.
  5. Create automated collections for categories and manual ones for curated edits.
  6. Write unique titles, descriptions and intro copy for important collections.
  7. Build menus from the tree and test them on mobile.
  8. Review collections periodically and redirect any you retire.

FAQs

Should I use manual or automated collections?

Use automated collections for core categories that change as stock comes and goes, because new products join automatically when they match the rules. Use manual collections for curated groupings where you want to pick every product yourself.

Can a product be in more than one collection?

Yes. A product can appear in as many collections as you like. That is useful for merchandising, but plan it so collections do not become near-duplicates of each other.

Should every colour or size have its own collection?

Usually not. Colour, size and similar properties tend to work better as filters on a category collection. Create a separate collection only where shoppers clearly search for that specific combination and you can make the page genuinely useful.

What happens if I change a collection's URL?

The old URL will stop working unless you add a redirect. Shopify can create a redirect when you edit a handle, but it is worth checking it has been set up, and updating any internal links.

Do collection descriptions help SEO?

Helpful, unique copy on collection pages can help search engines understand the page and help shoppers decide. Keep it relevant and avoid stuffing keywords. Short copy above the grid and longer advice below is a common, practical layout.
