LinkedIn Ads vs Google Ads for B2B: How UK Firms Can Choose

Google Ads catches B2B buyers already searching; LinkedIn Ads reaches decision-makers by job title before they search. Compare cost, intent and fit.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Google Ads captures existing demand from people searching for what you sell, while LinkedIn Ads creates demand by targeting people by role, company and industry.
  • LinkedIn clicks usually cost more, so it tends to suit higher-value B2B deals with a clearly defined buyer, while Google suits services people actively search for.
  • Many B2B firms start with whichever platform matches how their buyers behave, prove it with proper tracking, then add the second channel.

For most UK B2B firms, Google Ads is better at capturing buyers who are already searching for a solution, while LinkedIn Ads is better at reaching specific decision-makers by job title, seniority and company before they ever search. The right choice depends on whether demand for your service already exists in search, how much a new client is worth to you and how precisely you can describe the person who signs off the purchase.

This guide compares LinkedIn Ads vs Google Ads for B2B on intent, targeting, cost, creative and measurement, then gives a simple way to decide where to start. Both platforms change their features, ad policies and reporting regularly, so check their current official help pages before you launch anything.

Intent: searching versus scrolling

The biggest difference is intent. Someone typing "managed IT support for law firms" or "commercial cleaning contract Leeds" into Google has already decided they have a problem and is comparing options. Your ad meets them at that moment, which is why search is often the fastest route to enquiries when demand exists.

LinkedIn works the other way round. People are reading posts, checking updates and catching up on industry news. They are not looking for you, so your ad has to earn attention and start a conversation. That makes LinkedIn powerful for reaching the right people, but it usually takes longer for a click to become a sales opportunity.

A useful test is to look at search demand first. If keyword research shows people regularly search for your service in the UK, Google Ads is a natural place to begin. If your service is new, niche or bought by committees who rarely search for it by name, LinkedIn may be the better way to get in front of them. Our B2B SEO guide covers how to judge that demand.

Targeting: keywords versus job titles

Google Ads targets mainly by what people search for, refined by location, schedule, device and audience signals. You cannot reliably target by job title in standard search campaigns, so a search for "accounting software" might come from a finance director, a student or a sole trader. Good keyword choice and a strong list of exclusions do most of the filtering; our negative keywords guide explains how to cut irrelevant searches.

LinkedIn targets by professional attributes people and companies share on the platform: job function, seniority, job title, company size, industry, skills and named company lists. That precision is its main strength. If you sell to operations directors at UK manufacturers with 50 to 500 staff, you can reach that group fairly directly.

The trade-off is that precise B2B audiences are often small. Narrow targeting can limit how far your budget goes, so it is worth testing audience size before committing. Our LinkedIn Ads for B2B lead generation guide walks through targeting options in more detail.

Cost and budget

We do not quote typical click prices because they change with competition, industry and audience. In general, LinkedIn clicks tend to cost more than many Google search clicks, and LinkedIn audiences are smaller, so spend can go quickly. Google search costs vary widely too: some B2B keywords are cheap, while competitive terms in sectors such as software, legal or financial services can be expensive. Our Google Ads cost guide explains what drives search prices.

The useful question is not "which is cheaper per click" but "what does a qualified lead cost, and what is a new client worth?" If an average B2B client is worth tens of thousands of pounds over a contract, a higher cost per lead on LinkedIn can still be sensible. If your average sale is small, the economics of LinkedIn are harder to make work.

Creative and offers

On Google, the ad is mostly text: headlines and descriptions that match the search and give a reason to click. The landing page then has to answer the searcher's question quickly and make enquiring easy. Clarity beats cleverness.

On LinkedIn, the ad has to interrupt someone who is not looking. Images, short video, document ads and thought-leadership style posts tend to work better than a hard sales pitch. The offer matters even more: a useful guide, a benchmark checklist, a webinar or a short consultation usually performs better than "book a demo" when the audience has never heard of you.

Whichever platform you use, the page people land on carries a lot of the result. Our landing page design guide covers how to build pages that turn paid clicks into enquiries.

Lead quality and sales cycles

Google leads are often closer to buying, so sales teams can usually follow up quickly. The risk is that some searches attract the wrong people, such as job seekers, students or very small businesses outside your target market. Good keyword control and qualifying questions on forms help.

LinkedIn leads often match your target profile well on paper, but many are earlier in their thinking. Lead Gen Forms, which pre-fill details from a member's profile, make it easy to convert, yet that ease can also mean softer leads. A clear follow-up process, such as a helpful email sequence and a sales call when the timing suits, makes a big difference to whether LinkedIn pays back.

For longer sales cycles, it helps to judge each channel on pipeline and closed business over several months, not only on the number of form fills in the first few weeks.

Measurement and tracking

Neither platform can tell you which leads became clients unless you connect them to what happens afterwards. At a minimum, set up conversion tracking for form submissions and calls, use consistent UTM tags, and record lead source in your CRM. Our Google Ads conversion tracking guide covers the Google side.

LinkedIn has its own Insight Tag and conversion tracking. Bear in mind that cookie consent rules under UK GDPR and PECR affect what can be tracked, so your cookie banner and analytics setup need to be correct before you rely on the numbers. Check current ICO guidance and each platform's official documentation, as requirements and features change.

When to use both together

Many B2B firms end up running both, because they do different jobs. LinkedIn can build familiarity with a defined list of target companies, and Google can capture those same people when they later search for a solution. Retargeting on either platform can then keep you in front of people who visited your site but did not enquire.

That combination only works if each channel is measured properly and given enough budget to learn. Splitting a small budget across two platforms often means neither gets enough data. It is usually better to prove one, then add the second.

How to decide where to start

  1. Check search demand. If people search for your service in the UK, start with Google Ads.
  2. Define your buyer. If you can name the job titles and company types that buy from you, and they rarely search for you, test LinkedIn.
  3. Work out client value. Higher lifetime value gives you room for LinkedIn's typically higher costs.
  4. Prepare an offer. Search needs a clear service page; LinkedIn usually needs a useful, low-commitment offer.
  5. Set up tracking first. Make sure enquiries and their source are recorded before spending.
  6. Test for long enough. Give a channel time to gather data, and judge it on qualified leads and pipeline, not clicks.

FAQs

LinkedIn Ads vs Google Ads for B2B: which is better?

Neither is better in every case. Google Ads usually wins when buyers actively search for your service, while LinkedIn Ads usually wins when you need to reach a specific role or company type that rarely searches for you by name.

Is LinkedIn advertising more expensive than Google Ads?

LinkedIn clicks often cost more than many Google search clicks, and audiences are smaller. Some competitive Google keywords are expensive too, so compare cost per qualified lead and client value rather than cost per click.

Can a small B2B business use LinkedIn Ads?

Yes, but it needs a clear buyer profile, a valuable enough sale and enough budget to reach the audience repeatedly. Many small firms find it easier to prove Google Ads first.

Are LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms better than landing pages?

Lead Gen Forms usually produce more leads because details are pre-filled, but quality can be lower. Landing pages add a little friction, which can filter out less serious enquiries. Testing both is often worthwhile.

How long should I test each platform?

Give each channel enough time and spend to gather meaningful data, typically several weeks at least, and judge results over a sales cycle. Paid campaigns typically take three to six months to settle.

Should I run LinkedIn Ads and Google Ads at the same time?

Running both can work well once one channel is proven, because LinkedIn builds awareness and Google captures searches later. With a small budget, it is usually better to focus on one first.
