Law Firm Marketing in the UK: SEO, Paid Search and Content That Wins Enquiries

Law firm marketing in the UK works best through practice-area SEO, local search and focused paid ads, with the firm signing off every claim before it goes live.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Build a separate, genuinely useful page for each practice area and location you want enquiries for.
  • Paid search works for urgent legal needs, but only with tight keywords, negatives and fast enquiry handling.
  • Professional conduct rules apply to how firms promote themselves, so the firm should review and approve every claim.

Law firm marketing in the UK works best when each practice area has its own clear, helpful page, local search is set up properly, and paid search is used carefully for urgent, high-intent queries. Because solicitors and other legal providers are subject to professional rules on how they promote their services, the firm itself should review and sign off every claim before it is published.

This guide covers how people find a lawyer, how to structure a law firm website for search, where paid ads fit, and how to handle content and compliance. It is general marketing information, not legal or regulatory advice. Prism Works Studio is a marketing agency, not a regulated legal business, so final approval of anything you publish always rests with your firm.

How people look for a solicitor

Legal searches fall into two broad groups. Consumers with a personal problem (a divorce, a house purchase, a will, an injury claim, a dispute with an employer) usually search urgently and locally. Businesses looking for commercial, employment or property advice often research for longer, compare several firms and rely on recommendations as well as search.

Law firm marketing UK: the core channels

Practice-area SEO

The single most useful thing most firms can do is give every practice area its own page, and where it makes sense, its own sub-pages. A single "Our services" page listing ten areas of law will rarely rank for any of them. A dedicated page for divorce, another for children arrangements, another for financial settlements, each answering the real questions clients ask, gives search engines and people something specific to work with.

Good practice-area pages usually include:

  • A plain-English summary of what the service covers and who it is for.
  • The steps involved and roughly how long each stage tends to take, in general terms.
  • How fees work, where your firm chooses to publish them, and what is and is not included.
  • Who in the firm handles this work, described accurately and only with details the firm has approved.
  • A clear next step: call, form or booking, with realistic response times.

Our SEO and GEO service covers this structure, along with the technical work that sits underneath it. If you are starting from scratch, the technical SEO audit checklist is a sensible first pass over an existing site.

Local search

Many legal searches include a town or "near me". A complete Google Business Profile for each genuine office, consistent name, address and phone details across directories, and location pages that say something useful about each office all help. Avoid creating thin pages for towns where you have no presence; they tend to underperform and can read as misleading. Our local SEO guide and Google Business Profile guide go into the detail.

Paid search

Google Ads can produce enquiries quickly for urgent legal needs, but legal keywords are often competitive and clicks can be expensive. The firms that make paid search work tend to keep campaigns narrow: specific practice areas, specific locations, strong negative keyword lists (to filter out searches for free advice, jobs and training) and landing pages built for the service being advertised. Our guide to choosing a Google Ads agency explains what good management looks like, and our paid media service covers how we run campaigns.

Content and thought leadership

Commercial clients in particular want evidence that a firm understands their sector. Short, practical articles on changes that affect clients, written or reviewed by your lawyers, build trust and give you something to share on LinkedIn and in newsletters. See our notes on content marketing for how to plan this without it becoming a burden on fee earners.

Structuring a law firm website for search and enquiries

A clear structure helps both visitors and search engines. A typical layout for a mid-sized high-street or regional firm looks like this:

  1. Home page that makes clear who you help, where, and how to get in touch.
  2. Two hubs: one for individuals, one for businesses, so each audience finds its route quickly.
  3. Practice-area pages under each hub, with sub-pages for the most searched services.
  4. Office or location pages for each genuine office.
  5. People pages for fee earners, with qualifications and experience the firm has verified.
  6. Guides and insights that answer common questions and link back to the relevant service page.
  7. Fees and regulatory information pages, kept current by the firm.

Speed and mobile usability matter more than many firms expect, because a large share of urgent legal searches happen on phones. If your site is slow or awkward, our website development service can rebuild it, and our guide to redesigning without losing rankings explains how to protect existing search visibility during the move.

Compliance and sign-off: a general overview

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and other legal professionals and other parts of the UK have their own regulators and rules. Those rules generally cover things like how firms describe their services, publishing certain price and service information for some practice areas, how regulatory status is displayed, and restrictions on unsolicited approaches to members of the public. Advertising more broadly also falls under the CAP Code.

We are not able to advise on what those rules require for your firm, and the detail changes over time. What we do is build a review step into every piece of work so that the right person at your firm can check it. In practice that usually means:

  • Agreeing who at the firm approves web copy, ads and social posts before anything goes live.
  • Avoiding superlatives and outcome claims unless the firm has confirmed they are accurate and permitted.
  • Keeping any client feedback the firm chooses to use genuine, attributable and approved by the firm.
  • Leaving space on pages and templates for regulatory information the firm needs to display.
  • Recording what was approved and when, so changes can be traced.

If your firm also offers financial services, the approach in our financial services marketing page and our article on FCA-aware marketing will also be relevant.

Marketing to business clients

Commercial practices often behave more like B2B professional services than consumer brands. Decisions involve several people, take longer, and depend heavily on reputation. Useful tactics include:

  • Sector pages (for example, technology, property, manufacturing) that show how your practice areas apply to that industry.
  • LinkedIn activity by partners and associates, supported by firm content. Our LinkedIn Ads guide covers paid options.
  • Events and webinars on practical topics, with recordings reused on the site.
  • Digital PR around legal commentary on news, which can earn links and mentions. See digital PR for link building.

Our B2B marketing page explains how we approach longer, relationship-led sales cycles like these.

Measuring what works

Legal enquiries often arrive by phone, so tracking only form submissions misses much of the picture. Basic measurement for a law firm should include call tracking or at least clear phone-click tracking, form tracking in GA4, and a simple way for reception or intake staff to record how each new client heard about the firm. Our GA4 setup guide covers the analytics side.

It is also worth separating enquiry volume from enquiry quality. A campaign that brings fifty calls about free advice is less valuable than one that brings ten from people ready to instruct. Reviewing enquiry quality with the intake team each month usually tells you more than any dashboard.

FAQs

What is the best way to approach law firm marketing in the UK?

Start with a clear page for each practice area, a properly set-up Google Business Profile for each office, and accurate tracking of calls and forms. Add paid search for urgent services once your landing pages are ready, and build content for areas where clients research before instructing.

Do law firms need SEO?

Most do, because a large share of people looking for a solicitor start with a search. SEO builds visibility you do not pay for per click, but it usually takes three to six months to show a clear trend.

Is Google Ads worth it for solicitors?

It can be for urgent, high-value services, provided keywords are tightly chosen, negatives filter out free-advice searches and enquiries are answered quickly. Broad campaigns on generic legal terms tend to waste budget.

Can Prism Works Studio tell us what the SRA rules require?

No. We are a marketing agency, not a regulated legal business, and cannot give regulatory advice. We build a review step into our work so your firm can check and approve everything against the rules that apply to you.

Should law firms publish prices online?

Some practice areas have price transparency requirements and many clients look for fee information. Whether and how you publish prices is a decision for your firm, and we can help present the information clearly once you have approved it.

How long does it take to see results?

Paid search can bring enquiries within weeks of launch. SEO and content usually take three to six months to show a clear trend, depending on competition in your area and practice areas.
