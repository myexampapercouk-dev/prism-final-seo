SEO for Small Businesses in the UK: Where to Start and What to Spend

SEO for small businesses in the UK: what to fix first, realistic budgets, what you can do yourself and when it makes sense to bring in an SEO agency.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with the basics: a fast, mobile-friendly site, a complete Google Business Profile and one clear page for each service you sell.
  • A small business can do a lot of SEO itself, but an agency saves time on technical work, content planning and link building.
  • Budget for months, not weeks: SEO results typically show within 3 to 6 months, so plan cash flow and expectations accordingly.

SEO for a small business in the UK means making sure the people searching for what you sell, in the places you serve, can find you on Google and choose you. For most small firms, the priorities are a fast, clear website, a complete Google Business Profile, a dedicated page for each service and a steady stream of reviews.

You do not need a huge budget to get started. This guide sets out what to do first, what SEO typically costs, what you can do yourself and when it is worth paying an agency.

SEO for small business UK: why it is worth the effort

Search is often where buying starts. Someone needing an accountant, a dog groomer, a kitchen fitter or a wedding venue will usually search, compare a few results and contact one or two. If you are not visible, they never see you. Unlike paid ads, organic rankings keep working when you stop spending, which suits small businesses with tight cash flow, once the groundwork is done.

SEO is also increasingly about AI search. Tools such as Google's AI Overviews and ChatGPT draw on websites that explain things clearly. The same habits that help you rank, such as plain answers, specific service pages and consistent business details, help you get mentioned there too. Our guide to generative engine optimisation explains more.

Get your website right first

If your site is slow, hard to use on a phone or built on a platform you cannot edit, SEO work will struggle. A small business website does not need to be complicated, but it does need clear navigation, fast pages and room to add service and location pages. If yours needs replacing, a small website from us costs £1,000 to £2,000; see our website development service for what that includes.

One page per service

A common mistake is listing every service on a single page. If you offer boiler servicing, bathroom fitting and emergency plumbing, each deserves its own page with its own title, a clear description, prices or a price guide where you can, FAQs and a call to action. That gives Google a specific page to rank for each search.

Local visibility

If you serve customers in a particular town or region, local SEO is likely your biggest opportunity. Your Google Business Profile, reviews and consistent name, address and phone details across directories all feed into the map results. Make sure your town or area appears naturally on your service pages too.

DIY SEO versus hiring an agency

Many small business owners can handle a good share of SEO themselves. The question is whether the time is better spent elsewhere.

DIYAgency
CostYour time, plus tools if neededMonthly retainer
Best forGoogle Business Profile, reviews, writing about your own servicesTechnical fixes, keyword planning, content strategy, link building
RisksSlow progress, missed technical issues, inconsistent effortPaying for vague work if scope and reporting are unclear
SpeedDepends on how much time you can commitUsually faster, as work happens every month

A sensible middle path is to handle reviews and your Google Business Profile yourself while an agency covers technical work and content planning. If you do hire help, our guide on choosing a digital marketing agency in the UK lists the questions to ask, and our SEO and GEO service page explains how we work.

Warning signs when hiring

  • Guaranteed rankings or "page one in 30 days" promises
  • Vague monthly reports with no list of work completed
  • Bought links or large numbers of low-quality directory submissions
  • Long lock-in contracts before any results are possible
  • The agency owning your website, content or Google accounts

Small business SEO checklist

  • Google Search Console and analytics installed and checked monthly
  • Google Business Profile verified, complete and updated with photos and posts
  • Name, address and phone number identical across your site and directories
  • Website loads quickly and works well on mobile
  • A separate page for each main service, with FAQs and a clear call to action
  • Unique title tags and meta descriptions on key pages
  • A simple routine for requesting and replying to reviews
  • Redirects in place for any old or moved pages
  • Helpful content answering real customer questions, linked to service pages
  • Links and mentions from local organisations, suppliers or trade bodies, which our guide to digital PR and link building covers

A realistic first 90 days

For a typical UK small business starting from scratch, the first three months might look like this. In month one, set up tracking, complete your Google Business Profile, fix obvious technical problems and decide which services and areas matter most. In month two, rewrite or create a page for each main service, tighten title tags and meta descriptions, and start asking every customer for a review. In month three, add a few helpful articles answering questions customers genuinely ask, and look for easy local links, such as suppliers, trade associations, sponsorships and local business groups.

By the end of that period you should see more of your pages indexed and some movement in impressions in Search Console, even if enquiries have not yet grown much. That is normal. Enquiries usually follow once rankings settle, which is why SEO rewards consistency rather than short bursts of activity.

Common small business SEO mistakes

Changing website without planning redirects is one of the most costly. If you are redesigning, read our guide to redesigning without losing rankings first. Other frequent problems include targeting very broad national terms you cannot realistically win, stuffing town names into pages, and stopping after a couple of months just before results begin to appear.

FAQs

Is SEO for small business UK owners worth the money?

Usually, if customers search for what you sell. Once rankings build, they keep bringing enquiries without paying per click, though it typically takes 3 to 6 months to see results.

Can I do SEO for my small business myself?

Yes, much of it. Your Google Business Profile, reviews and service page copy are very DIY friendly. Technical fixes and link building are where outside help tends to pay off.

How much does SEO cost for a small business?

Our retainers start at £350 a month for small local businesses, with Growth and Scale plans at £750 and £1,500 a month. The right level depends on competition and goals.

What should a small business do first for SEO?

Set up Search Console, complete your Google Business Profile and make sure your website is fast, mobile-friendly and has one clear page for each service.

Do I need a blog for SEO?

Not at first. Strong service pages and a good Google Business Profile matter more. A blog helps later if each article answers real customer questions and links to your services.

Is local SEO different from normal SEO?

It is a part of SEO focused on map results and searches with a location. It relies heavily on your Google Business Profile, reviews and consistent business details.
