Shopify vs Squarespace for UK Businesses: Which Platform Fits?

Shopify vs Squarespace for UK businesses: how the two compare on selling, design, content, apps and growth, and how to decide which suits your plans.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify is built around selling; Squarespace is built around presenting a brand, with selling added on.
  • If products are the heart of the business and you expect to grow the catalogue, Shopify is usually the safer long-term choice.
  • If you mainly sell services, bookings or a handful of products alongside a content-led site, Squarespace can be simpler to run.

For most UK businesses the choice between Shopify and Squarespace comes down to one question: is your website a shop that also tells a story, or a story that also has a shop? Shopify is designed first and foremost for ecommerce. Squarespace is designed first for good-looking, content-led websites, with commerce features available on its higher plans. Both are hosted platforms, so you do not manage servers or software updates yourself.

This guide compares the two on the things that tend to matter in practice. Platform features and plan details change regularly, so check the current position with each provider before committing.

The short version

If this describes youUsually a better fit
Product range is your main revenue and likely to growShopify
You sell across several channels (marketplaces, social, in person)Shopify
You need variants, stock across locations or wholesale pricingShopify
You are a creative, consultant or studio selling a few items or servicesSquarespace
Bookings, portfolios and blog content matter more than the catalogueSquarespace
You want one tidy tool and minimal add-onsSquarespace

Selling features

Shopify's whole admin is organised around products, orders, customers and fulfilment. Variants, inventory, discounts, abandoned checkout recovery, shipping rules and tax settings are core features rather than extras. It also connects to a wide range of sales channels and point-of-sale hardware, which suits brands selling at markets or in a shop as well as online.

Squarespace handles the essentials well for smaller catalogues: product pages, variants, digital products, simple subscriptions and discount codes. Where it can feel limiting is in more complex scenarios, such as large catalogues, detailed product filtering, multi-location stock or bespoke checkout logic.

Design and content

Squarespace's strength is design out of the box. Its templates are polished, its editor is visual and its blogging, portfolio and gallery tools are pleasant to use. For a brand where imagery and storytelling carry the sale, that matters.

Shopify themes have improved a great deal and the theme editor now allows plenty of layout control without code. For deeper changes, Shopify's theme language lets a developer customise almost anything. Its blog is functional rather than inspiring, which is fine for most stores but less appealing if content is central to your marketing. Our Shopify theme customisation guide covers what is possible.

Apps and extensibility

Shopify has a very large app ecosystem covering reviews, subscriptions, loyalty, bundles, product options, translation and much more. That flexibility is useful, but every app adds a monthly cost and some weight to your pages, so it pays to be selective. Our Shopify speed guide explains why.

Squarespace has a smaller set of extensions. That keeps things simple but means fewer options if you hit a gap later.

SEO

Both platforms cover the basics: editable page titles and descriptions, clean URLs, sitemaps and mobile-friendly templates. Neither will rank a site on its own; content, structure and links do that. Shopify's fixed URL structure (such as /products/ and /collections/) is sometimes raised as a drawback, but it rarely stops a well-run store ranking. See our Shopify SEO guide and ecommerce SEO checklist.

Running costs

Both charge a monthly subscription, and both have payment processing fees. With Shopify, the total cost often depends more on which apps and theme you choose than on the base plan. With Squarespace, you need a commerce-capable plan to sell. Compare the current plan pages directly and include apps, payment fees and any design or development work in your sums. For build budgets, see our Shopify website cost guide or our pricing page.

Switching later

Moving platforms is possible but rarely painless. Products, customers and orders can usually be exported, but design, URLs, redirects and integrations need rebuilding. If there is a realistic chance your catalogue will grow significantly in the next few years, starting on the platform built for that scale can save a migration. If you already need to move, our guide to redesigning without losing rankings covers the SEO side.

Payments, checkout and UK selling details

Both platforms offer their own payment processing alongside support for other providers, and both handle common card and wallet payments. Shopify's checkout is one of its most developed features and is shared across a very large number of stores, which means shoppers often find it familiar. Customisation of the checkout itself is restricted on standard plans, with more control available on higher tiers.

For UK sellers, practical details matter as much as headline features. Think about how each platform handles VAT-inclusive pricing, shipping zones for the UK and abroad, Royal Mail and courier integrations, and selling to customers in Northern Ireland or the EU, where rules differ. Both platforms provide settings for these, but the depth and flexibility vary, so test the specific scenarios you need. Tax treatment is ultimately your responsibility, so confirm your settings with your accountant rather than relying on platform defaults.

Day-to-day management

The platform you choose also shapes how your team spends its time. In Shopify, product imports and bulk edits through spreadsheets are straightforward, which helps when you have hundreds of products or frequent price changes. Order management, refunds, customer records and reporting sit in one place, and staff accounts can be given different permissions.

Squarespace keeps website editing, commerce, email campaigns and scheduling in a single, consistent interface. For a small team where the same person writes the blog, updates the shop and answers enquiries, that unified feel can be a genuine advantage. The trade-off is less depth in each area as the business grows.

Three example scenarios

These illustrations are hypothetical, but they show how the decision tends to play out.

A homeware brand with a growing range

A brand launching with forty products and plans to add more each season, selling at pop-up markets and considering marketplaces, will usually be better served by Shopify. Inventory, point of sale and channel connections are central to how it operates.

An illustrator selling prints and commissions

An illustrator whose portfolio wins commissions, with a small print shop on the side, may find Squarespace the more natural fit. The portfolio does the selling, and the shop needs only basic features.

A consultancy adding digital products

A consultancy selling a few guides or templates alongside its services might choose either. If bookings and content drive the business, Squarespace keeps things tidy. If digital products are expected to become a significant revenue line, Shopify gives more room to grow.

Common mistakes when choosing

  • Choosing on template looks alone. Templates can be changed; platform limitations are harder to work around.
  • Ignoring the cost of apps. A low base plan can become a much higher monthly bill once essential add-ons are included.
  • Planning only for launch day. Consider where the business might be in two or three years.
  • Not testing the admin. Both platforms offer trials. Add a few real products and process a test order before deciding.

Questions to settle before you decide

  • How many products will you sell in two years' time, and how many variants each?
  • Will you sell anywhere other than your website?
  • How important is blog and portfolio content to winning customers?
  • Who will run the site day to day, and how comfortable are they with a more involved admin?
  • Do you need features like subscriptions, B2B pricing or bookings?

FAQs

Is Shopify better than Squarespace for selling online?

For businesses where products are the main source of revenue, Shopify is usually stronger because it is built around ecommerce. Squarespace can work well for smaller catalogues alongside a content-led site.

Is Squarespace good enough for a small online shop?

Often, yes, particularly if you sell a limited range and value design and simplicity. Check that it supports the specific features you need, such as subscriptions or the shipping rules you want.

Which is easier to use?

Many people find Squarespace quicker to learn because there is less to configure. Shopify has more settings because it does more, but its admin is well organised once you are familiar with it.

Can I move from Squarespace to Shopify later?

Yes. Core data can usually be exported and imported, but the design, redirects and integrations need to be rebuilt, so plan the move carefully to protect search traffic.

Which is better for SEO?

Neither has a decisive advantage. Both handle technical basics; rankings depend far more on content, site structure and links.
