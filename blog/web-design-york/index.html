Web Design in York: Websites for Heritage, Visitor and Food Businesses

Web design for York attractions, guest houses, tearooms and food producers: seasonal content, accessible visitor information, bookings and online shops.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • York visitor businesses need sites that handle seasonal change, from opening hours to events, without the owner relying on a developer for every update.
  • Clear access information matters for historic buildings, where steps, narrow spaces and uneven floors are common and visitors want to plan ahead.
  • Prism Works Studio works with York businesses remotely, planning, writing and building sites over video calls and shared drafts.

Web design in York often starts with a building. A tearoom in a timber-framed house near the Shambles, a guest house in a Victorian terrace off Bootham, a small museum inside the city walls or a café beside the river all trade partly on character and history. The website has to convey that atmosphere, but it also has to answer very practical questions for visitors who may never have been to York before: when are you open, how do I get there, can I book, and will I be able to get in with a pushchair or a wheelchair? Food producers and shops selling York-made goods face a further question: how do we keep selling to people once they have gone home? Prism Works Studio works with York businesses remotely, so planning, writing and design reviews happen over video calls and shared documents.

Our Edinburgh guide covers festival-driven tourism. This guide focuses on heritage settings, year-round seasonality and turning visitors into repeat online customers.

Designing for a year that changes shape

A York visitor business may have quite different trading patterns across the year: busy summer weekends, school holidays, the run-up to Christmas, quieter winter weekdays and special event periods. The website should make those changes easy to reflect.

  • Editable opening hours with space for seasonal patterns and one-off closures.
  • An events or what's on section that can be updated by staff and hides past events automatically.
  • Seasonal homepage panels that can be swapped to promote a Christmas menu, a summer tour or a winter offer.
  • A clear notice area for short-term messages such as weather closures or building works, which can be removed easily afterwards.

The aim is that the owner or a member of staff can make these changes in minutes. Our guide to website maintenance covers who should own which updates.

Accessibility information for historic buildings

Historic buildings are part of York's appeal, but they often come with steps, narrow doorways, steep staircases and uneven floors. Visitors with mobility needs, parents with pushchairs and older visitors want to know this before they arrive, not at the door. A good access page is honest and specific.

TopicWhat to describe
EntranceSteps, ramps, door widths and any alternative entrance
InsideWhich floors and rooms are step-free, seating availability and lighting
FacilitiesToilets, accessible toilets and baby changing, and where they are
Getting thereNearest drop-off point, Blue Badge parking options and walking routes from the station
SupportWhether assistance dogs are welcome and how to contact you with questions

Photos of entrances and key areas help people judge for themselves. The website itself should also be accessible, with readable text, good contrast and forms that work with a keyboard. Our website accessibility guide explains the basics.

Bookings for tours, rooms and tables

Many York businesses take bookings of some kind: guest rooms, tables for afternoon tea, places on a ghost walk or slots at a small attraction. Visitors increasingly expect to book on a phone, and a booking process that is slow or confusing can send them elsewhere.

  1. Show availability clearly, ideally without forcing someone to fill in details first.
  2. State prices, what is included and any deposit before payment.
  3. Explain cancellation and amendment terms in plain words.
  4. Send a confirmation email with the date, time, meeting point or address and what to bring.

Our guide to online booking systems compares approaches. Guest houses and small hotels may also find our guides to hotel and B&B websites and holiday let websites useful.

Telling the history without burying the essentials

A building's history can be a genuine draw, and many York businesses have stories worth telling. The risk is putting a long history on the homepage where visitors are trying to find opening times. A better approach is to lead with the practical information and a short, evocative introduction, then give the history its own page with good photography.

Keep historical claims accurate. If dates or stories are traditional rather than documented, say so. Visitors with a real interest in history will appreciate the honesty, and it protects you from awkward corrections later.

Food and drink businesses: from visitor to repeat customer

York and the surrounding Yorkshire countryside support a range of food and drink businesses: bakers, confectioners, tearooms selling their own blends, butchers, cheesemakers, breweries and distilleries. Many meet customers in person first, at a shop, café or market, and an online shop lets those customers keep buying after they leave.

To make that work:

  • Make the online shop easy to find from the homepage and on printed materials such as receipts, packaging and menus.
  • Choose products that travel well and explain delivery times, packaging and any temperature-sensitive handling.
  • Offer gifts and hampers with gift messages, which suit visitors who want to send a taste of York to someone else. Our guide to gift cards and vouchers covers related options.
  • Collect email sign-ups with consent so you can tell past visitors about seasonal products.

Food businesses selling online need to show allergen and ingredient information. Rules on food information, including for distance selling, are set out by the Food Standards Agency, and alcohol sellers need appropriate licensing and age checks. Check the current requirements for your products rather than relying on a website template. Our food and drink ecommerce guide and bakery website design guide go into more detail.

Cafés and tearooms

For a café or tearoom, the website often has a simple job: show the menu, opening times and location, and take bookings if you offer them. Menus should be text rather than an image or a PDF, so they work on phones and can be read by search engines and screen readers. If you offer afternoon tea or seasonal menus, give them their own pages. Our café website design guide covers this in depth.

Local and visitor search

York businesses compete for visitor attention with large attractions and booking platforms. You will rarely outrank those for broad searches, but you can be found for more specific ones.

  • Keep your Google Business Profile accurate, including seasonal hours, photos and booking links.
  • Create pages for specific experiences, such as afternoon tea, group bookings or private tours, rather than one page listing everything.
  • Answer visitor questions directly, such as whether children or dogs are welcome and how far you are from the station.
  • Encourage genuine reviews and respond to them politely, without offering incentives that breach platform rules.

Our local SEO guide and hospitality marketing guide explain more.

Photography for heritage settings

Old buildings can be hard to photograph well: low ceilings, small windows and mixed lighting. Investing in good photography of the interior, the food or products and the setting tends to pay off across the website, booking platforms and social media. Show the space as it really is, including accessible areas, so photos support the access information rather than contradict it.

Group and private bookings

School trips, coach parties, hen and stag groups, corporate visits and private hire can all matter to York visitor businesses. Group organisers need different information from individual visitors: capacity, group rates or how to request a quote, risk assessment information for schools, coach drop-off points and dietary arrangements for larger parties. A dedicated groups page with a simple enquiry form keeps these requests separate from everyday bookings.

FAQs

How do I keep seasonal opening hours up to date?

Choose a site where staff can edit hours and notices easily, update your Google Business Profile at the same time, and set reminders before each seasonal change.

What should a historic venue include on its access page?

Describe entrances, steps, step-free areas, toilets, parking and drop-off options, with photos. Be specific and honest so visitors can judge for themselves.

Should a York food shop sell online?

It can help you keep selling to visitors after they go home. Start with products that travel well, explain delivery clearly and check food information rules for distance selling.

Can I put my menu on the website as a PDF?

You can, but a text menu on the page is better for phones, search engines and screen readers. Keep a PDF only as an optional download.

How much history should go on the homepage?

A short introduction is enough. Put practical information first and give the full history its own page, keeping claims accurate.
