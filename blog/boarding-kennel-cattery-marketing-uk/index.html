Boarding Kennel and Cattery Marketing in the UK: Earning Owners' Trust

Boarding kennel and cattery marketing in the UK is about trust: clear licence details, honest photos, easy booking, reviews and local search done well.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Owners are choosing who looks after a family member, so transparency about facilities, routines and licensing outweighs clever slogans.
  • Local search, a complete Google Business Profile and steady reviews drive most enquiries for boarding kennels and catteries.
  • Peak holiday periods book up early, so marketing effort should go into the months before summer, Easter and Christmas.

Boarding kennel and cattery marketing in the UK is mainly about earning trust before an owner ever visits. People are handing over a much-loved pet, often for a week or more, so they look for clear information about the facilities, daily routines, licensing and staff care, then check reviews and ask friends. Good marketing makes all of that easy to find and believe, and then makes booking a stay or a visit straightforward.

This guide is for independent boarding kennels, catteries and combined pet hotels. It covers the website, local search, reviews, seasonal planning and where paid advertising earns its keep. We are a studio that builds websites and runs search and paid campaigns, so the focus is on the practical steps that bring in enquiries rather than broad brand theory.

How owners choose where to board a pet

Most owners start with a search such as "dog kennels near me", "cattery in Norwich" or "dog boarding near Gatwick". They compare the businesses in the map results, read reviews and look at photos. Many then visit the website to see the accommodation, check vaccination requirements and find out whether there is space on their dates. A large share will want to visit in person before the first stay.

The emotional side is important. Owners worry about whether their pet will be stressed, how much exercise or attention it will get, and what happens if it becomes unwell. Marketing that answers those worries calmly and specifically will outperform anything that simply says "your pet is in safe hands".

Boarding kennel and cattery marketing: the website

A kennel or cattery website does not need to be large. It needs to be honest, quick to load on a phone and complete. The pages below cover most of what owners want to know.

Accommodation pages

Show the actual kennels, runs, pens or suites with real photos, not stock images. Explain the size of each unit, heating and ventilation, outdoor access, bedding and whether pets from the same household can share. If you offer different levels of accommodation, a simple comparison table helps owners choose.

Daily routine

A page that walks through a typical day reassures owners more than any slogan. Include feeding times, exercise or play sessions, how medication is handled, and how you settle nervous or elderly animals. If you send photo updates during a stay, say so.

Licensing and requirements

In England, boarding for cats and dogs is a licensable activity under the animal welfare licensing rules, and similar regimes apply in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Many operators show their licence details and any star rating they have been awarded. State your vaccination requirements, flea and worming expectations and what owners should bring. Always check the current wording with your local authority before publishing anything about ratings or inspections.

Booking and enquiries

Some kennels take bookings online; others prefer an enquiry form followed by a phone call, especially for first-time guests. Either approach works if it is clear. Our guide to online booking systems explains the options, and our advice on website contact forms helps if you prefer enquiries. Ask only for what you need at first: dates, number and type of pets, and contact details.

Trust signals

Owners look for proof that you are a real, caring business. Useful signals include licence details, a clear address and visiting hours, reviews, photos of the team at work (names optional) and a straightforward explanation of what happens if a pet needs a vet. Our guide on website trust signals covers more ideas.

Local SEO for kennels and catteries

Because almost every booking is local, search visibility within a reasonable drive is the core of your marketing. Our local SEO guide explains the fundamentals.

  • Google Business Profile: choose the most accurate category, list services such as dog boarding, cat boarding and day care, add opening and collection times, and upload recent photos. Our Google Business Profile guide walks through it.
  • Service pages: separate pages for dog boarding, cat boarding and any extras, such as day care or grooming, help you rank for each type of search.
  • Area content: mention the towns and villages you serve naturally on the contact or about page. Avoid creating thin near-duplicate pages for every postcode.
  • Consistent details: keep your business name, address and phone number the same across your site, your profile and any directories.

Reviews and word of mouth

Reviews matter enormously in this sector, because owners are looking for evidence that other pets were happy. The best moment to ask is at collection, when the owner is relieved and pleased. A follow-up text or email a day later with a direct review link works well. Respond to every review, and handle complaints calmly and factually, without sharing private details. Our guide on responding to Google reviews has wording examples.

Never offer discounts in return for reviews, and do not write or commission fake ones. Both break platform rules and UK consumer protection law.

Seasonal planning

Demand for boarding follows the school holidays closely. The table below shows how marketing effort can be timed around the year.

PeriodDemand patternMarketing focus
January to MarchQuieter, but summer bookings beginRemind past customers to book summer dates; refresh photos and website content
Easter and May half termShort spikesMake availability clear; collect reviews from each stay
July and AugustPeak, often fully bookedManage waiting lists; pause ads if full
September to NovemberShoulder seasonPromote day care and off-peak stays; start Christmas reminders
Christmas and New YearPeakClear cut-off dates and collection times on the website

If you are full in August, there is little point in paying for ads. Use the quieter months to build the search visibility and email list that fill the peaks.

Paid advertising: when it helps

Paid search can be useful for new businesses, for filling shoulder-season gaps or for launching a new service such as dog day care. Keep targeting tight to your realistic catchment, add negative keywords for unrelated searches such as kennel building supplies, and track enquiries or bookings as conversions. Our guide to Google Ads for small businesses explains how to keep a modest budget under control.

Social ads on Facebook and Instagram can work for awareness, especially with friendly photos of resident pets, used with owners' permission. Any offers should state their conditions clearly, in line with the CAP Code.

Email and repeat customers

Most boarding businesses rely heavily on returning owners. With consent collected at booking, a short email or text before each holiday season can fill much of your calendar before new customers even start searching. Under PECR, marketing messages to individuals generally need opt-in consent, so keep the marketing tick box separate from the booking terms.

Useful messages include early-booking reminders, updates on new facilities and a note about changes to vaccination requirements. Keep them short and practical.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using stock photos of dogs and cats instead of your actual facilities.
  • Hiding vaccination and visiting requirements, so owners find out too late.
  • Making claims you cannot back up, such as "the best cattery in the county".
  • Letting the Google Business Profile go out of date, especially opening and collection hours.
  • Not updating availability, which leads to frustrated enquiries during peaks.

FAQs

What does boarding kennel and cattery marketing involve?

It usually covers a trustworthy website, a complete Google Business Profile, steady reviews, local SEO, seasonal email reminders and occasional paid ads. The aim is to reassure owners and make booking simple.

Should a cattery show its licence on its website?

Many operators do, because owners look for it. Check the current guidance from your local authority about how licence details and any star rating should be presented before you publish them.

How do I get more reviews for my kennels?

Ask at collection, when owners are happiest, and follow up with a direct review link by text or email. Make it easy, and never offer incentives in exchange for reviews.

Do kennels need online booking?

Not always. Many prefer an enquiry form and a call for new customers, then easier online booking for returning ones. What matters is that the process is clear and availability is accurate.

When should a boarding kennel advertise?

Advertise when you have space to fill, typically in shoulder seasons or when launching a new service. During peak periods you are often full, so ads would waste budget.

How long does local SEO take for a cattery?

Improvements to your profile and website can show early movement, but local SEO results typically take three to six months to build, depending on how competitive your area is.
