Why SEO matters in Gothenburg

Gothenburg is Sweden's second city and home to Scandinavia's largest port. Its economy leans heavily on manufacturing, automotive, engineering, logistics and shipping, alongside a lively scene in retail, food, events and technology. Many local companies sell far beyond Sweden, so their websites must work for Swedish customers and for international buyers searching in English.

Swedish searchers are highly digital, and many are comfortable reading English. That does not mean English pages alone are enough. People often search in Swedish first, especially for local services, and pages written in the searcher's language usually perform better. The right mix depends on who your customers are and where they search.

Technical SEO foundations

Many Gothenburg company sites are large, built up over years and sometimes spread across several systems. That often leads to technical issues that hold rankings back:

  • Duplicate content from product filters, old campaign pages or parallel language sites.
  • Broken redirects left over from rebrands or platform moves.
  • Slow pages weighed down by large images, scripts and embedded tools.
  • Missing hreflang so Google shows the English page to Swedish searchers, or the reverse.
  • Thin product or capability pages that say too little to rank.

We start with an audit and fix the most important problems first. Our technical SEO audit checklist shows the process, and our guide to Core Web Vitals explains how Google measures page experience.

SEO for B2B and industrial companies

Industrial and engineering buyers search differently from consumers. They use specific technical terms, compare suppliers carefully and often involve several people in a decision. Good B2B SEO in Gothenburg focuses on:

  • One clear page for each product line, capability or service.
  • Technical detail buyers need, such as materials, standards and applications.
  • Sector pages showing how your offer fits automotive, marine, energy or logistics.
  • Simple ways to request a quote or download specifications.

We write these pages in English, which suits international buyers, and your translator adapts them into Swedish where needed. Our B2B marketing page explains how we approach this sector.

Local SEO in Gothenburg

For businesses that serve people in person, such as clinics, gyms, shops, restaurants and trades, the Google map pack matters most. We complete and maintain your Google Business Profile, check that your details match across your site and directories, and set up a fair process for gathering and answering reviews. If you serve areas such as Mölndal, Partille or Kungälv, well-written location pages can help, as long as each contains genuinely useful local information.

Our guide to local SEO covers the principles, which carry over well to Sweden.

Ecommerce SEO

Gothenburg has a strong retail and fashion tradition, and many brands sell online across the Nordics and the EU. Ecommerce SEO focuses on category pages, product titles, structured data, internal links and handling out-of-stock products sensibly. If you run Shopify, our guide to Shopify in Sweden covers setup, and our guide to Shopify Markets in Europe explains selling into several countries.

Combining SEO with paid search

SEO grows over months, so many businesses run paid search alongside it. Google Ads brings enquiries sooner and shows which search terms convert best, which then guides SEO priorities. Content for search and AI answers

Search is changing as AI tools summarise answers. Pages that open with a clear, direct answer, use plain headings and keep facts consistent are easier for both search engines and AI assistants to quote. We write content with that structure as standard and make sure your business details, services and locations are described the same way everywhere.

Common SEO mistakes

  • Relying on English alone. Swedish customers often search in Swedish first.
  • Ignoring old redirects. Years of links and rankings can be lost after a rebuild.
  • Generic product pages. Buyers and search engines need specific detail.
  • No conversion tracking. Traffic means little without knowing what drives enquiries.
  • Short-term thinking. SEO rewards steady work over many months.

Working with a remote studio

Sweden is usually one hour ahead of the UK, so working hours overlap well. We agree a regular call slot, keep a shared task list and send written updates. Your team approves content and supplies translations and images, and we handle the technical work, strategy and reporting. This keeps the work transparent and easy to follow, even without a local office.

Before you choose any agency, ask how they will measure progress, what happens in the first ninety days and how they handle multilingual sites. Be cautious about promises of guaranteed rankings or cheap link packages, which can do more harm than good.

Building authority and links

Search engines still use links from other websites as a sign of trust. For Gothenburg businesses, the best links usually come from genuine relationships rather than paid schemes. Industry associations, trade fairs, supplier and partner pages, local business networks and sector publications can all be good sources. Useful resources on your own site, such as detailed guides, specification sheets or practical tools, also attract links naturally over time.

We help you find realistic link opportunities and create content worth linking to. We avoid bulk link packages, private networks and anything that breaks Google's guidelines, because the risk of a penalty outweighs any short-term gain. Digital PR, where your team shares expertise or useful information with relevant publications, can also build both links and brand awareness.

Measuring SEO progress

Rankings are useful signals, but the real goal is more customers. We track organic sessions, enquiries, quote requests, calls and online sales from search. Google Search Console shows which queries bring impressions and clicks, and Google Analytics 4 shows what visitors do once they arrive. Where you have Swedish and English versions, we report on each separately so you can see where translation or content gaps are holding pages back.

Each monthly report explains what we did, what changed and what comes next, in plain language. We also hold a regular review call, so you can ask questions and adjust priorities as your business changes, for example when you launch a new product line or enter a new export market.
