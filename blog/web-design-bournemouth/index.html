Why Bournemouth websites have a different job

Bournemouth combines a large visitor economy with a year-round business base. Hotels, guest houses, restaurants, bars and attractions along the seafront compete hard in spring and summer. At the same time, the conurbation has a substantial financial services and digital sector, a university, language schools, a large retirement population and thousands of trades and professional firms serving residents in every season.

That mix shapes the website. A hospitality business needs to win bookings directly rather than hand every sale to a third-party platform. A professional firm needs to look established and make it easy to request a consultation. A trade needs a phone number that works in one tap. The design, structure and content all follow from who you are trying to reach and what you want them to do.

Designing for seasonal demand

Seasonality is one of the biggest factors for many Bournemouth businesses, and a good website plans for it rather than reacting each spring.

  • Plan content ahead of the season. People start researching summer breaks months in advance. Pages for events, packages and offers should be live well before demand peaks.
  • Make availability obvious. If you take bookings, put the booking link or availability check where visitors expect it, on every key page.
  • Keep winter pages useful. Out-of-season offers, local events, conferences and longer stays can keep enquiries coming in the quieter months.
  • Update opening hours promptly. Nothing frustrates a visitor more than arriving at a closed door that the website said was open.

Compliance and accessibility basics

Keep this general and check official guidance or your own adviser for anything specific to your business.

  • UK GDPR. If you collect personal data through forms, bookings or accounts, you need a clear privacy notice.
  • PECR. Non-essential cookies, including analytics and advertising tags, generally need consent before they load, and marketing emails have their own consent rules.
  • Accessibility. Readable text, strong colour contrast, keyboard navigation and descriptive image text help more people use your site.
  • Sector rules. Health, finance and some other sectors have extra rules on website claims. Approval of that wording rests with you.

Common website mistakes we see

When businesses come to us for a new site, the same problems appear again and again. Avoiding them is often worth more than any design trend.

  • A homepage that tries to say everything. Visitors should understand what you do within a few seconds. Detail belongs on service pages.
  • Hidden contact details. If people have to hunt for a phone number or form, many will simply leave and try a competitor.
  • Thin or duplicated pages. Pages that repeat the same text with a different town name rarely help rankings and can undermine trust.
  • Oversized images. Uncompressed photos are one of the most common reasons a site feels slow on mobile.
  • No tracking. Without analytics and conversion tracking, it is impossible to tell which pages or campaigns bring enquiries.
  • Out-of-date information. Old prices, staff who have left or last year's opening hours quietly damage credibility.
