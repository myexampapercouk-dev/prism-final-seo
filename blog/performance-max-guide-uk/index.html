Performance Max Guide for UK Ecommerce: How It Works, Asset Groups, Feeds and Pitfalls

Performance Max uses one campaign to show ads across Google using your feed, assets and goals. See how it works for UK stores, asset groups and pitfalls.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Performance Max is only as good as its inputs: conversion values, product feed, audience signals and creative assets.
  • Asset groups should be built around product themes or margin bands, not one group for the whole catalogue.
  • Protect against the common pitfalls: brand traffic inflating results, weak feeds, too little data and no exclusions.

Performance Max (PMax) is a Google Ads campaign type that uses one budget to show your ads across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps, with Google's automation choosing where, when and to whom. For UK ecommerce stores it has largely replaced Smart Shopping, and it works well when you feed it accurate conversion values, a strong product feed and good creative.

This Performance Max guide for UK ecommerce explains how the campaign type works, how to build asset groups, why the feed matters so much, and the pitfalls that catch most stores. For the wider picture of Shopping ads, see our Google Shopping ads guide.

How Performance Max works

You give Google a goal (usually conversion value at a target return on ad spend), a budget, a product feed from Merchant Center, creative assets and optional audience signals. Google then decides which channels, placements, searches and users to bid on in real time.

The trade-off is control for reach. You see less detail than in Standard Shopping or Search, and you cannot set keyword bids. Google has added more controls over time, such as brand exclusions, negative keywords at campaign level, search term insights and channel reporting, but PMax is still an automated campaign at heart.

Asset groups explained

An asset group is a bundle of creative, listing groups (which products to show) and audience signals. One campaign can hold several asset groups. Think of each one as a theme.

How to split them

  • By product category. Dresses, coats and accessories each get their own imagery and headlines.
  • By margin or price band. Premium products with premium messaging, entry products with value messaging.
  • By audience. Gifting versus self-purchase, trade versus consumer, where the message genuinely differs.

Avoid one asset group with every product and generic copy. Google will still run it, but your YouTube and Display ads will say little about any specific product.

What each group needs

  • Up to 15 headlines and 5 long headlines, with product benefits, delivery and returns messages written for UK shoppers.
  • Landscape, square and portrait images, ideally lifestyle shots as well as packshots.
  • At least one video. If you do not supply one, Google may generate one from your images, which rarely looks good.
  • Descriptions that cover offer, proof and practical details such as free delivery thresholds or Klarna and Clearpay options where you offer them.
  • Final URL expansion settings chosen deliberately, so traffic is not sent to pages you would rather avoid.

Audience signals

Signals are starting hints, not targeting. Customer lists, site visitors and custom segments based on search terms help Google find the right people sooner. They matter most for new campaigns.

The product feed is the foundation

For retail PMax, most spend still flows through Shopping placements, so the feed decides a large part of performance. Titles should lead with the words people search, attributes should be complete, and GTINs should be present where products have them.

Custom labels let you split listing groups by margin, best seller status or season, which is how you stop low-margin products eating the budget. Merchant Center needs regular checks for disapprovals and policy issues. Common Performance Max pitfalls

PitfallSymptomFix
Brand traffic inflating resultsHigh ROAS but new customer sales flatApply brand exclusions and run a separate brand search campaign
Inaccurate conversion valuesReported revenue does not match ordersFix purchase tracking and remove duplicate or soft conversions from goals
Everything in one groupA few products take all spendSplit asset groups and listing groups by category or margin
Too little dataCampaign never stabilisesConsolidate campaigns and loosen targets until conversion volume builds
Weak or auto-generated videoPoor engagement off Shopping placementsSupply short, product-led videos per asset group
Frequent large changesPerformance swings after each editChange targets gradually and allow learning time

Two more are worth calling out. First, PMax reporting is generous to itself, so cross-check against GA4 and your store's own sales data. Second, it can drift into Display placements that look cheap but convert poorly, so check channel reports regularly.

Performance Max vs Standard Shopping

Performance Max usually wins on reach and on stores with plenty of conversion data. Standard Shopping still has a role when you need search term control, when data is thin, or when testing new products. Many UK stores run PMax as the main campaign with a Standard Shopping or Search campaign alongside for specific goals. A good Google Ads agency should explain which mix suits your account rather than defaulting to one.

How to read Performance Max results honestly

Because PMax blends channels, its headline numbers need context. A few habits make reporting more trustworthy.

  • Compare with your own sales. Check reported conversion value against orders in Shopify or WooCommerce for the same period. Large gaps point to tracking or attribution issues.
  • Look at new versus returning customers. If you can, enable new customer acquisition reporting or goals. A campaign that mostly reaches existing customers may be taking credit for sales that would have happened anyway.
  • Use channel performance reporting. See how much spend goes to Shopping, Search, YouTube and Display, and whether each channel earns its share.
  • Review product-level performance. Identify products that take spend without selling and products that sell but get little budget, then adjust listing groups or labels.
  • Allow for seasonality. UK retail has sharp peaks around Black Friday, Christmas and the January sales. Seasonality adjustments can help when you expect a short, predictable change in conversion rate.

When to scale budget

Scale when the campaign meets its target consistently and is limited by budget, not when one good week appears. Increase budgets in steps rather than doubling overnight, and watch whether efficiency holds. If returns fall sharply as spend rises, the campaign may have exhausted its best audiences, which is a signal to improve creative, the feed or the site rather than push harder.

When to pause or restructure

If a campaign has had several weeks of stable settings, accurate tracking and a sensible target yet still loses money, look at margins, pricing and landing pages before blaming the campaign type. PMax cannot make an uncompetitive offer profitable.

Performance Max setup checklist

  • Purchase conversion tracked with accurate values, deduplicated and checked against orders.
  • Consent mode configured for UK GDPR and PECR.
  • Merchant Center clean, with no major disapprovals.
  • Feed titles, attributes and custom labels optimised.
  • Asset groups split by theme, each with full creative and a video.
  • Audience signals added from customer lists and site visitors.
  • Brand exclusions set, with brand search handled separately.
  • Final URL expansion reviewed and unwanted pages excluded.
  • Realistic target ROAS based on margin, not hope.
  • Channel and search term insights reviewed monthly.

FAQs

What is Performance Max in Google Ads?

Performance Max is a goal-based Google Ads campaign that uses one budget and automated bidding to show ads across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps from a single campaign.

Is Performance Max good for UK ecommerce?

It can be, when conversion tracking is accurate, the product feed is strong and there is enough conversion volume for the automation to learn. With weak inputs it tends to spend on easy wins such as brand searches.

How many asset groups should I have?

Enough to give each product theme relevant creative, but not so many that data is spread thin. For many stores that means a handful of groups by category or margin band.

Can I add negative keywords to Performance Max?

Google now supports campaign-level negative keywords and brand exclusions in Performance Max. Account-level negatives also apply. Check the current options in your account, as Google updates them.

How long does Performance Max take to learn?

Expect an initial learning period of a few weeks, and avoid big changes during it. Stable paid results typically take three to six months to establish.

Do I still need Standard Shopping?

Not always, but it remains useful for testing, for low-data accounts and when you need search term control. Many stores run both for different purposes.
