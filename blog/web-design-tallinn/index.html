Looking for web design in Tallinn? What an Estonian business website needs, English and Estonian pages, consent, accessibility, timelines and fees in pounds.

Web design for a Tallinn business should give you a fast, credible website that wins enquiries or sales from international buyers and local customers alike. For many Estonian companies that means English pages that compete globally, Estonian pages for the home market, clean mobile templates, honest consent and a site your team can update without help.

What Tallinn businesses need from a website

Tallinn is Estonia's capital and its main business centre, known for a strong technology scene and a highly digital public sector. Software start-ups, IT service firms, logistics and maritime companies, manufacturers, professional services, tourism and hospitality businesses all have a base there. Estonia's e-Residency programme also means some companies registered in Estonia are run by founders living elsewhere. That shapes what a website needs to do:

  • English-first for international sales. Many Tallinn firms sell abroad from day one, so English pages often matter as much as Estonian ones.
  • Estonian for the home market. Local customers, partners and recruits expect clear Estonian pages.
  • Other languages where useful. Some businesses also serve Russian-speaking or Finnish customers, depending on their market.
  • Credibility for remote buyers. Buyers who will never visit Tallinn need clear services, process, company details and contact routes.
  • Speed and simplicity. Digital-savvy visitors expect pages to load quickly and work smoothly on mobile.

Designing for English, Estonian and more

Estonian words can be long, especially compound terms, so menus, buttons and headings need room to breathe. We design components with that length in mind and test real Estonian copy rather than placeholder text before launch. Fonts must display every Estonian character properly in headings, forms and email notifications, or names and addresses in enquiries can be corrupted.

Each language gets its own URLs, titles and meta descriptions, with hreflang tags so search engines show the right version to each searcher. Not every page needs every language. A software firm may lead with English and add Estonian for careers and contact pages, while a local service business may need full Estonian coverage first. Our multilingual website design guide explains language switchers, URL structures and how to keep each version manageable.

Prism writes English copy in-house. For Estonian and any other language, we give your team or a native-speaking translator a clear brief for each page, including structure, headings and search terms, then check the finished pages in the build.

Websites for Tallinn tech and SaaS firms

Tallinn's technology businesses often need more than a brochure site. A software or SaaS website usually has to explain a product clearly, show pricing or a route to a demo, support sign-ups and give investors and recruits a credible picture of the company. Key pages often include:

  • A clear home page. What the product does, who it is for and the next step, stated in the first screen.
  • Feature and use-case pages. One page per main use case so buyers can find what fits them.
  • Pricing or demo pages. A simple route to start a trial or book a call.
  • Documentation links and security information. Answers to the questions technical buyers ask before they commit.
  • Careers pages. Built as carefully as product pages for firms hiring internationally.

Our guide to SaaS website design covers these pages in more depth.

Choosing a platform

We build on the platform that suits the business:

  • WordPress for content-led sites, service businesses and firms that publish news, guides or job listings regularly.
  • Shopify for online shops, including Estonian brands selling to customers across the EU.
  • Static builds for small, very fast sites that rarely change, such as product launch pages or company profiles.

Whichever you choose, your team should be able to update text, images and listings without calling a developer. We set up editing roles and give you a short handover.

Consent, privacy and accessibility

Estonian websites fall under the GDPR for personal data, and the EU ePrivacy rules mean analytics and marketing cookies normally need consent before they load. That makes the consent banner part of the design. A banner that is clear, quick to use and honest about choices keeps visitors on the page and keeps your data usable. Our guide to cookie banner design explains our approach.

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing products and services, including a wide range of online shops and booking services. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but building to WCAG AA, the level referenced by the harmonised standard EN 301 549, is good practice for any business. Our European Accessibility Act guide sets out the basics. We design and build the privacy page, consent banner and accessible templates, but legal wording should come from your legal adviser, who has final approval. Check current official guidance before launch.

Performance, SEO and handover

Every build includes compressed images and lean templates for quick loading, clean headings and titles for each page and language, an XML sitemap, sensible internal links, analytics set up with consent, and full ownership of the domain, hosting, code and content in your name. If you are replacing an older site, our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings explains how we protect the search traffic you already have.

Common problems on Estonian business websites

  • An English site with no Estonian version, or the reverse. Local or international visitors are left with pages they cannot easily read.
  • Machine translation left unchecked. Awkward phrasing that undermines trust with buyers.
  • Template sites that look like everyone else's. Common start-up themes that make it hard to stand out.
  • Unclear next steps. Visitors cannot see whether to book a demo, request a quote or apply for a job.

Each is fixed with planning rather than a bigger budget.

Web design fees and timelines

Our prices are listed on our pricing page. A small business website is typically £1,000 to £2,000, and an ecommerce website £1,500 to £3,500, depending on pages, products and integrations. We quote in pounds, and quotes in other currencies are available on request. Translation is arranged and paid separately if you do not have an in-house writer.

FAQs

Do you have a web design office in Tallinn?

No. Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Estonia. We work remotely by video call, shared designs and staging links, two hours behind Tallinn time.

Can you build an English and Estonian website?

Yes. We plan, design and build each language version. English copy is written in-house, and Estonian copy comes from your team or a native-speaking translator, which we then check in the build.

How much does web design in Tallinn cost with your studio?

A small website is typically £1,000 to £2,000 and an ecommerce site £1,500 to £3,500, quoted in pounds with other currencies on request. Translation costs are separate.

Can you design websites for e-Residency companies?

Yes. If your company is registered in Estonia but run from elsewhere, we can work with you remotely in English, wherever you are based.

How long does a website take to build?

Most websites take three to five weeks once content is agreed. Ecommerce builds usually take five to twelve weeks, and multilingual sites may take slightly longer.

Does the European Accessibility Act apply to my website?

It applies to many consumer-facing products and services from June 2025, with an exemption for microenterprises providing services. We build to WCAG AA either way, and your legal adviser can confirm whether the Act applies to you.
