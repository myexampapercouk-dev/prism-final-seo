What London shoppers expect from an online shop

London customers are used to quick delivery options, smooth mobile checkouts and well-known payment methods. A shop that feels slow or unclear loses them to a competitor in one tap. In practice, that means:

  • Mobile-first browsing. Many shoppers browse on phones while commuting, so product pages and filters must work well on a small screen.
  • Clear delivery promises. Delivery cost, speed and any click-and-collect or local options should be visible before checkout, not revealed at the last step.
  • Familiar payments. Cards, wallets such as Apple Pay and Google Pay, and buy now pay later options such as Klarna or Clearpay where they suit your products.
  • Easy returns. A plain-English returns policy linked from product pages reduces hesitation. Our guide to delivery and returns pages covers what to include.
  • Trust signals. Genuine reviews, clear contact details and secure checkout cues matter more than ever in a crowded market.

The pages that make or break an ecommerce site

Good ecommerce web design is not just a striking homepage. Most sales happen on a small set of page types, and each needs careful design.

  • Homepage. Shows what you sell and who it is for within seconds, with clear routes into your main categories and current offers.
  • Category pages. Need useful filters, sensible sorting and product cards that show price, key details and stock clearly. Our guide to ecommerce category page design goes into detail.
  • Product pages. Need strong photography, honest descriptions, size or variant choices that are easy to use, delivery information and reviews.
  • Basket and checkout. Should be short, predictable and free of surprises, with guest checkout available.
  • Search. Shops with larger catalogues need site search that copes with typos and synonyms.
  • Information pages. Delivery, returns, size guides and contact pages answer the questions that stop people buying.

Product photography has a large effect on how premium a shop feels. Prism does not run photo shoots, but our ecommerce product photography guide explains how to brief a photographer so images fit the design.

Speed, SEO and tracking from day one

A beautiful shop that loads slowly on mobile data will underperform. We keep themes lean, limit apps and plugins, compress images and test speed on phones before launch. We also set up the foundations that let the shop grow through search and advertising:

  • Clean URLs, sensible headings and editable meta titles for products and categories.
  • Structured data for products where the platform supports it.
  • Google Analytics and conversion tracking for purchases, so later ad spend can be measured.
  • Redirects from any old shop, so existing rankings are protected.

Common ecommerce design mistakes to avoid

When we review existing London shops, the same problems come up again and again, and most are simple to avoid at the design stage:

  • Delivery costs hidden until checkout. Surprise costs are one of the most common reasons shoppers abandon a basket.
  • Too many apps or plugins. Each one adds scripts, slows pages and creates another thing to maintain.
  • Weak product information. Missing sizes, materials, care details or dimensions push shoppers to ask questions or leave.
  • Forced account creation. Requiring an account before checkout adds friction, so guest checkout should always be available.
  • Pop-ups on arrival. Large email sign-up pop-ups that cover the page on mobile frustrate visitors before they have seen a product.
  • No plan for out-of-stock items. Products that disappear or show errors waste search visibility and annoy returning customers.

Fixing these issues often improves sales without any extra ad spend, which is why we review them on every project, whether we are building a new shop or improving an existing one.
