About Page Design: How to Tell Your Story and Build Trust

How to write and design an about page that builds trust, explains what your business does and who it helps, and guides visitors towards their next step.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • An about page should explain who you help and how, not only your history.
  • Specific, honest detail builds more trust than vague claims or buzzwords.
  • Every about page should end with a clear next step for interested visitors.

The about page is one of the pages visitors check when they are deciding whether to trust a business. They want to know who is behind the website, what the business stands for and whether it is the right fit for them. Yet many about pages are either a short paragraph of vague statements or a long history that never gets to the point.

This guide explains what an about page is for, what to include, how to write it and how to design it so it builds confidence and moves people towards getting in touch or making a purchase.

What an about page is really for

Although the page is about you, it is really written for your visitors. People arrive with questions:

  • Who is this business?
  • What exactly do they do?
  • Do they understand people like me?
  • Can I trust them?
  • What should I do next?

A good about page answers those questions clearly. Your story matters, but mainly as evidence that you can help.

Start with who you help and how

Open with a short, clear statement of what the business does and who it serves. Avoid starting with a founding date or a broad mission statement. A visitor should understand within a few seconds whether they are in the right place.

For example, a short opening that names the type of customer, the problem you solve and the way you work is more useful than a general claim about being passionate or innovative.

Tell your story with purpose

Your background can build trust when it explains why you do what you do and how that benefits customers. Focus on the parts of the story that are relevant: why the business started, what you learned, and how that shapes your approach today.

Keep it concise. Visitors rarely need every milestone. A few well-chosen details are more memorable than a complete chronology.

Explain how you work

Many visitors want to know what working with you will be like. Describe your approach in practical terms. How do projects or orders start? How do you communicate? What can customers expect at each stage? For a remote business, explain how you work with clients without meeting in person, and what tools or routines keep things organised.

This kind of detail reduces uncertainty, which is often the biggest barrier to someone getting in touch.

Show your values through specifics

Values such as honesty or quality are easy to claim and hard to prove. Instead of listing them, show them. Describe the decisions you make, the standards you follow and the things you will not do. Specific examples are more convincing than adjectives.

Use real evidence carefully

Trust signals can strengthen an about page, but only if they are genuine. Examples include real customer feedback shared with permission, recognised memberships or accreditations you actually hold, and examples of work you can show. Never invent testimonials, figures or awards. Misleading claims can damage trust badly and may create legal problems, so if in doubt, leave it out or take advice.

Introduce the team where it helps

For many businesses, showing the people behind the brand helps visitors feel more comfortable. Team sections can include roles and a short line about what each person does. Keep it relevant and respectful of people's privacy, and only share what team members are happy to have published.

Some businesses prefer to present a brand rather than individuals. That is fine too, as long as the page still explains clearly who you are and how you work.

Write in a clear, human voice

The tone of the about page should match the rest of your site, but it is a good place to sound a little more personal. Write as you would speak to a customer. Avoid jargon and buzzwords. Use short paragraphs and plain language so the page is easy to scan.

Read it aloud before publishing. If a sentence sounds like something any business could say, try to make it more specific to yours.

Design for scanning

Most visitors will skim before they read. Design the page so key points stand out:

  • Use clear headings that summarise each section.
  • Keep paragraphs short.
  • Use images that are genuine and relevant, such as your workspace, products or team, rather than generic stock photography.
  • Highlight key points with simple lists where they fit.
  • Leave enough space so the page feels calm and readable.

Make it accessible

As with every page, accessibility matters. Use proper heading structure, give images meaningful alternative text, ensure good colour contrast and make sure any video has captions. If you include a team photo grid, make sure names and roles are available as text, not only within images.

Include a clear next step

An about page often reaches visitors at an important moment. They are interested but not yet committed. End the page with a clear next step, such as getting in touch, viewing services or browsing products. Without it, interested visitors may simply leave.

Make sure the about page is easy to reach too. A link in the main navigation and in the footer helps. Our guide to website footer design explains how footers support this kind of navigation.

About pages for online shops

For ecommerce businesses, the about page can answer questions that affect buying decisions: where products are made or sourced, how they are chosen, what the brand cares about and how customer service works. Link to practical pages such as delivery and returns information so shoppers can move from reassurance to action.

Keep it up to date

About pages are easy to forget once published. Review yours regularly. Check that team details, services, accreditations and descriptions of how you work are still accurate. Out of date information undermines the trust the page is meant to build.

Common about page mistakes

  • Focusing entirely on history while never explaining what you do.
  • Vague claims that could apply to any business.
  • Generic stock images that feel impersonal.
  • No call to action at the end.
  • Outdated team or service information.
  • Unverified or exaggerated claims.

Where the about page fits in your site

The about page works alongside your service pages, product pages and contact page. It should link naturally to them and be easy to reach from them. Clear site structure, including features like breadcrumb navigation on larger sites, helps visitors move between these pages as they build confidence.

A simple structure to follow

If you are starting from a blank page, a straightforward structure can help you organise your thoughts. It is not a rigid template, but it covers what most visitors want to know:

  1. A clear opening. One or two sentences on what you do and who you help.
  2. Why you exist. A short explanation of the problem you set out to solve or the gap you noticed.
  3. How you work. The practical steps, habits and standards customers can expect.
  4. Who is involved. The team or the people behind the brand, where appropriate.
  5. Proof. Genuine examples of work, feedback or credentials.
  6. Next step. A clear invitation to get in touch, explore services or start shopping.

You can reorder these sections to suit your business. A product brand might lead with the story behind its products, while a service business might lead with how it works with clients. What matters is that each section earns its place by answering a real question.

Gathering material for the page

Writing an about page can feel awkward, because talking about yourself rarely comes naturally. A useful approach is to gather material before you write. Note down the questions customers ask before they buy, the reasons existing customers chose you, and the things you explain repeatedly in calls or emails. These often reveal what the about page should cover.

It can also help to ask someone outside the business to read a draft. They will quickly notice where you have used insider language, skipped over something important or made a claim that feels unsupported.

Supporting search and AI answers

An about page is not usually a page that ranks for competitive search terms, but it still plays a role in how search engines and AI tools understand your business. Clear statements about what you do, where you operate and who you serve help them describe you accurately. Consistent business details across your about page, contact page and footer also support that understanding.

FAQs

How long should an about page be?

Long enough to answer visitors' key questions, and no longer. Many effective about pages are fairly concise, with clear sections that are easy to scan.

Should I include photos of my team?

Real photos can help build trust, but only include people who are happy to be shown. A brand-led page can work well too if it still explains clearly who you are.

Can I include customer testimonials on my about page?

Yes, if they are genuine and you have permission to use them. Never invent or alter testimonials.

What should the call to action be?

Choose the step that suits most visitors, such as getting in touch, viewing services or browsing products.

How often should I update my about page?

Review it whenever your services, team or approach changes, and check it periodically even if nothing major has changed.
