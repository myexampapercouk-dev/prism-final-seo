The Tulsa paid search market

Tulsa anchors a metro that stretches to Broken Arrow, Owasso, Bixby, Jenks, Sand Springs, Sapulpa and Claremore. Its economy mixes energy, aerospace and aviation maintenance, manufacturing, healthcare and a growing remote-work population. That variety shapes how people search:

  • Home services see sharp demand spikes after storms, hail and extreme temperatures. Roofers, restoration firms and HVAC companies need campaigns that can scale up quickly and stay disciplined.
  • Industrial and B2B firms attract fewer, more valuable searches with specific technical terms.
  • Healthcare, legal and professional services compete in auctions where clicks can be expensive.
  • Suburban searchers often name their own town, so location targeting and ad copy should reflect it.

Start with conversion tracking

Many accounts we review are optimising for clicks rather than leads, because tracking was never set up properly. Before increasing spend, we make sure Google Ads records the actions that matter: phone calls from ads, calls from your website, form submissions, bookings and, for online shops, purchases. Smart bidding strategies only work when they have accurate conversion data to learn from.

Our guides to Google Ads conversion tracking and enhanced conversions explain the setup in detail.

Location targeting across the Tulsa metro

Location settings are one of the most common sources of waste. By default, Google can show ads to people interested in your area, not only people in it. For most local service businesses, we set targeting to people located in or regularly in your chosen areas, and we decide carefully whether to include outlying towns such as Muskogee or Bartlesville.

We also review performance by location and adjust bids where some suburbs bring better leads than others. Our Google Ads location targeting guide covers the settings.

Keywords, negatives and search terms

We group keywords tightly by service so each ad and landing page matches the search closely. We then review search terms regularly and add negative keywords to block irrelevant searches. For a Tulsa roofer, that might mean blocking searches for jobs, training, DIY materials or roofing in other states. For an industrial supplier, it might mean blocking consumer searches altogether.

Read more in our negative keywords guide and our explanation of Quality Score, which affects how much you pay per click.

Storm season and seasonal demand

Oklahoma weather drives real swings in search demand. After hail or severe storms, searches for roof repair and restoration rise quickly and competition rises with them. We plan for this with separate campaigns or budgets that can be increased at short notice, ad copy ready to go, and clear daily caps so a surge does not drain the month's budget in days.

Home services firms should also be careful with claims in storm-related ads. Keep wording accurate and check any insurance-related statements with your own adviser, as approval of advertising claims rests with your business.

Performance Max and other campaign types

Search campaigns are usually the starting point for local service businesses because they capture people actively looking. Performance Max can work well for e-commerce and for businesses with strong conversion data, but it gives less control over where ads appear. We only recommend it when tracking is solid and there is enough budget for it to learn. We explain the trade-offs clearly before any budget moves into it.

For B2B and industrial firms, we may also recommend LinkedIn Ads alongside Google. Our comparison of LinkedIn Ads and Google Ads for B2B helps decide where to start.

Landing pages matter as much as ads

Sending ad clicks to a generic homepage usually wastes money. Each campaign should lead to a page that matches the search, shows your service area, includes trust signals and makes it easy to call or request a quote on a phone. If your current site cannot do this, we can build focused landing pages as part of the project. Our landing page design guide covers what to include.

Common Google Ads mistakes in Tulsa accounts

When we audit accounts for Oklahoma businesses, the same problems come up again and again:

  • Broad match keywords without negatives. Broad match can work with good data and smart bidding, but without a strong negative list it often pays for loosely related searches.
  • Search partners and display left switched on. New search campaigns often include extra networks by default, which can bring cheaper but lower-quality clicks.
  • One campaign for every service. Mixing services in one campaign makes it hard to control budget and match ads to searches.
  • No call tracking. For many local firms, most leads come by phone. Without call tracking, the best campaigns can look like the worst.
  • Ads running around the clock. If nobody answers the phone at night, ad schedules should reflect that, or calls should route to a service that can take them.
  • Auto-applied recommendations. Some of Google's automatic changes suit your goals and some do not. We review them rather than accepting them blindly.

Fixing these issues often reduces wasted spend before any new campaigns are built.

Ad copy that fits Tulsa searchers

Good ads are specific. They mention the service searched for, the area you cover, what makes you different and what happens next. For a Tulsa business, that might mean naming the suburbs you serve, offering same-day appointments if you genuinely can, or highlighting licensing and warranties you actually hold. We write responsive search ads with a range of headlines and descriptions, pin key messages where needed and test new versions over time. Our responsive search ads guide explains how this works. We avoid vague claims and anything we cannot support, and you approve all ad copy before it goes live.
