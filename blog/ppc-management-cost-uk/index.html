PPC Management Cost in the UK: Fee Models, What Is Included and Red Flags

PPC management cost in the UK explained: percentage of spend, flat fees and retainers, what a fee should cover, and the red flags to spot before you sign.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • PPC management in the UK is usually charged as a percentage of ad spend, a flat monthly fee or a retainer, and your ad spend is always paid on top.
  • The fee only makes sense once you know what is included: tracking, search term reviews, testing, landing page advice and reporting.
  • Walk away from agencies that hold your ad account, lock you into long contracts or promise fixed results.

PPC management cost in the UK usually comes down to one of three models: a percentage of your monthly ad spend, a flat monthly fee, or a retainer that bundles paid media with other work. Whatever the model, the management fee is separate from the money you pay Google, Microsoft or Meta for clicks, so always budget for both.

This guide explains how each fee model works, what a fair fee should include, how to judge value rather than headline price, and the warning signs that tell you to keep looking. If you want the wider picture first, our guide to choosing a digital marketing agency in the UK covers the general questions to ask.

PPC management cost in the UK: the three main fee models

Most UK agencies and freelancers use one of the models below, sometimes with a minimum fee or a set-up charge on top. None is automatically better; the right one depends on your spend, how stable it is and how much work sits around the ads.

Percentage of ad spendThe fee is a share of what you spend on media each monthLarger or fast-growing budgets where workload rises with spendAn incentive to raise spend rather than improve efficiency
Flat monthly feeA fixed amount regardless of spend, usually tied to a scopeStable budgets and businesses that want predictable costsScope creep, or a fee that stays the same while effort quietly drops
RetainerA monthly package covering paid media plus related work such as SEO, content or reportingBusinesses that want one partner across channelsUnclear split of hours between channels

Percentage of spend

Percentage models are common because they scale naturally: a larger account normally needs more campaigns, more keywords and more testing. The weakness is the incentive. If the agency earns more when you spend more, ask how they decide when to cut budget on campaigns that are not paying back. A good answer will reference target cost per lead or return on ad spend, not just volume.

Flat fee

A flat fee is easy to plan around and removes the spend incentive. It works best when the scope is written down: number of campaigns or platforms, how often the account is reviewed, and what reporting you receive. Without that, a flat fee can drift towards minimal effort.

Retainer

A retainer makes sense when paid search is one part of a wider plan. Ads and organic search often share keyword research, landing pages and tracking, so combining them with one partner can save duplicated work. Our paid media service sits alongside SEO and GEO for exactly this reason.

What should a PPC management fee include?

Two quotes at the same price can contain very different amounts of work. Before comparing numbers, compare scope. A competent PPC service should normally cover:

  • Conversion tracking. Google Ads and GA4 conversions set up and tested, ideally with consent mode configured for UK GDPR and PECR. Without reliable tracking, every other decision is guesswork.
  • Account structure. Campaigns grouped by intent, product line or location so budgets go where they earn the most.
  • Keyword and search term management. Regular reviews of the actual searches triggering your ads, with negatives added to stop wasted clicks.
  • Ad copy and asset testing. New headlines, descriptions and extensions tested against each other rather than left untouched for months.
  • Bid strategy and budget management. Choosing between manual and automated bidding and checking it is working towards your real goal.
  • Landing page feedback. Ads can only do so much if the page they send people to is slow or unclear.
  • Reporting you can understand. Cost, conversions, cost per conversion and what changed, in plain English.

For online shops, add product feed management and Performance Max or Shopping campaigns. Our Google Shopping ads guide explains what that work involves.

How to judge whether a PPC fee is good value

The cheapest fee is rarely the cheapest outcome. A poorly managed account can waste far more in clicks than you save on management. Think about value in three parts.

1. Size of the fee relative to spend

If your monthly ad spend is small, a high management fee can absorb most of the budget. In that case it may be better to start with a narrow campaign, fix tracking and landing pages, and grow once you can see what converts. A smaller retainer tier or a project-based set-up can make sense here.

2. Hours and seniority

Ask who will actually work on the account and how often. A low fee with a monthly check-in will not catch a broken conversion tag or a competitor bidding on your brand name for weeks.

3. Time to results

Paid search can bring traffic on day one, but finding the profitable mix of keywords, ads and bids takes testing. Across paid and organic work, meaningful results typically take 3 to 6 months to settle. Be wary of anyone promising a fixed return before they have seen your data.

Hidden costs to budget for

The management fee and the ad spend are the obvious costs. Others are easy to miss:

  • Set-up or onboarding fees for audits, tracking and account rebuilds.
  • Landing page design or development, if your current pages do not convert. Our website and ecommerce development service covers this when needed.
  • Creative for display, YouTube or social ads.
  • Software such as call tracking or feed tools, if used.
  • VAT on agency fees, which matters if you are not VAT-registered.

Red flags when hiring a PPC agency

Most UK agencies are honest, but these signs should make you pause:

  • They own the ad account. You should own your Google Ads account and grant the agency access. If you leave, the history and data stay with you.
  • Guaranteed results. No one controls auction prices or competitor behaviour, so promises of a fixed number of leads are not credible.
  • Undisclosed mark-ups on media. If the agency pays the platform and re-bills you, ask whether the invoice matches the platform spend exactly.
  • Long lock-in contracts with no performance review point.
  • Vague reporting that shows clicks and impressions but not conversions or cost per conversion.
  • No questions about your margins. An agency that does not ask what a customer is worth to you cannot set sensible targets.

Checklist: before you sign a PPC agreement

  • Is the fee model written down, including any minimums or set-up fees?
  • Is ad spend billed directly by the platform to you?
  • Do you own the Google Ads, Microsoft Ads and GA4 accounts?
  • Is conversion tracking part of the scope, and will it be tested?
  • How often will search terms, bids and ads be reviewed?
  • What does the monthly report contain, and is there a review call?
  • What is the notice period, and what happens to your data if you leave?
  • Has the agency asked about your margins, average order value or lead value?

Is PPC worth it alongside SEO?

Paid search buys visibility immediately, while SEO builds it over time. Many businesses use both: ads to test which keywords convert, and organic content to win those terms without paying per click later. FAQs

What is the typical PPC management cost in the UK?

It depends on the fee model, spend and scope. Our own retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 a month with ad spend paid separately, but other agencies charge a percentage of spend or a flat fee, so compare scope as well as price.

Is ad spend included in the management fee?

Usually not. Ad spend is paid to Google, Microsoft or Meta, ideally directly from your own card or invoice, and the management fee is paid to the agency on top.

Is a percentage of spend or a flat fee better?

Flat fees and retainers give predictable costs and remove the incentive to raise spend. Percentage models can suit large, growing budgets. The most important thing is a clear, written scope.

Should I own my Google Ads account?

Yes. Owning the account means you keep the history, data and conversion settings if you change agency. The agency should be granted access, not hold ownership.

How long before PPC shows results?

Ads can drive traffic immediately, but optimising for profitable leads or sales typically takes 3 to 6 months of testing and refinement.

Do I need to pay VAT on PPC management fees?

Agency fees from a VAT-registered UK business normally have VAT added, so ask whether a quote is shown with or without VAT. How ad platforms charge VAT can differ, so check your invoices and ask your accountant if you are unsure.
