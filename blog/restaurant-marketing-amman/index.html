Restaurant Marketing in Amman: Websites, Search and Ads for Restaurants

Restaurant marketing in Amman explained: how restaurants and cafes win more bookings and direct orders with a better website, local SEO and ads, run remotely.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 28 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Restaurant marketing in Amman works best when a fast menu-first website, a strong Google Maps listing and local social ads all make booking or ordering easy.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based, works remotely and has no office in Amman or Jordan; retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 per month, ad spend is paid separately and dinar pricing is quoted on request.
  • Delivery apps stay useful, but a direct website and booking route help restaurants keep more margin and build their own relationship with guests.

Restaurant marketing in Amman is about being the place people find and choose when they search for somewhere to eat, whether that is a family dinner in Abdoun, coffee on Rainbow Street or a late order in Sweifieh. For most restaurants that comes down to a fast website with an easy menu, a strong Google Maps listing with good reviews, and local ads on Instagram, Facebook and Google that reach diners nearby.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely. We have no office in Amman or anywhere in Jordan. We support independent restaurants, cafes, bakeries, dessert shops and small groups that are comfortable working with a remote team through video calls, shared dashboards and written monthly reports.

The Amman dining scene

Amman has a lively and varied food culture, from traditional Jordanian and Levantine kitchens to specialty coffee, international dining, burger and grill concepts, bakeries and dessert spots. Diners include local families, young professionals, students, expatriate residents and visitors from the Gulf and further afield, especially in summer.

Neighbourhood matters. People often search with an area in mind, such as Jabal Amman, Abdali, Shmeisani, Khalda or Dabouq, because traffic and distance shape where they go. Many decisions are made on a phone in the moment: a quick look at Google Maps, the photos, the reviews and the menu, then a call, a booking or a drive. A restaurant that makes those details easy to find wins more of those last-minute decisions.

Delivery is also a big part of the market, with apps such as Talabat widely used. Those apps bring orders, but they take commission and own the customer relationship. Good marketing balances app orders with direct bookings, pick-up and loyal regulars.

A menu-first restaurant website

Most diners visit a restaurant website for three things: the menu, the location and a way to book or order. If they have to pinch and zoom through a PDF menu or hunt for opening hours, many give up and pick somewhere else. A good restaurant website puts those three things on the first screen of a phone.

We build restaurant sites with a readable menu in plain text rather than images, clear prices where you choose to show them, opening hours that are easy to update, a map and parking notes, and buttons for calling, WhatsApp, booking and ordering. Separate pages for private dining, catering, events and each branch help both guests and Google. Real photos of your dishes and space make a far stronger impression than stock images.

If you want Arabic and English versions, we build the site to support both, including right-to-left layout. We write in English, so Arabic text comes from your team or a professional translator. A smaller restaurant website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000 and takes three to five weeks, while a site with direct online ordering is usually priced as an e-commerce project at £1,500 to £3,500. Google Maps and local SEO

For restaurants, the Google Maps listing is often more important than the website itself. When someone searches "breakfast Abdoun", "shawarma near me" or "best cafe Jabal Amman", Google shows a map pack of nearby places with ratings, photos and hours. Appearing there brings people through the door.

We set up and tidy your Google Business Profile with the right categories, accurate hours including holiday and Ramadan times, menu links, attributes such as outdoor seating or family areas, and fresh photos. We help you build a steady, honest review process and suggest friendly replies. On the website side, we create pages that match how people search by cuisine and area, and fix technical issues that slow Google down. Our guide to choosing an SEO agency in Jordan explains search in the country more broadly.

Local SEO usually shows meaningful movement within three to six months, while map listing improvements can help sooner.

Social and search ads for restaurants

Meta Ads on Instagram and Facebook are a natural fit for restaurants in Amman, because so much discovery happens on those platforms. We target people within a set distance of each branch and use short, appetising creative built from your own photos and clips your team provides. Typical campaigns promote openings, new menus, weekend brunches, iftar and suhoor menus during Ramadan, and private dining. Our guide to Meta Ads in Amman goes into campaign setup in more detail.

Google Ads work well for searches with clear intent, such as catering, group dining, birthday venues and event spaces. We send each click to the matching page and track calls, bookings and WhatsApp messages. Our article on Google Ads in Jordan explains how we structure campaigns. Our paid media service sets out how we manage ads across platforms.

Ad spend is always paid separately and directly to the platforms, so you stay in full control of the budget.

Planning around the Amman calendar

Restaurant demand in Amman shifts through the year. Ramadan changes everything, with iftar and suhoor becoming the main events and search interest building in the weeks before it starts. Eid brings family gatherings and busy weekends. Summer brings more visitors, including Jordanians living abroad and Gulf travellers, while cooler months shift attention to indoor dining and warm comfort dishes.

We build a simple marketing calendar for each restaurant so menus, landing pages and ads are ready before each peak. Ramadan campaigns, for example, work best when the iftar menu page and booking route are live and indexed before the month begins, not during it.

Common mistakes we fix

  • Menus uploaded only as images or PDFs that Google cannot read.
  • Out-of-date opening hours on Google Maps, especially during Ramadan and holidays.
  • No direct booking or order route, so every order goes through an app.
  • Ads shown across the whole city instead of near each branch.
  • Unanswered reviews, which make a restaurant look inattentive.

FAQs

What does restaurant marketing in Amman involve?

Restaurant marketing in Amman usually combines a fast menu-first website, a well-managed Google Maps listing, steady reviews and local ads on Instagram, Facebook and Google. The aim is more bookings, more direct orders and more regulars.

Do you have an office in Amman?

No. Prism Works Studio is UK-based and works remotely, with no office in Amman or Jordan. We work through video calls, email, shared dashboards and monthly written reports.

How much does restaurant marketing cost?

Our retainers are £350, £750 or £1,500 per month. A smaller restaurant website typically costs £1,000 to £2,000. Ad spend is paid separately, and dinar pricing is quoted on request.

Should we stop using delivery apps?

Usually not. Delivery apps bring useful orders and visibility. The goal is to add direct booking and ordering routes so a larger share of guests come straight to you over time.

Do you create food photos or videos?

No. We do not film or edit video. Your team or a local photographer provides photos and clips, and we use them in your website, map listing and ads.

Can you market our Ramadan menu?

Yes. We plan iftar and suhoor pages and campaigns ahead of Ramadan, update your map listing hours and run local ads so diners can find and book your Ramadan offer early.
