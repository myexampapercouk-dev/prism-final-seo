Mortgage Broker Website Design: Enquiries, Trust and FCA Considerations

Website design for UK mortgage brokers: enquiry forms, mortgage type pages, calculators, FCA status disclosures, risk warnings and building client trust.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A mortgage broker website is usually an enquiry tool first, so a clear, secure fact-find or enquiry form and simple next steps matter most.
  • Financial promotions rules and disclosure requirements apply to broker websites, so content should be checked against current FCA rules and, where relevant, your network's sign-off process.
  • Separate pages for first-time buyers, remortgages, buy-to-let and other situations help visitors find relevant information quickly.

Someone looking for a mortgage broker is usually facing one of the biggest financial decisions of their life. They want to know whether you can help with their situation, how the process works, whether there is a fee, and whether you are properly authorised. A well-designed broker website answers those questions calmly and clearly, then makes it easy to start a conversation.

This guide covers website design for UK mortgage brokers, from sole traders to small advice firms, whether directly authorised or appointed representatives of a network. It focuses on the website itself. For promoting your firm more widely, see our guide to mortgage broker marketing. Firms offering wider financial planning may find our guide to financial adviser website design more relevant. This guide is not regulatory advice.

What a broker website needs to do

For most brokers, the main jobs of the website are to:

  • Explain the types of mortgage and situations you can help with.
  • Show how the advice process works, step by step.
  • Be clear about fees and how you are paid.
  • Display regulatory status and required disclosures correctly.
  • Make it easy to book a call or submit an enquiry securely.
  • Build trust through clear information about the broker or team.

A site that does these well, without jargon or pressure, will usually serve visitors better than one full of rate headlines.

Regulation and the website

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority, and a broker's website is generally treated as a financial promotion. Financial promotions need to be clear, fair and not misleading, and there are specific disclosure expectations. The exact requirements depend on your permissions and circumstances, and the rules change, so check the current FCA Handbook, Consumer Duty expectations and any guidance from your network or compliance support. Points brokers commonly consider include:

  • Displaying your firm's regulatory status and FCA register number accurately, and for appointed representatives, the principal firm.
  • Including the risk warning about your home being repossessed if you do not keep up repayments, where required.
  • Being transparent about fees, including whether you charge a broker fee and when it is payable.
  • Explaining whether you search the whole of market or a limited panel of lenders, accurately.
  • Making clear which services, such as some buy-to-let or commercial lending, may not be regulated by the FCA.

Appointed representatives usually need website content approved by their network before it goes live. Building time for that into the project avoids delays at launch. For more on compliant promotion, see our guide to FCA-compliant marketing.

Pages for different situations

Mortgage needs vary widely, and visitors want to see quickly that you understand their circumstances. Separate pages, where you genuinely offer the service, might cover:

  • First-time buyers.
  • Home movers.
  • Remortgaging and product transfers.
  • Buy-to-let and landlord mortgages.
  • Self-employed and contractor applicants.
  • Later life lending, if you hold the required permissions and qualifications.
  • Protection insurance, such as life cover and income protection, if you advise on it.

For each, explain the common considerations in plain language, how you can help, and what information people will need. Avoid implying that you can secure a mortgage for anyone or guarantee approval or a particular rate. Separate pages can also help people searching for specific help, such as a mortgage broker for self-employed applicants in their area. Our guide to service page design covers layout principles.

Explaining the process

Many people have never used a broker and are unsure what happens. A simple page describing your actual process can be reassuring. This might cover the initial conversation, gathering documents, researching options, recommendation, application, offer and completion, along with roughly how long each stage can take. Only describe steps you genuinely follow, and be clear that timescales depend on lenders and individual circumstances.

Fees and how you are paid

Clarity on fees builds trust and is part of regulatory expectations. Explain whether you charge a fee, how much or how it is calculated, when it becomes payable, and whether you also receive commission from lenders. A dedicated fees page, linked from the main navigation, can make this easy to find. Avoid describing your service as free if a fee may apply in some circumstances.

Enquiry forms and data security

The enquiry form is often the most important element on a broker site. Some brokers use a short contact form to arrange a call, while others offer a more detailed online fact-find. Consider:

  • Keeping the first form short, with just enough to arrange a conversation.
  • Explaining what happens next and how quickly you aim to respond.
  • Offering a way to book a call directly into your diary.
  • Saving more detailed financial questions for a secure fact-find later.

Financial information is sensitive. Forms should be served securely, collect only what is needed, and be covered by a clear privacy notice in line with UK GDPR. If you use a client portal or CRM for fact-finds, link to it rather than collecting detailed financial data through a basic contact form. Never ask visitors to send passport or bank details by unsecured email. Our guide to website contact forms covers general good practice.

Calculators and tools

Mortgage calculators can be useful, giving visitors a rough idea of borrowing or repayments. If you include them, make sure they are accurate, clearly labelled as illustrations only, and do not imply a lending decision or recommendation. Outputs may count as part of your financial promotion, so they should be reviewed alongside the rest of the site. Keep calculators simple and fast on mobile, and pair them with a clear invitation to speak to a broker for advice.

Showing rates and products

Some brokers want to display rates on their website. This needs particular care, as rates change frequently and showing a rate can create an expectation that a visitor may not qualify for. If you show rates, ensure they are current, representative and accompanied by any required information. Many brokers find it simpler to explain that they research options for each client individually.

Building trust

Trust is central for brokers. A clear about page with real photos, qualifications such as CeMAP or equivalent where genuinely held, and how long the firm has operated, if relevant, can help. Display only genuine client reviews, with permission, and avoid presenting reviews in a way that implies outcomes others will achieve. Our guide to website trust signals covers other approaches.

Because you work with personal finances, a professional, uncluttered design with clear contact details, a physical address where appropriate and visible regulatory information helps visitors feel confident.

Helpful content and guides

Guides explaining topics such as how much deposit you might need, what affects affordability, or what happens when a fixed rate ends can attract people early in their research. Keep content accurate and up to date, date guides so readers know when they were written, and treat them as financial promotions for compliance purposes. Our guide to SEO for financial advisers covers related ideas that can also apply to brokers.

Accessibility and vulnerable customers

The FCA expects firms to consider the needs of customers in vulnerable circumstances. On the website, this can mean plain language, readable text, good contrast and offering several ways to get in touch, such as phone, email and form. Our guide to website accessibility covers the basics.

Choosing a platform

Most broker sites are service sites with enquiry forms, so WordPress, Squarespace and Wix can all work. The choice often depends on how forms connect to your CRM, whether you need calculators, and your network's requirements for content control. FAQs

What regulatory information should a mortgage broker website show?

Commonly your regulatory status and FCA register number, principal firm details for appointed representatives, fee information and relevant risk warnings. Check current FCA rules and your network's requirements.

Do appointed representatives need network approval for their website?

Usually, yes. Networks typically need to approve financial promotions, including website content, before publication.

Should a broker website include a mortgage calculator?

It can help, as long as results are clearly illustrative, accurate and reviewed for compliance alongside the rest of the site.

Is it safe to collect fact-find information through a website?

Detailed financial information is better collected through a secure portal or CRM. Keep initial website forms short and covered by a clear privacy notice.

Should brokers show mortgage rates on their website?

Rates change often and may not apply to every visitor, so many brokers prefer to explain their research process. If you show rates, keep them current and representative.
