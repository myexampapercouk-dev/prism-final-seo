Hiring a UK Shopify Agency as a US Brand: What to Expect

Considering a UK Shopify agency for your US brand? See how remote projects run, time zones, scope, US store settings, ownership and how to compare quotes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A UK Shopify agency can build and improve a store for US shoppers remotely, as long as the scope, US settings and working hours are agreed in writing first.
  • The time difference works in your favour when handovers are planned: UK mornings overlap with the US East Coast afternoon and can turn feedback round overnight.
  • Compare agencies on process, store ownership, US checkout and tax setup, and aftercare rather than on a headline quote alone; currencies other than pounds are quoted on request.

Hiring a UK Shopify agency as a US brand works well when the agency plans for your market from day one: US checkout settings, sales tax configuration with your accountant, US shipping carriers and copy written for American shoppers. The practical questions are about time zones, contracts, ownership and communication, and each one has a clear answer you should get before you sign.

We are a UK-based studio and we work remotely with brands in the USA. This guide explains how a cross-border Shopify project runs, what to check in a proposal, and where the real risks sit, so you can decide whether an overseas agency is the right fit for your store.

Why US brands look at UK Shopify agencies

Shopify is the same platform on both sides of the Atlantic. The admin, the themes, the app store and the checkout are shared, so a team that builds well for UK shoppers already knows the platform you are using. What changes is the market layer on top: currency, tax, shipping, payment methods and tone of voice.

US brands usually consider a UK partner for a few reasons:

  • Fresh eyes on the store: an outside team questions assumptions that a local agency might share with you.
  • Working hours that extend your day: work can progress while your team is offline, then land in your inbox ready for review.
  • Plans to sell into the UK and Europe: a UK team understands those shoppers, which helps if expansion is on your roadmap. Our guide to selling internationally from an online store covers the store side of that.
  • A single team for build and growth: design, development, SEO and paid media under one roof, which reduces handoffs.

None of these is a reason to choose an agency on its own. The deciding factors are the same as for any Shopify partner: clear scope, a sensible process and honest answers about cost and timing.

A remote Shopify project follows the same stages as a local one. The difference is that every stage needs a written output, because you will not be in the same room. Here is how we would expect a well-run project to flow.

Time zones and communication

The UK is five hours ahead of the US East Coast and eight hours ahead of the West Coast for most of the year, although the clocks change on different dates in spring and autumn, so the gap shifts briefly. In practice that gives a reliable overlap in the UK afternoon, which is the US morning.

  • Book a regular call slot in the overlap window, for example once a week during build.
  • Use written updates for everything else, so decisions are recorded and nobody waits for a call.
  • Agree response times for normal questions and for urgent issues such as a checkout fault after launch.
  • Name one decision maker on your side, so feedback does not arrive in conflicting pieces.

Handled this way, the time difference becomes an advantage. You review in your afternoon, the agency works on changes in the UK morning, and the next version is ready when you start your day.

US store settings an overseas agency must get right

This is where a cross-border project can go wrong if the agency treats your store like a UK one. Ask how they will handle each of the following.

  • Currency and pricing: prices shown in US dollars, with sensible rounding and no stray pound signs in templates or emails.
  • Sales tax: US sales tax rules vary by state and depend on where you have nexus. Shopify has tax tools, but the settings should be confirmed by your accountant or tax adviser, not decided by your agency.
  • Shipping: zones, rates and delivery promises that match the carriers you actually use. Our guide to Shopify shipping profiles explains how rates are organised.
  • Payments: the wallets and buy-now-pay-later options your customers expect, configured through Shopify Payments or your chosen gateway.
  • Policies and legal pages: returns, privacy and terms written for US customers. State privacy laws differ, so check current official guidance or ask your lawyer to review the final text.
  • Spelling and tone: we write in UK English for our own site, but your storefront copy should use the US spelling and phrasing your customers expect. Agree this in the brief.

Accessibility deserves a mention too. Many US brands are concerned about accessibility claims, so ask how the agency checks contrast, keyboard use, alt text and form labels. Our website accessibility guide covers the practical checks; for legal questions, take advice from your own lawyer.

Contracts, payment and ownership

A cross-border contract should be just as clear as a local one, and arguably clearer. Before you sign, confirm:

  1. Who owns the store: the Shopify account should be in your name, with the agency added as a collaborator or staff account, never the other way round.
  2. Who owns the theme code and designs once the final invoice is paid.
  3. What currency you will be invoiced in. Our published prices are in pounds; other currencies are quoted on request.
  4. What is out of scope, such as copywriting, photography, product data entry or custom apps.
  5. How changes are priced once the agreed scope is signed off.
  6. What aftercare looks like: a support window, a monthly retainer or ad hoc help.

For a deeper checklist, see our guides to website ownership and handover and marketing agency contracts.

When comparing quotes from agencies in different countries, compare the scope line by line. A lower number that leaves out US settings, testing or aftercare is not cheaper once the gaps are filled.

Red flags when hiring an overseas Shopify agency

  • They want to create the Shopify store under their own account.
  • They cannot explain how they will handle US sales tax settings or who signs them off.
  • Every answer is a template, with no questions about your products or customers.
  • There is no named point of contact or agreed response time.
  • They promise rankings, revenue or conversion rates before seeing your data.
  • The proposal has no testing stage and no aftercare.

FAQs

Is hiring a UK Shopify agency as a US brand a good idea?

It can be, provided the agency plans for US shoppers from the start and agrees time zones, scope and ownership in writing. Judge them on process and clarity, not on location.

Can a UK agency set up US sales tax in Shopify?

An agency can configure the settings in Shopify, but which states you collect tax in depends on your nexus, so the settings should be confirmed by your accountant or tax adviser.

Will my store copy use US spelling?

It should. Agree in the brief that customer-facing copy uses US spelling and phrasing, even if the agency writes in UK English elsewhere.

How long does a remote Shopify build take?

Our published timeline for an ecommerce build is 5 to 12 weeks. Remote working does not need to slow it down if feedback and content arrive on schedule.

Can you invoice in US dollars?

Our published prices are in pounds sterling. Other currencies, including US dollars, are quoted on request.

Who owns the Shopify store after launch?

You should. The store should sit in your own Shopify account from day one, with the agency given collaborator or staff access that you can remove at any time.
