Selling Food and Drink Online in the UK: An Ecommerce Guide for Producers

How UK food and drink producers can sell online: platform choice, product pages, allergen and labelling information, delivery, age checks and repeat orders.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Food and drink stores carry extra responsibilities online, including clear allergen and product information before purchase.
  • Delivery is a core part of the product: packaging, courier choice, delivery days and shelf life shape what you can sell and where.
  • Consumable products suit repeat orders and subscriptions, so plan for returning customers from the start.

Selling food and drink online looks similar to selling any other product: a website, product pages, a checkout and delivery. In practice it comes with extra layers. Shoppers cannot taste or smell what they are buying, information about ingredients and allergens has to be available before they order, perishable products need careful delivery planning and some products, such as alcohol, bring age checks and licensing into play. Get these right and food and drink can be a strong fit for ecommerce, because good products are bought again and again.

This guide is for UK producers, makers, bakeries, coffee roasters, breweries, distilleries, delis and farm shops planning to launch or improve an online store. It covers the website and ecommerce side; regulatory points are summarised in general terms, so always check current guidance from the relevant authorities or take professional advice.

Start with what you can actually ship

Before choosing a platform or designing pages, work out which products travel well and how far. A few questions shape everything else:

  • Shelf life. Ambient products with long shelf lives, such as coffee, tea, sauces, spirits and confectionery, are the easiest to sell nationally. Fresh and chilled products need faster, more controlled delivery.
  • Temperature. Chilled or frozen goods may need insulated packaging, cool packs and next-day services, and possibly restricted delivery days so parcels do not sit in a depot over a weekend.
  • Fragility. Glass bottles, jars and delicate bakes need protective packaging and couriers that accept them.
  • Weight. Liquids are heavy, and delivery costs can quickly overtake the product price on small orders.
  • Delivery area. Some businesses deliver nationally, others locally with their own vans, or offer click and collect. Mixing approaches is common.

It can be sensible to launch online with a focused range that ships reliably, then expand once processes are proven.

Choosing a platform

Most food and drink businesses choose between Shopify, WooCommerce on WordPress, or a builder such as Squarespace or Wix for smaller ranges.

  • Shopify suits businesses where online sales are central, with strong checkout, inventory tools, subscription options through apps and connections to other sales channels.
  • WooCommerce offers more flexibility, for example for complex delivery rules or custom ordering flows, but needs more maintenance.
  • Squarespace or Wix can suit a small range sold alongside a café, shop or events business.

Check how each option handles the features you need: delivery date selection, postcode-based delivery rules, product options such as grind size or gift messages, subscriptions and age verification. Our comparisons of Shopify vs WooCommerce and Shopify vs Squarespace go into more depth.

Product pages that sell food and drink

Because shoppers cannot taste the product, the page has to do that work. Strong food and drink product pages usually include:

  • Appetising, honest photography. The product itself, the packaging, a serving suggestion and a sense of scale. Our product photography guide covers the essentials.
  • Descriptive copy. Flavour, texture, aroma, origin and how to enjoy it. Specific words help more than generic ones such as "delicious".
  • Full ingredients and allergens clearly presented, not hidden in an image.
  • Size, weight or volume, and for drinks, ABV where relevant.
  • Storage and shelf life, including what to expect on arrival.
  • Dietary information such as vegan or gluten-free, only where it is accurate and you can support it.
  • Delivery information on the page, such as dispatch days and any delivery restrictions.

Our guide to product page SEO explains how to make these pages work for search too.

Allergen and labelling information online

UK food law generally requires that mandatory food information, including allergen information, is available to customers before they complete a distance purchase, and again when the food is delivered. In practice that means ingredient and allergen details should be shown clearly on product pages, matching what is on the label. The 14 major allergens are usually emphasised.

A few practical points for the website:

  • Keep ingredient and allergen text as real text rather than only in photos of the label, so it is readable on all devices and by screen readers.
  • Have a process for updating the website whenever a recipe or supplier changes.
  • Be cautious with claims such as "free from", "healthy" or nutrition and health claims, which are regulated.
  • Give a clear way for customers to ask questions about allergens.

The Food Standards Agency (and Food Standards Scotland in Scotland) publishes guidance on distance selling, allergens and labelling, and food businesses usually need to register with their local authority. This is a general overview only; requirements depend on your products and business, so check official guidance or take advice.

Selling alcohol online

If you sell alcohol, you will generally need the appropriate licence, and you must take steps to avoid selling to under-18s. Online, that usually means age confirmation during checkout and, importantly, age checks at delivery where required. Courier services differ in whether and how they handle age-restricted deliveries. Marketing of alcohol is also subject to advertising rules. Check current requirements with your licensing authority and the ASA's guidance before launching.

Delivery, packaging and costs

For food and drink, delivery is part of the product experience. A chocolate bar that arrives melted or a bottle that arrives broken undoes all the work on the website. Plan for:

  • Packaging suited to the product, with consideration for recyclability and presentation. Our guide to packaging and unboxing covers the options.
  • Dispatch days that avoid parcels sitting over weekends for perishable goods.
  • Delivery date selection at checkout if customers need goods for a specific day, such as gifts or events.
  • Clear delivery pricing shown early, with any free delivery threshold explained. Unexpected costs at checkout are a common reason for abandoned baskets.
  • Postcode restrictions for local delivery or areas couriers cannot reach on time.

Write a clear delivery and returns page. Perishable goods are often exempt from the usual right to cancel under consumer contracts rules, but you still need to explain your policy, and faulty or damaged goods must be handled fairly. Our guide to delivery and returns pages explains what to include.

Repeat orders and subscriptions

Consumable products are naturally bought again. Coffee, tea, wine, beer, snacks and pantry staples can suit subscriptions, regular boxes or simple reorder reminders. Options include:

  • Subscribe-and-save on individual products.
  • Curated monthly or seasonal boxes.
  • Gift subscriptions for a set number of months.
  • Easy reorder from the customer account.

Make subscriptions easy to pause, skip, change and cancel, and be clear about billing. Our guide to ecommerce subscriptions covers the details, and our guide to loyalty programmes explores other ways to encourage returning customers.

Gifting and seasonal peaks

Food and drink is a popular gift, and many producers see demand rise around Christmas, Father's Day, Mother's Day and Valentine's Day. Gift sets, gift messages, gift cards and delivery to a different address can all help. Plan stock, packaging and courier cut-off dates well ahead, and make last order dates clear on the website. Our guide to ecommerce gift cards covers one useful option.

Getting found

Search, social media and email all play a part. On the website side:

  • Write unique category and product descriptions rather than copying supplier text.
  • Create helpful content such as recipes, pairing guides and brewing or serving tips, which can attract searches and support product pages.
  • Tell your origin story honestly: where ingredients come from and how products are made.
  • Use product structured data so search engines understand prices and availability.
  • Set up product feeds for Google Shopping if you plan to advertise or appear in free listings.

Our SEO and GEO service and paid media service pages explain how we approach search and advertising.

Food and drink ecommerce checklist

  • A launch range that ships reliably within your delivery area.
  • A platform that supports the delivery rules and options you need.
  • Product pages with full ingredients, allergens, storage and delivery details.
  • A process for keeping product information accurate.
  • Age verification and licensing in place if selling alcohol.
  • Packaging tested for the journey.
  • Clear delivery, returns and privacy pages.
  • A plan for repeat orders and seasonal peaks.

FAQs

Do I need to show allergens on my food website?

Generally, yes. UK rules usually require mandatory food information, including allergens, to be available before a distance sale is completed. Check Food Standards Agency guidance for your products.

What is the best platform for selling food online in the UK?

Shopify suits businesses where online sales are central. WooCommerce offers more flexibility for complex delivery rules. Builders can suit small ranges.

Can I sell homemade food online?

Often, yes, but food businesses usually need to register with their local authority and follow food hygiene and labelling rules. Check official guidance before you start.

How do I ship perishable food?

Use suitable insulated packaging, next-day services and dispatch days that avoid weekend delays. Test packaging before launch.

Do customers have a right to return food?

Perishable goods are often exempt from the standard cancellation right, but faulty or damaged goods must still be dealt with fairly. Explain your policy clearly.

Do I need a licence to sell alcohol online?

Generally, yes, and you must take steps to prevent sales to under-18s, including at delivery. Check with your licensing authority.
