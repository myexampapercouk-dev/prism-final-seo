For restaurants, fashion labels, beauty brands, event organisers, apps and online shops, TikTok can be one of the fastest ways to get noticed, provided the campaign is planned around clear goals and solid tracking.

Why TikTok works in Montreal

Montreal has a large student population, a lively food and nightlife scene, a strong creative and fashion sector, and festivals that draw crowds through the year. Many of the people who drive that culture spend a lot of time on TikTok, searching it for places to eat, things to do and products to try.

The city is also bilingual in a way that matters for advertising. Many people use French day to day, others use English, and plenty switch between the two. That affects how you plan creative and audiences. Quebec also has its own language rules for commercial communication, so it is worth checking official guidance and your own adviser on how they apply to your ads, websites and packaging. Final compliance approval rests with your business.

TikTok Ads agency Montreal: what the work includes

  • Goal setting. We agree what success means, whether that is online sales, table bookings, app installs, event tickets or leads.
  • Audience planning. We use location, age, interests, behaviours and custom audiences, and we plan French and English versions where they make sense.
  • Creative briefs. We write briefs for your team or creator, covering hooks, structure, captions and calls to action, so footage fits the platform.
  • Tracking. We set up the TikTok Pixel and events API where possible, and check that purchases, bookings or leads are recorded accurately.
  • Testing. We test hooks, formats and audiences, then move budget towards what works.
  • Reporting. You get plain written reports on spend, results and next steps.

Creative that fits the platform

TikTok ads that look like polished TV spots often struggle. The best ads usually feel like ordinary posts: a strong hook in the first seconds, real people, quick cuts, clear captions and a simple call to action. For a Montreal brand, that might mean a chef plating a dish, a stylist showing three ways to wear a jacket, or a founder explaining a product in thirty seconds.

Because we do not film or edit, we focus on the brief. We tell your team or creator what to capture, which hooks to try, how long each clip should be and which caption and on-screen text to use. We then test several versions to find what works. If you run French and English versions, your team or translator supplies the French wording.

Landing pages and tracking

A TikTok campaign is only as good as the page it sends people to. If the page is slow, hard to read on a phone or does not match the ad, most of your spend is wasted. We check speed, layout, offer and checkout or booking flow before scaling. If your site needs work, our guide to web design in Montreal covers what a fast, mobile-friendly site needs.

Tracking matters just as much. We set up the TikTok Pixel, test key events and compare platform data against your shop or booking system. This helps avoid decisions based on misleading numbers.

Budgets, testing and patience

TikTok's delivery system needs data to learn. If you spread a small budget across too many ad groups, none of them gets enough results to optimise properly. We usually start with a small number of ad groups, a handful of creative variations and a clear conversion event, then expand once we can see what works.

Creative also wears out faster on TikTok than on many other platforms. An ad that performs well in its first week can lose impact within a few weeks as the same people see it again. Planning a steady flow of fresh clips from your team or creator, even simple ones, keeps campaigns healthy. We set a testing rhythm with you so new footage arrives before older ads tire.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Sending traffic to the home page. Send people to the exact product, menu or event page the ad mentions.
  • Skipping tracking. Without the Pixel and tested events, you cannot tell which ads lead to sales.
  • Reusing ads from other platforms unchanged. Square or landscape videos with small text rarely perform on TikTok.
  • Ignoring comments. Questions and comments on ads are a chance to answer objections, so someone on your team should reply.
  • Judging too early. A few days of data is rarely enough to judge a campaign fairly.

Avoiding these mistakes does not need a large budget. It needs a clear goal, a landing page that matches the ad, accurate tracking and a steady supply of simple, honest clips. With those in place, even a modest test can show whether TikTok is worth scaling for your brand, and which audiences and messages deserve more of your budget over the following months.
