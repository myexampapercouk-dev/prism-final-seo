Web Design London: How Professional Firms Can Plan a Website Redesign

Redesigning a London law, finance or consultancy firm's website? How to plan the brief, keep rankings, write for clients, pick a platform and compare quotes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most London professional firms already have a website, so the real job is a careful redesign rather than a fresh start.
  • Clients choose advisers on trust, so clear expertise pages, real people and plain language matter more than visual trends.
  • Redirect planning and content audits protect the search visibility a firm has already built.

For most London professional firms, web design means redesign. The practice already has a site, it probably has some search visibility and a few pages that bring in enquiries, and it has started to look dated or no longer reflects how the firm works. The best redesign keeps what already earns its place, fixes what holds the firm back and gives prospective clients a clear reason to get in touch.

This guide is written for partners, practice managers and marketing leads at law firms, accountants, financial advisers, consultancies and similar firms who are planning a new site.

Why London firms redesign

From the City and Holborn to Mayfair, Canary Wharf and the suburbs, London has a dense concentration of professional services. A prospective client looking for a solicitor, accountant or adviser will usually compare several firms online before calling one. Common reasons firms decide their website needs work include:

  • The site no longer matches the firm's services, sectors or people after mergers, new hires or changes in focus
  • It looks poor on a phone, which is where many clients first check you out
  • Updating pages needs a developer, so content has gone stale
  • Enquiries arrive by phone and email with no way of knowing which pages produced them
  • The brand has moved on but the website has not

Start with an audit, not a mood board

Before anyone designs anything, look hard at the current site. Which pages get visits from search? Which pages lead to enquiries? Which pages are thin, out of date or duplicated? An honest audit stops a redesign from throwing away pages that quietly bring in work.

Writing for clients, not for the firm

Many professional websites talk about the firm: its history, its values, its awards. Clients care about their own problem. A good redesign rewrites key pages around the questions clients actually ask.

  • Service pages should explain who the service is for, what typically happens and how to start, in plain English.
  • Sector pages help a business client see that you understand their industry.
  • People pages matter in professional services. Clients want to see who they will deal with, with real photos and genuine experience.
  • Insight articles show expertise and answer early-stage questions, but only if they are kept current.

Regulated firms should check every claim against their regulator's rules and any advertising codes before publishing. A web designer should flag wording that might need compliance sign-off rather than inventing marketing claims. Our financial advisers page touches on this for that sector.

Structure that matches how clients search

A London firm might serve individuals, businesses or both, across several practice areas. The site structure should make it easy to find the right route in two or three clicks. Separate pages for distinct services usually work better than one long page listing everything, both for visitors and for search engines. Clear internal links between related services, sectors and people help clients move around naturally.

Location also needs thought. A firm with offices in the City and one outer borough may want a page for each; a firm serving clients nationwide may barely mention location at all. Match the structure to where you genuinely want work from.

Design that builds trust

Professional services design does not need to be dull, but it does need to feel credible. That usually means generous spacing, readable type, restrained colour, real photography rather than generic stock images and obvious contact routes on every page. Overly busy layouts, autoplay video and heavy animation tend to work against the calm, competent impression most firms want to give.

Accessibility is part of this. Good contrast, sensible heading structure, descriptive links and forms that work with keyboards and screen readers make a site usable for more clients and reflect well on the firm.

Choosing a platform for a professional site

Most professional firms do not need an online shop, so the choice is usually between a well-built WordPress site and a custom build. WordPress suits firms that want to edit pages, publish articles and add people profiles without a developer, provided it is kept lean and updated. A custom build makes sense when the firm needs unusual functionality, such as client portals or complex integrations. Whichever you choose, make sure the firm owns the domain, hosting account and content.

Speed, security and maintenance

Clients often check a firm on a phone between meetings. A fast, simple site makes a better impression than a heavy one. Keep images compressed, avoid piling on plugins and test on real devices. Security matters too, particularly for firms handling sensitive enquiries, so forms should be secure and software kept up to date. Where SEO fits

A well-structured redesign gives search engines clear pages, sensible titles and fast loading, and protects what the old site had built up. That is the foundation, not the full job. If search is a major source of new clients for your firm, our guide to choosing an SEO agency in London covers the ongoing work.

After launch

A new site is the start of a cycle, not the end. Check enquiries against the baseline from your audit, watch for any pages that lost search visits and fix redirects quickly. Keep people profiles, services and articles current, especially after hires, departures or changes in focus, so the site never drifts out of date again.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Will you audit our current site before designing the new one?
  • How will you handle redirects from our existing URLs?
  • Who writes or edits the content, and how will compliance review fit into the timeline?
  • Can our team update people profiles and articles ourselves?
  • Will we own the domain, hosting, design and content?
  • What support is available after launch?

Working with a remote agency

Professional firms are used to working with clients and suppliers they rarely meet in person, and a redesign is no different. Content reviews, design feedback and approvals all work well over video calls and shared documents, and written records of decisions are often clearer than notes from a meeting room. If your partners strongly prefer in-person workshops, a London studio may suit you better. Otherwise, judge any agency on how well it understands your clients.

FAQs

Should a London law or accountancy firm redesign or start again?

Usually redesign. An existing site often has pages that bring in search visits and enquiries. Keeping or redirecting those while improving everything else is safer than starting from nothing.

How do we avoid losing search traffic during a redesign?

Audit current pages first, keep URLs where possible, set up redirects for any that change and check the new site carefully after launch. Some short-term movement can still happen.

Do people pages really matter?

In professional services, often yes. Clients want to know who they will work with, so real photos and clear experience help build trust.

How should compliance review fit into a website project?

Build it into the timeline from the start. Content for regulated services may need sign-off, and leaving it until the end is a common cause of delays.

Can Prism Works Studio redesign a London firm's website remotely?

Yes. We work with London businesses through video calls and shared documents. We do not have a London office.

Is WordPress suitable for a professional services firm?

It often is, especially if your team wants to publish articles and update profiles. It needs to be built cleanly and kept updated.
