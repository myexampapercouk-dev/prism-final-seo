Shopify Markets: How to Sell Across Europe From One Shopify Store

How to use Shopify Markets to sell across Europe: currencies, languages, domains, pricing, VAT and delivery, plus the set-up mistakes that cost sales.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify Markets lets one store present local currencies, languages, domains and pricing to different countries, so you can test new European markets without running separate shops.
  • The platform settings are the easy part; pricing strategy, VAT treatment, delivery, returns and localised content decide whether a market actually converts.
  • Features and limits depend on your Shopify plan and payment set-up, so check current Shopify documentation and take tax advice before switching a market on.

Shopify Markets is the part of Shopify that lets a single store sell to several countries with local currencies, languages, domains and pricing. For brands selling across Europe, it means you can serve customers in Ireland, the Netherlands, Germany, France, Spain or the Nordics from one admin, rather than building and maintaining separate stores.

We are a UK-based Shopify studio working remotely with ecommerce brands across Europe. This guide explains how Shopify Markets works at a practical level, the decisions you need to make before switching a market on, and where we see stores lose sales. Shopify changes its features and plan limits regularly, so treat this as a planning guide and check Shopify's current documentation for exact settings.

How Shopify Markets works for European selling

A market in Shopify is a country or group of countries that share settings. You might create a single EU market to start, then split out individual countries once you have enough orders to justify local pricing or content. Within each market you can usually control:

  • Currency. Showing prices in euros, Swedish krona, Danish krone and so on, with conversion handled at checkout where your payment set-up supports it.
  • Pricing. Automatic conversion with rounding, percentage adjustments, or fixed prices for specific products, depending on plan.
  • Languages. Which published languages are offered in that market.
  • Domains or subfolders. For example a /de/ or /fr/ subfolder, or a separate country domain pointed at the same store.
  • Products and availability. Which products can be sold in which market, useful where regulation or shipping rules differ.
  • Duties and taxes. Options for showing tax-inclusive prices and, on some plans and payment set-ups, collecting duties at checkout.

Because everything sits in one store, your products, stock, apps and order processing stay in one place. That is the main advantage over running a separate store per country, which multiplies maintenance.

One store or several?

Most growing brands are better served by one store with Markets. Separate stores can make sense when markets need very different catalogues, separate legal entities, or different warehouses with their own stock systems. Larger brands sometimes use Shopify Plus expansion stores for this. The table below summarises the trade-offs.

ApproachBest forAdvantagesDrawbacks
One store with Shopify MarketsMost brands entering EuropeOne admin, shared stock and apps, simpler reportingSome per-market customisation is limited by plan and theme

Decisions to make before you switch a market on

Turning on a currency takes minutes. Making a market work takes planning. Before you launch, agree answers to these questions.

  1. Which markets first? Start where you already see visits, enquiries or marketplace sales, and where delivery is practical.
  2. How will you price? Automatic conversion is quick, but rounded local prices (such as ending in .95 or .99) usually look more natural. Decide whether prices include VAT and delivery.
  3. What will delivery cost and how long will it take? Customers compare you with local retailers.
  4. How will returns work? Returning goods across a border can be slow and expensive for both sides.
  5. Which languages? English alone may work in Ireland, the Netherlands and the Nordics for some audiences, but German, French and Spanish shoppers usually expect their own language.
  6. Who handles customer service in each language and time zone?

VAT, duties and the One Stop Shop

Tax is where cross-border selling gets complicated. Within the EU, distance sales to consumers in other member states are generally subject to the VAT rate of the customer's country once a threshold is passed, and the One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme lets eligible businesses report that VAT through one registration. Businesses based outside the EU, including UK businesses, may face different rules, such as the Import One Stop Shop (IOSS) for some low-value consignments, plus customs declarations and duties.

These rules depend on where you are established, where your stock ships from, and what you sell. They also change. Please check current official guidance from the relevant tax authorities and take advice from an accountant who handles cross-border ecommerce before you configure tax settings. Shopify's tax settings then need to reflect that advice; our guide to Shopify VAT and tax settings explains the admin side.

Delivery, returns and payment methods

Shopify shipping profiles let you set different rates and zones for each market. Be realistic about delivery times and show them clearly on product pages and at checkout. A shopper in Madrid or Stockholm wants to know when the parcel will arrive and who will deliver it. Our guide to Shopify shipping profiles walks through the set-up.

Payment preferences vary by country. Card payments are common everywhere, but many shoppers expect local methods. iDEAL is widely used in the Netherlands, Bancontact in Belgium, and buy now pay later options are popular in several markets. Check which methods your payment provider supports in each country and turn on the ones your customers expect. Our overview of payment gateways explains the main options.

Returns policies should be written for each market, in the right language, and should state who pays for return postage. Clear policies reduce support tickets and abandoned checkouts.

Localised content and SEO

Translating the storefront is only the start. Product titles, descriptions, collection pages, size guides, policies and checkout text all need to read naturally. Shopify supports translation through its own tools and apps; whichever you use, have a fluent speaker review the pages that sell.

For search, each language version should have its own URLs, translated titles and meta descriptions, and correct hreflang annotations. Shopify handles some of this automatically when Markets uses subfolders or domains, but it is worth checking the output rather than assuming. Keyword research should be done in each language, because people in Germany or Spain search differently from a direct translation of your English terms. Common Shopify Markets mistakes

  • Forcing redirects by location. Suggest a market, but let shoppers choose. Forced redirects frustrate travellers and can hide versions from search engines.
  • Unrounded converted prices. Odd-looking prices reduce trust.
  • Untranslated apps and emails. Review widgets, pop-ups and order notifications often stay in English.
  • Hidden delivery costs. Surprise charges at checkout are a common reason for abandoned baskets.
  • Ignoring cookie and privacy rules. EU GDPR and ePrivacy rules affect tracking and consent; check current guidance in each country and make sure your consent banner works on every market version.
  • No market-level reporting. Set up analytics so you can see sales and conversion by market, not just in total.

FAQs

What is Shopify Markets?

Shopify Markets is a set of features for selling to multiple countries from one Shopify store. It lets you group countries into markets and control currency, pricing, languages, domains and product availability for each one, with options that depend on your plan.

Do I need a separate Shopify store for each European country?

Usually not. Most brands can serve several European countries from one store using Markets. Separate stores tend to suit businesses with very different catalogues, separate legal entities or separate stock systems per region.

Can Shopify show prices in euros?

Yes. You can show local currencies such as euros, krona or krone to shoppers in each market, with conversion handled where your payment set-up supports it. Many brands set rounded or fixed prices so they look natural to local shoppers.

Does Shopify handle EU VAT for me?

Shopify can calculate and display tax based on your settings, but it does not decide your tax obligations. Rules such as OSS and IOSS depend on your circumstances, so check current official guidance and take advice from an accountant before configuring tax.

Which payment methods do European shoppers expect?

Cards are common everywhere, but local methods matter. Examples include iDEAL in the Netherlands and Bancontact in Belgium, along with buy now pay later options in several markets. Check what your payment provider supports in each country.

How long does a Shopify Markets set-up take?

It depends on how many markets and languages you add and whether content is ready. Adding markets to an existing store is often quicker than a full build, while new ecommerce builds typically take five to twelve weeks.
