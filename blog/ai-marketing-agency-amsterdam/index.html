AI Marketing Agency for Amsterdam Businesses: Practical AI-Powered Marketing from a UK Studio

Looking for an AI marketing agency in Amsterdam? How AI-powered search, ads, content and automation work for Dutch firms, with human review and our fees.

An AI marketing agency for an Amsterdam business should use artificial intelligence to make your marketing faster and better informed, not to replace judgement. In practice, that means using AI tools to research keywords and competitors, draft and test content, structure ad campaigns, analyse data and automate repetitive tasks, with people checking every output before it reaches your customers.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio offering AI-powered marketing remotely to businesses across Europe. We have no office in Amsterdam or anywhere in the Netherlands. We work by video call, shared documents and monthly reports, with Amsterdam one hour ahead of UK time. Our core SEO and GEO service is where much of our AI-powered work sits, alongside paid media and website projects.

What AI-powered marketing actually means

The phrase is used loosely, so it helps to be specific. For us, AI-powered marketing covers two separate ideas:

  • Using AI to do marketing work. Research, first drafts, ad variations, data analysis, reporting and workflow automation, all reviewed by a person.
  • Marketing to AI search. Making sure your business is visible and accurately described when people ask ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Gemini, Perplexity and similar tools for recommendations.

Both matter for Amsterdam businesses. The first saves time and lets a smaller budget go further. The second is becoming a real source of discovery, particularly for software, professional services and considered purchases where buyers ask detailed questions before shortlisting suppliers. Our explainer on generative engine optimisation covers the second idea in depth.

Being found in AI search

AI assistants draw on what is published about your business across the web. If your website is thin, unclear or contradictory, they may describe you poorly or not mention you at all. Improving AI visibility largely builds on good SEO: clear service pages, answers to the questions buyers really ask, accurate business information, structured data and mentions on reputable sites.

For Amsterdam firms, this often means building content in both Dutch and English, because buyers ask questions in both. International companies based in Amsterdam may find English carries most of their demand, while consumer brands usually need strong Dutch pages. Our guide to AI Overviews and SEO explains how Google's AI answers choose sources, and our post on SEO for Amsterdam businesses covers traditional search in the city.

AI in paid advertising

Google Ads and Meta Ads already rely heavily on machine learning for bidding, targeting and placement. The job of an agency is to feed those systems good data and good creative, then keep them in check. We use AI tools to generate ad variations for testing, review search term reports faster and spot unusual changes in spend, but every live ad and budget decision is checked by a person.

Accurate conversion tracking matters more than ever, because automated bidding learns from the conversions it sees. Our post on Google Ads for Amsterdam businesses covers the search side, including Dutch and English campaigns.

Automation for e-commerce and lead generation

Many Amsterdam businesses sell online, often across the Netherlands, Belgium and further into Europe. Automation can take care of repetitive work such as tagging orders, alerting staff to low stock, routing enquiries to the right person or cleaning product data before it goes into a feed. On Shopify, many of these tasks can be handled with built-in tools, which our guide to Shopify Flow automation explains.

For marketplace sellers, AI can help draft and improve product listings, but each marketplace has its own rules. Our post on bol marketplace SEO covers listing optimisation for one of the main Dutch and Belgian marketplaces.

Privacy, consent and the EU AI Act

Dutch businesses fall under the GDPR for personal data, and the EU ePrivacy rules, as applied in Dutch law, mean analytics and advertising cookies normally need consent before they load. That affects AI-powered marketing in two ways. First, the data that ad platforms and analytics tools learn from depends on consent, so we set up consent mode and server-side options where appropriate. Second, putting customer data into AI tools needs care. We avoid entering personal data into tools unless there is a clear lawful basis, a suitable agreement with the provider and your approval.

The EU AI Act is being introduced in stages and includes transparency expectations for some uses of AI, such as making clear when people are interacting with an AI system. The Dutch data protection authority and the European Commission publish official guidance. We keep our notes general, and decisions on legal compliance should come from your own adviser.

Websites and accessibility

AI cannot fix a website that is hard to use. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing products and services, including a wide range of online shops and booking services. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, but building to WCAG AA, the level referenced by the harmonised standard EN 301 549, is good practice for every site. Our post on web design for Amsterdam businesses covers the website side, and whether the Act applies to your business is a question for your adviser.

What we will not do with AI

  • Publish AI-written content without human editing and fact-checking.
  • Generate fake reviews, testimonials or invented statistics.
  • Mass-produce thin pages aimed only at search engines.
  • Put your customers' personal data into tools without a lawful basis and your agreement.
  • Promise rankings or mentions in AI answers, which no agency controls.
