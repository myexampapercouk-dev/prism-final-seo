Hotel Marketing for Nice and the Riviera

Hotel marketing in Nice explained: how Riviera hotels can win more direct bookings with a better website, SEO, Google Ads and Meta Ads, managed remotely by us.

Hotel marketing in Nice is about winning more bookings directly, rather than relying entirely on large booking platforms that take a commission on every stay. For most Riviera hotels that means a fast, multilingual website with a clear booking engine, strong visibility in Google searches and maps, and targeted ads that reach travellers while they are planning a trip.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that works remotely. We have no office in Nice or anywhere in France. We support independent hotels, boutique properties, aparthotels and guesthouses that are happy to work with a remote team through video calls, shared dashboards and written monthly reports, and we explain that clearly at the start.

The Nice hotel market

Nice is the main gateway to the Côte d'Azur, with an international airport, a long seafront, an old town, museums and easy links to Monaco, Cannes, Antibes and the hill villages. Guests arrive from France, the UK, Italy, Germany, Scandinavia, the USA and the Middle East. Demand changes through the year, with summer beach stays, spring and autumn city breaks, winter visitors seeking mild weather, and event-driven peaks such as the carnival and major conferences.

That variety brings opportunity but also strong competition. A small hotel near the Promenade des Anglais competes with chains, apartments and the booking platforms themselves for the same searches. Clear positioning and good digital marketing help it stand out.

A website that wins direct bookings

Your website is where every channel sends people. If it is slow, confusing or only available in one language, the guest goes back to a booking platform and books there instead.

  • Fast mobile pages. Most travel browsing happens on phones. We compress photos and keep scripts lean.
  • Clear booking path. A visible book button on every page, linking straight to your booking engine with dates pre-filled where possible.
  • Reasons to book direct. Flexible cancellation, breakfast, late checkout, parking or room upgrades, shown clearly near the booking button.
  • Room pages that answer questions. Size, beds, view, balcony, accessibility and what is included.
  • Location content. How to arrive from the airport and station, and what is within walking distance.

Small hotel websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and usually take 3 to 5 weeks. Our guide to hotel direct bookings explains the principles in more detail, and our booking systems article covers how booking engines connect to a site.

Multilingual content for international guests

A Nice hotel website usually needs at least French and English, and often Italian, German or other languages depending on your guest mix. Each language should have its own pages, with hreflang tags so search engines show the right version.

Prism writes in English. French and other language versions come from your team or a translator, and we build the structure, menus and booking links around them. Our multilingual website design guide explains the options.

SEO for Nice hotels

Hotel SEO is about more than ranking for the hotel name. Travellers search for areas, trip types and features, such as a hotel near the seafront, a family hotel in Nice, a hotel with parking, or a boutique hotel in the old town. Pages that answer these searches well can attract guests at an early stage of planning.

  • Optimise room, area and offer pages for the searches guests actually make.
  • Create helpful guides to nearby beaches, markets, day trips and events.
  • Add hotel structured data so search engines understand your property.
  • Fix technical issues such as slow galleries, duplicate language pages and broken booking links.

Our hotel SEO guide sets out the method, which applies equally to Riviera properties.

Google Business Profile and reviews

When someone searches for hotels in Nice, the map results often appear before the regular listings. A complete Google Business Profile with accurate details, good photos, amenities and regular replies to reviews helps your hotel appear and look trustworthy there. We help set up and maintain the profile; the reviews themselves must come from genuine guests. See our Google Business Profile posts guide for ideas on keeping it active.

Google Ads and Meta Ads for hotels

Paid advertising gives you control over timing. You can push harder before a busy season or quieter months, and pull back when the hotel is full.

  • Brand search campaigns. Booking platforms often bid on hotel names. A brand campaign keeps your own site at the top of those searches.
  • Area and trip searches. Targeted ads for searches like boutique hotel Nice or Nice seafront hotel, sent to matching pages.
  • Meta remarketing. Ads on Facebook and Instagram to people who visited your site but did not book.
  • Seasonal offers. Campaigns for off-season breaks, long stays or event dates.

We manage Google Ads, Meta Ads and, where they fit, TikTok and YouTube Ads using creative you already have. We do not film or edit video. More on our approach is on our paid media service page.

Measuring what matters

Hotels need to know which channels bring bookings, not just clicks. We connect your booking engine to analytics where the engine supports it, track booking starts and completions, and report revenue by channel when the data allows. Consent and privacy notices must follow EU rules; the wording should come from your own adviser using official French and EU guidance.

Nice is one hour ahead of the UK, so scheduling calls is simple. We work in English, share access to every account so you keep ownership, and send a written report each month with clear next steps. Planning a year of hotel marketing

Riviera demand follows a clear rhythm, and marketing works best when it is planned ahead of each peak rather than in reaction to empty rooms.

  • Winter. Promote mild-weather breaks, long stays and carnival dates to guests from colder countries.
  • Spring. Push city breaks, Easter trips and early summer booking offers.
  • Summer. Protect brand searches, keep rates and availability clear, and reduce spend when the hotel is full.
  • Autumn. Target conference visitors, couples and off-season deals, and email past guests about returning.

We agree a simple calendar with you at the start, so pages, offers and ad budgets are ready weeks before each period begins. That approach usually gets more value from the same budget than last-minute campaigns, and it gives SEO content time to rank before guests start searching.

We also review results after each season. Which pages brought bookings, which campaigns cost too much, and which guest markets grew all feed into the next year's plan. Over time this builds a clear picture of where your direct bookings really come from, so budget moves towards the channels that work best for your property.
