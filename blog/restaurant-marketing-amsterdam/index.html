Restaurants we support in Amsterdam

Amsterdam has a busy, competitive food scene, with residents, commuters and a steady flow of international visitors. Different venues need different approaches. We support:

  • Neighbourhood restaurants in areas such as De Pijp, Jordaan and Oost that rely on repeat local diners.
  • Central restaurants and cafés that attract tourists searching on their phones.
  • Bars and wine bars that want bookings for groups and events.
  • Restaurant groups with several sites and a shared brand.
  • Takeaway and delivery-led kitchens that want more direct orders.

A restaurant website that turns visits into bookings

Most diners check a restaurant website on a phone, often while walking or on a tram. The site has to load quickly and answer the basic questions straight away: what you serve, where you are, when you are open and how to book. We build restaurant sites with:

  • Menus as real text, not PDFs, so they load fast and can be read by search engines.
  • A clear booking button linking to the reservation system you already use.
  • Dietary information such as vegetarian, vegan and allergen notes, supplied and checked by your team.
  • Location details with a map, nearby transport and accessibility information.
  • Dutch and English versions with a working language switcher.

Local SEO for Amsterdam restaurants

For restaurants, the map listing often matters more than the website. We help you set up and maintain your business profile with accurate hours, menu links, photos, categories and booking links. We check your details are consistent on the listing sites diners use. We encourage you to ask for honest reviews and reply to them, but we never write fake reviews or offer rewards that break platform rules.

Paid ads for restaurants

Paid ads work well for openings, new menus, seasonal events and quieter weekday evenings. On Google, we target people searching for your cuisine or area. On Meta, we show your food and atmosphere to people nearby or planning a visit. Our posts on Google Ads in Amsterdam and Meta Ads in the Netherlands explain our setup. Ad spend is paid separately to the platforms.

If you serve alcohol, check any rules on advertising alcohol with official guidance or your adviser, and keep ads responsible. Approval of ad content rests with you.

Privacy, cookies and accessibility

Restaurant websites collect personal data through bookings, newsletter forms and tracking. These notes are general, so please check official guidance or ask your adviser.

  • GDPR covers booking details, email lists and analytics data. Your privacy notice should say what you collect and why.
  • ePrivacy and cookie consent rules generally require consent before analytics and advertising cookies are set. We set up tracking to respect your consent banner.
  • The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to certain consumer-facing digital services, including many e-commerce services such as online ordering. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt, which covers many single-site restaurants. The harmonised standard EN 301 549 points to WCAG level AA, and we design to that level anyway because it helps every diner.

Planning around the Amsterdam restaurant calendar

Restaurant demand in Amsterdam rises and falls through the year, and good marketing plans for that rather than reacting at the last minute. Spring and summer bring terrace season and many more visitors. Major city events and holidays can fill tables on their own. Winter evenings and January can be much quieter, especially for venues away from the centre.

We build a simple calendar with you that sets out when to promote set menus, terrace openings, private dining, group bookings and seasonal dishes. Ads can then be switched on for quieter weeks and turned down when you are already full, so the budget goes where it is most useful.

We also look at the small details that affect bookings day to day:

  • Opening hours kept accurate on the website and map listing, including holiday changes.
  • Photos refreshed regularly so they show the current menu and space.
  • Menu updates published on the site quickly so diners never see old dishes or prices.
  • Group and event enquiries handled through a simple form that goes straight to the right person.
  • Gift vouchers sold online where your booking or payment system supports them.

For restaurant groups with several venues, we set up a clear structure with one page per location, each with its own hours, menu, booking link and map listing. This helps diners pick the right venue and helps each one rank for searches in its own neighbourhood.

Finally, we keep reporting simple. Restaurant owners rarely have time for long dashboards, so our monthly summary focuses on booking clicks, calls, direction requests, website visits and ad spend, along with a short list of what we will do next month. If something is not working, we say so plainly and suggest a change, so you always know where your marketing budget is going and why.
