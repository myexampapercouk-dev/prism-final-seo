Why choose a web design and SEO company

Many businesses hire a designer for the website and someone else for SEO. That can work, but it often leads to problems that a single team avoids.

  • Lost rankings at launch: redesigns that change URLs without redirects can wipe out years of search visibility.
  • Pages that cannot rank: beautiful layouts with little text, poor headings or content hidden in images give Google little to work with.
  • Slow pages: heavy design choices can hurt speed and Core Web Vitals.
  • Blame between suppliers: when traffic drops, two separate providers can each point at the other.

When the same team plans the site and the search strategy, the sitemap reflects what people actually search for, each page has a clear purpose and the launch is handled with rankings in mind. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings explains the risks in more detail.

What the web design side covers

The design and build stage creates a site that is clear, fast and easy to use. Our process includes discovery, sitemap planning, mobile-first design, development on WordPress or Shopify, content support, testing and launch. A standard business site usually takes three to five weeks and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks.

From an SEO point of view, the most important decisions here are the sitemap and the page templates. Each service, product category or location you want to rank for usually needs its own page, with its own heading, copy and internal links.

Search is changing: AI answers and GEO

Search now includes AI-generated answers, such as Google's AI Overviews, as well as traditional results. Pages that answer questions clearly, use well-structured headings and show genuine expertise tend to be easier for these systems to understand and quote. We build sites with that in mind, using clear question-led sections, concise answers near the top of each page and tidy structured data. Questions to ask a web design and SEO company

  1. How do you decide which pages the site needs? The answer should involve keyword research, not just a standard template.
  2. What happens to my existing rankings? Ask for a redirect plan and how they will check it after launch.
  3. Which SEO tasks are included in the build? Titles, headings, sitemap, schema, analytics and Search Console should be standard.
  4. What does ongoing SEO include each month? Look for content, technical fixes, local work and reporting.
  5. How will you report progress? Rankings, organic traffic and enquiries, explained in plain English.
  6. Who owns everything? Your site, content, analytics and Search Console should all be in your name.

Be cautious of any company that promises guaranteed rankings or first-page results within weeks. Nobody controls Google's results.

For businesses that serve a town, city or region, local search is often the biggest opportunity. A combined approach means your site has clear location and service pages, your contact details are consistent and your Google Business Profile links to the right pages. The same landing pages and tracking can serve both channels, and paid search data can show which keywords convert best, which then guides the SEO plan. Ad spend is always paid directly to Google.

What a joined-up launch looks like

The launch is where design and SEO most often collide. Handled well, it is a quiet event. Handled badly, it can undo years of search progress in a day. This is the order we follow on every launch.

  1. Crawl the old site: we record every URL, title and description, plus the pages that bring in the most traffic and links.
  2. Map redirects: each old URL is pointed at the closest new page, so visitors and search engines land in the right place.
  3. Check on staging: titles, headings, internal links, structured data and page speed are reviewed before anything goes live.
  4. Go live and resubmit: the new sitemap is submitted in Google Search Console and key pages are requested for indexing.
  5. Monitor: for the following weeks we watch for errors, broken links and unexpected ranking changes, and fix them quickly.

Our SEO migration checklist sets out these steps in more detail if you want to follow them yourself.

Content: where design and SEO meet

Most of the long-term value in SEO comes from content that answers real customer questions. Good design makes that content easy to read, with clear headings, short paragraphs, useful tables and obvious next steps. Good SEO makes sure each page targets a distinct topic and links sensibly to related pages. When one team handles both, new service pages, guides and FAQs slot naturally into the site rather than feeling bolted on. Over time that creates a site that is easier to navigate for visitors and easier for search engines and AI tools to understand. We also review older pages regularly, updating facts, tightening copy and merging pages that compete with each other, because a tidy site often ranks better than a large untidy one.

FAQs

What is a web design and SEO company?

It is a company that both builds your website and optimises it for search. Handling both together means search is planned into the site from the start rather than fixed afterwards.

Is it better to use one company for web design and SEO?

Often, yes. One team avoids lost rankings at launch, keeps the sitemap aligned with search demand and removes blame between suppliers. Separate specialists can still work if they coordinate closely.

How much does web design and SEO cost together?

Our websites cost £1,000 to £2,000, or £1,500 to £3,500 for ecommerce. Ongoing SEO retainers cost £350, £750 or £1,500 a month.

How long does SEO take after a new website launches?

SEO results typically build over three to six months. A well-planned site with good technical foundations usually gets there faster than one that needs fixing first.

Can you guarantee first page rankings?

No. Nobody can guarantee rankings, because Google controls its results. We focus on the work that tends to improve visibility and report progress honestly each month.

Will my rankings drop if I redesign my website?

Not if the redesign is handled carefully. We map old URLs to new ones, keep valuable content and check indexing after launch. Some short-term movement is normal.
