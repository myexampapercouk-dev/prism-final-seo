How UK Hotels and Restaurants Can Get More Direct Bookings

Practical ways for UK hotels, B&Bs and restaurants to win more direct bookings and rely less on OTAs: website, booking engine, Google, ads and guest loyalty.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Direct bookings start with a fast, mobile-friendly website where the booking button is always visible and the booking engine is simple.
  • Google is where many guests decide: a complete Business Profile, accurate details and brand-name ads protect your direct channel.
  • Give guests a reason to book direct, such as flexible terms, perks or best-rate assurance, and keep past guests close with email.

UK hotels, B&Bs, pubs with rooms and restaurants can win more direct bookings by making their own website the easiest place to book, being highly visible on Google, and giving guests a clear reason to book direct instead of through an online travel agent (OTA) or booking platform. None of this means abandoning OTAs, which bring valuable reach; it means rebalancing so more of your bookings carry no commission.

This guide covers the practical steps that make the biggest difference, in the order we would usually tackle them.

Why direct bookings matter

Every booking through an OTA or third-party platform carries a commission, which comes straight off your margin. Direct bookings also give you the guest's contact details and the relationship, which makes repeat stays and visits far more likely. Even a modest shift in the mix from OTA to direct can make a noticeable difference to profit over a year.

OTAs still have a role: they help guests discover you, particularly first-time visitors and international travellers. The goal is to make sure that when a guest has found you, they choose to book on your site.

Step 1: Make your website the easiest place to book

Many guests check your website after finding you elsewhere. If it is slow, confusing or awkward on a phone, they go back to the OTA. Focus on:

  • Mobile speed. Most hospitality browsing happens on phones. Compress images, avoid heavy scripts and test on a real mobile connection.
  • A visible booking button on every page, including a sticky button on mobile.
  • A simple booking engine that shows availability and prices quickly, with as few steps as possible.
  • Clear room or table information: photos, sizes, features, accessibility details and policies.
  • Up-to-date menus, opening times and events for restaurants and pubs.
  • Trust signals you genuinely have, such as real guest reviews and awards actually held.

Our website development work for hospitality businesses is built around these basics, including clean integration of your chosen booking or reservation system.

Step 2: Own your presence on Google

Google is often where the booking decision is made. Guests search your name, look at photos, check reviews and compare prices, sometimes without visiting any website.

Google Business Profile

Complete every section: categories, description, opening hours, photos, menu or room information, attributes such as accessibility and parking, and a link to your booking page. Respond to reviews, positive and negative, politely and promptly.

Hotel prices on Google

For hotels, Google can show room prices from different booking sites alongside your listing. Connecting your own rates, usually through your booking engine or channel manager provider, means your direct price appears next to the OTAs, which is often the moment guests choose where to book.

Local SEO

Beyond your name, guests search for things like "boutique hotel in the Cotswolds" or "dog-friendly pub with rooms near me". Location pages, content about your area and consistent details across directories help you appear. Our SEO and GEO service covers local search.

Step 3: Give guests a reason to book direct

If your direct price and terms are the same as an OTA's, many guests stick with the platform they know. A small, clear benefit changes that. Options include:

Best-rate assuranceHotels and B&BsCheck it is accurate and that your rate parity agreements allow it
Flexible cancellationAll accommodationHighly valued by guests; set clear terms
Small perksHotels, restaurantsWelcome drink, late checkout, parking or breakfast
PackagesHotels, venuesDinner, bed and breakfast; spa or event packages
Member or returning guest offersAllEncourages sign-ups and repeat bookings

Show the benefit clearly on your website and in the booking engine, not hidden in small print.

Step 4: Protect your brand searches with ads

When someone searches your hotel or restaurant name, OTAs and booking platforms may bid on it and appear above your own listing. Running a small brand campaign on Google Ads keeps your own website at the top for these searches. Brand campaigns usually have low costs per click and high conversion rates, because the searcher already wants you.

Beyond brand, paid search and Meta ads can promote events, seasonal offers, private dining and quieter periods. Set up conversion tracking first so you can see bookings, not just clicks. Our paid media service works this way.

Step 5: Keep past guests close

Your past guests are your most valuable audience. With their consent, collect email addresses at booking and stay, and send occasional, useful emails: seasonal menus, events, offers for returning guests and reminders before key dates such as Valentine's Day or Christmas. Make sure your email marketing follows UK GDPR and PECR rules on consent.

Social media helps keep you front of mind too, especially for restaurants and venues. A steady rhythm of genuine content about food, rooms, events and your team works better than occasional bursts.

Step 6: Measure your booking mix

Track where bookings come from each month: direct website, phone, OTAs and other platforms. Set up GA4 with booking engine tracking so you can see which marketing channels lead to direct bookings. Watch the share of direct bookings over time, and the cost of each channel, including commission.

This makes decisions easier. If a modest ad budget brings in direct bookings at a lower cost than OTA commission, it is worth expanding.

Content that helps guests choose you

Guests rarely book on price alone. They want to picture the stay or the meal. Content on your own website can do that better than an OTA listing, and it also helps you appear in searches beyond your name.

  • Local area guides: walks, attractions, events and places to eat nearby, written from genuine local knowledge.
  • Occasion pages: anniversaries, weddings, business stays, family breaks or dog-friendly trips, each with the practical details guests need.
  • Room and menu detail: honest descriptions, good photography and answers to common questions about parking, accessibility and dietary needs.
  • Event listings kept up to date, with booking links.

This kind of content also gives AI assistants clear, accurate information to draw on when someone asks for a recommendation, which is increasingly how travellers start planning. Our guide to generative engine optimisation explains why accuracy and consistency matter.

Quick wins for this month

  • Test booking a room or table on your own website on a phone, and fix anything frustrating.
  • Complete and refresh your Google Business Profile, including recent photos.
  • Add a clear "book direct" benefit to your website.
  • Check whether competitors or OTAs appear above you for your own name on Google.
  • Reply to recent reviews.

Mistakes that push guests back to OTAs

  • A booking engine that opens in a separate, unbranded site with a different look and feel.
  • Direct rates that are higher than those on booking platforms.
  • Hidden fees that only appear at the last step.
  • Out-of-date photos, prices or opening times.
  • No way to contact you quickly with a question before booking.

Restaurants and pubs: extra points

For restaurants, the equivalent of OTA commission is often the fee charged by reservation or delivery platforms. The same principles apply: make reservations easy on your own website, keep your Google listing accurate with menus and opening times, and build a list of regular customers. Keeping menus as web pages rather than PDFs helps both mobile users and search engines read them.

FAQs

How can a small hotel get more direct bookings?

Make booking on your own website fast and easy on mobile, complete your Google Business Profile, offer a clear benefit for booking direct and run a small brand campaign on Google Ads.

Should hotels stop using OTAs?

Usually not. OTAs bring reach and new guests. The aim is to rebalance so that more guests who find you choose to book directly.

Why do OTAs appear above my hotel when people search my name?

They may be bidding on your brand name in Google Ads. Running your own brand campaign helps keep your website at the top.

What booking engine should a hotel use?

Choose one that is mobile-friendly, integrates with your channel manager and can share your rates with Google. We can help integrate the system you choose into your website.

Do restaurants need a website if they are on booking apps?

Yes. Your own website and Google listing are where many guests check menus and book, and direct reservations avoid platform fees.
