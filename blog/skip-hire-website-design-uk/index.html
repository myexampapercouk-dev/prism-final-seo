Skip Hire Website Design: Sizes, Online Ordering and Waste Rules Explained

Skip hire website design for UK companies: skip size guides, postcode checks, online ordering, permit and waste wording, and pages for grab and waste services.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Skip hire customers mostly want to know which size they need, whether you deliver to their postcode, the price and when it can arrive, so put those answers up front.
  • Online ordering can suit skip hire well, but only if postcode coverage, availability and permit needs are handled properly in the checkout.
  • Explain what can and cannot go in a skip and how permits work in hedged, general terms, since waste rules and council requirements vary.

Skip hire is one of the more transactional local services. Someone clearing a garage, renovating a kitchen or landscaping a garden usually knows they need a skip; what they do not know is which size, whether it can go on the road outside, what they are allowed to put in it and how much it will cost. A skip hire website that answers those questions quickly, and ideally lets the customer order there and then, turns a comparison search into a booking.

This guide covers the website itself. For wider marketing, including reviews and local visibility, see our guide to tradesman marketing in the UK. If you also run a man-and-van or clearance service, the advice in our guide to removals company website design on quoting variable jobs may be useful alongside this.

Lead with the four key answers

Most skip hire visitors are trying to answer the same four questions:

  1. Which size skip do I need?
  2. Do you deliver to my postcode?
  3. How much will it cost?
  4. When can it arrive and how long can I keep it?

A homepage that puts a postcode checker, a size chooser and a clear route to price and booking near the top respects the visitor's time. Supporting content, such as permit information and waste rules, can sit lower down or on its own pages.

A skip size guide that people can actually use

Skip sizes are usually described in cubic yards, which mean little to most homeowners. A good size guide translates them into everyday terms. For each size you offer, consider showing:

  • The size and approximate dimensions.
  • A rough sense of capacity, such as how many bin bags it might hold, described as an approximation.
  • The kinds of jobs it typically suits, such as a small bathroom refit or a full house clearance.
  • Any restrictions, such as heavy waste limits on larger skips.
  • A photo of your actual skip, ideally with something for scale.

A simple comparison table works well here. Make clear that capacity examples are guides and that heavy materials such as soil or rubble may be limited by weight rather than volume, if that applies to your service.

Postcode coverage and availability

Skip hire is limited by how far your lorries can travel economically. A postcode checker, whether built into the order flow or offered as a standalone tool, tells visitors immediately whether you can help. If you cannot, say so politely and quickly rather than letting them fill in a long form first.

Area pages can help with local search, but only if they say something real, such as typical delivery times or local permit arrangements. Dozens of copy-and-paste town pages are unhelpful and may conflict with Google's guidelines. Keep your business details consistent with your Google Business Profile, and see our local SEO guide for more.

Online ordering for skips

Skip hire suits online ordering better than many trades because the product is fairly standard. An effective order flow might include:

  • Postcode entry first, so only available sizes and prices appear.
  • Size selection with the guide close at hand.
  • Placement choice: on a driveway or private land, or on the road.
  • Delivery date selection based on real availability.
  • Clear statement of the hire period and what extensions cost.
  • Checkout with the total price, including any permit fee and VAT treatment, shown before payment.

Platforms such as WooCommerce or Shopify can handle this with suitable configuration or extensions, and some skip firms use booking-style systems instead. Whatever you choose, test it on a phone and make sure confirmation emails explain what happens next. Our guide to website online booking systems covers the trade-offs between booking and ecommerce approaches.

Online payment brings obligations, such as clear terms, cancellation information and a privacy notice. Consumer contract rules can give customers cancellation rights in some situations, so check how they apply to your service and present your terms clearly rather than relying on general assumptions.

Skip permits, explained carefully

If a skip goes on a public road, a permit from the local council is usually needed, and councils may set conditions such as lighting and markings. Customers often do not know this. A clear permit page can explain, in general terms, when a permit may be needed, whether you apply on the customer's behalf, how long it can take and how the cost is passed on. Because requirements and fees differ between councils, avoid stating fixed timescales or fees for every area unless you keep them accurate, and suggest customers check with their council if they are unsure.

What can and cannot go in a skip

This is one of the most useful pages a skip hire site can have, and it can prevent problems on collection. Explain in plain language the types of waste you accept, the items you cannot take, and items that may incur extra charges. Commonly restricted items include things such as asbestos, certain electrical items, tyres, gas cylinders, paint and plasterboard, but your own list depends on your permits and processing arrangements, so base the page on what actually applies to your business.

Waste handling in the UK is regulated, and environmental regulators in each nation set rules for carriers and sites. Mention your waste carrier registration or relevant permits accurately if you hold them, and avoid broad claims about legal duties. Householders also have a duty of care over who takes their waste, and a short, hedged note on this can reassure customers that they are dealing with a legitimate business.

Recycling and environmental claims

Many skip firms sort and recycle waste, and customers may value this. If you mention recycling, be specific and honest. Avoid headline figures or percentages unless you can back them up, and avoid vague phrases such as "eco-friendly" that are hard to substantiate. UK guidance on green claims from the Competition and Markets Authority applies to environmental marketing, so precise, true statements are safer than broad ones.

Other services: grab hire, wait and load, and clearances

Many skip companies also offer grab lorries, wait-and-load services, roll-on roll-off containers or rubbish clearance. Each deserves its own page explaining when it is the better option. For example, a grab lorry may suit large amounts of soil where a skip would be impractical, while wait-and-load suits customers without space to keep a skip. Helping visitors choose the right service builds trust and reduces wasted calls. Commercial customers, such as builders and businesses, may also want trade accounts or regular collections, which could warrant a separate section.

Pricing transparency

Skip hire is a price-compared service, so being clear helps. If you show prices online, show the full price for the customer's postcode and size, with extras such as permits, overweight charges and extensions explained before checkout. Avoid "from" prices that most customers will never pay. Misleading pricing can breach consumer protection law. Our guide to pricing page design covers how to present prices clearly.

Mobile, speed and trust

Many skip orders happen on phones, often mid-project. Keep pages fast, buttons large and the order flow short. Our guides to mobile website design and website speed optimisation explain how. Show genuine reviews and photos of your own lorries and skips; fake reviews are prohibited under UK consumer law.

FAQs

Should a skip hire website take orders online?

It often makes sense, provided the order flow checks postcode coverage, availability and permit needs, and shows the full price before payment.

How should I present skip sizes?

Translate cubic yards into everyday terms, with approximate capacity, typical jobs, restrictions and photos of your own skips.

Do I need a page about skip permits?

It is helpful. Explain when a permit may be needed and whether you apply for it, noting that council rules and fees vary.

What should I say about prohibited waste?

List what you do and do not accept based on your own permits and processing, in plain language.

Can I claim high recycling rates?

Only if you can back the figure up. Specific, true statements are safer than vague environmental claims.
