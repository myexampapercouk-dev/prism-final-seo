Shopify Cart Drawer vs Cart Page: Choosing and Setting Up Your Basket

Shopify cart drawer vs cart page: how each basket style works, which suits your store, what to include in the basket and how to test it on mobile.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A cart drawer keeps shoppers on the page they are browsing, while a full cart page gives more room for detail, notes and reassurance.
  • Many Shopify themes let you switch basket style in the theme settings, so you can try each one without rebuilding the store.
  • Whichever style you choose, keep the basket clear: accurate totals, delivery information, an obvious checkout button and nothing that slows it down.

In Shopify, a cart drawer slides out from the side of the screen when a customer adds a product, while a cart page is a separate full page that shows the basket. A drawer suits stores where people buy several items in one visit and want to keep browsing, and a cart page suits stores where customers need more detail, notes or reassurance before they check out.

This guide compares the two, explains how to switch between them in most themes, and covers what to put in the basket, whichever style you choose. Theme settings vary and Shopify's own themes change over time, so check your theme's documentation for the exact options available.

What each basket style does

Most modern Shopify themes offer two or three basket behaviours when someone adds a product.

  • Cart drawer: a panel slides in from the side, showing the basket contents, subtotal and a checkout button. The customer can close it and carry on browsing.
  • Cart page: the customer is taken to a dedicated basket page, or sees a link to it, with the basket laid out in full.
  • Notification or pop-up: some themes show a small confirmation message with links to view the basket or check out, leaving the customer on the product page.

Even when a theme uses a drawer, the cart page usually still exists at its own address. That matters because some links, apps and customers will still land on it, so it needs to look right too.

There is no universal winner. The right choice depends on what you sell and how people buy it. If you are unsure, look at your order data. If most orders contain one item, a cart page or notification might be enough. If many orders contain several items, a drawer that lets people keep browsing may suit them better. How to switch basket style in Shopify

In many themes, including Shopify's own free themes, the setting sits in the theme editor's general settings, often under a cart or basket heading. The usual steps are:

  1. Duplicate your live theme so you can test changes safely.
  2. Open the duplicate in the theme editor and find the theme settings, usually shown as a gear or settings icon.
  3. Look for the cart type setting and choose drawer, page or notification.
  4. Adjust related settings, such as whether to show order notes or delivery information in the basket.
  5. Preview on desktop and mobile, add products and go through to checkout.
  6. Publish the theme once you are happy, at a quieter time of day.

If your theme does not offer the option you want, adding it is a development job rather than a setting. Our guide to Shopify theme customisation covers what can be changed in the editor and what needs code. Always make and test changes in a duplicate theme first, so customers never see a half-finished basket on the live store.

What to include in your basket

Whichever style you choose, the basket has one job: to give customers confidence to continue to checkout. These elements matter most.

  • Clear product details: image, name, chosen variant and any personalisation, so customers can check they have the right item.
  • Easy quantity changes and removal: controls large enough to tap on a phone.
  • Accurate subtotal: with a short note on whether delivery is calculated at checkout.
  • Delivery information: delivery cost or free delivery threshold and typical dispatch times. Our guide to online shop shipping rates covers how to set these.
  • An obvious checkout button: high contrast, near the top on mobile and clearly labelled.
  • Express payment options where suitable: such as wallet buttons, if your payment setup supports them. Check your Shopify checkout settings to see what is enabled.
  • Order notes or gift messages if customers genuinely use them. Our guide to Shopify gift wrapping and order notes explains the options.

Upsells and free delivery bars in the basket

The basket is a natural place for a small, relevant suggestion, such as a matching accessory or a refill. It is also a common place for a free delivery progress bar, which tells customers how much more they need to spend to qualify.

Used sparingly, both can help. Used heavily, they distract customers at the point they are ready to pay. Keep suggestions to one or two genuinely related products and make them easy to ignore. Our guide to Shopify upsells and cross-sells covers how to choose suggestions that help rather than annoy.

Many basket features come from apps. Each one adds scripts, so check speed after installing them, and remove any that do not earn their place. Our Shopify speed optimisation guide explains how to measure the impact.

Testing the basket properly

Basket problems are easy to miss because staff rarely shop on their own store. Before and after any change, run through these checks on a phone and a desktop:

  • Add a single product, several products and a product with variants or custom options.
  • Change quantities and remove items, checking the subtotal updates correctly.
  • Apply a discount code at checkout and confirm the basket and checkout totals match.
  • Check that the drawer closes easily and does not trap keyboard users or hide content on small screens.
  • Visit the cart page directly by its address and make sure it still works and looks consistent.
  • Test with any upsell, delivery bar or subscription apps switched on, since these can conflict.

Accessibility matters here as well. Drawers should be usable with a keyboard and announced properly to screen readers. If you are unsure, try opening and closing the drawer using only the Tab, Enter and Escape keys, and check that focus moves into the drawer when it opens and returns to the page when it closes. Make sure text and buttons have enough contrast, and that nothing important is hidden behind a fixed header or chat widget on small screens.

Measuring whether the change helped

If you switch basket style, give it time and compare like with like. Look at the proportion of sessions that reach checkout and at average order value over a few weeks, avoiding periods with big promotions or seasonal peaks. Small stores may not have enough traffic for firm conclusions, so pair the numbers with your own observation and customer feedback.

FAQs

Shopify cart drawer vs cart page: which is better?

Neither is better for every store. A drawer suits stores where customers often buy several items and keep browsing. A cart page suits higher-value or more complex orders where customers want more detail before checking out.

How do I turn on the cart drawer in Shopify?

In many themes, open the theme editor, go to theme settings, find the cart section and choose drawer as the cart type. If your theme has no such setting, adding a drawer needs theme development or an app.

Does a cart drawer increase sales?

It can help some stores by keeping customers browsing, but results vary by product, price and audience. Test it on your own store and compare checkout rates and order values over a fair period.

Do I still need a cart page if I use a drawer?

The cart page usually still exists, and some customers, links and apps will reach it. Keep it working and consistent with the drawer, even if most shoppers never see it.

Should I add upsells to my Shopify basket?

One or two closely related suggestions can work well. Too many distract customers who are ready to pay and can slow the basket down, so keep them relevant and light.

Why does my basket total differ from the checkout total?

The basket usually shows a subtotal before delivery, and sometimes before discounts or taxes are calculated. Make this clear in the basket so customers are not surprised at checkout.
