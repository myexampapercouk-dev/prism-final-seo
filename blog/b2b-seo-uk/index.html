B2B SEO in the UK: A Strategy for Long Sales Cycles

B2B SEO in the UK means targeting low-volume, high-intent searches with service, comparison and pricing pages, then nurturing buyers over long cycles.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with bottom-of-funnel pages such as services, use cases, comparisons and pricing, because those are the searches closest to a sales conversation.
  • B2B keywords often have tiny search volumes, so judge them by deal value and intent rather than traffic.
  • Measure B2B SEO by enquiries and pipeline, which means joining up analytics with your CRM.

B2B SEO in the UK works best when you start at the bottom of the funnel: service pages, use-case pages, comparison pages and honest pricing content that match what buyers search when they are close to talking to a supplier. Then you support those pages with guides that help committees research, and you measure success by qualified enquiries and pipeline rather than traffic.

This is different from consumer SEO, where volume often wins. In B2B, a search with twenty monthly searches can be worth more than one with twenty thousand, if the people typing it sign five-figure contracts. What makes B2B SEO different

Business buyers rarely buy on the first visit. Several people are usually involved: the person who finds you, a manager who signs off budget, perhaps procurement, IT or legal. Each of them may search for different things at different times, and the process can take weeks or months.

That has a practical consequence: keyword research tools often show zero volume for perfectly good B2B terms. Use them as a guide, then add terms from sales calls, proposals, support tickets and Search Console queries.

A B2B SEO strategy for UK companies: bottom of funnel first

Most B2B sites publish blog posts first and service pages last. Reverse that. Pages that capture buying intent tend to produce enquiries sooner, and they give later content somewhere useful to link.

Service and solution pages

One page per service, written for the buyer rather than about you. Explain the problem, who it is for, how you deliver it, what it typically involves, what it costs or what drives cost, and what happens after someone gets in touch. Avoid lumping five services onto one page.

Industry and use-case pages

Buyers often search with their sector attached: "payroll software for construction firms", "IT support for law firms in Manchester". If you genuinely serve those sectors, a dedicated page with relevant detail can match that intent far better than a generic service page.

Comparison and alternative pages

Searches such as "X vs Y", "X alternatives" or "in-house vs outsourced" come from people actively weighing options. Write fair, factual comparisons. Stick to publicly verifiable information about competitors, avoid disparaging claims, and remember the CAP Code covers comparative advertising in marketing communications. Our post on SEO agency vs freelancer is an example of the format.

Pricing and cost content

"How much does X cost" is one of the most common buyer searches and one of the most avoided by B2B companies. Even if you quote case by case, you can explain the factors, typical ranges in the market and what changes the price. Buyers who have seen a range before calling are usually better qualified.

Supporting content for the research phase

Once your money pages exist, build guides that help buyers understand the problem and build a business case. Good B2B guides often include:

  • Checklists and templates that someone can share with colleagues.
  • Explanations of UK regulations or standards that affect the purchase.
  • Buyer guides: what to ask suppliers, red flags, contract terms.
  • Glossaries for technical sectors where newer staff search definitions.

Each guide should link to the relevant service or comparison page with natural anchor text. That is how informational content passes value and readers towards a conversation. Our content marketing guide goes deeper on planning this.

Technical foundations still matter

B2B sites are often built on ageing CMS platforms, carry years of thin news posts, or hide useful content in PDFs. A crawl usually turns up duplicate pages, slow templates and orphaned service pages. Work through our technical SEO audit checklist before you invest heavily in content, and if a rebuild is planned, protect existing rankings with the steps in our redesign without losing rankings guide.

Authority: links and mentions for B2B

Links still matter, and B2B companies have good sources available: trade associations, industry publications, event and conference pages, supplier and partner directories, and original research that journalists can cite. Digital PR built around genuine sector data can earn coverage in trade press as well as national titles. Our guide to digital PR and link building explains the approach.

AI search is also changing how buyers shortlist suppliers. Clear, factual pages that state who you serve, where and how are easier for AI tools to summarise and cite. Our explainer on generative engine optimisation covers this.

Writing for several decision makers

A finance director, an operations manager and an IT lead will read the same service page looking for different things. The finance director wants cost, contract length and risk. The operations manager wants to know how the service fits day-to-day work. The IT lead wants security, integrations and data handling. Good B2B pages answer all three, often in clearly labelled sections, so each reader can find their part quickly and forward the page to colleagues.

Plain English helps here. Jargon that impresses insiders can confuse the people who sign off budgets, and it makes pages harder for search engines and AI tools to summarise accurately. State who you work with, where in the UK you operate, what the service includes and what it does not. Clear, factual statements like these are also more likely to be quoted when buyers ask AI assistants for supplier shortlists.

Proof without invented numbers

B2B buyers want evidence, but it must be real. Use accreditations you actually hold, named case studies only with client permission, and specific descriptions of your process. Vague or inflated claims damage trust with procurement teams, who often check them.

Converting and measuring B2B search traffic

A visitor from search may not be ready to book a call. Offer more than one next step: a short enquiry form, a callback request, a downloadable guide or a pricing breakdown. Keep forms short; you can qualify later.

Then connect the dots. Track form submissions as conversions in GA4, pass the source into your CRM, and review which pages and queries produce enquiries that turn into proposals and deals. Without that link, B2B SEO is judged on traffic, which is the least useful measure. As a rule of thumb, expect three to six months before results start to show, and longer in competitive sectors.

B2B SEO checklist

  • List every service and create one focused page for each.
  • Add industry or use-case pages only where you have real experience.
  • Collect buyer language from sales calls, proposals and Search Console.
  • Publish fair comparison and alternative pages.
  • Explain pricing factors, even if you quote individually.
  • Build research guides that link to service pages.
  • Fix technical issues: duplicates, speed, orphaned pages, PDF-only content.
  • Earn links from trade bodies, publications and original research.
  • Track enquiries in GA4 and pass source data into the CRM.
  • Review pipeline by landing page every quarter.

FAQs

What is B2B SEO in the UK?

It is search engine optimisation for companies that sell to other businesses. It focuses on high-intent, often low-volume searches, content for several decision makers and measuring success by pipeline rather than traffic.

How long does B2B SEO take to work?

Results typically start to show in three to six months, and longer for competitive terms. Because sales cycles are long, the effect on closed revenue may lag further behind rankings.

Are low-volume keywords worth targeting in B2B?

Often yes. A term with a handful of monthly searches can be valuable if the buyers behind it are ready to talk and your deals are large. Judge keywords by intent and deal value.

Should B2B companies publish pricing online?

It depends on your model, but explaining pricing factors and typical ranges helps buyers and often filters out poor-fit enquiries. Even a "what affects the cost" page captures valuable searches.

Is it risky to write competitor comparison pages?

It can be done responsibly. Stick to accurate, verifiable facts, avoid denigrating competitors and keep information up to date. If in doubt, ask for legal advice before publishing.

Should B2B SEO be combined with LinkedIn ads?

They complement each other well. LinkedIn targets by job title and company, while search captures active demand. Our guide to LinkedIn ads for B2B lead generation explains how to run them together.
