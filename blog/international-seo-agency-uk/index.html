International SEO Agency UK: What They Do and How to Choose One for Overseas Growth

What an international SEO agency does for UK businesses, from market research and hreflang to localisation, plus how to choose one and avoid costly mistakes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • International SEO helps the right version of your site reach people in each target country and language, which takes more than translating existing pages.
  • Decisions on markets, URL structure, hreflang and localisation should come before content production, because they are harder and riskier to change later.
  • A good agency will challenge whether a market is ready, and will plan for the local operations, content and upkeep that each new version of the site needs.

An international SEO agency helps a business get found in search in more than one country or language. The work covers choosing markets, researching how people search there, planning the site structure, setting up hreflang and producing properly localised content. For a UK business, the first question is usually not how to translate the site, but which market is worth entering and whether the business can serve customers there once they arrive.

This guide is for UK owners and marketing managers planning overseas growth, or trying to fix an international site that is not performing. If you run an online shop, read it alongside our guide to selling internationally from a UK online store, which covers more of the operational side.

What international SEO covers

  • Market research: whether there is search demand, who competes for it and what buyers expect.
  • Keyword localisation: finding the terms people really use, rather than translating UK keywords.
  • Site structure: deciding how each country or language version should be organised.
  • Technical setup: hreflang, canonical tags, sitemaps, crawling and speed for each version.
  • Content localisation: adapting language, examples, currency, units and calls to action.
  • Authority: earning relevant mentions and links in the markets you are targeting.
  • Measurement: tracking rankings, traffic and leads or sales by market.

Start with the market decision

Rankings have little value if the business cannot deliver. Before committing to SEO, check the basics for each market: delivery, returns, tax, payment methods, legal requirements, customer service hours and the languages your team can support. Take professional advice on tax and legal points.

Then look for evidence of demand. Existing enquiries from abroad, analytics data, search data and competitor activity can all help. Search Console can also show which countries already see your site. These signals do not prove a market will work, but they make a better starting point than simply choosing the biggest country on the map.

Starting with one or two markets is often more practical than launching widely. It gives your team room to learn, and it keeps content and upkeep at a level you can sustain.

Hreflang in plain English

Hreflang is a set of annotations telling search engines which language and country version of a page is meant for which audience. It helps Google show your Irish page to searchers in Ireland, for example, rather than the UK version. It is not a ranking boost, and it does not make weak pages strong.

Common errors include:

  • Pages that do not link back to each other, since hreflang annotations need to be reciprocal.
  • Incorrect language or region codes, such as using "uk" when the correct English UK code is "en-GB".
  • Annotations pointing at redirected, blocked or broken pages.
  • Canonical tags that point every version at one main page, which sends a conflicting signal.
  • No fallback page for visitors outside your listed markets, which an x-default annotation can help with.

These problems are easy to introduce and hard to spot by eye, particularly on large sites. Include hreflang in routine checks, along with the other points in a technical SEO audit.

Translation is not localisation

Translation changes the words. Localisation makes the page feel right for the person reading it. People may use a different term from the direct translation, compare different things or expect different proof before buying. Even between English-speaking markets, spelling, vocabulary and search habits differ.

A localised page may need changes to:

  • Currency, measurements, dates and sizes.
  • Delivery, returns and contact details.
  • Payment options and legal information.
  • Examples and references that make no sense outside the UK.
  • Calls to action and the tone of your copy.

AI translation can speed up a first draft, but have a fluent reviewer check anything customers rely on. If nobody on your team can read the language, agree in advance who will review the copy and who will answer the enquiries it brings in.

Other mistakes that cost time

  • Redirecting visitors automatically by location. This can frustrate people and may stop search engines reaching every version. Offering a clear choice is usually safer.
  • Copying pages across similar markets with no real differences. Near-identical pages can compete or be folded together in search results.
  • Leaving navigation and templates half translated. Mixed-language pages look careless and undermine trust.
  • Launching and moving on. Every version needs updates, so a new price or policy may have to change in several places.
  • Reporting only a global total. Strong results in one market can hide problems in another.

How to choose an agency

Look for a partner who asks about your commercial plans as well as your site. Useful questions include:

  • Which market would you start with, and what evidence supports that?
  • Which site structure do you recommend for our platform, and why?
  • How do you research keywords in languages your team does not speak?
  • Who writes and reviews the localised content?
  • How will you implement and check hreflang?
  • How will you report results by country and language?
  • What ongoing work will each new market need from our team?

Be wary of anyone who promises rankings by a set date, proposes translating every page without research or cannot explain their reasoning simply. Ask to see how similar work has been planned and measured, and judge the answer on its detail.

Measuring international SEO properly

Report each market separately from the start, so that you can see whether a new version of the site is earning its keep. Search Console can filter performance by country and by page, while analytics can show enquiries or sales by market when tracking is set up correctly. Keep your existing UK reporting running as well, because structural changes can affect your home market too.

Useful measures include:

  • Which pages are indexed in each country or language section.
  • Whether searchers are reaching the version intended for them.
  • Impressions and clicks for the local terms you researched.
  • Enquiries or sales by market, including their quality and profitability.
  • The cost of serving each market, such as delivery, returns and customer support.

Early signs often appear in indexing and impressions before they show up in leads or revenue. Agree which signs you will review, and what would make you continue, change approach or leave a market. A decision to stop is a valid result if the market does not justify the work, and it is much cheaper to reach that conclusion after one focused test than after a wide launch.

What to prepare before you speak to an agency

  • Your target markets and why you are considering them.
  • What you can deliver in each market, and any current limits.
  • Access to analytics and Search Console.
  • Any existing international pages and their history.
  • Your platform and who can make technical changes.
  • Who will review translations and handle overseas customers.

FAQs

Do I need a separate website for each country?

Not necessarily. Many businesses run separate country or language sections in subfolders of one domain. The right structure depends on your platform, teams and long-term plans.

Does Google translate my pages for international SEO?

Browser or search translation features are not a substitute for localised pages. If you want to compete in a language, publish content in that language, with keywords and details researched for that market.

Do I need hreflang for only two versions?

It can still help when you have similar pages for different audiences, such as the UK and Ireland. Your site's size and structure will affect how much care the setup needs.

Can I target other English-speaking countries with my UK content?

Sometimes, but local spelling, pricing, delivery and search terms may differ. A dedicated page often serves those visitors better when the market really matters to your business.

How long does international SEO take?

There is no fixed answer. It depends on competition, your site's existing authority and how much content is needed. Be cautious of guaranteed dates, and agree which early signs you will review.

Next steps

Write down the one market you would enter first, then list what your business would need to serve a customer there. If the list looks realistic, you are ready to look at search demand.
