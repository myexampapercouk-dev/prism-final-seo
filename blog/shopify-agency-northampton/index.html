Why Northampton brands choose Shopify

Northampton is famous for footwear and leather, and that heritage still shapes the local economy alongside distribution and logistics hubs near the M1, food and drink producers, financial services and a growing number of independent product brands. Many of these businesses sell directly to customers across the UK, and some ship further afield.

Shopify suits many of them because it is hosted, secure and relatively simple to run. Your team can add products, run discounts, manage stock and fulfil orders without needing a developer for every change. Its app ecosystem covers reviews, subscriptions, bundles and loyalty, and it connects to the major UK carriers and payment options.

Shopify is not right for every business. Stores with complex content needs or unusual product logic sometimes suit WordPress with WooCommerce instead. Our comparison of Shopify and WooCommerce in the UK explains the trade-offs, and we will tell you honestly which fits.

Designing a store that sells

A good Shopify store makes buying easy. We start with your customers and products, then design the store around how people shop.

  • Clear navigation. Collections that match how customers think, such as by product type, use or occasion.
  • Strong product pages. Good photos, honest descriptions, sizing or specification details and visible delivery information.
  • Fast mobile layouts. Most shoppers browse on phones, so we design for small screens first.
  • Trust content. Clear delivery, returns and contact pages, secure checkout and genuine customer reviews.
  • Simple checkout. Familiar UK payment options, including card, digital wallets and buy now, pay later where suitable.

Our guide to e-commerce conversion rate optimisation covers the details that help more visitors become customers once the store is live.

Setting up UK payments, delivery and VAT

We configure the operational side of your store carefully. That includes Shopify Payments or your chosen provider, delivery rates by zone and weight, carriers such as Royal Mail, Evri or DPD where you use them, tax settings, and order notification emails. We check that delivery and returns information on the store matches what your team actually does.

VAT and consumer law can be complex for online sellers. We set up the store according to your instructions and point you to official HMRC guidance or your accountant for advice on your specific position. Our guide to Shopify store setup in the UK walks through the main settings.

Shopify SEO from day one

A new store needs visitors. We build SEO foundations into every Shopify project: clean URLs, sensible collection and product titles, structured data, image alt text, fast page templates and an internal linking structure that helps Google understand your range.

After launch, our SEO service can continue with collection page content, buying guides and technical fixes. Our article on Shopify SEO in the UK explains the platform's strengths and common pitfalls, such as duplicate product URLs and thin collection pages.

How a Shopify project runs

  1. Discovery. We agree goals, products, customers, integrations and budget, and confirm the scope in writing.
  2. Structure. We plan collections, navigation and the key page templates.
  3. Design. We design the homepage, collection and product templates for your review.
  4. Build and content. We set up the theme, import products and configure payments, shipping and tax.
  5. Testing. We test orders, emails, mobile layouts and speed on a preview before launch.
  6. Launch and handover. We go live, set up analytics and train your team on daily tasks.

Choosing apps without slowing the store

Shopify's app store is one of its strengths, but every app adds code, cost and another thing to maintain. We see many stores weighed down by apps that were installed for a test and never removed, or by several apps doing the same job.

  • Start with built-in features. Shopify now handles many tasks natively, such as basic bundles, discounts and some product filtering.
  • Add apps for a clear reason. Reviews, subscriptions and advanced search are common, worthwhile additions when the business needs them.
  • Check speed after each install. We test page speed before and after adding apps, and remove those that slow the store without earning their place.
  • Review monthly costs. App subscriptions add up, so we list them clearly at handover.

Our guide to Core Web Vitals explains why speed matters for both rankings and sales.

Common Shopify problems we fix for growing brands

Many brands come to us with a store that launched quickly and has grown untidily. Collections overlap, product titles are inconsistent, images are oversized and delivery information is hard to find. Some stores have lost search visibility after a theme change, because headings, content or internal links were removed without anyone noticing.

We start with a short review of the store, its analytics and its search performance, then agree a list of fixes in order of impact. Often the best gains come from tidying the catalogue, improving collection pages and making delivery and returns information clearer, rather than a full redesign. When a rebuild is the right answer, we plan it carefully so existing rankings and redirects are protected.
