Local SEO for UK Businesses: Google Business Profile, Reviews and Location Pages

Local SEO UK guide: set up Google Business Profile, build citations, earn reviews and create location pages that help you rank in Maps and local searches.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Your Google Business Profile is the foundation of local SEO: verify it, choose accurate categories and keep it complete and active.
  • Consistent name, address and phone details plus a steady flow of genuine reviews build the trust Google looks for in local results.
  • Location pages work only when each one is genuinely useful to people in that area; thin pages with swapped town names do more harm than good.

Local SEO helps UK businesses appear when people nearby search for what they offer, both in the Google Maps results and in the ordinary listings below them. The core of it is a complete Google Business Profile, consistent business details across the web, a steady flow of genuine reviews and website pages that clearly cover the areas you serve.

This guide explains each part, with a checklist and a table showing which local ranking factors you control and how to improve them.

Local SEO UK guide: how local rankings work

Google broadly describes local results as being based on three things: relevance (how well your listing matches the search), distance (how close you are to the searcher or the place named in the search) and prominence (how well known and trusted your business is). You cannot move your premises, but you can strongly influence relevance and prominence.

That matters for searches such as "plumber near me", "Italian restaurant Leeds" or "accountant in Guildford". The map pack at the top of those results often attracts a large share of clicks and calls, so getting into it can make a real difference to a local business.

Google Business Profile: the foundation

Your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is the listing shown in Maps and the local pack. If you do only one thing for local SEO, make it this.

Setting it up properly

  • Claim and verify the profile so you control it.
  • Use your real business name, as shown on your signage and website. Adding keywords or town names to the name can breach Google's guidelines and risk suspension.
  • Choose the most accurate primary category, then add relevant secondary ones.
  • Set your address or service area. Service-area businesses that visit customers, such as electricians, can hide their address and list the areas they cover.
  • Add opening hours, including bank holiday hours, and keep them up to date.
  • List services or products with short descriptions.
  • Upload real photos of your premises, team at work (without naming individuals if you prefer) and finished jobs.
  • Link to the most relevant page on your website, often a location or service page rather than the homepage.

Keeping it active

A profile that is updated regularly with posts, photos, offers and answers to questions sends useful signals and gives searchers more reasons to choose you. Check it monthly, because the public can suggest edits and details sometimes change without you noticing.

Citations and NAP consistency

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address and phone number (often called NAP). Directories such as Yell, Thomson Local, Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Facebook and trade associations all count. Industry-specific listings matter too: hospitality businesses benefit from accurate details on booking and review sites, while trades may use approved-trader schemes.

Consistency is the key. If your phone number, address format or business name differ across the web, Google is less sure which details are correct. Audit the main listings, fix inconsistencies and close duplicates. You do not need hundreds of directories; a set of accurate, relevant ones is better.

Reviews: quantity, quality and replies

Reviews influence both rankings and whether people click. Build a simple habit of asking every satisfied customer for a Google review, ideally soon after the job or visit, with a direct link to make it easy.

  • Reply to every review, positive or negative, politely and promptly.
  • Never buy reviews, write your own or offer incentives in return for positive reviews. In the UK, fake and incentivised reviews may also raise consumer protection concerns, so take advice if you are unsure what is allowed.
  • Do not gate reviews by only asking customers you expect to be happy; Google's policies discourage this.
  • Handle negative reviews calmly and offer to resolve issues offline.

Location pages on your website

If you serve several towns or have several branches, location pages help you rank in each area. Done badly, they are one of the most common local SEO mistakes: dozens of identical pages with only the town name swapped. Google sees these as low quality.

What a good location page includes

  • The address, phone number and opening hours for that branch, or a clear description of the area you cover
  • Services available in that location, with links to the main service pages
  • Genuinely local detail: parking, nearby landmarks, areas covered, local projects described without naming clients
  • Reviews or FAQs relevant to that area
  • An embedded map and a clear call to action

If you only operate from one place, you probably do not need location pages for every nearby town. Focus on strong service pages that mention your area naturally. Measuring local SEO

Local SEO is easy to measure if you set it up from the start. Your Google Business Profile performance view shows how many people found you through search and Maps, and how many called, requested directions or clicked through to your website. Add tracking parameters to the website link in your profile so those visits are separated in your analytics. In Search Console, filter queries containing your town or "near me" to see how your local visibility changes over time.

Ranking checks are useful too, but remember that local results change depending on where the searcher is standing. A business can appear first for someone on the high street and not at all for someone a few miles away. Grid-based rank tracking tools show this spread more honestly than a single ranking number. Above all, track the outcomes that matter: calls, bookings, enquiry forms and walk-ins, so you can judge whether the effort is paying for itself.

Local SEO checklist

  • Google Business Profile claimed, verified and fully completed
  • Accurate primary and secondary categories
  • Opening hours, including bank holidays, up to date
  • Name, address and phone identical on your website and key directories
  • LocalBusiness structured data on your website
  • A routine for requesting reviews and replying to all of them
  • Service pages that mention your area naturally
  • Location pages only where each one is genuinely useful
  • Local links from organisations you actually work with
  • Calls and direction requests tracked so you can measure results

FAQs

What is covered in a local SEO UK guide?

The essentials are Google Business Profile, consistent citations, reviews, location and service pages, local links and technical health. Together these influence whether you appear in Maps and local results.

How long does local SEO take?

Profile fixes can help within weeks, but stronger local rankings typically build over 3 to 6 months, depending on competition and how established your business is.

Can I rank in a town where I have no office?

It is harder, because distance matters in the map pack. Service-area businesses can list areas they cover, and good location pages can help in ordinary results.

Should I add my town to my business name?

No, unless it is genuinely part of your registered trading name. Adding keywords to the name can breach Google's guidelines and risk your profile being suspended.

How many reviews do I need?

There is no fixed number. Aim for a steady flow of recent, genuine reviews and compare yourself with the businesses currently appearing in the map pack.

Do directory listings still matter?

Accurate listings on major and industry directories still help confirm your details. Focus on consistency and relevance rather than submitting to hundreds of low-quality sites.
