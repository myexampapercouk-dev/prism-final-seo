Microsoft Ads Management in the UK: When It Is Worth Adding and What Good Management Covers

When Microsoft Ads is worth adding for a UK business, how it differs from Google Ads, what good management covers and how to test it without wasting budget.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 7 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Microsoft Ads can add extra search demand alongside Google Ads, but its value depends on whether your audience, product and tracking suit a separate test.
  • An imported Google Ads campaign is a useful starting point rather than a finished account, because settings, audiences, search terms and bids all need checking.
  • Judge the channel on qualified enquiries or profitable sales, measured separately, rather than on cheaper clicks alone.

Microsoft Ads management means planning, building and improving paid campaigns across the Microsoft Advertising network, which includes Bing search and partner placements. For many UK businesses it works best as an addition to Google Ads rather than a replacement. It is usually worth testing when your Google campaigns already convert, your tracking is reliable and you want more of the same kind of demand. It is rarely the right first fix for a weak offer or a poor landing page.

This guide is for UK business owners and marketing managers deciding whether to add the channel, and what to expect from whoever runs it. For the main search channel, see our guide to choosing a Google Ads agency in the UK.

What Microsoft Ads is

Microsoft Advertising is the platform used to run ads on Microsoft's search engine and on sites and services in its wider network. The basic idea will feel familiar if you have used Google Ads. You choose keywords or other signals, write ads, set budgets and bids, then pay according to the campaign settings and the results of the auction.

The audience is the main reason to consider it. Some people search through Bing because of the browser or device they use, or because it is the default at work. Those searches may never reach your Google campaigns, so the channel can give you another chance to appear when someone is looking for what you sell. Available formats and placements change over time, so check the current options in the platform before you plan around any one of them.

How it differs from Google Ads

AreaWhat to think about

AudienceThe people reached may differ from your Google audience. Compare enquiry quality rather than assuming they behave the same way.
CompetitionAuctions vary by sector. Costs may be lower in one market and similar in another, so check your own results.
TargetingAudience options differ, including LinkedIn profile targeting on eligible campaigns, which may interest B2B advertisers.
Campaign setupCampaigns can be imported from Google Ads, but not every setting carries across in the way you might expect.

When it is worth testing

A test makes most sense when several of these are true:

  • Your Google Ads campaigns already produce leads or sales you are happy with.
  • Google is close to using up the search demand you can profitably reach, so more budget there brings weaker returns.
  • You sell to office-based or business buyers, where work devices and default browsers may play a larger part.
  • Your conversion tracking is accurate and each lead source can be seen separately.
  • You can spare a modest test budget without taking money from campaigns that are working.

When to wait

Hold off if your site does not convert, your Google account has clear unresolved problems or you cannot tell which ads produce real enquiries. Adding a channel spreads attention. A PPC audit may find more value in your current account first. Very small local campaigns can also struggle, because there may be too few searches to learn anything useful in a reasonable period.

Why importing from Google Ads is only the start

The import tool saves time and keeps the structure consistent. It can bring over campaigns, keywords, ads and other assets. That makes it a sensible first step for many accounts, although the result still needs a careful review.

Check these points before you let an imported campaign spend:

  • Locations: confirm the campaign targets only the UK areas you serve, and review the location options rather than trusting the import.
  • Network settings: decide whether partner and audience placements belong in the test, or whether search should come first.
  • Budgets and bids: copied figures may not suit a smaller auction, so set them for this test.
  • Bid strategy: automated bidding needs conversion data. A new account may have little to learn from at first.
  • Tracking: set up conversion tracking for Microsoft Ads and test each goal. Google Ads tags do not do this job.
  • Negative keywords: carry over your exclusions, then build on them using this channel's own search terms.
  • Ad assets: check that links, offers, phone numbers and approved wording are all current.

Scheduled imports need particular care. They can be handy while accounts are aligned, but they may overwrite changes made for this channel. Decide early which account leads and which settings stay separate.

What good management covers

Managing the channel well is more than launching a copy of your campaigns. Ask whether the following work is included:

  • A clear goal for the test, based on your commercial numbers rather than clicks alone.
  • Account setup, billing access and ownership in your organisation's name.
  • Conversion tracking that records real actions, such as qualified forms, calls or purchases.
  • Regular search term reviews, so irrelevant traffic can be excluded.
  • Ad testing and landing page feedback, not just bid changes.
  • Budget and bid changes based on enough data, with a clear reason recorded.
  • Reporting that shows Microsoft results separately from Google, including what changed and what happens next.

Ask too who will do the work day to day and how often the account is reviewed. Small accounts do not need constant changes, but they do need someone paying attention.

How to run a sensible test

Start with your best demand

Choose campaigns where the intent is clear and your Google results are already understood, such as high-intent service terms or best-selling product groups. Leave broad awareness campaigns until you know the channel can work for you.

Agree the measure first

Decide what success means before spending. For lead generation, that may be the cost of a qualified enquiry. For ecommerce, it may be profit after product and delivery costs. Agree how long you will run the test too, allowing for your sales cycle and the volume available.

Read the results carefully

Do not judge the channel only on cheaper clicks. A low cost per click means little if those visitors never become customers. Ask your sales team to mark lead quality where possible, and compare results by campaign rather than looking only at the account total. If results look promising, increase budget in steps and keep watching quality.

Common mistakes

  • Importing everything at once, then leaving it unmanaged.
  • Counting page views or button clicks as conversions, which can mislead both reports and automated bidding.
  • Letting partner placements spend without checking what they deliver.
  • Comparing results with Google without allowing for lower data volumes.
  • Ending a test too early, or continuing a poor one because the clicks look cheap.
  • Giving a provider sole control of the account, which complicates any later handover.

Questions to ask a provider

  • Would you recommend this channel for our business, and why or why not?
  • What would you check before importing our campaigns?
  • How will tracking be set up and tested?
  • Which settings will differ from our Google account?
  • Who owns the account and data if we part ways?
  • What result would make you advise stopping the test?

A useful answer can include "not yet". A provider should be willing to say your budget is better spent elsewhere. Our guide to PPC management costs can help you compare proposals.

FAQs

Is Microsoft Ads the same as Bing Ads?

Bing Ads was the earlier name for the platform. Microsoft Advertising is the current name, and people still use both terms in everyday conversation.

Should Microsoft Ads replace Google Ads?

Usually not. Most UK businesses will find more search demand on Google, so Microsoft Ads tends to work best as an extra channel, tested once the core campaigns are in reasonable shape.

Are clicks always cheaper on Microsoft Ads?

No. Costs depend on the sector, the competition and your campaign quality. Test with your own keywords and judge the outcome by the cost of a qualified lead or a profitable sale.

Can I copy my Google Ads campaigns across?

Yes, the platform offers an import option. Review the settings, tracking, locations, budgets and ads afterwards, because an imported campaign still needs managing in its own right.

Is it useful for B2B?

It can be, particularly where buyers search on work devices. Eligible campaigns may also use LinkedIn profile targeting, but check the current options and whether your volumes can support them.

Next steps

Look at your current Google campaigns and pick the one or two with the clearest commercial value. Those are your likely test candidates.
