Web Design Dubai: How to Plan, Brief and Budget a Website for UAE Customers

Web design for Dubai businesses: how to plan a fast, bilingual, mobile-first website, what to ask an agency, typical timelines and what our builds cost.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Dubai business website should be planned around mobile visitors, English and Arabic content, fast loading and obvious ways to call, message or book.
  • Agree ownership, hosting, approvals and data handling before design starts, and check current official UAE guidance on data protection and advertising.
  • Our small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and typically take three to five weeks; quotes in other currencies are available on request.

Web design in Dubai is about building a site that works for a mixed, mobile-first audience: residents, visitors and businesses who may read in English, Arabic or both, and who expect a page to load quickly and make contact easy. The right website explains your offer in a few seconds, looks credible next to well-funded competitors and gives your team a simple way to keep content up to date.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio. We design and build websites remotely for businesses in Dubai and across the UAE, and we have no office or staff in the Emirates. We work through video calls, shared design files and staged sign-offs, which suits many Dubai teams who already work with partners in several time zones. This guide covers how to plan a site, what to ask any agency and what our website design and development service costs.

What Dubai customers expect from a business website

Dubai is a competitive market with a high bar for presentation. Visitors compare you with polished regional and international brands, often on a phone, and often in a hurry. A few expectations come up for almost every sector.

  • Mobile first, not mobile later. Most enquiries will start on a phone, so layouts, buttons and forms should be designed for small screens before they are stretched to desktop. Our guide to mobile website design explains the principles we follow.
  • English and Arabic where it matters. Many Dubai businesses lead with English, but Arabic pages can widen your reach and build trust with local customers and government-linked buyers. Decide early whether you need full bilingual parity or a smaller set of Arabic pages.
  • Fast ways to make contact. Click-to-call, a messaging button, a short enquiry form and a booking link should be easy to find on every key page.
  • Clear trust signals. Licence details where relevant, real photography, clear policies and straightforward service descriptions all help. Our article on website trust signals covers what to include.
  • Speed. Large videos and heavy scripts slow pages down and cost you enquiries, even on good connections.

Planning your Dubai website before design starts

The decisions made before any design work have the biggest effect on cost, timeline and results. We usually start with a short discovery stage, much like the process in our website discovery workshop guide. It answers a handful of practical questions.

  1. Who is the site for? Residents, tourists, trade buyers, investors or job seekers each need different content and calls to action.
  2. What should visitors do? Call, message, book, request a quote or buy online. Pick one primary action per page.
  3. Which languages, and in what order? English only, English first with Arabic pages, or full bilingual parity.
  4. Which pages are essential at launch? A focused launch with strong service pages usually beats a large site full of thin content.
  5. Who writes and approves content? Content delays are the most common reason projects overrun, so agree owners and deadlines early.

If you are still gathering copy and photos, our guide on preparing website content before the build gives a simple checklist.

Bilingual design: English and Arabic on one site

Arabic reads right to left, so a bilingual site is more than a translated copy of the English version. Navigation, icons, forms, tables and image placement all need to mirror correctly. Fonts need proper Arabic support, and line lengths and spacing behave differently. We plan the layout so both directions work from the start rather than adding Arabic as an afterthought.

Translation should be done or reviewed by a fluent speaker who understands your sector. Machine translation can be a starting point, but it often misses tone and terminology. Your team, or a translator you trust, should sign off every Arabic page. Search engines also need clear signals about which language each page is in, so the technical setup matters as much as the words. Our multilingual website design guide goes into more detail on structure and language switching.

Choosing a platform

Most Dubai service businesses are well served by a content-managed site that the team can edit without a developer. Online shops usually suit Shopify or WooCommerce, depending on product range, payment providers and how much flexibility you need. We recommend a platform after discovery, based on what you sell and who will maintain the site, not on habit.

Regulation, data and hosting: what to check

The UAE has its own data protection framework, and some free zones have separate rules. Advertising in certain sectors, such as health, finance and real estate, may also be subject to specific requirements. We are a design and marketing studio, not a legal adviser, so we keep this general: check current official UAE guidance, and the rules of your free zone if relevant, before launch. Your legal or compliance team should approve privacy notices, cookie wording and any regulated claims.

On hosting, we will discuss where your site and any form data are stored and help you choose a reputable provider. If your sector has data residency requirements, tell us at the start so we can plan around them.

Web design Dubai: costs and timelines

We price in pounds sterling, and we can quote in other currencies on request. These are our standard ranges, which you can also see on our pricing page:

  • Small business website: £1,000 to £2,000, typically three to five weeks.
  • Ecommerce website: £1,500 to £3,500, typically five to twelve weeks depending on products, integrations and languages.
  • Ongoing SEO or marketing retainers: Launch £350 a month, Growth £750 a month, Scale £1,500 a month.

A full bilingual build, a large product catalogue or custom integrations will sit towards the upper end of a range or need a specific quote. Translation is usually best handled by a specialist, and we will be clear about what is and is not included before you commit.

How remote web design works across time zones

The UK is three or four hours behind Dubai depending on the time of year, which leaves a comfortable overlap for calls in the UAE afternoon. Most of the work happens asynchronously, so you are never waiting on a single person to be online.

  1. Discovery call and brief. We agree goals, pages, languages and the primary actions.
  2. Structure and wireframes. We map pages and layouts before visual design, so content and navigation are right first.
  3. Design. You review designs in shared files, comment directly and approve at each stage.
  4. Build and content. We build on a staging site you can test on your own phones and devices.
  5. Launch and handover. We set up analytics, check redirects if replacing an old site and train your team to edit content.

You own your domain, hosting account and content. Our guide to website ownership and handover explains what you should receive at the end of any project.

Questions to ask any Dubai web design agency

Whether you choose us, a local studio or another overseas team, these questions help you compare like with like.

  • Who will actually design and build the site, and where are they based?
  • How do you handle Arabic, right-to-left layouts and translation sign-off?
  • What is included in the price, and what counts as an extra?
  • Who owns the domain, hosting, design files and content?
  • How will the site be built for search, and what happens to existing rankings if we are replacing a site?
  • What support is available after launch, and what does it cost?

If you are planning to grow organic traffic after launch, our SEO and generative engine optimisation service can be added once the site is live, so the structure you launch with is ready for search from day one.

FAQs

How much does web design in Dubai cost with Prism Works Studio?

Our small business websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites cost £1,500 to £3,500. We price in pounds sterling and can quote in other currencies on request. Bilingual builds and custom features may need a specific quote.

Do you have an office in Dubai?

No. We are a UK-based studio working remotely with clients in Dubai and the wider UAE. We run projects through video calls, shared design files and staging sites, with clear sign-off points at each stage.

Can you build an English and Arabic website?

Yes. We plan bilingual sites with right-to-left layouts, Arabic-ready fonts and clear language switching. Translation should be done or reviewed by a fluent speaker, and your team approves all Arabic content before launch.

How long does web design in Dubai take?

A small business website typically takes three to five weeks and an ecommerce site five to twelve weeks. Content readiness and approval speed are usually the biggest factors in the final timeline.

Which platform is best for a Dubai business website?

It depends on what you sell and who maintains the site. Service businesses often suit WordPress, while online shops often suit Shopify or WooCommerce. We recommend a platform after discovery rather than by default.

Do I need to follow UAE data protection rules on my website?

Most websites that collect personal data need a clear privacy notice and sensible data handling. The UAE and some free zones have their own rules, so check current official guidance and ask your legal adviser to approve your privacy wording.
