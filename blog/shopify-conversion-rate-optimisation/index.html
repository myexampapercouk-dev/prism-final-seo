Shopify Conversion Rate Optimisation: How to Turn More Visitors Into Customers

Shopify conversion rate optimisation guide for UK stores: product pages, navigation, cart and checkout, trust, speed and testing to grow sales.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 31 October 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify CRO starts with data: find where shoppers drop off before changing anything.
  • Clear product pages, honest delivery information and a smooth cart usually matter more than clever tricks.
  • Test meaningful changes and measure revenue, not just clicks.

Shopify conversion rate optimisation is the process of making it easier for visitors to your store to find what they want and buy it. Rather than spending more on traffic, CRO improves what happens once people arrive: clearer product pages, simpler navigation, fewer surprises at checkout and more reasons to trust you. Small improvements across the journey can add up.

This guide is for UK Shopify store owners and ecommerce teams. For platform-neutral advice, see our ecommerce CRO guide.

Start with your data

Before changing anything, find out where shoppers drop off. Shopify's analytics show sessions, add-to-cart rate, checkout reached and conversion rate. Pair these with your analytics tool to look at behaviour by device, traffic source and landing page. Session recordings and heatmaps can reveal confusion, while customer service emails and reviews often tell you exactly what puts people off.

Look at mobile separately. Most shoppers browse on phones, and problems there can be hidden in blended averages.

Improve navigation and search

  • Use menu labels that match how customers describe products.
  • Keep collections focused and add useful filters, such as size, colour or price.
  • Make search prominent and check it handles common misspellings and synonyms.
  • Review searches that return no results and fix gaps.

Make product pages do the selling

The product page is where most buying decisions happen. Strong pages typically include:

  • High-quality images and, where useful, video showing the product in use.
  • Descriptions that answer real questions about fit, materials, dimensions or compatibility.
  • Clear pricing, variant selection and stock information.
  • Delivery costs, times and returns information near the add-to-cart button.
  • Genuine customer reviews.

Avoid fake urgency, such as countdown timers that reset, or invented stock warnings. They can damage trust and may breach consumer protection rules.

Reduce friction in the cart and checkout

Unexpected costs are a common reason for abandoned baskets, so show delivery costs early. Keep the cart simple, make discount code fields unobtrusive and offer the payment methods your customers expect, including digital wallets. Shopify's checkout is already well optimised, so focus on what leads up to it. Abandoned checkout emails can recover some lost sales; our ecommerce email marketing guide covers this.

Build trust

New visitors need reasons to believe you are legitimate. Show clear contact details, an about page, easy-to-find returns and delivery policies and secure payment options. Display real reviews honestly, including less positive ones.

Do not forget speed

Slow pages lose shoppers before they see your products. Apps and scripts added for conversion can ironically slow the store down. Our website speed optimisation guide explains how to balance features and performance.

Test changes properly

Where you have enough traffic, A/B test meaningful changes such as product page layout, delivery messaging or navigation, using a testing tool that works with Shopify. With lower traffic, make careful changes one at a time and compare results over comparable periods. Measure revenue per visitor and conversion rate, not just clicks.

Common mistakes

  • Copying another store's design without understanding your own customers.
  • Installing many conversion apps that slow the site.
  • Hiding delivery costs until checkout.
  • Calling tests too early.
  • Using pop-ups that block mobile shoppers immediately.

Homepage and landing pages

Your homepage should quickly tell visitors what you sell, who it is for and why they should buy from you. Lead with your main categories or best sellers rather than a slow rotating slider. Paid traffic usually lands deeper in the store, so make sure the landing page matches the advert: if an ad promotes a specific product or offer, send people straight to it.

Collection page improvements

Collection pages are where shoppers compare options. Help them by showing the information they use to decide, such as price, key variants, ratings and delivery promises, directly in the product grid. Sensible default sorting, clear filter labels and the ability to see more products without endless page loads all reduce effort. Check that out-of-stock products are handled sensibly rather than cluttering the top of the page.

Mobile-specific fixes

Mobile shoppers have less screen space and less patience. Make sure the add-to-cart button is easy to reach, images can be swiped and zoomed, and key details are not hidden behind several taps. Sticky add-to-cart bars can help on long product pages. Test forms and pop-ups on real phones, not only in desktop previews.

Average order value

CRO is not only about conversion rate. Increasing average order value can grow revenue too. Options include relevant product recommendations, bundles, free delivery thresholds and well-placed cross-sells in the cart. Keep recommendations genuinely useful; irrelevant upsells add clutter and can reduce trust.

Post-purchase and repeat customers

Returning customers often convert more readily than new visitors. A smooth post-purchase experience, with clear order updates, easy returns and helpful follow-up emails, encourages repeat orders. Make it easy for returning customers to reorder and find what they bought before.

Building a CRO routine

Treat CRO as an ongoing habit rather than a one-off project. Keep a list of ideas, prioritise them by likely impact and effort, make changes or run tests, and record what you learn. Over time this builds a clear picture of what your customers respond to, which is more valuable than any generic checklist.

Use customer feedback

Numbers tell you where people drop off; customers tell you why. Short post-purchase surveys, questions to customer service and product reviews often reveal missing information, confusing sizing or worries about delivery. Add the answers to product pages and FAQs. If several people ask the same question before buying, that question deserves a prominent answer on the page.

Prioritising what to fix

Most stores have more ideas than time. A simple way to prioritise is to consider three things for each idea: how many visitors it affects, how much difference it is likely to make and how much effort it takes. Fixes on high-traffic pages, such as top product pages and the cart, usually come first. Broken features, missing information and slow pages should be fixed before experimenting with design tweaks.

Shopify features that help

Shopify includes several features that support conversion without extra apps, such as accelerated checkout buttons, saved customer details for returning shoppers, product recommendations in many themes and abandoned checkout emails. Check what your theme and plan already offer before adding new tools.

Keeping changes honest

Good CRO makes buying easier, not more pressured. Accurate stock messages, real reviews and clear prices build lasting trust and repeat custom. Tactics that mislead shoppers might lift sales briefly but tend to cost more in returns, complaints and reputation.

FAQs

What is a good conversion rate for a Shopify store?

It varies widely by sector, price point, traffic source and device. Your own trend over time is the most useful benchmark.

Where should I start with Shopify CRO?

Start with your analytics to find the biggest drop-off point, often product pages or the cart, and fix clear problems first.

Do I need apps for CRO?

Not necessarily. Many improvements come from better content, layout and information. Add apps only where they solve a clear problem.

Can I A/B test on Shopify?

Yes, using compatible testing tools, though you need enough traffic for reliable results.

Does CRO help SEO?

Indirectly. Clearer pages and faster load times help shoppers and can support search performance, but CRO and SEO are different disciplines.

How long should an A/B test run?

Long enough to reach a reliable result and cover normal weekly patterns, usually at least a couple of weeks. Avoid stopping a test as soon as one version looks ahead.

Do free delivery thresholds help conversion?

They can encourage larger orders and reduce surprise costs, but they must suit your margins. Show the threshold clearly and test its effect.

Should I add live chat to my Shopify store?

It can help with pre-sale questions, especially for complex or expensive products, but only if someone can respond promptly. Check its impact on speed too.

Are reviews important for conversion?

Genuine reviews help shoppers feel confident. Display them honestly, ask customers for feedback after purchase and never write or filter reviews misleadingly.

Is CRO worth it for a small Shopify store?

Yes. Even without enough traffic for formal testing, fixing clear problems such as missing delivery information or confusing navigation can help shoppers buy.
