SEO Agency for Lisbon Businesses: Remote SEO from a UK Studio

Looking for an SEO agency in Lisbon? How we handle Portuguese and English search, technical SEO, content, local listings and AI answers, with fees in pounds.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • SEO for a Lisbon business means separate Portuguese and English keyword research, a clean technical base, useful content and accurate local listings.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based with no office in Portugal; we work remotely on the same clock as Lisbon, write in English and work with your team or a translator on Portuguese copy.
  • SEO retainers are Launch £350, Growth £750 or Scale £1,500 a month, quoted in pounds with other currencies on request; results typically take three to six months.

An SEO agency for a Lisbon business should help you rank for the searches your customers actually make, in Portuguese for local buyers and in English for visitors, expats and international clients. That means researching each language separately, fixing technical problems, publishing useful pages and making sure your business shows up accurately in local results and AI-generated answers.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that provides SEO remotely to businesses across Europe. We have no office in Lisbon or anywhere in Portugal. Our SEO and generative engine optimisation service runs by video call, shared documents and monthly reporting, and Lisbon shares the same time zone as the UK, which makes scheduling simple. We write in English and work with your team or a translator on Portuguese content.

What makes SEO in Lisbon different

Lisbon has a mixed economy of tourism and hospitality, technology firms and start-ups, property and relocation services, professional services, retail and food businesses. A large international community and a steady flow of visitors mean many Lisbon businesses serve two audiences at once:

  • Portuguese-speaking customers who search in European Portuguese and expect local terms, prices and contact details.
  • English-speaking visitors and residents who search in English, often on mobile and often with travel, relocation or service questions.
  • International buyers for tech, design and business services who compare suppliers across several countries.

The same service can be described in quite different words in each language, and European Portuguese differs from Brazilian Portuguese in spelling and vocabulary. Keyword research has to be done for each language and market rather than translated from one list. Our keyword research guide explains the process we follow.

Portuguese and English search, done properly

Most multilingual SEO problems come from structure rather than words. We set up separate URLs for each language, usually in folders such as /pt/ and /en/, with hreflang tags that tell search engines which version to show. Each page gets its own title, meta description and headings written for the searcher in that language. A language switcher should keep visitors on the matching page rather than sending them back to the home page.

Prism writes English copy in-house. For Portuguese pages we prepare a brief for each page, with target terms, headings and questions to answer, and your team or a native-speaking translator writes the copy. We then check the finished pages for on-page SEO before they go live. Our multilingual website design guide covers the structural side, and our post on international SEO explains how we plan search across several countries.

Technical SEO foundations

Before content can rank, search engines need to crawl and understand the site. Our technical work usually covers:

  • Crawl and index checks in Google Search Console, including pages excluded by mistake.
  • Page speed and Core Web Vitals on mobile, where most local searches happen.
  • Hreflang, canonical tags and redirects for sites with more than one language.
  • Structured data for local businesses, services, products and FAQs where it fits.
  • Internal linking so important pages are easy to reach from the rest of the site.

Our technical SEO audit checklist shows the full set of checks we run at the start of a project. If your website itself needs a rebuild, our post on web design for Lisbon businesses covers that brief.

Local SEO for Lisbon

For restaurants, shops, clinics, property firms and service businesses, local results often matter more than anything else. We help you set up and maintain your Google Business Profile with accurate categories, opening hours, photos and services, and keep your business details consistent across directories. Reviews matter too, so we suggest simple ways to ask happy customers for them and to reply in the language the review was written in.

If you serve several neighbourhoods or nearby towns, we plan location or area pages only where you genuinely serve those places and have something useful to say. Thin pages that swap one place name for another rarely help and can hold back the rest of the site.

Content and AI-generated answers

Search now includes AI-generated summaries as well as the usual list of links. Pages that answer a question clearly in the first few sentences, use descriptive headings and include accurate facts are more likely to be quoted. We write and structure content so it serves both readers and these AI features. Our guide to AI Overviews and SEO explains the approach in more detail.

Good content for a Lisbon business might include service pages for each main offer, guides that answer common customer questions, comparison pages and FAQs. We agree a content plan with you each quarter and adjust it as search data comes in.

Privacy and consent

Portuguese websites fall under the GDPR, and the EU ePrivacy rules mean analytics and marketing cookies normally need consent before they load. That affects how much data your analytics tools collect, so we set up tracking that respects consent and explain what the numbers can and cannot show. The European Accessibility Act has also applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing services, with an exemption for microenterprises providing services; building pages to WCAG AA, the level referenced by EN 301 549, is sensible for any site. Legal wording and decisions should come from your legal adviser.

SEO fees and timelines
