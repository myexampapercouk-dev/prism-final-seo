Web Design for Prague Businesses: Fast Czech and English Websites, Built Remotely

Need web design in Prague? How we design and build fast Czech and English websites for Prague businesses remotely, with costs, timelines and compliance notes.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

  • A good website for a Prague business loads quickly on mobile, works properly in Czech and English, makes the next step obvious and has consent, privacy and accessibility basics in place.
  • Prism Works Studio is UK-based, works remotely and has no office in the Czech Republic; we build in English, with Czech copy from your team or a translator.

Web design for a Prague business should produce a website that loads fast on a phone, reads naturally in Czech and, where needed, English, and makes it easy for visitors to call, book or buy. It should also launch with the compliance basics in place: cookie consent, a clear privacy notice and accessible design.

What Prague businesses need from a website

Prague has a broad mix of businesses: a large tourism and hospitality sector, technology and software firms, shared service centres for international companies, professional services, and a busy community of independent shops, restaurants and trades. The details vary, but the essentials are the same.

  • Speed on mobile. Most visitors arrive on a phone, often on the move. A slow site loses them quickly.
  • Clear structure. Visitors should understand what you offer and where to go next within a few seconds.
  • Proper language versions. Many Prague businesses serve Czech customers, international residents and visitors. Each language needs its own properly translated pages, not an automatic widget.
  • Strong calls to action. Call, book, enquire or buy, made obvious on every key page.
  • Search-friendly foundations. Clean code, sensible headings and structured data so Google and Seznam can understand the site.

Designing for Czech and English visitors

Czech uses diacritics such as č, ř, š and ž, and words can run longer than their English equivalents. Layouts need room for this, and fonts must support Czech characters fully. URLs, page titles and navigation should be planned per language from the start.

For businesses serving tourists or international clients, an English version is often worth building. Hospitality businesses may also want German or other languages. We set up language versions with correct hreflang tags so search engines show the right version to the right visitor. Our multilingual website design guide explains this in more detail.

Seznam remains a familiar search engine and portal in the Czech Republic alongside Google, so we make sure your site is easy for both to crawl, with clean code and a proper sitemap.

Compliance basics for Czech websites

This is general information, not legal advice. Check official guidance or speak to your own adviser.

  • GDPR. Czech businesses are subject to the EU General Data Protection Regulation. Your site needs a clear privacy notice and a lawful basis for collecting personal data through forms, bookings and analytics.
  • Cookie consent. Under EU ePrivacy rules, non-essential cookies, including most analytics and advertising tags, generally need consent before they are set. Rejecting should be as easy as accepting. Our cookie banner design article covers practical options.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 to many consumer-facing products and services, including e-commerce. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt. EN 301 549, which refers to WCAG at level AA, is the usual technical reference. Our website accessibility guide explains what accessible design involves.

In regulated sectors such as healthcare, finance and law, approval of website content rests with your own compliance team or professional body.

How a remote web design project runs

  1. Discovery call. We discuss your business, customers, languages and goals, and review any existing site.
  2. Structure and content plan. We agree pages, navigation and what each page needs to say. Your team or translator prepares Czech copy.
  3. Design. We design key pages for mobile and desktop and refine them with your feedback.
  4. Build. We build the site on WordPress or Shopify, add content and set up analytics and consent.
  5. Testing and launch. We test speed, forms, languages and accessibility basics, then launch and monitor.
  6. Handover. We show your team how to update content and offer ongoing support if you need it.

Sector notes for Prague websites

Hospitality businesses such as hotels, guesthouses, restaurants and tour operators need strong photography, simple booking paths and clear information for international visitors. Direct bookings through your own site can reduce reliance on third-party platforms. Our hospitality sector page covers how we approach this.

Technology and B2B firms need clear explanations of what they do, credible case information they are allowed to share and easy routes to a demo or call. Professional services need trust signals such as clear service descriptions, team qualifications where permitted, and plain contact details.

Local shops and trades need a fast, simple site with opening hours, location, services and an obvious way to get in touch, connected to a well-kept Google Business Profile.

Choosing a web designer for your Prague business

Whether you hire a local Prague studio or a remote one, ask:

  • Will you own the website, domain and content outright?
  • How are language versions handled, and who writes the Czech copy?
  • What is included in the price, and what costs extra?
  • How fast will the site load on mobile, and how is that tested?
  • What support is available after launch?

A local studio may suit you if you want meetings in person and work fully in Czech. A remote UK studio can suit businesses comfortable working in English, especially those that also serve UK or international customers.

Common website problems for Prague businesses

When we review existing sites, the same issues come up again and again. Many were built years ago and are slow on mobile, with large images and heavy page builders. Some have English and Czech content mixed on the same page, or rely on automatic translation widgets that search engines cannot index properly and that read awkwardly to visitors.

Others hide the most important information. Opening hours, prices or booking links sit several clicks deep, and contact forms ask for too much. Cookie banners on older sites often load tracking before the visitor makes a choice, which creates compliance risk and slows the page.

A rebuild is a chance to fix all of this at once: a lighter site, separate language versions, clear calls to action and a consent setup that loads tags only after permission is given.
