Breadcrumb Navigation: When to Use It and How to Get It Right

What breadcrumb navigation is, when UK websites and online shops should use it, how to design it for mobile and accessibility, and how to add structured data.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 1 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Breadcrumbs show visitors where they are and give a quick route back up the site structure.
  • They suit sites with depth, such as shops and large service sites, more than small flat sites.
  • Use clear labels, accessible markup and structured data that matches what visitors see.

Breadcrumb navigation is a short trail of links, usually near the top of a page, showing where the page sits within a website. A typical breadcrumb on an online shop might read Home, then Women, then Coats, then the product name. Each step is a link back up the structure. Breadcrumbs help visitors understand where they are, move to broader sections quickly and recover when they land deep in a site from a search result.

This guide explains the types of breadcrumb, when they are worth using, how to design them well on desktop and mobile, how to make them accessible and how structured data can help search engines understand them.

Why breadcrumbs matter

Many visitors do not arrive on your homepage. They come from Google, social posts or ads directly onto a product, category or article. Without context, they may not know what else your site offers or how to look at similar items. Breadcrumbs answer that in a single line.

  • Orientation. Visitors see where the page sits in your site.
  • Quick movement. One click takes them to a broader category instead of using the back button or the main menu.
  • Internal linking. Breadcrumbs link pages to their parent sections consistently, which can help search engines understand site structure.
  • Search appearance. Google may show breadcrumb-style paths in search results in place of a raw URL, though this is at Google's discretion.

Breadcrumbs support your main navigation; they do not replace it. Our guide to website navigation structure covers the wider picture.

Types of breadcrumb

Location-based breadcrumbs

These show the page's place in the site hierarchy, such as Home, Services, Web Design. They are the most widely used and the most useful for most websites.

Attribute-based breadcrumbs

Used mainly in ecommerce, these reflect filters a shopper has chosen, such as Coats, Wool, Navy. They can help on large catalogues but can become messy if every filter combination creates a new trail.

Path-based breadcrumbs

These show the pages a visitor has clicked through, similar to browser history. They tend to be confusing because the trail changes from visitor to visitor, and the browser back button already does the job. Most sites are better served by location-based breadcrumbs.

When to use breadcrumbs

Breadcrumbs earn their place on sites with depth, where pages sit two or more levels below the homepage. Good candidates include:

  • Online shops with categories, subcategories and products.
  • Service businesses with grouped services, locations or sectors.
  • Blogs and resource libraries organised by topic.
  • Documentation or help centres.

On a small site with a handful of top-level pages, breadcrumbs add little. A trail reading Home, About tells the visitor nothing they could not see in the menu. Leave them off the homepage too, since there is nowhere further up to go.

Designing breadcrumbs well

Placement

Place breadcrumbs near the top of the content area, below the main header and above the page heading. That is where visitors expect them, and it keeps them close to the content they describe.

Visual style

Breadcrumbs should be noticeable but secondary. Use a smaller text size than body copy, a clear separator such as a chevron or slash, and link styling that makes clickable steps obvious. The current page is usually shown as plain text, not a link, since linking to the page you are on is pointless.

Labels

Use the same names as your navigation and page headings so the trail feels consistent. Short labels work best. If a category is called Women's Outerwear in the menu, do not call it Coats and Jackets in the breadcrumb.

Mobile

Long breadcrumb trails can wrap over several lines on a phone and push content down. Options include shortening the trail to show only the immediate parent, such as Back to Coats, allowing the trail to scroll horizontally within its own row, or truncating middle steps. Test on real devices. Our mobile website design guide covers related considerations.

Breadcrumbs on ecommerce sites

Shops often have products that belong to several categories. A pair of boots might sit in Footwear, Sale and New In. Decide on one primary category for each product and use it consistently in the breadcrumb. Some platforms build breadcrumbs from the route a shopper took, which can create different trails for the same product; this is worth checking.

Shopify themes handle breadcrumbs differently. Some include them, others need small theme customisations, and collection-aware product URLs can affect the trail shown. Our guide to Shopify theme customisation explains how changes like this are usually approached. On WooCommerce, breadcrumbs are built in and SEO plugins often provide their own versions with more control.

Breadcrumbs work closely with category pages. If your categories are well organised, the trail will make sense. Our category page design guide covers structuring them.

Accessibility

Breadcrumbs should be usable by everyone, including people using screen readers or keyboards. Good practice includes:

  • Wrapping the breadcrumb in a navigation landmark with a label such as Breadcrumb, so screen reader users can find or skip it.
  • Using an ordered list for the steps, since order matters.
  • Marking the current page appropriately so assistive technology announces it as the current location.
  • Hiding decorative separators from screen readers so they are not read aloud.
  • Ensuring links have enough colour contrast and visible focus states.

Our website accessibility guide covers the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines more broadly.

Breadcrumb structured data

Structured data is code that describes page content to search engines. Schema.org includes a BreadcrumbList type that lists each step in the trail with its name and URL. Google documents how it uses this markup and may use it to display a breadcrumb path in search results.

  • Make sure the structured data matches the breadcrumb visitors can see.
  • Use full, canonical URLs for each step.
  • Avoid conflicting markup from both a theme and a plugin, which can produce duplicate or contradictory data.
  • Test pages with Google's Rich Results Test and check the enhancements reports in Google Search Console.

Many SEO plugins for WordPress and many Shopify themes add breadcrumb structured data automatically. Check what is already in place before adding more. Our product page SEO guide covers other structured data relevant to shops.

Planning your hierarchy first

Breadcrumbs can only be as clear as the structure behind them. If your categories overlap, use inconsistent names or skip levels, the trail will expose that confusion. Before adding breadcrumbs, sketch your site as a simple tree: the homepage at the top, main sections beneath it, then subsections and individual pages. Each page should have one obvious parent.

This exercise often reveals useful improvements. You may find services that belong together, blog topics that need a hub page, or product categories with too few items to justify their own level. Fixing the structure first means the breadcrumb, the main menu and your URLs can all tell the same story.

Hub pages for each level

Every step in a breadcrumb should lead to a real, useful page. If a trail shows Services, there should be a Services page that introduces each service and links to them. Empty or thin hub pages make breadcrumbs feel like dead ends, while well written hub pages give visitors a genuine overview and give search engines clear context for the pages beneath them.

Common breadcrumb mistakes

  • Adding breadcrumbs to a flat site where they add no information.
  • Labels that do not match the menu or page headings.
  • Linking the current page to itself.
  • Different trails for the same product depending on how a shopper arrived.
  • Steps that link to pages which do not exist, such as a category level with no page behind it.
  • Structured data that does not match the visible trail.
  • Trails that take up a large part of the screen on mobile.

FAQs

Do breadcrumbs help SEO?

They can support SEO by creating consistent internal links and helping search engines understand site structure, and Google may show them in results. They are a supporting element rather than a ranking shortcut.

Should breadcrumbs appear on the homepage?

No. The homepage is the top of the structure, so a breadcrumb there has nothing to show.

Should the current page in a breadcrumb be a link?

Usually not. Show the current page as plain text so visitors understand where they are, and mark it as the current location for assistive technology.

Do small websites need breadcrumbs?

Often not. If every page sits one level below the homepage, the main navigation already does the job and breadcrumbs add clutter.

Can I add breadcrumbs in Shopify?

Yes. Some themes include breadcrumbs, and others can be adjusted with theme customisation. Check how your theme chooses the category shown for products that sit in several collections.
