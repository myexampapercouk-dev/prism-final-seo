Web Design for Saudi Arabia Businesses: Planning a Site for Riyadh and Beyond

Web design for Saudi Arabia businesses: how to plan a fast, mobile-first site for Riyadh and Jeddah customers, what to ask an agency, timelines and costs.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 9 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A website for Saudi customers should be planned around mobile use, Arabic-first or bilingual content, clear calls to action and fast loading on phones.
  • Agree content ownership, approvals, hosting and data handling at the start, and check current official guidance on data protection and advertising rules.
  • Our small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and typically take three to five weeks, with quotes in other currencies available on request.

Web design for Saudi Arabia businesses starts with the people you want to reach: mostly mobile users, many of whom prefer Arabic, who expect a site to load quickly, explain your offer clearly and make it easy to call, message or book. A good website for a Riyadh, Jeddah or Dammam business combines that local understanding with solid structure, search-friendly pages and a content plan your team can keep up to date.

Prism Works Studio is a UK-based studio that designs and builds websites remotely for businesses in Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar. We have no office or staff in the Kingdom, and we are open about that. We work through video calls, shared design files and staged sign-offs. This guide explains how to plan a site for the Saudi market, what to ask any agency and what our website design and development work costs.

What Saudi customers expect from a business website

Expectations are shaped by how people browse. Phones dominate, so a site that looks fine on a laptop but feels cramped on a phone will lose enquiries. Beyond that, several themes come up again and again when planning sites for the Saudi market.

  • Arabic as a first-class language. For many consumer-facing businesses, Arabic should be the default or an equal option, not an afterthought. B2B firms dealing with international partners may lead with English but still need Arabic pages.
  • Quick ways to make contact. Visitors often want to call, send a message or book straight from a page. Make those actions obvious on every key page.
  • Trust and clarity. Clear company details, real photography where possible, straightforward service descriptions and transparent policies all help.
  • Respectful, appropriate imagery. Visuals should suit local culture and your audience. Your team will usually be the best judge of what feels right.
  • Speed on mobile data. Heavy video, large images and too many scripts slow pages down. Our guide to mobile website design covers the principles we follow.

Web design for Saudi Arabia businesses: key features

Every site is different, but most business websites we plan for the Saudi market share a common core.

  • A clear homepage that says what you do, who you serve and how to get started within the first screen.
  • Dedicated service pages, one per main service, so each can rank in search and answer specific questions.
  • Location information covering the cities or regions you serve, with accurate contact details.
  • Bilingual navigation where needed, with a visible language switcher and correctly mirrored right-to-left layouts in Arabic.
  • Simple forms that work in both languages and ask only for what you need.
  • Analytics and conversion tracking so you can see which pages and campaigns bring enquiries.
  • Accessible design, with good contrast, readable text sizes and clear labels. These choices help every visitor, including people using screen readers or larger text settings.

If you also plan to advertise or invest in SEO, build that in from the start. Search-friendly page structure, fast loading and clear headings make later marketing far more effective. Our SEO and GEO service can run alongside or after the build.

Speed, hosting and data

Speed matters for both visitors and search rankings. We keep page weight low, compress images, limit third-party scripts and test on mobile connections. Our website speed optimisation guide explains what we check.

Hosting location and data handling deserve attention too. Saudi Arabia has a personal data protection law and related regulations, and some sectors, such as government suppliers, finance and healthcare, may have additional requirements about where and how data is stored. These rules are updated from time to time, so check current official guidance and take local legal advice where needed. We can recommend hosting options, set up consent-aware analytics and keep forms minimal, but compliance decisions rest with your business.

Choosing a web design agency for a Saudi business

Whether you work with a local agency or a remote studio, a few questions will tell you a lot about how a project will go. Our general guide on how to choose a web designer goes into more depth, but these are the essentials:

  1. Can they show how they handle Arabic? Ask about right-to-left layouts, Arabic fonts and who checks Arabic copy.
  2. Is the price clear? You should know what is included, what costs extra and what ongoing fees to expect.
  3. Who owns the site? Your domain, hosting, content and accounts should be in your name. Our guide to website ownership and handover explains what to insist on.
  4. How will they communicate? Agree meeting times, response times and how feedback is collected.
  5. What happens after launch? Ask about support, updates, backups and training for your team.

Working remotely with a UK studio

Saudi Arabia is a few hours ahead of the UK, which leaves a good overlap in the working day for calls. The working week differs slightly, so we agree regular meeting slots that suit both sides and avoid weekends and public holidays in either country. During Ramadan, we adjust schedules and expect approvals to take a little longer.

Most of the work happens through shared files and staged approvals. You see the sitemap, then page designs, then a working staging site, and you sign off each stage before we move on. That structure keeps projects on track even when decision-makers are busy or travelling.

After launch: keeping the site useful

A website keeps earning its keep only if it stays current. Plan who will update services, prices, opening hours and offers, and make sure both language versions change together if you run a bilingual site. Seasonal moments such as Ramadan, Eid and national holidays are good prompts to review banners, hours and contact options.

Technical care matters too. Software updates, backups, security checks and occasional speed reviews keep the site reliable. Some businesses handle this in-house after training, while others prefer an ongoing support arrangement. Either way, agree the approach before launch so nothing falls between the gaps.

Finally, look at the data. Analytics will show which pages bring enquiries and where visitors drop away, which makes it easier to decide what to improve or expand next.

Timeline and cost

A typical business website takes three to five weeks from approved plan to launch. Bilingual sites, larger page counts and integrations add time, and online stores usually take five to twelve weeks. Our guide to website build timelines explains what affects the schedule.

Our small business websites cost £1,000 to £2,000, and ecommerce sites cost £1,500 to £3,500. Prices are in pounds sterling, and we can quote in riyals or another currency on request. Hosting, premium plugins or apps, translation by external specialists and any ad spend are paid separately. You can see the full breakdown, including marketing retainers, on our pricing page.

FAQs

What should web design for Saudi Arabia businesses include?

A mobile-first layout, Arabic or bilingual content where your audience needs it, clear service pages, easy contact options, fast loading, analytics and a structure that supports SEO.

Should my Saudi business website be in Arabic or English?

It depends on your customers. Consumer-facing businesses often lead with Arabic, while B2B firms may need both. Many businesses choose a fully bilingual site.

Can a UK agency design a website for a business in Riyadh?

Yes. Design and development are done online, with video calls and staged approvals. Agree meeting times and how Arabic content will be written and checked.

How long does a website take to build?

A typical business website takes three to five weeks, and online stores take five to twelve weeks. Bilingual content and integrations can extend the timeline.

How much does a business website cost?

Our small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and ecommerce sites £1,500 to £3,500. We quote in pounds, with other currencies available on request.

Do I need to host my website in Saudi Arabia?

Not always, but some sectors and data types may have requirements. Check current official guidance on data protection and take local advice before choosing hosting.
