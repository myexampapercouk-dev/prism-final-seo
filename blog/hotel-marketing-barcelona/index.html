A hotel website that converts

Your website is the one booking channel you fully control, so it needs to work hard.

  • Fast on mobile. Many guests book on their phone, so we compress images and keep scripts lean.
  • Clear room pages. Each room type gets its own page with photos, size, bed type, view and amenities.
  • Visible booking button. A persistent "Book" button linked to your booking engine.
  • Location content. Clear information about the neighbourhood, transport links and what is nearby.
  • Several languages. Barcelona welcomes guests from all over the world, so we build proper language versions with hreflang tags.
  • Reasons to book direct. Perks such as flexible cancellation, breakfast or late checkout, clearly shown.

We usually build hotel sites on WordPress, which handles multilingual content well and connects to most booking engines. Our hospitality marketing page explains the full approach.

SEO for Barcelona hotels

Search is crowded with booking sites and large brands, so independent hotels win by being specific.

  • Neighbourhood keywords. Guests often search by area, such as the Gothic Quarter, Eixample, Gràcia or near the beach. Pages that cover your area properly can rank where generic pages cannot.
  • Guest-intent content. Guides to nearby sights, getting from the airport or planning a weekend help travellers and build relevance.
  • Google Business Profile. Accurate details, good photos and replies to reviews help in map results.
  • Structured data. Hotel schema helps search engines understand your property.
  • Technical health. Fast pages, clean URLs and no duplicate language versions competing with each other.

Our hotel SEO guide covers these tactics step by step, and our page on SEO in Barcelona explains how we approach the wider local search market.

Paid ads for hotels

Ads put you in front of travellers at the right moment. We run:

  • Google Ads. Search campaigns on your hotel name and high-intent phrases, so booking sites do not take your own brand traffic.
  • Meta Ads. Facebook and Instagram campaigns built around strong photos, short videos and seasonal offers.
  • TikTok and YouTube Ads. Short video that shows the experience of staying with you.
  • Remarketing. Reminders for people who looked at rooms but did not book, where consent allows.

We target the countries your guests come from and adjust for seasons and events. Our guide to running Meta Ads in several countries explains how we split budgets and creative by market. Ad spend is paid separately and directly to the platforms. More detail is on our paid media service page.

Planning around Barcelona's seasons and events

Demand in Barcelona shifts through the year. Summer brings beach and city-break travellers, spring and autumn suit culture and food trips, and major trade fairs and conferences can fill the city for days at a time. A good marketing plan follows these patterns.

  • Build content early. Guides to seasonal events and the best times to visit need to be live well before travellers start searching.
  • Shift ad budgets. Spend more when demand and room rates are high, and use quieter periods to promote longer stays or special offers.
  • Target by market. Guests from different countries book at different times, so we adjust campaigns for each source market.
  • Promote what makes you different. A rooftop, a quiet courtyard, a family room or a restaurant can be the reason a guest picks you over a similar hotel nearby.
  • Keep availability honest. Ads and offers should only run when you have rooms to sell, so we link campaigns to your booking calendar where possible.

We also track which channels bring bookings, not just clicks, so budgets move towards what actually fills rooms.

GDPR, cookies and accessibility

Hotels handle a lot of personal data, from bookings to email lists, so it pays to get the basics right. These notes are general; please check official guidance or your own legal adviser.

  • GDPR. Guest data needs a lawful basis, a clear privacy notice and secure handling. Marketing emails generally need consent or a valid existing-customer basis.
  • Cookie consent. ePrivacy rules generally require consent before non-essential cookies, including many ad pixels, are set. We configure tracking to respect the choice.
  • European Accessibility Act. From June 2025, the EAA covers many consumer services, and accommodation booking is among the areas it can apply to. Microenterprises providing services are generally exempt. The harmonised standard EN 301 549 refers to WCAG AA.

We build to WCAG AA as standard so guests using screen readers or keyboards can book easily. Our accessibility guide covers the checks we run. Compliance decisions rest with you and your advisers.
