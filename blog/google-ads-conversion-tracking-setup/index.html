How to Set Up Google Ads Conversion Tracking for a Small Business

Google Ads conversion tracking shows which clicks become enquiries or sales. Learn what to track, how to set it up and how to test it before spending more.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 8 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Decide which actions really matter, such as calls, forms or purchases, before touching any settings.
  • Set up tracking with a Google tag, Tag Manager or a GA4 import, then test every conversion.
  • Respect UK cookie consent rules and keep primary conversions focused so bidding learns from real value.

To set up Google Ads conversion tracking, decide which actions count as a conversion (such as an enquiry form, a phone call or a purchase), create those conversion actions in Google Ads, install the tag on your website or import goals from GA4, and then test that each one records correctly. Without it, you are paying for clicks with no reliable way to tell which keywords and ads bring in business.

This guide is for UK small business owners who run, or plan to run, their own Google Ads. It covers the decisions to make first, the main set-up options, consent rules and testing. Google changes the Ads interface and tagging options regularly, so check the current Google Ads help pages for exact steps.

Why conversion tracking matters

Clicks and impressions only tell you that people saw and clicked your ads. Conversion tracking tells you what happened next. It lets you see cost per enquiry or sale, compare keywords and campaigns fairly, and switch on automated bidding strategies that need conversion data to work well.

It is also the first thing we check in any PPC audit. An account with missing or double-counted conversions can look healthy while wasting money, or look poor while actually doing well.

Decide what to track first

Before opening any settings, write down the actions that genuinely matter to your business. A simple way to think about it:

Business typeMain conversionUseful secondary actions
Local service, such as a trades businessPhone calls and enquiry formsClicks on email links, quote calculator use
Online shopPurchases with order valueAdd to basket, begin checkout
Clinic or salonCompleted bookingsBooking page visits, calls
B2B servicesQualified enquiry formsBrochure downloads, meeting bookings

Keep main, or primary, conversions to the actions that really represent business. Secondary actions are useful for insight but should usually not drive bidding, because Google will optimise for whatever you tell it matters most.

How to set up Google Ads conversion tracking

There are three common routes. Pick one main method per action to avoid counting conversions twice.

Option one: the Google tag directly

  1. In Google Ads, go to the conversions area and create a new conversion action for your website.
  2. Choose the category, such as purchase, submit lead form or contact.
  3. Set the value, counting method and attribution options. For leads, counting one per click is common; for purchases, every conversion is usually right.
  4. Install the Google tag on every page, then add the event snippet or set up the event on the page or action that marks success, such as a thank-you page.

Option two: Google Tag Manager

Tag Manager lets you manage tags without editing site code each time. You add the Tag Manager container once, then create a Google Ads conversion tag with the ID and label from your conversion action, and a trigger such as a thank-you page view or form submission. This is often the tidiest option for sites with several tags.

Option three: import from GA4

If GA4 is already set up well, you can link it to Google Ads and import key events as conversions. This is convenient, but the data depends on GA4 being configured properly. Our GA4 setup guide covers that side.

Tracking phone calls

For many UK service businesses, the phone is the main source of leads. Google Ads generally offers ways to track calls from call assets on your ads and calls from your website using forwarding numbers, as well as clicks on a phone number on mobile. Availability and set-up vary, so check the current help pages. Set a minimum call length so very short calls do not count, and make sure your number is clearly visible, as covered in our guide to website call to action buttons.

Ecommerce tracking

For online shops, track purchases with the actual order value and currency so you can see return on ad spend. Shopify and WooCommerce both offer ways to connect to Google, either through official apps, built-in integrations or plugins, and these often handle purchase tracking for you. Check that you are not also adding a manual tag on top, which can double count. Our Google Merchant Center setup guide and Google Shopping ads guide cover the product feed side.

Cookie consent and UK rules

Under PECR and UK GDPR, non-essential cookies such as advertising tags generally need consent. In practice that means:

  • Use a cookie banner that lets visitors accept or reject advertising cookies, as explained in our cookie banner design guide.
  • Make sure tags respect the choice. Google offers Consent Mode, which adjusts how tags behave based on consent; check the current documentation for what it does.
  • Expect some gaps in reported conversions where people decline cookies. That is normal and better than ignoring the rules.

Check the ICO guidance on cookies for the current position, and update your privacy policy to mention advertising tags.

Testing your set-up

Never assume tracking works because the settings look right. Test each conversion action:

  1. Use Google's tag diagnostics or Tag Assistant to confirm the tag fires on the right page.
  2. Complete a test form submission or purchase yourself and note the time.
  3. Check the conversion action status in Google Ads over the following day or so; reporting is not always instant.
  4. Confirm a form only counts once, even if someone refreshes the thank-you page.
  5. Check the order value for purchases matches the real order, including whether VAT and shipping are included in the way you intend.

Reading the data once it flows

Once conversions are recording, give the account time before making big decisions. A handful of conversions in the first week is not enough to judge a keyword. Look at trends over several weeks, and compare campaigns on cost per conversion rather than cost per click alone. A keyword with expensive clicks can still be the cheapest source of enquiries if those clicks convert well.

Add the conversions column to your keyword, ad and search terms views so you can see which searches lead to business. Combine this with your negative keyword work: searches that spend steadily and never convert are good candidates to block or move into their own ad group for closer watching.

Keep a simple change log too. Note the date you added or changed tracking, switched bidding strategy or relaunched a landing page. When numbers move, the log helps you tell a real change in demand from a tracking change. If you later switch to automated bidding, such as maximising conversions, the quality of this data becomes even more important, because Google learns from exactly what you tell it is valuable.

Finally, compare what Google Ads reports with what you see in your own records, such as your enquiries inbox or order system. The figures will rarely match perfectly because of consent, attribution and timing, but large gaps are a sign something needs checking.

Common mistakes

  • Tracking page views as conversions, such as the contact page loading, rather than a completed form.
  • Double counting by tracking the same action through both a direct tag and a GA4 import.
  • Too many primary conversions, which confuses automated bidding.
  • Forgetting tracking after a redesign, when thank-you page addresses change. See our guide to website staging and testing.
  • Ignoring offline outcomes. If many enquiries do not become customers, consider whether importing offline conversions makes sense later.

FAQs

How do I set up Google Ads conversion tracking?

Create a conversion action in Google Ads for each important action, then install the Google tag and event, use Google Tag Manager, or import key events from GA4. Test each one before relying on the data. Check Google's current help pages for exact steps.

What counts as a conversion?

Any action that is valuable to your business, such as a purchase, enquiry form, booking or qualifying phone call. Focus on actions that represent real business rather than page views.

Should I use GA4 or the Google Ads tag?

Either can work. The native Google Ads tag is often more direct for bidding, while GA4 imports are convenient if GA4 is already well set up. Avoid tracking the same action both ways.

Why are my conversions not showing?

Common causes include a tag not firing, a changed thank-you page address, consent settings blocking tags, or simply reporting delay. Test with Tag Assistant and check the conversion status in your account.

Do I need cookie consent for conversion tracking in the UK?

Advertising cookies are generally non-essential, so consent is usually needed under PECR. Check the ICO guidance and use a banner that lets visitors accept or reject them.

Can I track phone calls from Google Ads?

Yes, Google Ads generally offers call tracking from ads, from your website and from mobile number clicks. Options vary, so check the current help pages.
