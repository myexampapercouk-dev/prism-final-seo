Conversion Rate Optimisation Agency UK: What a CRO Agency Does and How to Choose One

A conversion rate optimisation agency finds why visitors do not buy or enquire, then fixes and tests pages. What UK CRO agencies do, costs and how to choose.

By the Prism Works Studio team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good CRO agency starts with research into where and why visitors drop out, not with a list of button colours to test.
  • A/B testing needs enough traffic; smaller UK sites often get more from evidence-led fixes measured before and after.
  • Judge agencies on their research process, how they handle tracking and consent, and how they report on revenue or leads.

A conversion rate optimisation agency helps you turn more of your existing website visitors into customers or enquiries. It does this by researching where and why people drop out, fixing the problems it finds and, where traffic allows, testing changes to prove what works.

CRO is often the cheapest way to grow, because it improves the return on every channel at once. This guide explains what a CRO agency does for UK businesses, the process to expect, what it costs and how to choose one. If you run an online store, our ecommerce CRO guide covers checkout and product page specifics in more depth.

What a conversion rate optimisation agency UK businesses hire actually does

CRO is a process rather than a single task. Most agency work falls into four stages.

  1. Measurement. Making sure analytics and conversion tracking are accurate, so changes can be judged fairly.
  2. Research. Finding where visitors drop out and why, using data, recordings, surveys and usability reviews.
  3. Prioritisation. Ranking ideas by likely impact, confidence and effort.
  4. Implementation and testing. Making changes, A/B testing where traffic allows, and measuring results.

The best agencies spend more time on research than on testing. A test built on a guess is a slow way to learn. A test built on clear evidence is far more likely to win.

Common conversion problems an agency looks for

Many CRO wins come from removing friction rather than clever ideas. Typical issues include:

  • Unclear value. Visitors cannot tell quickly what you offer, who it is for and why they should choose you.
  • Slow pages. Especially on mobile. Our site speed guide covers the main fixes.
  • Hidden costs. Delivery charges, VAT treatment or fees that appear late.
  • Long or confusing forms. Asking for information you do not need yet.
  • Weak trust signals. Missing contact details, unclear returns or cancellation policies, or no information about who is behind the business.
  • Mismatched landing pages. Ads that promise one thing and land on a page about something else.
  • Mobile layout problems. Buttons hidden below the fold, pop-ups that are hard to close, menus that are hard to use.

For lead generation sites, landing pages are often the biggest lever. Our landing page design guide sets out what a strong page includes.

A/B testing: when it makes sense

A/B testing shows two versions of a page to different visitors and compares results. It is powerful, but it needs enough conversions to give a reliable answer. On a site with modest traffic, a test might need months to reach a trustworthy result, or never reach one.

A good CRO agency will be honest about this. For smaller UK sites, the better approach is often to make research-led improvements, record the date of each change and compare performance before and after, while watching for seasonality and promotions. Larger sites can run structured testing programmes alongside this.

Be cautious of agencies that report every test as a large win, stop tests early or test many variations on low traffic. These habits produce results that do not hold up.

What a typical CRO engagement looks like

Most CRO work follows a predictable rhythm, although the detail depends on the site and the agency.

  • Weeks one to four: audit and research. Tracking checks, funnel analysis, a usability review of key pages on mobile and desktop, and a first round of recordings or surveys where consent allows. The output should be a prioritised list of problems with the evidence behind each one.
  • Months two and three: fixes. Clear problems, such as broken form fields, slow templates or missing delivery information, are fixed rather than tested. Each change is logged with its date.
  • Ongoing: testing and iteration. Where traffic supports it, larger ideas such as new page layouts or different offers are A/B tested. Where it does not, changes are measured before and after.

You should also expect the agency to ask for input from your team. Sales staff, customer service and anyone who answers the phone often know exactly which questions and objections stop people buying. That knowledge is some of the most valuable CRO research available, and it costs nothing to collect.

Finally, agree how decisions will be made. Someone on your side needs authority to approve changes quickly, or the programme will stall while ideas wait for sign-off.

Tracking and consent come first

CRO depends on accurate data. Before any changes, an agency should check that conversions fire correctly, GA4 events are configured, and consent is handled properly under UK GDPR and PECR. Tools such as recordings and heatmaps usually need consent. Our GA4 setup guide covers the basics.

How CRO works with paid media and SEO

CRO multiplies the value of every visitor. If paid ads bring traffic at a set cost per click, a higher conversion rate lowers the cost per sale or lead directly. That is why CRO works well alongside paid media management and SEO.

There can be tension between CRO and SEO. Removing text to make a page simpler may help conversions but hurt rankings. A joined-up agency weighs both, often by restructuring content rather than deleting it.

How to choose a conversion rate optimisation agency

  1. Ask about their research process. They should describe how they find problems before suggesting solutions.
  2. Ask how they decide whether to A/B test. Good answers mention traffic, conversion volume and test length.
  3. Check how they handle tracking and consent. They should audit your tracking first.
  4. Ask what they measure. Revenue per visitor, qualified leads or profit, not just conversion rate.
  5. Ask who builds the changes. Some agencies only recommend; others implement. Know which you are buying.
  6. Review an example report. It should explain what was changed, why, and what happened.

Is a CRO agency right for you?

CRO tends to pay off when you already have steady traffic but conversions feel low, when paid media costs are rising, or when you are planning a redesign and want evidence to guide it. FAQs

What does a conversion rate optimisation agency UK firms hire do?

It researches why website visitors do not buy or enquire, fixes the problems it finds and, where traffic allows, A/B tests changes, with the aim of increasing sales or leads from existing traffic.

What is a good conversion rate?

It varies widely by sector, offer, device and traffic source, so general benchmarks can mislead. Track your own rate over time and by channel, and focus on the weakest step in your funnel.

Do I need lots of traffic for CRO?

You need traffic for reliable A/B testing, but not for CRO itself. Smaller sites can still gain from research-led fixes measured before and after.

How long does CRO take to show results?

Fixes to clear problems can show results quickly. Testing programmes take longer, as each test needs enough data to reach a reliable answer.

Will CRO changes hurt my SEO?

They can if content that pages rank for is removed. A joined-up approach restructures content to be clearer rather than deleting it.

Is CRO the same as web design?

No. Web design creates the site; CRO uses evidence to improve how well it converts. The two work well together, especially before and after a redesign.
