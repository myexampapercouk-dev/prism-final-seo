Why Wroclaw brands choose Shopify

Wroclaw is one of Poland's fastest-growing business cities, with a strong base in technology, shared services, manufacturing and logistics, and a large student population. Its position in Lower Silesia, close to the German and Czech borders, makes it a natural base for brands that want to sell both in Poland and across Central Europe.

Shopify suits many of these brands because it handles hosting, security, payments and checkout reliably, so your team can focus on products and marketing. It supports several languages and currencies, connects to a wide range of apps and scales from a first collection to a large catalogue without a rebuild. For brands that plan to sell into Germany or elsewhere in the EU, that flexibility matters.

Setting up Shopify for Polish shoppers

Polish online shoppers have clear expectations. They want familiar payment options, flexible delivery and transparent costs before checkout. When we set up a store for a Wroclaw brand, we plan for:

  • Payments. Fast online bank transfers and BLIK are widely used in Poland, alongside cards and digital wallets. We help you choose a payment provider that supports the methods your customers expect.
  • Delivery. Parcel lockers are a very popular delivery choice in Poland, along with courier delivery and pick-up points. We set up shipping rates and delivery options clearly, and connect the apps your fulfilment partners support.
  • Prices and taxes. We display prices in zloty with VAT shown correctly. For how tax rules apply to your business, check official guidance or your own accountant.
  • Legal pages. Returns, terms and privacy pages must be clear and easy to find. Your own adviser should approve the wording.

Selling beyond Poland from Wroclaw

Many Wroclaw brands want to sell to Germany, Czechia and the rest of the EU. Shopify Markets lets you run several country versions from one store, each with its own currency, language and pricing. We explain how this works in our guide to Shopify Markets for Europe.

Each new market needs its own translated content, delivery rules and, ideally, its own search research, because shoppers in each country search differently. We plan the structure and brief your translators so every market launches cleanly.

Allegro and other marketplaces

Allegro is Poland's largest online marketplace, and many shoppers start their product search there rather than on Google. For many Wroclaw brands, selling on Allegro alongside a Shopify store makes sense. The store builds your brand and margin, while the marketplace brings reach.

We help optimise Allegro listings through clear titles, accurate parameters, strong images and well-structured descriptions, using content your team or translator provides in Polish. Our guide to Allegro SEO in Poland explains how listings rank. For brands selling into the Netherlands and Belgium, we can also support marketplaces such as bol.

Paid campaigns for Shopify stores

Ads can speed up growth once tracking is solid. We run Google Shopping and Performance Max campaigns, along with Meta and TikTok Ads, from the same retainer. Ad spend is paid separately to each platform. We focus first on accurate purchase tracking with correct values, because automated bidding only works when the data is reliable. Our e-commerce marketing page explains how we combine ads, SEO and store improvements.

Themes, apps and keeping the store lean

Shopify's theme store and app store make it easy to add features, but every extra app adds scripts, monthly fees and another thing that can break. Many stores we review run several apps that overlap or are no longer used, and the result is a slower site and a higher bill.

We start from a well-built theme and customise it to fit your brand, rather than piling on apps to change the look. We only add apps that solve a real problem, such as reviews, subscriptions, translations, parcel locker delivery or invoicing, and we check how each one affects page speed before it goes live. Our guide to Shopify theme customisation explains what can be changed safely without a full rebuild.

We also think about who will run the store day to day. Your team should be able to add products, update collections, change banners and run discounts without calling us. At handover we provide training and simple documentation in English, so the store stays easy to manage as your catalogue grows and new people join the team.

Migrating to Shopify from another platform

Some Wroclaw brands start on WooCommerce, PrestaShop or another platform and later move to Shopify for easier management. A careful migration protects your search rankings and customer data. We map every old URL to its new home, move products, customers and order history where possible, set up redirects and check everything on a staging store before launch.

The biggest risk in any migration is lost traffic from broken links, so we test redirects thoroughly and monitor search performance closely in the weeks after launch.

How a remote Shopify project works

  1. Discovery. We learn about your products, customers, markets, payments and fulfilment, and agree the scope.
