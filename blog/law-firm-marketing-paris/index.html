Who this guide is for

Paris has a very large legal market. It includes international firms, French business law firms, boutique practices in areas such as employment, tax, real estate, intellectual property and family law, and many firms that advise English-speaking clients and international companies. This guide is most useful for firms that want English-language marketing, serve international clients, or want a clear, well-structured website and search presence.

For the wider principles that apply to any legal practice, see our law firm marketing guide. Many of the same ideas work in Paris, with local rules in mind.

How clients look for a lawyer in Paris

Business clients often start with referrals, then check the firm's website and the lawyers' LinkedIn profiles. Private clients are more likely to search Google for a practice area and location, such as "employment lawyer Paris" or "English-speaking divorce lawyer Paris". In both cases, the website is where trust is won or lost.

English-speaking expats, international companies setting up in France and foreign investors often search in English. That gives firms with English-language pages a real opportunity, because fewer competitors cover these searches well.

A website that builds trust

A law firm website should explain clearly what you do, who you help and how to contact you. Good sites include a page for each practice area, profiles of your lawyers with their qualifications, office and contact details, and a clear statement about fees or how fees are discussed, where your rules allow it.

Design should be clean and calm. Visitors are often worried or busy, so the site should be easy to read on a phone and quick to load. Our article on solicitor website design covers the layout principles, and our website development service can build or rebuild your site. Small websites cost £1,000 to £2,000 and typically take three to five weeks.

If you serve both French and English speakers, the site should have separate language versions with correct hreflang tags. Our guide to multilingual website design explains how to set this up. French pages should come from your team or a legal translator, as precise legal wording matters.

SEO for practice areas

Each practice area deserves its own page, written for the questions clients actually ask. A page on employment law, for example, might explain the types of matters you handle, who you act for, how the process works and how to get in touch. Avoid promises about outcomes.

Technical SEO matters too: fast loading, clean URLs, a sitemap, structured data for your firm and a well-kept Google Business Profile for your office. Our SEO service covers both content and technical work. For more on search in the city, see our article on the SEO approach in Paris.

Paid ads within professional rules

Lawyers in France are subject to professional conduct rules that cover how they communicate and advertise. In general terms, communication should be honest, accurate and dignified, and should not mislead or make comparisons that are unfair. The detail is set by the profession's own rules and your bar, so check official guidance before running any campaign.

Within those limits, Google Ads can work well for practice areas with clear search demand, and LinkedIn Ads can reach in-house counsel, founders and finance directors. We write English ad copy and set up campaigns, and your firm approves every line before launch. Our guide on LinkedIn Ads versus Google Ads for B2B helps decide where to start, and our article on the Google Ads approach in Paris covers paid search in the city.

Turning enquiries into clients

Marketing only works if enquiries are handled well. Make sure every form and call is answered quickly, that conflict checks happen early and that potential clients know what happens next. Short, clear forms usually work better than long ones. Our article on conversion rate optimisation for law firms explains how to improve this step.

Confidentiality matters at every stage. Forms should collect only what is needed to make first contact, link to a clear privacy policy and be stored securely in line with GDPR.

Content that shows expertise

For many law firms, useful articles and guides are the most effective long-term marketing. A clear explanation of a new rule, a guide to setting up a company in France or a checklist for international employers can attract the exact clients you want, and it shows your expertise without making claims about results.

Good legal content has a few things in common. It answers a real question, it is accurate and dated, it explains things in plain language and it tells the reader when they need personal advice. It also avoids anything that could be read as advice given to a specific person.

A simple content plan for a Paris firm might include:

  1. One cornerstone guide for each main practice area, written for the client's questions.
  2. Shorter articles that explain changes in law or common situations.
  3. English-language guides for international clients and expats, a gap many firms leave open.
  4. Regular LinkedIn posts that summarise each article and link back to the website.

We can draft English articles based on notes or outlines from your lawyers, and every piece is checked and approved by the firm before publishing. French articles should be written by your team or translated by a legal translator. Each article should link to the relevant practice area page so readers can take the next step.

Over time, this content builds search visibility, supports your LinkedIn activity and gives existing clients something worth sharing. It also helps AI search tools understand what your firm does, which is becoming more important as more people ask those tools for recommendations.
