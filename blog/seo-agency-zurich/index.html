Search behaviour in Switzerland

Google is the main search engine in Switzerland, and most people search on google.ch. That sounds simple, but Swiss search has some features that make SEO different from a single-language market.

First, language. Zurich is in the German-speaking part of the country, but written Swiss Standard German differs from German used in Germany in small ways. One example is that Swiss spelling uses "ss" where Germany uses the sharp s. People also search in English, especially for technology, finance and business services. If you serve clients in Geneva, Lausanne or Lugano, you may also need French or Italian pages.

Second, competition. Many Swiss searches return results from German and Austrian websites, which have larger markets and often more links. A Zurich business can compete by being clearly Swiss: local pricing in francs, Swiss contact details, references to Swiss delivery and service areas, and pages written for Swiss readers.

Third, buyers here often research carefully before they get in touch. Detailed service pages, clear pricing guidance and honest answers to common questions tend to work better than thin pages with a contact form.

Our guide to keyword research explains how we group search terms by intent, which we apply to each language you target.

Multilingual SEO and hreflang

Most Zurich businesses that grow beyond the city end up with a site in two or more languages. Done well, each language version ranks for its own audience. Done badly, Google may show the wrong version, or treat the pages as duplicates.

We set up language versions on clear URLs, add correct hreflang tags so Google knows which page suits which audience, and make sure each version has properly translated titles, headings and metadata. Prism writes in English. Content in German, French or Italian comes from your team or a translator, and we brief them on keywords and page structure so the translated pages are built to rank.

For a deeper look at this, see our guides to international SEO and multilingual website design.

Technical SEO for Swiss websites

Technical problems hold back many well-written sites. Our technical work usually starts with a full audit, followed by a prioritised list of fixes. Common issues we see on Swiss sites include:

  • Language versions that redirect visitors automatically based on location, which can stop Google crawling them.
  • Slow pages caused by large images, heavy scripts or cheap hosting.
  • Old URLs left without redirects after a redesign.
  • Missing or incorrect structured data for organisations, products, services and FAQs.
  • Thin location or service pages that compete with each other.

Our technical SEO audit checklist sets out what we check, and our guide to Core Web Vitals explains the speed measures Google uses.

Local SEO in Zurich

If clients visit your office, shop or venue, local SEO matters. We set up and improve your Google Business Profile, make sure your name, address and phone number match across the web, and build local pages for the areas you actually serve. We do not create fake addresses or pages for places you do not serve.

Local SEO also covers reviews. We help you set up a simple, honest process for asking happy customers for reviews and replying to them. Read more in our local SEO guide.

Privacy, cookies and accessibility

SEO work touches analytics, tracking and site changes, so it is worth keeping the rules in mind. These notes are general information, not legal advice. Please check official guidance or speak to your own adviser.

  • Swiss data protection. Switzerland is not in the EU and has its own data protection law, the revised Federal Act on Data Protection. If you also target people in the EU, the GDPR may apply to that data as well.
  • Cookie consent. If you serve EU visitors, EU ePrivacy rules generally require consent before non-essential cookies, such as analytics and advertising tags, are set. We set up analytics so it respects the consent choices your banner records. Our guide to cookie banner design covers the basics.
  • Accessibility. The European Accessibility Act has applied to many consumer-facing digital products and services sold in the EU since June 2025. Microenterprises that provide services are generally exempt. The usual technical reference is EN 301 549, which points to WCAG at level AA. Swiss firms selling into the EU should check whether it affects them.
