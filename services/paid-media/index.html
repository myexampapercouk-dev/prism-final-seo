How it works

What a PPC agency in the UK should do for your ad spend

A PPC agency in the UK should make sure your tracking is accurate, put your budget behind the searches and audiences most likely to buy, and keep cutting waste every week. Good paid media management is less about launching campaigns and more about the steady decisions that follow: which terms to exclude, which ads to rotate out, and where the next pound of spend should go.

This guide explains how paid media management works with Prism Works Studio, how to judge whether an account is being run well, and what to ask before you hire anyone. For market-wide fee ranges, see our guide to PPC management costs in the UK.

How paid media management works

Measurement before budget

Ad platforms optimise towards whatever you tell them is a conversion. If that signal is wrong, for example counting page views as leads or missing purchases entirely, automated bidding will happily spend more on the wrong thing. So the first job is checking GA4, platform conversion tags and, for shops, the Merchant Center feed.

Structure around margin and intent

Campaigns are then organised around what matters commercially: high-margin products, priority services, or the locations you actually cover. Brand and non-brand search are usually kept apart so you can see the real cost of winning new customers.

Weekly optimisation

Ongoing management means reviewing search terms, adding negatives, testing ad copy, adjusting budgets between campaigns and checking landing page performance. Smart bidding and Performance Max automate some of this, but they still need clean data, sensible exclusions and human judgement about what the business can afford.

Signs an ad account needs attention

If you already run ads, these are common signals that money is being wasted:

  • Search term reports full of irrelevant or competitor queries.
  • Conversions that do not match the leads or orders you actually receive.
  • All traffic sent to the home page rather than a matched landing page.
  • Brand searches reported as wins, hiding the true cost of new customers.
  • No changes logged in the account for weeks at a time.
  • Reports that show clicks and impressions but not cost per lead or return on ad spend.

Our PPC audit checklist walks through each of these so you can check an account yourself.

How to choose a paid media agency: questions to ask

  1. How will you check tracking before spending more? A good answer names specific checks, not just “we will set up GA4”.
  2. Who owns the ad accounts? They should be in your name, with the agency given access.
  3. How do you report? Look for cost per lead, cost per sale or return on ad spend, explained in plain English.
  4. How often is the account worked on? Ask what a typical week of optimisation includes.
  5. Is ad spend separate from the fee? It should be, and you should pay platforms directly.
  6. What happens to landing pages? Ads can only convert as well as the page they point to; see our guide to ecommerce conversion rate optimisation.
  7. What is a realistic test budget? Platforms need enough data to learn, so ask how long the test period is and what success looks like.

Landing pages, creative and the rest of the funnel

Many underperforming accounts are not really an ads problem. The targeting is reasonable, but the page that follows the click loses the visitor. A landing page should match the promise in the ad, load quickly on mobile, show the price or next step clearly and remove distractions such as full site navigation where it is not needed.

Creative matters just as much on social platforms, where people are not searching for you. Meta campaigns usually need several images or short videos in rotation, refreshed as performance drops. For shops, product feeds act as the creative for Shopping ads, so titles, images and attributes deserve the same care as ad copy.

We look at the whole path from impression to enquiry or sale, because improving the conversion rate of a page lowers the cost of every click that lands on it.

Offline steps count too. For lead generation, a phone call or a form that never gets followed up is a lost sale, whatever the ad cost. Where possible, we feed qualified leads or completed sales back into the ad platforms as offline conversions, so bidding learns which clicks turn into real customers rather than just form fills.

How remote paid media projects run across the UK

Paid media is managed entirely online, so location makes no difference to how the work runs. We work with businesses across the UK through video calls, shared reports and access to your ad accounts.

  1. Discovery call covering goals, margins, average order or lead value, and current accounts.
  2. Access and audit of ad platforms, GA4 and any feeds, with findings shared in writing.
  3. Tracking fixes before budgets increase.
  4. Launch or restructure of campaigns, with ad copy and landing pages agreed with you.
  5. Ongoing optimisation and monthly review of spend, results and next tests.

Paid campaigns can start generating traffic within days, but stable, efficient performance typically takes 3–6 months of testing and refinement, depending on budget, competition and how quickly the platforms gather data.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is ad spend included in your fee?

No. Advertising spend is paid separately by the client, directly to the ad platform. Our fee covers strategy, set-up and management.

Which platform should I start with?

For most businesses with existing demand, Google Search or Google Shopping comes first because people are already looking. Meta and TikTok suit products that sell visually; LinkedIn suits B2B.

How quickly will paid ads work?

Ads can bring traffic from day one, but campaigns need a learning period. Expect the first weeks to be about testing, then steady improvement as data builds.

Can you take over my existing Google Ads account?

Yes. We start with an audit of tracking, structure and search terms, then fix wasted spend before making bigger changes. You keep ownership of the account.

Do you build landing pages for ads?

Yes. Landing page design is part of our website service, and pages are built around a single goal and tested to improve conversion rates.

What should I expect from a PPC agency in the UK?

Accurate tracking, campaigns structured around your margins and priorities, regular search term and bid reviews, ad copy testing and reports that show cost per lead or return on ad spend in plain English.

What does a paid media agency do that I cannot do in-house?

Mostly time and focus: daily account checks, structured testing, feed and tracking fixes, and experience of how campaigns behave across many accounts. Many businesses run ads themselves well once the set-up is right.

How much ad spend do I need to start?

Enough for the platform to gather meaningful data over a test period. That depends on your click costs and conversion rate, so we suggest a test budget after looking at your market rather than quoting a fixed figure.

Do you set up conversion tracking?

Yes. Tracking set-up is included in the Growth and Scale retainers, and we check it before any budget is increased.

Will the ad accounts be in my name?

Yes. Ad accounts, billing and data stay with you, and we work through granted access.

Do you manage Microsoft Ads as well as Google Ads?

Yes. Microsoft Ads is often a sensible extension once Google Search is working, as campaigns can be imported and adapted.
