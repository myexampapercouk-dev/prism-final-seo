How it works

From idea to coverage

1. Research

We look at what your audience searches, what journalists in your sector cover and where competitors earn links.

2. Plan

A content calendar and PR angles agreed with you, with the number of posts and channels defined up front.

3. Create

Articles, guides and press materials written in UK English, checked for accuracy and aligned to your brand voice.

4. Promote and report

Outreach to relevant publications, internal linking from your own site, and reporting on coverage, links and traffic.

Why it matters

Content and links are what move rankings

Good technical SEO gets your site ready to rank, but authority decides who wins competitive searches. Quality links and brand mentions from relevant websites are one of the clearest signals of authority, and they also feed the sources AI assistants rely on.

Our approach is simple: create things worth covering, then tell the right people about them. We do not buy links or use private blog networks. For a practical overview, read our guide to digital PR for link building in the UK, and see how it connects with SEO and GEO.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between digital PR and traditional PR?

Digital PR focuses on online coverage that earns links, mentions and search visibility, and its impact can be measured in rankings and referral traffic.

Do you guarantee a number of links?

No. Coverage depends on editors and journalists. We never promise results we cannot control, but we do report every piece of coverage and link earned.

How many blog posts do I need?

Our retainers include 4, 8 or 12 posts a month. Quality and relevance matter more than volume, so we agree the right number for your goals.

Who writes the content?

Content is researched and written to brief in UK English, and reviewed against your input so facts about your business are accurate.

Can you update my old blog posts?

Yes. Refreshing posts that already have some visibility is often quicker to pay off than writing new ones.
