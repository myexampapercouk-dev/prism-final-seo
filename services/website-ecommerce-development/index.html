Ecommerce website development in the UK: how to plan a build that sells

Ecommerce website development in the UK means choosing the right platform, structuring your catalogue so shoppers and search engines can find products, and building a fast, simple checkout that works on mobile. Whether you need a Shopify agency in the UK or a WooCommerce or WordPress build, the decisions made before design starts usually matter more than the design itself.

This guide explains how Prism Works Studio plans and runs website and ecommerce projects, what each option involves, and what to check before you hire anyone. For budget ranges across the market, read our guide to ecommerce website costs in the UK.

What goes into an ecommerce build

Catalogue and navigation

Category structure shapes everything: menus, filters, URLs and which pages can rank. We plan categories around how shoppers search and browse, then decide which filters should be indexable and which should not.

Product pages

Product pages need clear images, accurate variants, delivery and returns information, and unique copy. Structured data helps search engines show prices and availability.

Checkout and payments

The fewer steps and surprises at checkout, the better. Delivery costs, payment options and guest checkout should be clear before a shopper reaches the final page.

Integrations

Stock, shipping, email marketing, reviews and accounting tools often need connecting. Each app or plugin adds cost and maintenance, so we agree integrations during scoping rather than adding them late.

Tracking and SEO foundations

GA4, conversion events, a clean sitemap, sensible redirects and fast templates are set up before launch, so marketing can start on solid ground.

Protecting your rankings during a rebuild

A new site can lose years of search visibility in a weekend if URLs change without a plan. Before any redesign or migration we crawl the existing site, record which pages bring in traffic and links, map every important old URL to its new equivalent, and check redirects after launch. Our guide to redesigning without losing rankings explains the process step by step.

How to choose a Shopify agency or web developer: questions to ask

  • What exactly is in scope? Pages, templates, product uploads, apps and revisions should be written down.
  • Who owns the site and accounts? Domain, hosting, platform and theme licences should be in your name.
  • How will you handle redirects and SEO? Any rebuild of an existing site needs a URL map.
  • How will the site be tested? Ask about mobile, speed, accessibility and test orders through checkout.
  • Can my team update it? Training and simple editing should be part of handover.
  • What happens after launch? Ask about fixes, updates and support; our guide to website maintenance covers what ongoing care involves.
  • Which apps will I pay for monthly? Platform and app subscriptions add to running costs.

Shopify, WooCommerce or WordPress: deciding factors

Platform choice is easier when you focus on how you will run the business day to day, rather than feature lists.

Who will maintain the site?

Shopify is hosted, so updates and security are handled by the platform. WooCommerce and WordPress give more control but need hosting, plugin updates and backups, either by your team or through a maintenance plan.

How many products and variants?

Both Shopify and WooCommerce handle small and large catalogues, but complex variants, bundles or custom pricing may need apps or plugins. We check this during scoping so the platform fits the catalogue.

Do you already have content on WordPress?

If your blog and service pages already sit on WordPress, WooCommerce can keep everything in one place. If you are starting fresh with a product brand, Shopify is often simpler to run.

What will it cost to run?

Build cost is only part of the picture. Add platform subscriptions, paid apps or plugins, hosting and payment fees to understand the true monthly cost before you commit.

Preparing for your project

Projects move fastest when a few things are ready before design starts:

  • A list of pages or product categories you need.
  • Product data, images and prices in a spreadsheet.
  • Logo files, brand colours and any existing guidelines.
  • Delivery rates, returns policy and payment options.
  • Logins for your domain, current site and analytics.
  • Two or three websites you like, and why.

Delays in content and feedback are the most common reason builds run longer than planned, so we agree deadlines for both sides at the start.

After launch: turning a new site into sales

A new store is a starting point. Most of the gains come from what happens next: improving category pages for search, testing product page layouts, and reducing drop-off at checkout. Our guide to ecommerce conversion rate optimisation covers the most common fixes. Ongoing marketing, including SEO and content, can run on one of our monthly retainers.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I update the website content myself?

Yes. We build on user-friendly platforms like Shopify, WooCommerce and WordPress, so you can manage products, update content and run promotions without coding. We also provide training.

How long will my project take?

A standard website typically takes 3–5 weeks, while ecommerce builds range from 5–12 weeks depending on catalogue size and custom functionality.

Can you redesign my existing website or store?

Yes. We audit your existing site for performance, UX, conversion and SEO issues, then build a new version, with redirects planned to protect rankings.

Do you offer maintenance and support?

Yes. We offer ongoing maintenance for websites and stores, covering platform updates, security monitoring, performance and support.

Do you migrate stores to Shopify?

Yes, including Shopify Plus migrations. We move products, customers and content where possible and map redirects from old URLs.

How much does ecommerce website development in the UK cost?

Our ecommerce sites start from £1,500–£3,500, with product volume and apps scoped separately. Platform subscriptions and paid apps are ongoing costs on top of the build.

What should I look for in a Shopify agency in the UK?

A written scope, accounts and licences in your name, a plan for redirects and SEO, proper checkout testing and training at handover. Ask what monthly app costs the build will add.

Do I need to provide product photos and descriptions?

Usually you supply product data and images. We agree in the scope who writes copy and who uploads products, so there are no surprises later.

Can you connect my store to stock or accounting systems?

Often, yes, through platform apps or plugins. Integrations are agreed during scoping because each one affects cost, testing and ongoing maintenance.

Will my new site be set up for SEO and tracking?

Yes. Builds include sensible URL structure, sitemaps, structured data, redirects where needed and GA4 conversion tracking before launch.

Who owns the website once it is built?

You do. Domain, hosting, platform account and theme licences should all be in your name, with us given access to do the work.
